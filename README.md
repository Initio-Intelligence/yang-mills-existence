# Four-dimensional Yang–Mills fields from the Wilson measure

**Initio Intelligence**

*Partial results toward the Yang–Mills existence problem.*

[Long manuscript with proofs (714 pages)](paper/Yang_Mills_Manuscript_with_Proofs.pdf) · [Editable sources by chapter](paper/LONG_MANUSCRIPT_CONTENTS.md)

[Concise paper (16 pages)](paper/Yang_Mills_Existence.pdf) · [LaTeX source](paper/Yang_Mills_Existence.tex) · [Theorem catalogue and open problems](THEOREMS_AND_OPEN_PROBLEMS.md)

The question is whether, for a fixed group $SU(N)$, the 21 gauge-invariant curvature bilinears of four-dimensional Wilson lattice theory admit a common, nontrivial continuum limit with the normalization, covariance, ultraviolet coefficients and field domains specified in the paper. The paper gives a **conditional existence theorem**. The estimates required to realize its hypotheses for the Wilson measure have not all been proved. A positive spectral gap is not a conclusion.

## Reading the construction

1. **Start with the conditional theorem.** The standalone 16-page paper is also pp. 5–20 of the long manuscript. Read either copy. It fixes the fields and the normalization, states the main theorem on its first page, and shows how the hypotheses lead to a continuum limit and reconstruction.
2. **Locate the proofs and their limits.** Pages 21–575 contain all 65 annexes A–BM. Pages 576–699 collect further derivations on the physical scale, boundary derivatives, transport and continuum estimates. Use the [chapter concordance](paper/LONG_MANUSCRIPT_CONTENTS.md) for editable texts and the subject index below for additional proofs outside the volume.
3. **Choose an open theorem.** Read the [scope of the main estimates](SCOPE.md), then the [theorem catalogue](THEOREMS_AND_OPEN_PROBLEMS.md), also on pp. 700–714. Each numbered entry gives the theorem sought, the scope of the available result, the required conclusion and its dependencies. The 64 entries include established finite identities, conditional implications and open estimates. GC and CL state two further common hypotheses.

Three concrete places to continue are the **signed transverse Gaussian remainder** in GC (pp. 576–581), the **anchored exterior derivative with earlier innovations fixed** in the all-history application of C-05 (pp. 623–626), and **uniform transport beyond three regular integrations** in M-04 (pp. 635–656). Each can be examined separately. To use the resulting estimates together, they must hold for the same Wilson law, field normalization and cutoff sequence.

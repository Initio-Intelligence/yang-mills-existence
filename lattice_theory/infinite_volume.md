## 0. Status, in one paragraph (read first)

The main theorem of this paper (Theorem M, §3.7) proves I-6(a)–(c) **conditionally**, on two displayed hypotheses about Bałaban's construction, (H1) and (H2) of §3.3; the chessboard/reflection-positivity/limit-point chain is proved (P)/(L) in full, with the required convention checks. Two things in this paper are new and change the program. **First (Theorem 3.1, (P)):** the interfaces I-0a and I-0b, *as stated*, with one common E_vac(a) and a-independent constants, are **jointly inconsistent** for Bałaban's small-field thresholds. The reason is elementary and is proved from the primary text of *Large field renormalization I* (CMP 122, p. 175, (0.1)): the first-step small-field condition is |U(∂p) − 1| ≤ g_0 p_0(g_0) with p_0(g_0) = A_0 (log g_0^{−2})^{p_0}, and a conditional-probability argument shows that restricting the Wilson measure to this event costs at least Vol(T) a^{−4} exp(−C p_0(g_0)^2) in log Z, which along any trajectory with g_0(a)^{−2} = O(log(1/a)) is ≫ Vol(T). Hence the large-field sector carries a vacuum-energy density that diverges as a → 0, Bałaban's counterterm cannot be "the accumulated small-field vacuum energy", and the route of [M-loc] Proposition 2.7 ("lower bound on the large torus by restriction to the small-field sector") **cannot** supply LB(T) with the E_vac(a) of UB(T*). The simplification that only the small-field sector need be uniform in T is therefore unavailable; this is recorded as a negative result. **Second (Theorem 3.2, (P)):** what replaces it costs nothing new in principle: because the Wilson theory on Λ_{a,T} is identical to the theory on Λ_{a/T, 1}, *depth-uniformity* of Bałaban's fixed-torus density bounds at intermediate steps is the same thing as *torus-uniformity* at the fixed physical scale ℓ_0. So the uniform-in-T input is exactly (H1): the intermediate-step bounds of LFR II Theorem 1 with constants independent of the step, plus (H2): the common-scalar comparison (9) of [M-rem] for the **complete** finite-torus scalar (Gaussian, nonlinear E-terms **and** the large-field R′′-terms). For (H2) this paper proves (Theorem 3.4) that it follows from a naturality-under-copying property (N_k) and a decay property (D_k) of the per-step scalar recipe, proves the Gaussian-determinant, coordinate-measure and zero-mode parts of (N_k)/(D_k) outright, proves the summation over scales, and reduces the rest to a check of specified formulas in CMP 109, 119 and 122-II, whose required hypotheses remain to be verified in the cited primary articles. Everything with observables remains a fixed-volume (T*) statement, as in [M-loc] and [M-rem]; what is *not* fixed-volume is Bałaban's large-field analysis, which must be run on every torus up to scale ℓ_0 — by (H1), via scaling, it already is.

## 2. Definitions and conventions

2.1 **Lattice, measure, pressure.** G = SU(N), d_G = N² − 1, |X| = (tr X^*X)^{1/2} the Hilbert–Schmidt norm, ‖X‖ the operator norm. Λ_{a,T} = (aZ/TZ)^4, T/a ∈ 2N. Links b, plaquettes p; U_p the ordered plaquette product. Wilson measure dμ_{a,T} = Z_{a,T}^{−1} e^{−β_a S} dHaar, S = Σ_p (N − Re tr U_p), β_a = 2N/g_0(a)^2, normalized Haar on each link. p_{a,T} = Vol(T)^{−1} log Z_{a,T}. Sourced versions Z_{a,T}[J], p_{a,T}[J] with a source J coupled linearly to a family of local gauge-invariant lattice observables; the renormalized sourced generating function G_T(J) = e^{−L_a(J)} Z_{a,T}[J]/Z_{a,T}[0] with the local counterterm functional L_a of I-5(a). Renormalized Schwinger functions are the derivatives of G_T at J = 0 (I-3/I-5 conventions).

2.2 **Scaling identity (P, trivial).** For λ > 0 the map x ↦ λx is a graph isomorphism Λ_{a,T} → Λ_{λa,λT}, and the Wilson weight depends only on the graph and on β. Hence Z_{a,T}(β) = Z_{λa,λT}(β) and p_{λa,λT} = λ^{−4} p_{a,T}. In particular the theory on the torus of side T with spacing a *is* the theory on the unit torus with spacing a/T at the same bare coupling.

2.3 **Scales.** L odd ≥ 3; ℓ_k = L^k a; ℓ_0 fixed; K = log_L(ℓ_0/a); t* = n_0 ℓ_0 with n_0 ∈ 2N the side of the reference torus T*; T = qT*. Cells: side ℓ_c with t*/ℓ_c = n_* ∈ 2N, faces on lattice site-planes x_i ∈ ℓ_c Z; n_T = q n_* cells per direction of T (even). N_R := Vol(R)/ℓ_0^4 for a torus R.

2.4 **Reflections and replication.** For a cell face plane Π = {x_i = s ℓ_c}, θ_Π is the lattice reflection through Π; on the torus it fixes Π and the antipodal plane Π′ = {x_i = sℓ_c + T/2} (T/2 ∈ ℓ_c Z because n_T is even). Θ_Π F := conj(F ∘ θ_Π) (antilinear). For a function F_c of the links of the closed cell c̄ (both endpoints in c̄), the **dissemination** D F_c := Π_{c′} Θ_{c→c′} F_c, where Θ_{c→c′} is any composition of face reflections carrying c to c′ (well defined: compositions differ by translations by 2ℓ_c Z^4 and by an even number of conjugations). For F_c = exp(J_c·O) with a real local observable O covariant under lattice reflections, D F_c = exp((W J_c)·O) with **W J_c** = J_c reflected into every cell and complex-conjugated in the cells at odd reflection distance. "Margin": supp J_c is at distance ≥ r_O from the faces, r_O ≥ the support radius of O, so that e^{J_c·O} is a function of the links of c̄ only.

2.5 **Bałaban's small-field sets and the step-K representation (names only).** χ_1, …, χ_K are the small-field characteristic functions of I-0a; by [B122I, p. 175, (0.1)] the first-step large-field condition is |U(∂p) − 1| ≥ g_0 p_0(g_0), p_0(g) = A_0 (log g^{−2})^{p_0}, p_0 ∈ N, so {χ_1 = 1} ⊂ E_δ := {U : |U_p − 1| ≤ δ for all plaquettes p} with δ = c_N g_0 p_0(g_0) (c_N converts Bałaban's matrix norm to the HS norm). Z^{sf}_{a,T} := ∫ χ_1 ⋯ χ_K e^{−β_aS} dHaar (written through the block-spin variables as ∫ dU_K χ_K ρ^{sf}_{K,T}). The *complete* step-K representation on a torus R is written, as in [M-rem] (8), Z_{a,R} = e^{A_a(R)} Ξ_{a,R}, Ξ_{a,R} = ∫ ρ̃_{K,R} dν_R, with A_a(R) the finite-torus scalar (all identity-values subtracted during the K steps: Gaussian normalizers, coordinate measures, nonlinear E-terms, large-field R′′-terms) and ν_R normalized Haar on the step-K links.

## 3. Main theorems

### 3.1 The large-field sector carries a divergent vacuum-energy density (P)

**Theorem 3.1 (large-field entropy).** There are constants c_N, C_N > 0 depending only on N such that for every a, T with T/a ≥ 3, every β > 0 and every 0 < δ ≤ 1/6,

  μ_{a,T}(E_δ) ≤ exp( − (Vol(T)/(3a^4)) · ε_N(β, δ) ),   ε_N(β, δ) := c_N δ^{d_G} e^{−108 β δ²}.

Consequently, for every family of small-field functions χ_1, …, χ_K with {χ_1 = 1} ⊂ E_δ,

  log Z_{a,T} − log Z^{sf}_{a,T} ≥ −log μ_{a,T}(E_δ) ≥ (Vol(T)/(3a^4)) ε_N(β_a, δ).            (3.1)

With Bałaban's threshold δ = c_N g_0 p_0(g_0) and β_a = 2N/g_0², the right side equals Vol(T) · ℓ_0^{−4} L^{4K} exp(−C′_N p_0(g_0)² − C″_N log g_0^{−2}) with p_0(g_0)² = A_0² (log g_0^{−2})^{2p_0}; along any trajectory with g_0(a)^{−2} ≤ C log(ℓ_0/a) (I-4 gives g_0^{−2} = b_0 log(ℓ_0/a) + O(log log)) this is Vol(T) ℓ_0^{−4} exp(4K log L − O((log K)^{2p_0})) → ∞ as a → 0.

**Corollary 3.1′ (I-0a and I-0b are jointly unsatisfiable as stated; P).** Suppose I-0a(A1)(A2)(A3) and I-0b hold for a → 0 with one E_vac(a), with constants C, C′, δ_0, C_+ independent of a, and with the δ_0-ball B_{δ_0} = {U_K : |U_K(b) − 1| ≤ δ_0 ∀b} contained in {χ_K = 1} (implicit in (A2)). Then for T = T*, Proposition 4.3 (the crude step) gives log Z^{sf}_{a,T*} ≥ −Vol(T*)E_vac(a) − C_−Vol(T*), I-0b gives log Z_{a,T*} ≤ −Vol(T*)E_vac(a) + C_+Vol(T*), hence log Z_{a,T*} − log Z^{sf}_{a,T*} ≤ (C_+ + C_−)Vol(T*), contradicting (3.1) for a small. The same contradiction arises for any choice of the first-step threshold δ_1(a) with limsup_{a→0} β_a δ_1(a)² / log(ℓ_0/a) < 4/108: only thresholds of order one (β_aδ² ≳ log(1/a), the non-perturbative, compact-group scale) make the restriction to the small-field sector cost O(Vol). **Binding consequence (R5):** the route "LB(T) by restriction of the positive integrand to the small-field sector, UB(T*) by Bałaban at fixed volume, same counterterm" ([M-loc] Prop. 2.7, Theorem B scope (i); plan §2 and I-0b scope check (i)) is dead. The vacuum energy of the large-field sector, ≈ Vol Σ_k ℓ_k^{−4} e^{−p_0(g_k)·O(1)}, is dominated by the finest scales and is not O(Vol); this is the quantitative content of Bałaban's remark [B122I, p. 175] that the small factor "does not give any positive power of ε".

### 3.2 Depth-uniformity equals torus-uniformity (P)

**Theorem 3.2 (scaling transfer).** Let R(ε; k) denote any statement about the step-k block-spin density of the Wilson theory on the unit torus Λ_{ε,1} (spacing ε, side 1, bare coupling g_0(ε) on the trajectory of I-4), of the form "ρ̃_k ≤ e^{b_+ N_k} ν-a.e. and ρ̃_k ≥ e^{−b_−N_k} on a ν-set of measure ≥ e^{−b_0N_k}, where N_k = number of step-k sites and Z_{ε,1} = e^{A(ε;k)} ∫ρ̃_k dν", with constants b_± , b_0 independent of ε and of k (as long as g_j ≤ ḡ for j ≤ k). Then for every torus T = qT* and every a: the same statement holds for the step-K density of the theory on Λ_{a,T} with the same constants, with N_K = N_T = Vol(T)/ℓ_0^4 and A_a(T) := A(a/T; K). *Proof.* §4.2. ∎

So the only torus-dependence that can survive is in the scalar A_a(T); the "infrared problem" of running the coupling to the scale T never arises because the recursion is stopped at step K on every torus.

### 3.3 The two hypotheses, and S94 from them (P given H1, H2)

**(H1) [Bałaban's complete step-K bounds, depth-uniform; status (L*) + (C)].** For all ε and all k with g_j ≤ ḡ (j ≤ k): the statement R(ε; k) of Theorem 3.2 holds for the complete step-k density of Bałaban's construction (large fields included, after the R-operation), with A(ε;k) his finite-torus scalar (identity-values of all Gaussian normalizers, coordinate measures, E-terms and R′′-terms generated in steps ≤ k). [M-rem] §3 reports this as the content of [B122II, Theorem 1 and (0.1), pp. 355–356] ("constants independent of depth k, torus, and coarse field, assuming the effective couplings remain sufficiently small"). Not verified here.

**(H2) [complete-scalar comparison = (A3′); status (C), reduced in Theorem 3.4].** There are E_a ∈ R and C_sc < ∞ independent of a and of the torus such that for every compatible R ⊇ T*: |A_a(R)/Vol(R) − E_a| ≤ C_sc ℓ_0^{−4}. (This is [M-rem] (9).)

**Theorem 3.3 (S94 and two-sided stability).** Under (H1) and (H2), for every compatible torus R ⊇ T* and every a on the trajectory,
  |p_{a,R} − E_a| ≤ (B + C_sc) ℓ_0^{−4},  B := max(b_+, b_− + b_0),
hence I-6(a): p_{a,T*} − p_{a,qT*} ≤ C_0 := 2(B + C_sc)ℓ_0^{−4}, and also 0 ≤ p_{a,T*} − p_{a,qT*} (RP, Theorem 3.5(ii)). The stability input |log Z_{a,R} − V_{a,R}| ≤ C_st Vol(R) of (SV3)–(SV4) ([source estimates](source_estimates/README.md), printed p. 630) is supplied with V_{a,R} := A_a(R), C_st = Bℓ_0^{−4}. *Proof.* §4.3. ∎

### 3.4 The copying lemma for the complete scalar (P for the abstract statement and for its Gaussian/measure/zero-mode parts; (N_k),(D_k) to be checked)

**Definition (scalar recipe; carriers).** For a torus R and step k, Λ^{(k)}_R = (ℓ_kZ/RZ)^4 is the step-k lattice; a *carrier* X is a finite connected union of step-k cubes (M-cubes in Bałaban's language, M fixed) in Λ^{(k)}_R, with tree-length d(X) (length, in step-k units, of the shortest tree through the cubes of X) and |X| cubes. A *scalar recipe* assigns to each R, k, X a number e_k^{(R)}(X) ∈ R such that

  A_a(R) = Σ_{k ≤ K} Σ_{X ⊂ Λ^{(k)}_R} e_k^{(R)}(X).                                           (3.2)

(e_k^{(R)}(X) is the identity-value of the step-k term localized at X: Gaussian log-normalizer density, measure factor, −E_k(X, 1), −R″_k(X, 1), each produced by Bałaban's operations.) Let π: Λ_T → Λ_{T*} be the covering map; it induces coverings of all step-k lattices because t* ∈ ℓ_0 Z = ℓ_K Z ⊂ ℓ_k Z.

**(N_k) Naturality under copying.** There is ρ_k ≥ 1 (a fixed multiple of Bałaban's localization radius at step k, allowed to grow polynomially in r = K − k) such that: if X ⊂ Λ^{(k)}_{T*} has diam(X) + 2ρ_k < t*/(2ℓ_k), then for every lift X′ ⊂ Λ^{(k)}_T of X (π(X′) = X, π|_{X′} injective), e_k^{(T)}(X′) = e_k^{(T*)}(X).

**(D_k) Decay.** For every torus R, every step-k site x, every D ≥ 0: Σ_{X ∋ x, d(X) ≥ D} |e_k^{(R)}(X)| ≤ C_dec (1 + r)^u exp(−κ D/(1 + r)^v), with C_dec, κ, u, v independent of a, R, k.

**Theorem 3.4 (copying ⇒ (H2)).** (i) (P) If (N_k) and (D_k) hold for all k ≤ K with ρ_k ≤ ρ_0(1+r)^v, then (H2) holds with E_a := A_a(T*)/Vol(T*) and C_sc = C_sc(L, κ, u, v, ρ_0, t*/ℓ_0) independent of a and q; moreover the same bound holds between any two compatible tori R, R′ ⊇ T*. (ii) (P) The Gaussian-normalizer part of the recipe satisfies (N_k)+(D_k) whenever the step-k fluctuation quadratic form is given by a real matrix K_R built by one local stencil of range ≤ ρ (in step-k units) from the step-k data, with singular values in [m, M] uniformly in R (Lemma 4.5: the per-cube log-determinant densities on T and T* agree up to d_0 ρ_*^{k_0}/(k_0(1 − ρ_*)), ρ_* = 1 − m²/M², k_0 = 1 + ⌊(t*/ℓ_k − 1)/(4ρ)⌋). (iii) (P) Coordinate measures (Haar-to-Lebesgue Jacobians, block-axial-gauge Faddeev–Popov factors, the averaging over gauge-fixing graphs of [B122I, (1.100)]) are products of block-local factors and satisfy (N_k) exactly with zero remainder. (iv) (L/P) There is no zero-mode factor in the scalar: the step-k fluctuation forms are bounded below by γ_0 > 0 depending on d and L only, uniformly in k and in the regular background [B96, after Lemma 2.4, p. 246; B99, p. 428], so every Gaussian normalizer is a determinant of a uniformly invertible finite-range operator, covered by (ii); torus holonomies (flat coarse fields) are configurations of the step-K integration variable and enter Ξ_{a,R}, never A_a(R), and are bounded by (H1). (v) (C — the property to be checked in [B109], [B119], [B122II]) (N_k) and (D_k) for the nonlinear E-terms and the R′′-terms; the exact list of formulas is in §7, item R-2.

### 3.5 The chessboard chain (L/P)

**Theorem 3.5.** Let T = qT*, cells as in §2.3, sources with margin as in §2.4.
(i) (P; the argument of Osterwalder–Seiler) *Site-plane reflection positivity on the torus.* For every cell-face plane Π and every bounded function F of the links of the closed half-torus H_Π^+ (the links with both endpoints in {sℓ_c ≤ x_i ≤ sℓ_c + T/2}), ⟨Θ_Π F · F⟩_{a,T} ≥ 0 and |⟨Θ_ΠF · F′⟩|² ≤ ⟨Θ_ΠF·F⟩⟨Θ_ΠF′·F′⟩; the sourced measure with a replicated source W J has the same property.
(ii) (P) *RP subadditivity.* For every reflection-compatible replicated source W J (including J = 0) and every q: Z_{a,qT*}[WJ] ≤ Z_{a,T*}[WJ]^{q^4}; hence p_{a,qT*}[WJ] ≤ p_{a,T*}[WJ] and in particular 0 ≤ p_{a,T*} − p_{a,qT*}.
(iii) (L: FILS Theorem 4.1 (abstract chessboard) and the torus pattern of Theorem 4.2, adapted to closed cells and site-plane reflections in §4.5; P for the adaptation) *Chessboard estimate.* For cell-local functions F_c, c ∈ C ⊂ cells, |⟨Π_{c∈C} F_c⟩_{a,T}| ≤ Π_{c∈C} ⟨D F_c⟩_{a,T}^{1/n_T^4}, and for F_c = e^{J_c·O − L_a(J_c)} with L_a additive over cells and reflection/conjugation covariant: ⟨D F_c⟩ = e^{−L_a(WJ_c)} Z_{a,T}[WJ_c]/Z_{a,T}[0] = exp(Vol(T)(p_{a,T}[WJ_c] − p_{a,T}[0]) − L_a(WJ_c)) ≥ 0.
(iv) (P given I-5(a) and S94) *Three-term split and m-point bounds.* For sources J_1,…,J_m with margin in cells c_1,…,c_m (repetitions allowed), ‖J_j‖_{C^5} ≤ r_0, the renormalized generating function G_T(λ) = e^{−L_a(Σλ_jJ_j)}Z_{a,T}[Σλ_jJ_j]/Z_{a,T}[0] is holomorphic on the polydisc P = {|λ_j| ≤ r_0/(k_{c_j}‖J_j‖_{C^5})} (k_c = number of sources in c) and satisfies |G_T(λ)| ≤ exp(m ℓ_c^4 (C_1 + C_0)) there; hence the renormalized Schwinger functions obey
  |S^{a,T}_m(J_1,…,J_m)| ≤ e^{mℓ_c^4(C_1+C_0)} e^m m! Π_j ‖J_j‖_{C^5}/r_0,
and for arbitrary test functions |S^{a,T}_m(f_1,…,f_m)| ≤ C^m m! Π_j ‖f_j‖_s, ‖f‖_s := Σ_c ‖fχ_c‖_{C^5}, with C independent of a and T. The separated-cube bounds of I-5(b) then hold on every T with T-independent constants (same proof as I-5(b), the input being this bound).

### 3.6 Limit points (P)

**Theorem 3.6.** Assume the bound of Theorem 3.5(iv) for a family of renormalized observables {O_α}. ## 4. Proofs

### 4.1 Proof of Theorem 3.1 (large-field entropy)

*Step 1: a family of plaquettes with private links.* Let n = T/a ≥ 3 and P the set of (1,2)-plaquettes with base point x such that x_2/a ∈ {0, 2, 4, …, 2(⌊n/2⌋ − 1)}. To p ∈ P with base point x assign the link b_p = (x, x + ae_1). The link b_p lies in exactly two (1,2)-plaquettes, those with base points x and x − ae_2; the latter has x_2/a − 1 odd (or equal to n − 1 if x_2 = 0, which is not in the index set whether n is even or odd). Hence b_p ∉ ∂p′ for p′ ∈ P∖{p}. |P| = ⌊n/2⌋ n^3 ≥ n^4/3.

*Step 2: conditional lower bound for one plaquette.* Fix p ∈ P, b = b_p, and all links other than b. Under μ_{a,T} the conditional law of U = U_b is e^{−βf(U)} dU / ∫e^{−βf} dU with f(U) = Σ_{i=1}^{6} (N − Re tr(U W_i)), W_i ∈ G fixed (the six plaquettes containing b; cyclicity and tr(U^{−1}W) = conj tr(W^{−1}U) put every term in this form). f is smooth, f ≥ 0; let U^* be a minimizer on G. For A in the Lie algebra (traceless Hermitian, |A| ≤ 1, so ‖A‖ ≤ 1 < π and A ↦ e^{iA}U^* is injective) put φ(A) = f(e^{iA}U^*). Then φ has a critical point at 0 and d²/dt² φ(tA) = Σ_i Re tr(A² e^{itA}U^*W_i) ≤ 6|A|², so by Taylor's theorem φ(A) ≤ φ(0) + 3|A|². Let W = W_p be the product of the other three links of p in the order making U_p = U W, and U_p^* := U^*W. For U = e^{iA}U^*, U_p = e^{iA}U_p^*. Two elementary facts: |e^{iA} − 1| ≤ |A| and |e^{iA} − 1| ≥ (2/π)|A| for ‖A‖ ≤ π (eigenvalue-wise, |e^{iλ} − 1| = 2|sin(λ/2)| ∈ [(2/π)|λ|, |λ|]). Since e^{iA}U_p^* − 1 = (e^{iA} − 1)U_p^* + (U_p^* − 1) and the HS norm is unitarily invariant:
 - if |U_p^* − 1| ≥ 2δ: for |A| ≤ δ, |U_p − 1| ≥ 2δ − |A| ≥ δ;
 - if |U_p^* − 1| < 2δ: for (3π/2)δ ≤ |A| ≤ (3π/2 + 1)δ, |U_p − 1| ≥ (2/π)|A| − 2δ ≥ δ.
In either case the set S = {A : |A| ≤ 6δ, |U_p(e^{iA}U^*) − 1| ≥ δ} contains a ball or a shell of Lebesgue measure ≥ v_{d_G} δ^{d_G} (v_d = volume of the unit ball of R^d); 6δ ≤ 1 by assumption. Normalized Haar measure in these coordinates is J(A)dA with J ≥ c′_N > 0 on |A| ≤ 1. Therefore
  μ(|U_p − 1| ≥ δ | links ≠ b) ≥ ∫_S e^{−βφ(A)} J dA / ∫_G e^{−βf} dU ≥ e^{−βφ(0)} e^{−3β(6δ)²} c′_N v_{d_G} δ^{d_G} / e^{−βφ(0)} = ε_N(β, δ),
using ∫_G e^{−βf} dU ≤ e^{−β min f} (normalized Haar). This bound is uniform in the conditioning configuration.

*Step 3: sequential conditioning.* Enumerate P = {p_1,…,p_n′}. The event {p_1,…,p_{j−1} small} is measurable with respect to the links other than b_{p_j} (Step 1), so μ(p_1,…,p_j small) = E[1_{p_1..p_{j−1} small} μ(p_j small | links ≠ b_{p_j})] ≤ (1 − ε_N) μ(p_1,…,p_{j−1} small). Induction gives μ(E_δ) ≤ μ(all p ∈ P small) ≤ (1 − ε_N)^{|P|} ≤ exp(−ε_N n^4/3). Since Z^{sf}_{a,T} ≤ ∫ 1_{E_δ} e^{−βS} dHaar = Z_{a,T} μ_{a,T}(E_δ), (3.1) follows.

*Step 4: size along the trajectory.* With δ = c_N g_0 p_0(g_0) and β = 2N/g_0²: βδ² = 2N c_N² p_0(g_0)², δ^{d_G} = (c_N g_0 p_0)^{d_G} ≥ exp(−(d_G/2) log g_0^{−2} − O(1)), so ε_N ≥ exp(−C′_N p_0(g_0)² − C″_N log g_0^{−2}). With a^{−4} = ℓ_0^{−4}L^{4K}, log g_0^{−2} ≤ log(C K) and p_0(g_0)² = A_0²(log g_0^{−2})^{2p_0} ≤ A_0²(log(CK))^{2p_0} = o(K), the lower bound in (3.1) divided by Vol(T) is ℓ_0^{−4} exp(4K log L − o(K)) → ∞. ∎

*Proof of Corollary 3.1′.* Proposition 4.3 below is the crude step of [M-loc] Prop. 2.7, which needs only (A1), (A2) and B_{δ_0} ⊂ {χ_K = 1}; it gives log Z^{sf}_{a,T*} ≥ −Vol(T*)E_vac(a) − C_−Vol(T*) with C_− = [C + C′δ_0²g_K^{−2} + C″]ℓ_0^{−4}, a-independent because g_K = g_* is fixed by I-4. Combining with I-0b and (3.1) gives (Vol(T*)/(3a^4))ε_N(β_a, δ) ≤ (C_+ + C_−)Vol(T*), false for a small by Step 4. The threshold remark follows from Step 4 with βδ² in place of 2Nc_N²p_0²: (3.1)/Vol ≥ ℓ_0^{−4}L^{4K} e^{−108βδ² − O(log K)} is bounded only if 108 β_aδ² ≥ 4K log L − O(log K) = 4 log(ℓ_0/a) − O(log log(1/a)). ∎

**Remark 4.1 (what the theorem does and does not say).** It does not say that Bałaban's bounds depend on the volume, nor that UV stability fails; it says that the *complete* finite-torus scalar A_a(R) must contain the large-field (R″) identity-values, whose density ≈ a^{−4}e^{−O(p_0(g_0)^2)} diverges, so that no bound on the large torus that discards the large-field sector can match A_a(T*) to O(Vol). Dimock's scalar template is consistent with this: his Theorem 1 ("for all M, N", [D1] (7)) uses the full counterterm E^N and his lower bound comes from the complete large-field analysis on the large torus ([D1] §1.3 scales the UV problem on the unit torus into an IR problem on a large unit-spacing torus, which is Theorem 3.2 below), not from a restriction to the small-field sector.

### 4.2 Proof of Theorem 3.2 (scaling transfer)

By §2.2 with λ = 1/T, the Wilson theory on Λ_{a,T} is the theory on the unit torus Λ_{ε,1} with ε = a/T and the same β_a = 2N/g_0(a)². Bałaban's block-spin steps are defined by the lattice geometry (blocks of L^k ε-cubes of the unit torus) and by β; after k steps the lattice has spacing L^kε = L^k a/T, i.e. physical spacing L^k a on T. The hypotheses "g_j ≤ ḡ for j ≤ k" are properties of the trajectory as a function of a only (I-4: the same trajectory is used on every torus). The step-K lattice has N_K = (T/ℓ_0)^4 = Vol(T)/ℓ_0^4 sites. Therefore R(a/T; K) *is* the statement about the step-K density on Λ_{a,T}; the number of steps actually performed, K = log_L(ℓ_0/a), does not depend on T, while the number of steps that would be available on the unit torus, log_L(T/a), is irrelevant because the recursion is stopped at step K. The constants are those of R(ε;k), independent of ε and k by hypothesis, hence of a and T. ∎

**Remark 4.2.** The statement R(ε;k) requires Bałaban's theorem to be an induction over steps with constants uniform in the step, which is what "ultraviolet stability uniformly in ε" means; what has to be checked in [B122II] is only that his Theorem 1 is stated (or proved) for every intermediate k and not merely at the last step, and that the lower bound is on a set of controlled Haar measure (a product of balls around the identity in the step-k variables). This is (H1). The weak-coupling hypothesis of [B122II, p. 355] is then exactly I-4's g_j ≤ ḡ for j ≤ K.

**Lemma 4.5 (Gaussian normalizers under copying; P).** Let Γ_n = (Z/nZ)^4, Γ_{qn} its q-fold cover, with at most d_0 real coordinates per cell. Let K_n, K_{qn} be real square matrices on these coordinate spaces such that (a) the entry of K between coordinates attached to cells at distance > ρ vanishes, (b) the entries of K_{qn} are the pull-back of those of K_n (the entry between cells y′, z′ of Γ_{qn} with |y′ − z′| ≤ ρ equals the entry between π(y′), π(z′) of K_n; well defined since |π(y′) − π(z′)| ≤ ρ < n/2 determines the pair), (c) m ≤ σ(K_n), σ(K_{qn}) ≤ M for all singular values. Then with ρ_* = 1 − m²/M² and k_0 = 1 + ⌊(n−1)/(4ρ)⌋,
  | |Γ_n|^{−1} log|det K_n| − |Γ_{qn}|^{−1} log|det K_{qn}| | ≤ d_0 ρ_*^{k_0}/(k_0(1 − ρ_*)).
*Proof.* A_n = K_n^tK_n is positive with spectrum in [m², M²]; B_n = I − M^{−2}A_n has ‖B_n‖ ≤ ρ_* and range ≤ 2ρ; log|det K_n| = ½ log det A_n = ½[D_n log M² − Σ_{j≥1} Tr B_n^j/j] with D_n = dim; the series converges absolutely since |Tr B_n^j| ≤ D_nρ_*^j. Tr B_n^j is a sum over closed walks of j steps on Γ_n, each step of cell-length ≤ 2ρ, of products of entries. For j < k_0 one has j ≤ (n−1)/(4ρ), so the total length 2ρj of such a walk is < n/2. A walk of total length < n/2 cannot wind around Γ_{qn} (winding needs unrolled displacement ≥ n), so every closed walk of j steps on Γ_{qn} projects to a closed walk on Γ_n of the same length with the same product of entries by (b), and conversely every closed walk rooted at y ∈ Γ_n has exactly one lift rooted at each y′ ∈ π^{−1}(y), which is closed. Hence |Γ_{qn}|^{−1}Tr B_{qn}^j = |Γ_n|^{−1}Tr B_n^j for j < k_0 (source construction (V6.10), printed p. 815). The tails j ≥ k_0 are bounded on each torus by d_0 Σ_{j≥k_0}ρ_*^j/j ≤ d_0ρ_*^{k_0}/(k_0(1−ρ_*)) per cell, and the D log M² terms agree per cell. ∎
*Application.* The step-k fluctuation form (B, Δ_kB) restricted to the constrained subspace is, in Bałaban's parametrization B = C B̄ [B99 (3.156)–(3.158)], C^*Δ_kC with "a lower bound γ_0 > 0 independent of k and U" and "uniform exponential decay" [B99 p. 428]; its matrix entries are given by the local stencil of the covariant Laplacian, the averaging operators and the block gauge conditions, all of range ≤ ρ (a fixed multiple of L·M) in step-k units, and bounded above by a constant depending on d, L, N. Thus (a)–(c) hold with m = γ_0^{1/2}, uniformly in k, in R and in the regular background, *provided the background entering the stencil on T is the pull-back of the background on T** — which is the case for the identity configuration at which the scalar is evaluated (and, for the Gaussian normalizer as a function of U, for any pulled-back U). The Gaussian scalar at step k therefore satisfies (N_k) with ρ_k = ρ and (D_k) with the geometric tail ρ_*^{k_0} = exp(−c n_k) (c = |log ρ_*|/(4ρ)), u = v = 0. If the normalizer is instead a *Dirichlet* determinant on a finite domain Λ (B99 §E), it is a finite-domain object and lifts exactly ([Ordinary spectral estimates and mixed current transfer](block_and_transfer/conditional_estimates/ordinary_spectral_estimates_and_mixed_current_transfer.tex), §8, Prop. 8.1: conjugacy of all typed products, traces and determinants under a record isomorphism).

### 4.5 Proof of Theorem 3.5 (reflection positivity, subadditivity, chessboard, split, Cauchy)

**(i) Site-plane RP on the torus.** Fix Π = {x_1 = sℓ_c} and Π′ = {x_1 = sℓ_c + T/2}.   ⟨Θ_ΠF · F⟩ Z = ∫ dU_{Π∪Π′} e^{−βS_0} ∫ dU_{int(H^+)} e^{−βS_+} F · conj( ∫ dU_{int(H^−)} e^{−βS_−} (F∘θ_Π) )

  A_j(U, V) := ∫ dU_{int Σ_j} exp(−β S_{Σ_j}[U, ·, V] + Σ_{c ⊂ Σ_j}(WJ)_c·O_c),

Then Z_{T_1×Σ}[WJ] = ∫Π_j dU_j Π_j A_j(U_j,U_{j+1}) = Tr(A_0A_1⋯A_{n_1−1}), n_1 = T_1/ℓ_c. A_j has a bounded continuous kernel on the compact space X × X, hence is Hilbert–Schmidt. The pattern WJ is invariant under translation by 2ℓ_c e_1 and satisfies (WJ)|_{Σ_{j+1}} = Θ_{Π_{j+1}}-image of (WJ)|_{Σ_j}; by the change of variables of (i), A_{j+1}(U,V) = conj(A_j(V,U)), i.e. A_{j+1} = A_j^*, and A_{j+2} = A_j. Thus with A := A_0, Z_{T_1×Σ}[WJ] = Tr((AA^*)^{n_1/2}) (n_1 even). Put P := (AA^*)^{n_*/2} ≥ 0, trace class. Then Z_{t*×Σ}[WJ] = Tr P and Z_{qt*×Σ}[WJ] = Tr P^q ≤ (Tr P)^q (eigenvalues μ_i ≥ 0: Σμ_i^q ≤ (Σμ_i)^q). Gauge invariance: each A_j commutes with the projection onto gauge-invariant functions of the slice (the temporal links adjacent to a slice are integrated inside A_j, and Haar invariance absorbs a slice gauge transformation), so the same identity holds on the gauge-invariant subspace; no gauge-volume factor is introduced ([Physical Wilson contacts and local conditional subtraction](block_and_transfer/conditional_estimates/physical_wilson_contacts_and_local_conditional_subtraction.tex), §8, Lemma 8.1, is the one-step version T = M_bC_βM_b with the explicit projection). Apply in the four directions successively (each application is on a torus with arbitrary transverse sides): Z_{(qT*)^4}[WJ] ≤ Z_{T*×(qT*)^3}[WJ]^q ≤ … ≤ Z_{T*^4}[WJ]^{q^4}. The J = 0 case gives p_{a,qT*} ≤ p_{a,T*}. ∎ The hypothesis "reflection-compatible replicated source" is exactly what makes A_{j+1} = A_j^*; for a general complex source the pressures are not even real and no inequality is asserted ([M-rem] §2, "Scope of sourced (ii)").

**(iii) Chessboard.** Apply FILS Theorem 4.1 [IHES P/78/224, pp. 33–34; CMP 62 (1978) 1–34] in the direction-1 slab form: 𝔞 = bounded functions of the links of a closed slab, r = Θ_{Π_0}∘(translation) realizing the identification of slab j with slab 2n_1 − 1 − j as in FILS Theorem 4.2's proof, F(G_1,…,G_{2n_1}) = ⟨Π_j G_j⟩_{a,T} with G_j a function of the links of the closed slab Σ_j. (4.1) is translation invariance mod n_1 (periodicity in direction 1); (4.2) is the Schwarz inequality of (i) for the plane between slabs n_1 − 1 and n_1 and its antipode, with the product of the slab functions on each side as the F, F′ of (i) (closed slabs on the two sides share only links in the fixed planes, which is allowed in (i)). FILS's Remark 4 gives F(G, rG, …, rG) ≥ 0. The conclusion of Theorem 4.1 is |⟨Π_jG_j⟩| ≤ Π_j ⟨Π_{j′}(G_j)^{(j′)}⟩^{1/n_1} where (G_j)^{(j′)} is G_j carried to slab j′ by reflections. Take G_j = Π_{c⊂Σ_j}F_c and repeat in directions 2, 3, 4 (FILS Theorem 4.2's induction), obtaining |⟨Π_cF_c⟩| ≤ Π_c ⟨DF_c⟩^{1/n_T^4}; cells without a source carry F_c = 1. For F_c = e^{J_c·O − L_a(J_c)}: DF_c = Π_{c′}Θ_{c→c′}F_c = exp(Σ_{c′}(WJ_c)_{c′}·O_{c′} − Σ_{c′}L_a((WJ_c)_{c′})) using the reflection covariance of O and L_a and L_a(conj J) = conj L_a(J); additivity gives Σ_{c′}L_a((WJ_c)_{c′}) = L_a(WJ_c) (= (n_T^4/2)(L_a(J_c) + conj L_a(J_c)), real). Then ⟨DF_c⟩ = e^{−L_a(WJ_c)}Z_{a,T}[WJ_c]/Z_{a,T}[0], and it is ≥ 0 as an F(G, rG, …) value. ∎ (The FILS statements were read in the preprint; [M-loc] cites "Theorem 2.2", which in FILS is the theorem on commuting RP functionals, not the chessboard theorem — corrected here.)

**(iv) Split and Cauchy.** Let the sources J_1,…,J_m with margin sit in cells, k_c of them in cell c, Σ_c k_c = m, and let λ ∈ P. Put F_c(λ) = exp(Σ_{j∈c}λ_jJ_j·O − L_a(Σ_{j∈c}λ_jJ_j)); by additivity of L_a over cells, G_T(λ) = ⟨Π_c F_c(λ)⟩_{a,T}. G_T is entire in λ (finite-dimensional integral of an entire integrand; L_a is holomorphic on the polydisc by I-5(a)). By (iii), |G_T(λ)| ≤ Π_{c occupied}⟨DF_c(λ)⟩^{1/n_T^4}. For each occupied c, J^{(c)} := Σ_{j∈c}λ_jJ_j has ‖J^{(c)}‖_{C^5} ≤ Σ_j |λ_j|‖J_j‖ ≤ r_0 on P and is cell-supported with margin, so I-5(a) applies to it on T*: Vol(T*)^{−1}[log Z_{a,T*}[WJ^{(c)}] − L_a(W_{T*}J^{(c)})] − p_{a,T*}[0] ≤ C_1. By (ii), Z_{a,T}[WJ^{(c)}] ≤ Z_{a,T*}[WJ^{(c)}]^{q^4}, and L_a(W_TJ^{(c)}) = q^4L_a(W_{T*}J^{(c)}) by additivity (same replicated pattern on the two tori). Hence
  n_T^{−4}log⟨DF_c⟩ ≤ ℓ_c^4{[p_{a,T*}[WJ^{(c)}] − Vol(T*)^{−1}L_a(W_{T*}J^{(c)}) − p_{a,T*}[0]] + [p_{a,T*}[0] − p_{a,T}[0]]} ≤ ℓ_c^4(C_1 + C_0),
the second bracket being S94 (I-6(a)). With at most m occupied cells, |G_T(λ)| ≤ e^{mℓ_c^4(C_1+C_0)} on P. Π_j Σ_c‖f_jχ_c‖_{C^5}, where C absorbs e^{ℓ_c^4(C_1+C_0)}e/r_0 and the C^5 norm of the χ_c; and ‖f‖_s ≤ C_s p_6(f) for the Schwartz seminorm p_6, since the cell sum converges by the polynomial weight. ### 4.6 Proof of Theorem 3.6 (limit points and what passes to them)

*Compactness.* For fixed m and types, S^{a,T}_m is a continuous m-linear functional on (𝒮, ‖·‖_s) (𝒮 = Schwartz space, ‖·‖_s ≤ C p_6) with norm ≤ C^m m!, uniformly in (a, T). 𝒮 is separable; choose a countable set 𝒟 ⊂ 𝒮 dense for ‖·‖_s. For each m and each m-tuple from 𝒟 the scalar sequence is bounded; a diagonal subsequence over the countable set of all (m, types, tuples) converges on all of them. For general f_1..f_m, approximate by tuples from 𝒟: |S_j(f) − S_k(f)| ≤ 2C^m m!Σ_i(Π_{i′≠i}‖f_{i′}‖_s)‖f_i − g_i‖_s + |S_j(g) − S_k(g)|, so the sequence is Cauchy; the limit S_m is m-linear with the same bound, hence a tempered distribution on R^{4m} (nuclear theorem). The "placement" of f ∈ 𝒮 on the torus T_j = q_jT* is by restriction of f to the fundamental domain [−T_j/2, T_j/2)^4 followed by sampling on the lattice (the lattice observable O_α(f) := a^4Σ_x f(x)O_α(x)); for compactly supported f the placement is exact once T_j > 2·diam(supp f); for Schwartz f the periodization error is bounded by the tails and goes to 0 in ‖·‖_s.
*Translations.* For y ∈ R^4 choose y_j ∈ a_jZ^4 with |y_j − y| ≤ 2a_j. Lattice translation invariance of μ_{a_j,T_j} and of the observable family gives S_j(τ_{y_j}f_1,…,τ_{y_j}f_m) = S_j(f_1,…,f_m). And ‖τ_yf − τ_{y_j}f‖_s ≤ C p_7(f)|y − y_j| → 0. Hence S_m(τ_yf) = lim S_j(τ_{y_j}f) + lim[S_j(τ_yf) − S_j(τ_{y_j}f)] = S_m(f).
*Hypercubic symmetry.* Each S^{a,T}_m is H_4-covariant (the Wilson measure is H_4-invariant on the torus; the O_α transform under H_4 by the finite-dimensional representation attached to their tensor type; scalar observables are invariant); covariance is a linear identity for each fixed test tuple and passes to the limit.
*Permutation symmetry.* S^{a,T}_m is symmetric under simultaneous permutation of (type, test) pairs, being an expectation of a product of commuting functions; passes to the limit.
*Reflection positivity.* Let Θ be the antilinear reflection in {x_0 = 0}. For finitely many polynomials F_i = Σ_m f_{i,m} in the fields with f_{i,m} ∈ 𝒮(R^{4m}) (product tests suffice by density, the joint-test extension being continuous), each supported in {x_0 ≥ ε_i > 0}: once T_j/2 > max support radius + 2r_O and a_j < ε_i, the functions Π O(f_{i,m}) are functions of the links of the closed half-torus {0 ≤ x_0 ≤ T_j/2} of Λ_{a_j,T_j} (the lattice supports of the O_α(x) have radius r_O a_j), and Theorem 3.5(i) with Π = {x_0 = 0} (a site plane; the cell grid is irrelevant here, only T_j/(2a_j) ∈ N is used) gives Σ_{i,i′}⟨Θ(F_i)F_{i′}⟩_{a_j,T_j} c̄_i c_{i′} ≥ 0 for all c ∈ C^n. Each entry converges to the corresponding combination of the S_m; positive semi-definite matrices converge to positive semi-definite matrices. For the renormalized observables the Gram kernel is the diagonal congruence of the bare one by e^{−L_a} ([M-rem] §2, last (P)), which preserves positivity provided L_a factorizes over the two half-spaces as stated there; this is part of I-5's conventions (§6.3).
*Bounds and E0′.* The product bound passes to the limit with the same constants; the Hermite-basis argument of [M-rem] §2 ("Joint-test promotion", (P)) extends it to joint tests with the OS II (4.1) growth, with fixed seminorm order and factorial exponent 1. ∎

**Remark 4.7 (order of limits, renormalization conditions).** The subsequence is diagonal in (a_j, T_j); no interchange of limits is used or claimed. The trajectory g_0(a) and the counterterm functionals (E_a or A_a(R)/Vol(R), and L_a) are functions of a alone (I-4; I-5(a); (H2) for the scalar, which in any case cancels in all normalized expectations); the renormalization condition is imposed at scale ℓ_0 on T* (I-4), never on the large torus. Different diagonal subsequences may have different limits; existence needs one. The direct routes to S94 by gluing and by transfer-matrix covering fail for the reason given in [M-loc] §5 and [M-rem] §4: gluing costs β_a a^{−3}/t* per unit volume, the transfer-matrix covering costs 12 a^{−3}[C + (d_G/2)log(1+β)], and both are O(a^{−3}log(1/a)) ≫ O(1); only the RG accounting of A_a(R) exhibits the cancellation. Theorem 3.1 adds that the cancellation must include the large-field sector.

**R-4 (negative result); (P).** The route I-0a+I-0b with a common small-field E_vac(a) is closed by Theorem 3.1. Any future attempt to obtain LB(T) by discarding the large-field sector on the large torus, with thresholds δ(a) satisfying β_aδ(a)² = o(log(1/a)), reproduces the inconsistency.

## 8. Summary

# Source estimates for Wilson fields and constrained reconstruction

Volumes 1–9 collect selected mathematical sections from the foundational Wilson-source construction. They retain the original equations, subsection numbers and arguments. The footer gives the printed source page.

Volumes 1–9 comprise 719 numbered sections or subsections. Each listed unit extends from its heading to the next numbered heading. A chapter number on its own denotes its opening argument before the first subsection. Subsections within a chapter can be read individually through the PDF bookmarks. Citations to material outside the selection retain their original numbering. The full list of included units is in the [section concordance](sections.md).

Finite-cutoff identities, clean-sector estimates, mathematical counterexamples and continuum implications have different hypotheses. Uniform Gaussian calibration (GC), compatibility of the chosen conditional representation (CL), identification of the interacting coefficients, and the continuum estimates remain assumptions wherever the corresponding result uses them. The [theorem and open-problem catalogue](../../THEOREMS_AND_OPEN_PROBLEMS.md) states the hypotheses of the assembled construction.


The [companion volume](10_companion_estimates.pdf) adds 470 mathematical units, including the physical two-source compensation, signed selector boundary and a retained-row obstruction on a constrained Wilson minimum. Its [concordance and exclusions](companion_index.md) gives source locations and the scope of the recovered arguments.

## Reading individual estimates

**Constrained periodic inversion.** The raw constraint \(Q_{\rm raw}\) fixes coarse link data, while a second condition fixes the longitudinal gauge slice. In §58 a fixed longitudinal forcing detects a transverse alias at successive fine scales. Orthogonality gives \(\|u_T\|_{\dot H^1_a}^2\ge c k-C\) in (FI25). Thus (FI6), the inverse estimate for unrestricted forcing, fails already in the flat problem. The reconstruction from compatible coarse data in §59 has \(Q_{\rm raw}H_{\rm raw}=I\) and \(\|H_{\rm raw}B\|_{W^{1,p}_a}\le C_{p,L,T}\|B\|_{W^{1,p}_r}\) for \(2<p<\infty\), (FW1)–(FW5). The assumptions on the force and the gauge slice determine which estimate applies. [Volume 3](03_periodic_reconstruction.pdf)

**Higher curvature jets on an actual Wilson minimum.** Sections 94.1–94.4 solve a scalar Wilson flux equation on a commuting subgroup, then verify the raw constraint, the full first variation and that the solution belongs to the regular minimizing orbit. The first differences in (HJ15) see the jump of a coarse box adjoint. The dimension-eight observable in (HJ16) consequently grows as \(4\varepsilon^2T^4/a\), despite the uniform first-order bounds (HJ14). This rules out the pointwise higher-jet estimate (HJ17). It leaves open integration and subtraction estimates for quantum insertions. [Volume 5](05_mixed_geometry.pdf)

**Dimension-six mixing.** The two principal representatives have a quadratic and a cubic form factor. Sections 95.2–95.4 test them on free polarizations, including the nonzero cubic contraction (DN7)–(DN8). Their separated free Gram form is positive definite. If its smallest eigenvalue is \(m_0\), (DN10) gives a sufficient perturbative stability test. Bounding products of the noncommuting step matrices and their inverses costs the logarithmic factor (DN11)–(DN13); the actual interacting coefficient estimate in (DN11) remains an input. [Volume 6](06_dimension_six.pdf)

**The physical quadratic source.** For the fixed \(O_{H,a}(f)=g^{-2}H_a(f)\) of (PD12.1), §§155.2 and 155.4–155.5 expand the complete two-source coefficient, including ordinary and kernel responses. In §155.6, conditioning on separated links reduces the lower bound to a one-link bath. The Hessian of the rescaled local term has a strictly positive lower bound on a ball where the conditional density is bounded below. Testing its Laplacian against a bump gives (PD12.52), a local variance lower bound in (PD12.53), and then the summed lower bound (PD12.54)–(PD12.56). For a real test with the stipulated plateau the connected diagonal coefficient is at least \(c_f a^{-8}\). Centering one-source means leaves that variance invariant. The argument does not yield the upper bound on the ordinary remainder. [Volume 9](09_physical_source_current.pdf)

**Sharp boundary words and unequal spatial weights.** In §157.4 the operator commutator with the indicator of the reference domain telescopes over the ordered differentiations in (NB12.14). A first derivative of the indicator is a surface measure; subsequent derivatives can be transferred to the remaining factors. This avoids products of boundary distributions. The boundary trace estimate and the internal Cauchy estimates concern the admitted reference coordinates. In §§159.4–159.5, (NM12.13)–(NM12.16) give a counting example in which the faster exponential and the weighted volume sums converge while the slower derivative-rate sum diverges. A bound on the actual source metric at the required decay rate is a separate input. [Volume 9](09_physical_source_current.pdf)

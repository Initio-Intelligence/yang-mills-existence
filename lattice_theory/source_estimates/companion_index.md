# Additional source estimates and corrections

The [companion volume](10_companion_estimates.pdf) contains 470 numbered units absent from volumes 1–9. It restores derivations, counterexamples, definitions governing the original retained rows, and restrictions on the domain or measure of an estimate. The original section and equation numbers identify the corresponding argument in either edition. The eight entries marked “scope note” restate source comparisons and mathematical limitations in new prose. Other entries retain the original mathematical text and displayed equations with editorial references removed or shortened.

## Selected derivations

- **Different analytic extensions can agree on the physical graph.** In §101.4, repairing the background after evaluating a source readout produces a different off-graph map from repairing the background and replacing the independent current before evaluating the response. Equality on physical fields supplies neither a common analytic domain nor a radial estimate for both maps. The repaired family keeps the original reference cutoff and data; a Cauchy parameter alone changes no real measure. [§101.4, companion p. 442](10_companion_estimates.pdf#page=442).
- **A pinned plaquette on a Wilson minimum.** Set the mixed target to the identity except for a retained bond with \(V_{b_*}=e^{\delta T}\), \(T=\operatorname{diag}(i,-i,0,\ldots,0)\). On a plaquette whose four links are fixed, (AE10.8) makes its holonomy exactly \(e^{\pm\delta T}\). For the proposed weak potential norm the necessary curvature bound forces \(\tau\ge c\delta a^{4/p-2}\). This tests the passage from a small native action to a cutoff-uniform weak ball. Membership of this very example in an original prepared history remains a separate selector question. [§141.3, companion p. 785](10_companion_estimates.pdf#page=785) and [§141.8, companion p. 790](10_companion_estimates.pdf#page=790).
- **A vanishing conditional covariance has a narrow scope.** In (PE12.14)–(PE12.15) the protected carrying function is measurable with respect to the actual output \(q\). Thus \(\operatorname{Cov}(h,t\mid q)=0\) for every integrable \(t\). This cancellation holds with the same conditional law and does not remove a nonzero inherited two-source coefficient. [§154.5, companion p. 870](10_companion_estimates.pdf#page=870).
- **Two-source compensation must retain the degree-one defects.** Write \(O_i=t_i+r_i\) with \(t_i=\rho^{-1}D_{V_i}\rho\). For the actual inherited coefficient \(A_{12}\), (PD12.12)–(PD12.13) give \(R_{12}=A_{12}+\tfrac12(V_1t_2+V_2t_1)\) and \(b_{12}=R_{12}+V_1r_2+V_2r_1\). The original bare prescription has \(A_{12}=0\). The resulting contact and its transported coefficient are distinguished from those of a purely Ward-generated source. [§155.3, companion p. 875](10_companion_estimates.pdf#page=875) and [§156.4, companion p. 879](10_companion_estimates.pdf#page=879).
- **A sharp selector adds a signed boundary term.** In (NB12.18)–(NB12.19), test the distributional integration-by-parts identity with \(h\), repeat with \(1\), and subtract the latter times \(K(h)\). The result identifies \(K(Vh)\) with the conditional covariance minus the centered boundary flux. Dropping the flux would change the identity even when the bulk density is smooth. [§157.5, companion p. 881](10_companion_estimates.pdf#page=881).
- **Summability at the native rate does not pay the derivative rate.** The majorant in source (162), the kernel in (189) and the derivative in (190) use exponential rates \(\delta_0/2,\delta_0/4,\delta_0/8\), respectively; see (NM12.1). The cell counting estimate (NM12.5) retains the actual volume multiplicity. Neither this count nor the faster exponential gives uniform summability at the slowest derivative rate. [§159.1, companion p. 892](10_companion_estimates.pdf#page=892) and [§159.3, companion p. 895](10_companion_estimates.pdf#page=895).

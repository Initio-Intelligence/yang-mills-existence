\paragraph{Analysis of the nonflat input.}
Use the boundary only preparation and nonflat block calculation, with its filling multiplicity and amplitude:
boundary-only BFS gauge, interior plaquettes used only as probability witnesses,
canonical boundary norm $M\le CR^5$, center $CR^8$, radial gradient

\paragraph{1. An exact coarse action on literal retained links.}
Partition the original periodic lattice into cubical/rectangular cells with
complete boundaries. Keep the ORIGINAL link variables $Y$ on their skeleton;
integrate all links $u_\alpha$ strictly belonging to one cell interior.
Unequal final intervals may be used on the original torus; this is not a
change to a divisible or dyadic torus. Each cell is contractible and smaller
than the torus. A plaquette containing interior links meets only one such
interior. Assign that plaquette to the corresponding $S_\alpha(u_\alpha;Y)$;
assign every remaining plaquette to $S_0(Y)$. Every original plaquette is
counted ONCE. Then exactly
\[
 S=S_0+\sum_\alpha S_\alpha,\qquad
 Z_\alpha(Y)=\int e^{-S_\alpha(u_\alpha;Y)}du_\alpha,
\]
\[
 d\mu_Y=Z^{-1}e^{-A_1(Y)}dY,\qquad
 A_1(Y)=S_0(Y)-\sum_\alpha\log Z_\alpha(Y).                             \tag{1}
\]
All Haar factors, induced couplings and global retained loops remain.
If a local comparison included fixed-only collar plaquettes, subtract their
EXACT scalar from that local action and put it in $S_0$; they cannot be counted
once per adjacent cell. Boundary preparations are evaluation charts on these
original variables, not independent replacements of $dY$ by prepared Haar.

For a nested larger cell, integrate its internal skeleton links $z$. Fubini
and the literal plaquette assignment give
\[
 Z_{\rm large}(\eta)=\int e^{-S_{\rm sep}(z;\eta)}
                    \prod_{\alpha\subset\rm large}Z_\alpha(\eta_\alpha(z,\eta))dz.
                                                                            \tag{2}
\]
The right side is EXACTLY the original larger-block conditional integral,
including its remaining fixed-only scalar convention. This is not a reset of
$A_1$ to a Wilson action: the full induced $\log Z_\alpha$ functions were used
inside the integral. It also avoids summing small-block Taylor errors. One
may apply the boundary only preparation and nonflat block calculation directly to the larger original block if its actual outer
record and larger size meet that theorem's hypotheses.

\paragraph{2. Which source-zero scalars cancel.}
Choose fixed boundary-independent reference values $c_\alpha=Z_\alpha(\eta^0_\alpha)$
and $\Gamma_\alpha=-\log(Z_\alpha/c_\alpha)$. The product
$\prod c_\alpha$ factors out of(2) and cancels in the EXACT ratio
$Z_{\rm large}(\eta)/Z_{\rm large}(\eta^0)$. The ratio is still
\[
 \frac{\int e^{-S_{\rm sep}(z;\eta)-\sum\Gamma_\alpha(\eta_\alpha(z,\eta))}dz}
      {\int e^{-S_{\rm sep}(z;\eta^0)-\sum\Gamma_\alpha(\eta_\alpha(z,\eta^0))}dz}.
                                                                            \tag{3}
\]
If a reference is instead a live prepared boundary $\bar\eta_\alpha(z,\eta)$,
its $Z_\alpha(\bar\eta_\alpha)$ remains INSIDE the integral as a scalar
function. The nonflat baseline $vK_{\rm eff}b$ likewise remains in increments.
Neither is a common constant that may be canceled. This distinction persists
at every further scale.

\paragraph{3. Complete exact derivative and current state.}
For original retained left directions $e,f$, let $F_e=X_eS$ and let $H_{ef}$
be the symmetric original second derivative. Differentiating(1) in ORIGINAL
coordinates gives
\[
 j_e:=X_eA_1=E(F_e\mid Y),\qquad
 (\operatorname{Hess}A_1)_{ef}=E(H_{ef}\mid Y)
                            -\operatorname{Cov}(F_e,F_f\mid Y).         \tag{4}
\]
Conditionally independent interiors split the covariance into complete block
covariances; fixed skeleton terms remain in $S_0$. They are not dropped when
two retained links did not share an original plaquette. The interacting
Ward--Schur/regression formula of the original covariant hessian and ward schur calculation can be applied inside each EXACT
conditional fiber, or inside the larger original fiber in(2).

For an actual retained insertion $\bar O(Y)=E(O\mid Y)$, its response is
\[
 X_e\bar O=E(X_eO\mid Y)-\operatorname{Cov}(O,F_e\mid Y).                \tag{5}

Thus the inherited current contains the conditional score response and the
original denominator. A scalar value, a current baseline and a connected
second response are distinct pieces of the state. Equations(1),(4),(5) retain
all of them. No hard good-record selector has been inserted into the action;
if one is inserted later, its boundary response would be additional data.

\paragraph{4. A literal overlap bound, including frame acceleration.}
In a four-dimensional cubical skeleton, a positive-oriented retained link
lies on at most three transverse grid hyperplanes and belongs to at most
$2^3=8$ cell boundaries. A plaquette belongs to at most four and a vertex to
at most sixteen. Consequently, in ONE common original-link metric,
\[
 \sum_\alpha\|w|_{\partial\alpha}\|^2\le8\|w\|^2.                      \tag{6}
\]
This is a response count, not independence of boundary records.

At a fixed admitted record, freeze each actual boundary-only gauge at that
base point. Along a declared common original path
$Y_e(t)=e^{t\sqrt{N/B}w_e}Y_e$, let $b_\alpha(t)$ be its prepared canonical
logs in that frozen frame. Exact gauge invariance permits this local choice;
the random boundary law itself is unchanged. Then
$\|\dot b_\alpha\|\le C\|w|_{\partial\alpha}\|$ and
$\|\ddot b_\alpha\|\le C B^{-1/2}\|w|_{\partial\alpha}\|^2$ on the small
admitted chart. The complete local Gaussian comparison along this SAME path is
\[
 Q_\alpha=\dot b_\alpha^*K_\alpha\dot b_\alpha
                  +(K_\alpha b_\alpha)\cdot\ddot b_\alpha.             \tag{7}
\]
The second term is the baseline-current/coordinate-acceleration partner; it
cannot be discarded by using different canonical paths for adjacent blocks.
The boundary only preparation and nonflat block calculation bounds the Hessian and gradient errors by
\[
 \epsilon_2(R)=C[R^{14}/\sqrt B+R^{38}/B]+\mathcal E,
 \qquad \epsilon_1(R)=CR^{19}/\sqrt B+\mathcal E.
\]
The gradient times acceleration error is at most
$C\epsilon_1 B^{-1/2}\|w|_{\partial\alpha}\|^2$ and is absorbed into the
stated $\epsilon_2$ after increasing constants. Therefore on this admitted
record the COMPLETE induced Hessian satisfies
\[
 \left|\frac{d^2}{dt^2}A_1(Y(t))\big|_0
       -\frac{d^2}{dt^2}S_0(Y(t))\big|_0-\sum_\alpha Q_\alpha\right|
 \le C\epsilon_2(R)\|w\|^2.                                           \tag{8}
\]
The constant includes eight but not the number of cells. All exact $S_0$
responses remain. The bound is local on an actually admitted collection of
records; its probability has not been upgraded to an all-volume statement.

By polarization/integration, (8) controls a four-term connected boundary
difference in any common chart whose full interpolation stays admitted.
Such a difference cancels constant and linear Taylor pieces automatically;
those pieces must still be stored for the absolute action and current.
A connection-dependent quadratic form such as(7) is not a new globally flat
Gaussian action silently replacing(1).

\paragraph{5. An exact invariant marginal class, with a real but unquantified contraction.}
Let $Y_2\subset Y_1$ be nested sets of literal retained ORIGINAL links and fix
one deterministic tangent supported on $Y_2$. For its ORIGINAL score $F$, the
inherited scores satisfy the tower identity
\[
 j_2=E(F\mid Y_2)=E(j_1\mid Y_2),\qquad
 \|j_1\|_2^2-\|j_2\|_2^2=E\operatorname{Var}(j_1\mid Y_2)\ge0.          \tag{9}
\]
Haar integration by parts in the original law gives the depth-independent bound
\[
 \|j_k\|_2^2\le (24B/N)\|w\|^2,                                      \tag{10}
\]
in angular units, or $24\|w\|^2$ in the canonical normalization above.
The same fixed-exterior qualification applies when an outer record is fixed.
Thus EXACT original marginals, their retained scores and their raw scalar
state form an invariant class under the next integration; their Fisher norm
cannot increase. No assumption that the induced density is Wilson is used.
The loss in(9) can be zero, so this is NOT a uniform strict contraction for
physical modes or a state-count bound.

\paragraph{6. What one more scale does and does not establish.}
Equation(2) proves exact composition of the full scalar/current/response state.
For a larger side $R'=LR$ that remains in the boundary only preparation and nonflat block calculation's range, its direct theorem
can be used once on an actually prepared outer record. Previous local errors
are not accumulated, and original global loops remain on the retained
skeleton. The new boundary gauge is computed from those original outer links;
previous prepared coordinates are not mistaken for independent new Haar data.

However the connected local estimate itself has
$\epsilon_2(LR)$ growing roughly as $L^{38}\epsilon_2(R)$ at fixed $B$,
not contracting. Comparing its norm to a physical Schur mode of energy
$\asymp R^{-1}\|w\|^2$ adds another factor $R$. The norm bound(10) also lacks
that wavelength factor. Neither (8) nor (9) is the desired physical-scale
induction. The missing main estimate is a connected boundary/current norm
with wavelength-sensitive cancellation (including live scalar and frame
responses) that contracts, or stays uniformly summable, under this EXACT
composition. It cannot be obtained by resetting the induced action, by
summing unsummable per-cell errors, or by assuming all cells good. FullS94
remains open.

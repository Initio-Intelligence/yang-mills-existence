\paragraph{Actual block and normalization.}
Take a complete spatial cubical patch, with every plaquette incident to its
free links included and all lateral exterior/collar links fixed. Cross a
selective temporal separator with an interval of $T$ temporal steps. Do NOT
integrate one color while dropping its compensating neighboring links: every
free link of this four-dimensional block is included. For the original action
$B\sum_p[1-N^{-1}\operatorname{ReTr}U_p]$, choose generators with
$-\operatorname{Tr}T^aT^b=\delta^{ab}$ and write $U_b=\exp(\theta_b)$ near the
flat reference. Its exact quadratic Taylor term is
\[
 S^{(2)}=\frac B{2N}\|d\theta\|^2=\frac12\|d x\|^2,
 \qquad x=\sqrt{B/N}\,\theta.                                          \tag{1}
\]
The incidence $d$ contains ALL original block plaquettes and boundary collars.
Thus it includes every compensating neighboring-link term, not just the
isolated cross coefficient of the selective separator original seam calculation.

Fix an actual relative axial/spanning-tree gauge on a contractible block; the
compact Haar gauge change has Jacobian one. In the linearized coordinates use
the corresponding independent non-tree links. If $J$ are the integrated
coordinates and $R$ the retained boundary coordinates, the literal incidence
matrix $K=d^*d$ in this chart gives
\[
 K_{\rm eff}=K_{RR}-K_{RJ}K_{JJ}^{-1}K_{JR},\qquad
 x_J^*(b)=-K_{JJ}^{-1}K_{JR}b.                                         \tag{2}
\]
This is the full block Schur complement. Zero gauge modes must be fixed before
inverting; a zero harmonic mode must instead be retained or fixed by actual
boundary data. It cannot be silently assigned a positive eigenvalue.

For $r=|J|$ independent integrated link coordinates per color,
$d_G=N^2-1$, and a nonsingular relative block, its EXACT Gaussian integral is
\[
 Z_{\rm G}(b)=h_N^r (N/B)^{d_Gr/2}(2\pi)^{d_Gr/2}
             (\det K_{JJ})^{-d_G/2}
             \exp[-\tfrac12\langle b,(K_{\rm eff}\otimes I_{d_G})b\rangle]. \tag{3}
\]
Here $h_N$ is the Haar density at zero in the $d_G$-dimensional Lie coordinate
chart on one link. Every fixed linear change from the tree chart to an
orthonormal physical chart contributes its actual Jacobian to(3).
Formula(3) is an exact Gaussian normalizer and the
fixed-block leading Laplace expression, NOT the exact nonlinear Wilson
normalizer. The latter remains the original compact integral $Z_B(b)$.
In particular no determinant error is dropped by calling it vacuum energy.

\paragraph{Gauss reduction and actual spatial modes.}
Let $D_0:C^0\to C^1$ and $D_1:C^1\to C^2$ be the spatial incidence maps with
the stated relative boundary conditions, so $D_1D_0=0$. Integration of the
temporal scalar variable at each step minimizes
$\|a_{t+1}-a_t-D_0\phi_t\|^2$ and yields the projection onto
$\mathcal V=\ker D_0^*$. Its Gaussian integration factor is
$(2\pi)^{\operatorname{rank}D_0/2}\det{}'(D_0^*D_0)^{-1/2}$ per step and
color; the chosen tree/physical-chart Jacobians and gauge volumes must be
retained consistently with(3). Longitudinal endpoint representatives are
identified by the original gauge projection, not counted as new physical
oscillators. On $\mathcal V$, set $C=D_1^*D_1$.

For a contractible cubical patch with full relative boundary data there is no
harmonic one-form. For the periodic spatial torus, instead, there are exactly
three linearized harmonic one-form directions per Lie component, and
$\dim\mathcal V=2n^3+1$: the nonzero physical modes have total real dimension
$2(n^3-1)$, plus three harmonic directions. This is the linearized tangent
count, not a claim that the genuine compact global sector is three independent
$\dim G$ rotors. Nonabelian commuting holonomy constraints and the original
compact harmonic integrations remain outside a noncompact Gaussian replacement.

\paragraph{Exact temporal Dirichlet-to-Neumann matrix.}
For a normalized spatial physical eigenvector $Cf=\lambda f$, the action of
its temporal coefficient is
\[
 \frac12\sum_{t=0}^{T-1}(q_{t+1}-q_t)^2
  +\frac\lambda4(q_0^2+q_T^2)+\frac\lambda2\sum_{t=1}^{T-1}q_t^2.        \tag{4}
\]
The half-endpoint factors are the original symmetric-transfer convention.
Let $\omega=\operatorname{arcosh}(1+\lambda/2)$. Solving its second-order
recurrence, or taking the Schur complement of the tridiagonal matrix, gives
\[
 S_{\partial,\lambda}=\frac12\left[
 a_T(q_0^2+q_T^2)-2b_Tq_0q_T\right],\quad
 a_T=\sinh\omega\coth(T\omega),\quad
 b_T=\sinh\omega\operatorname{csch}(T\omega).                           \tag{5}
\]
The exact interior determinant is
\[
 \det K_{\lambda,\rm int}=\frac{\sinh(T\omega)}{\sinh\omega},            \tag{6}
\]
with value $T$ at $\lambda=0$ and value one when $T=1$.
Its contribution $(2\pi)^{(T-1)/2}\det K_{\lambda,\rm int}^{-1/2}$ stays
in the raw Gaussian kernel. Formula(5) contains all compensating links through
$C$; it is not obtained by tensorizing isolated seam responses.

For $q_{\rm av}=(q_0+q_T)/\sqrt2$ and
$q_{\rm dif}=(q_0-q_T)/\sqrt2$, the two precision eigenvalues are
\[
 \Lambda_{\rm av}=\sinh\omega\tanh(T\omega/2),\qquad
 \Lambda_{\rm dif}=\sinh\omega\coth(T\omega/2).                          \tag{7}
\]
For $\lambda=0$ they are respectively zero and $2/T$; the common harmonic
coordinate is not confined. On the bounded lattice spectral interval, (7)
gives universal comparability
\[
 \Lambda_{\rm av}\asymp\min\{T\lambda,\sqrt\lambda\},\qquad
 \Lambda_{\rm dif}\asymp T^{-1}+\sqrt\lambda.                            \tag{8}
\]
Here the zero-mode convention is explicit. For a relative cube of lattice
side $R$, componentwise discrete Poincare and the relative Hodge identity
$\|D_1a\|^2+\|D_0^*a\|^2=\|\nabla a\|^2$ give
$cR^{-2}\le\lambda\le C$ on $\mathcal V$.
The identity follows by expanding the commuting coordinate differences; cross
terms cancel with the relative boundary conditions. Each one-form component
has a transverse Dirichlet direction, giving the stated Poincare bound.
Boundary conditions, not a mass term, produce this finite-block lower bound.

\paragraph{Surface/volume and physical-wavelength scaling.}
A complete spatial cube has $O(R^3)$ physical modes and two temporal faces of
that order; the lateral collar has $O(R^2T)$ coordinates. Its Gaussian bulk
determinant involves $O(R^3T)$ variables and must cancel only in exact
normalized comparisons. It is not bounded uniformly merely because the
retained temporal boundary has fewer coordinates.

Let lattice mesh be $a$, $T\sim t/a$, and a low spatial mode have
$\lambda\sim a^2|p|^2$. Then
\[
 \Lambda_{\rm av}/a\to |p|\tanh(t|p|/2),\qquad
 \Lambda_{\rm dif}/a\to |p|\coth(t|p|/2).                               \tag{9}
\]
For a smooth canonical physical boundary field $A$, the link coordinate is
$x_b=aA_b$. Thus $\sum_b|x_b|^2\sim a^{-1}\int|A|^2$ in three spatial
dimensions, while multiplying by the precision in(9) gives a FINITE physical
boundary quadratic form. For physical wavelength at least $\ell_*$ its cost
is controlled by physical volume times $(\ell_*^{-1}+t^{-1})$ and the squared
field amplitude, with the common-mode low-frequency factor in(9) retained.
By comparison, the exact same-exterior translation/Fisher bound of the exact joint block translation bound calculation
charges $\sum|x_b|^2$ and therefore loses a factor $a^{-1}$ on such a boundary.
This is the explicit stiffness improvement a complete block comparison would
need to recover. For a torus, the difference harmonic mode has finite
$1/t$ cost; its common mode must still use the true compact global sector.

\paragraph{A testable nonlinear comparison, and its actual unresolved loss.}
The original compact block integral in a fixed tree chart is
$Z_B(b)=\int e^{-S_B(b,z)}J(b,z)dz$, with its real domain and Haar Jacobian.
The exact boundary action difference is
$\Gamma_B(b)=-\log[Z_B(b)/Z_B(0)]$; this cancels only the ACTUAL reference
normalizer. A useful interacting statement would control, for physical coarse
boundary displacements $v$, the centered difference
\[
 \Gamma_B(b+v)+\Gamma_B(b-v)-2\Gamma_B(b)
 \ \le C\,v^*K_{\rm eff}v+\mathcal R_B(b,v),                            \tag{10}
\]
with a proved exponential or overlap bound for $\mathcal R_B$ in the ACTUAL
two-slice/window law, uniform in cutoff and complete-block size. A Jacobian
term must be included if the displacement is implemented by a non-Haar chart
map. The coefficient $C$ cannot grow with microscopic volume. A flat-reference
formula at $b=0$ alone does not establish(10) under that law.

There is a precise reason the elementary Taylor comparison is insufficient.
On a DECLARED common near-flat link chart of angular radius $\eta$, finite
plaquette smoothness and bounded incidence give, in canonical coordinates,
\[
 |D^2S_B(X)[w,w]-\|dw\|^2|\le C\eta\sum_{\rm block}|w_b|^2.             \tag{11}
\]
This is a sufficient rough bound, not an asserted optimal remainder. The chart
has not been supplied by an arbitrary gauge-invariant plaquette-good record.
On a physical block the Poincare comparison costs the inverse lowest mode,
of order $\max\{R,T\}^2$. Thus direct use of(11) requires
$\eta\max\{R,T\}^2$ small to preserve the physical stiffness. At
$R,T\asymp a^{-1}$, a fixed small angle, or an inverse power of the
logarithmic coupling, does not suffice for this particular proof.
In canonical coordinates the local log-Haar Hessian is itself $O(B^{-1})$
per link, so a similarly crude treatment also loses an inverse-mode factor
of order $R^2/B$. This is another term to retain or cancel, not a proof of
physical growth. Haar-coordinate contributions and boundary-dependent determinant changes must
also be compared after actual vacuum subtraction; they cannot be dropped as
per-link errors over $O(R^3T)$ variables.

The sparse original bad-plaquette record controls exceptional sets, and the
actual output $L^p$ Hessian bound controls averaged coordinate responses.
Neither presently proves the wavelength-sensitive remainder(10) or cancels
the inverse-mode loss in(11). This is an upper-bound loss, NOT evidence of
actual divergence. A covariant/coarse response estimate, retaining the joint
seam interactions and all changing-record determinants, is the indispensable
interacting step. The Gaussian complete-block prerequisite and its physical
scaling are proved here; the Wilson comparison is explicitly open.

\paragraph{Analysis of the exact joint block translation bound calculation.}
Its pointwise $24B/N$ directional Hessian bound, original Haar integration by
parts, exact marginal-score contraction, finite-translation relative entropy
bound $12B\|w\|^2/N$, and affinity bound all hold. The fixed-exterior scope is
essential: these do not implement an exterior change or delete fine boundary
coordinates. The improvement from their norm cost to the physical form(9) is
precisely what remains to establish nonlinearly.

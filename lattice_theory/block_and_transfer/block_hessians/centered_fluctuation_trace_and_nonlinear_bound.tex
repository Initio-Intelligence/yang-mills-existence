\section*{The leading finite-block Gaussian expression, with its measure factors}
Use the moving RIGHT fluctuation coordinates
\[
 U_j=U_j^*(t)\exp(\alpha z_j).
\]
They are precisely transported by the actual minimum-following LEFT Haar flow. The Haar density is $h_N^r\alpha^n\prod_j j_N(\alpha z_j)$, independent of $t$; $r$ is the number of independent links. Let $H_t$ be the complete interior action Hessian in these transported coordinates. The exact quadratic integral is
\[
 Z_{{\rm quad},t}=e^{-V(t)}h_N^r\alpha^n(2\pi)^{n/2}
                      (\det H_t)^{-1/2}.              \tag{1}
\]
This is the Gaussian fluctuation integral about the ACTUAL minimum, not the original nonlinear partition function. The tree/linear-chart Jacobian must additionally be retained if one changes these coordinates; a moving orthogonal adjoint frame has determinant one.

Its normalized score covariance and fluctuation free-energy derivatives are
\[
 I_{\rm quad}(t)=\tfrac12\operatorname{Tr}[(H_t^{-1}\dot H_t)^2],        \tag{2}
\]
\[
 F_{\rm quad}'=\tfrac12\operatorname{Tr}(H_t^{-1}\dot H_t),\quad
 F_{\rm quad}''=\tfrac12\operatorname{Tr}(H_t^{-1}\ddot H_t)
                -\tfrac12\operatorname{Tr}[(H_t^{-1}\dot H_t)^2].       \tag{3}
\]
These follow from the covariance of $\frac12z^T\dot H_tz$ in the complete centered Gaussian law. In a LEFT fluctuation frame, $\dot H$ also contains the actual moving adjoint-frame commutator; dropping it would not describe this pullback score. Higher Haar terms are not zero: they remain in the nonlinear density and its estimates below. If one instead uses a parameter-dependent non-Haar chart, its Jacobian response is additional data. Relative gauge fixing makes $H_t$ nonsingular here; a compact global/harmonic sector cannot be replaced by this determinant.

The available local derivative and velocity bounds give
\[
 \|\dot H_t\|\le C\alpha R^3,\qquad
 I_{\rm quad}\le\tfrac n2\kappa^{-2}\|\dot H_t\|^2
                    \le CR^{22}/B.                  \tag{4}
\]
The trace contracts one coordinate sum rather than estimating the square of an uncentered quadratic norm. It still contains the inverse-mode loss; (4) is not yet a physical curl/Schur bound.

\section*{The same variance improvement for the ACTUAL nonlinear law}
This step does not infer the nonlinear result from(1). On the fixed convex canonical chart the actual density potential $S_B-\log J_{\rm Haar}$ has Hessian at least $\kappa I$. The convex-domain Poincare inequality therefore gives, for the normalized conditional chart law,
\[
 \operatorname{Var}_{\rm chart}G_t
 \le \kappa^{-1}E_{\rm chart}\|\nabla G_t\|^2.
\]
This is the sharp convex-cutoff inequality established in \S3 of \emph{Nonlinear dispersion and unequal spatial weights} (\texttt{../conditional\_estimates/nonlinear\_dispersion\_and\_unequal\_spatial\_weights.tex}), applied only to this verified chart. Exact stationarity gives $\nabla G_t(x_t^*)=0$, and the literal third-derivative/flow bound is
$\|\nabla^2G_t\|\le C\alpha R^3$. Hence
\[
 \operatorname{Var}_{\rm chart}G_t
 \le C R^6\alpha^2R^6 E_{\rm chart}\|x-x_t^*\|^2
 \le CR^{22}/B.                                      \tag{5}
\]
Restoring the original compact complement by total variance adds only a polynomial times its actual exponentially small probability. No chart selector has been inserted into the original block integral.

\section*{Centering the expected Hessian difference rather than its absolute value}
One more cancellation improves the connected curvature. Put $z=x-x_t^*$ in the ORIGINAL canonical coordinates, and let $H_*=\nabla^2S_B(x_t^*)$, so $H_*\ge\kappa I$.
Choose a smooth cutoff equal to one on half the chart radius and zero outside the chart. Its transition annulus has the same geometric action-gap bound with a smaller positive constant. Original coordinate integration by parts, retaining Haar density, gives
\[
 H_*E z=-E\,\mathcal R(z)+E\nabla\log J_{\rm Haar}(x)
                    +\text{annulus terms},           \tag{6}
\]
up to replacing cutoff moments by full moments with exponentially small error.
Here $\nabla S_B=H_*z+\mathcal R(z)$ and
$\|\mathcal R(z)\|\le C\alpha\|z\|^2$.
The Haar bound is
$\|\nabla\log J_{\rm Haar}(x)\|\le C B^{-1}\|x\|$.
Consequently the ACTUAL mean displacement obeys
\[
 \|Ez\|\le C R^{16}/\sqrt B+\text{tail}.               \tag{7}
\]
Explicitly the two non-tail terms before simplification are
$C R^6[R^{10}/\sqrt B+R^8/B]$.
The cutoff derivative is at most $CR^6/\sqrt B$; its annular expectation and moment corrections are controlled by the original gap and dimension-dependent normalizer, not set to zero as if the chart had no boundary.

In $DG_t$ separate the interior acceleration force:
\[
 DG_t=J_t+\dot w_t\cdot F_J.
\]
Its expectation is exactly zero and its value at the minimum is zero, by the original interior Haar Ward identity; this is not a pointwise deletion.
The remaining $J_t$ consists of the same-field Hessian on the fixed instantaneous velocity and the ACTUAL boundary acceleration term. Local fourth-derivative and frame estimates give
\[
 \|\nabla J_t(x_t^*)\|\le C\alpha R^6,\qquad
 \sup_{\rm chart}\|\nabla^2J_t\|\le C\alpha^2R^6.       \tag{8}
\]
For the acceleration partner, the boundary canonical acceleration is $O(\alpha)$ and the relevant third action derivative is $O(\alpha)$, so it is included in(8). All velocities here are frozen only for spatial differentiation, not for the time derivative.

Taylor expansion inside the expectation, now using the SIGNED mean(7), yields
\[
 |EJ_t-J_t(x_t^*)|
 \le C\alpha R^6\|Ez\|+
           C\alpha^2R^6E\|z\|^2+\text{tail}
 \le C(R^{22}+R^{16})/B+\text{tail}.                  \tag{9}
\]
This replaces an absolute first-moment estimate of order $R^{11}/\sqrt B$.

Let $F(t)=-\log[Z(\eta(t))/Z(\eta(0))]-[V(t)-V(0)]$, with BOTH terms exact. Since $V''=DG_t(U^*)$, equations(5),(9) and the exact variance subtraction prove
\[
 \boxed{\quad \operatorname{Var}G_t\le CR^{22}/B+\mathcal E,\qquad
                  |F''(t)|\le CR^{22}/B+\mathcal E.\quad}             \tag{10}
\]
A conservative common tail is
\[
 \mathcal E=C B^3R^{40}
   \exp[-cBR^{-18}+CR^{15}+CR^4\log(2+BR)],
\]
with a smaller $c>0$ for the annulus. It absorbs the coordinate cutoff, inverse floor and global group-derivative polynomial factors. For $R\le B^{1/36}$ this tail is negligible and the two bounds in(10) are $O(B^{-7/18})$. Admission still requires $B\gg R^{34}$; no larger range is claimed here.

\section*{What has improved and what still lacks a physical-scale bound}
The fluctuation current $F'(0)$ remains stored; its prior bound $CR^{13}/\sqrt B$ is not erased by(10). None is asserted here.

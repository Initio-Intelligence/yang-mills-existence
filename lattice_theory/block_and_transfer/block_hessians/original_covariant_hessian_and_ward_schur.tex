\section*{Joint displacement with the complete interior Haar measure}
Take a boundary left-geodesic path $\eta(t)=e^{tv}\eta$ linkwise and any deterministic interior left translation $u\mapsto e^{tw}u$. Haar invariance gives the EXACT representation
\[
 Z(\eta(t))=\int \exp[-S(e^{tw}u;e^{tv}\eta)]du.
\]
Consequently, at zero, with $D=X_v+X_w$,
\[
 \Gamma''(0)=E D^2S-\operatorname{Var}(DS).             \tag{1}
\]
For a nonconstant boundary velocity there is additionally $E X_{\dot v}S$ in the geodesic acceleration convention; it cannot be dropped. Interior translation is a change of integration variables, so the value in(1) is independent of $w$. There is no Haar-coordinate correction in this representation. Hard selectors or nonlinear chart maps would require their own response; none is present in this original compact block identity.

For the flat quadratic reference choose $w$ as the COMPLETE Gaussian harmonic extension, with actual gauge zero modes handled as in the complete block schur and physical stiffness calculation. Then the interior-linear part of $DS$ vanishes, its conditional variance is zero, and(1) equals the full Gaussian Schur boundary energy. This explains the desired wavelength scale without asserting the same cancellation in the interacting integral.

\section*{An exact interacting Schur upper bound, before any estimate}
Choose divergence-free left-invariant tangent bases for the free interior links and boundary links. Write $F_i=X_iS$, $F_b=X_bS$, and let $H$ denote the symmetric second directional derivative matrix in these bases. Haar integration by parts on interior links gives
\[
 E F_i=0,\qquad C_{ii}:=E H_{ii}=\operatorname{Cov}(F_i,F_i),\qquad
 C_{ib}:=E H_{ib}=\operatorname{Cov}(F_i,F_b).           \tag{2}
\]
Symmetrization causes no error: the expectation of an interior commutator derivative is zero by the same Haar identity. Interior and boundary vector fields commute. The actual fixed-exterior conditional law is used everywhere.

Let $C_{bb}=E H_{bb}$ and let $C_{ii}^{\dagger}$ be the Moore--Penrose inverse. Every column of $C_{ib}$ lies in the range of $C_{ii}$: a null interior-score direction is zero in $L^2$, and hence has zero covariance with every boundary score. Therefore for any boundary tangent $v$,
\[
 \begin{split}
 \Gamma''(v,v)
 &=v^*\left(C_{bb}-C_{bi}C_{ii}^{\dagger}C_{ib}\right)v\\
 &\quad-\operatorname{Var}\left(v^*F_b-v^*C_{bi}C_{ii}^{\dagger}F_i\right).
 \end{split}                                                        \tag{3}
\]
This is an EXACT interacting identity, not a Schur complement of the flat reference. It follows by subtracting the optimal linear regression onto the original interior scores in
$\Gamma''(v,v)=v^*C_{bb}v-\operatorname{Var}(v^*F_b)$.
In particular the first line of(3) is a valid upper bound. Equivalently it is
$\inf_w E D^2S$; all covariance cross terms in(1) cancel the dependence on the chosen change of variables before that upper bound is taken.
The pseudoinverse handles genuine null vectors of the deterministic score covariance, not an artificial positive mass. Actual nonlinear gauge velocities have field-dependent coefficients and need not lie in this deterministic null space; their divergence and acceleration require the separate exact gauge-flow identity of the field dependent ward and exact gauge alignment calculation. No positivity of $C_{bb}$ or uniform spectral floor for $C_{ii}$ is assumed.

\section*{Literal nonabelian plaquette Hessian}
At one plaquette write the original oriented factors as $V_1V_2V_3V_4=P$.
A positive-oriented varied link contributes $A_j=w_b$ in
$V_j(t)=e^{tA_j}V_j$. For an inverse-oriented link $V_j=U_b^{-1}$, the original left variation gives
$A_j=-\operatorname{Ad}_{U_b^{-1}}w_b$.
Define the ACTUAL prefix transports
\[
 X_j=\operatorname{Ad}_{V_1\cdots V_{j-1}}A_j,\qquad
 C_p=\sum_jX_j,\qquad K_p=\sum_{j<k}[X_j,X_k].
\]
The exact path is $P(t)=e^{tX_1}\cdots e^{tX_4}P$ at the base field, and hence
\[
 P''(0)=(C_p^2+K_p)P,\qquad
 D^2s(P)=-N^{-1}\operatorname{ReTr}[(C_p^2+K_p)P].      \tag{4}
\]
Thus the covariant curl is $C_p$, not an ordinary curl evaluated on arbitrary fine links. The commutator trace vanishes at $P=I$ but need not vanish in the interacting law.

Since $C_p$ is anti-Hermitian, $\operatorname{Re}P\le I$ gives
$-\operatorname{ReTr}(C_p^2P)\le\|C_p\|_{\rm HS}^2$.
Also $\operatorname{Tr}K_p=0$ and
$\|P-I\|_{\rm HS}^2=2N s(P)$. Therefore the exact upper estimate is
\[
 D^2S\le {B\over N}\sum_p\|C_p\|_{\rm HS}^2
       +B\sum_p\sqrt{{2s(P)\over N}}\ \|K_p\|_{\rm HS}. \tag{5}
\]
The SIGNED commutator term and the negative variance in(1) or(3) should be retained if a sharper comparison is sought; taking absolute values in(5) may lose the needed cancellation. Furthermore
$\|K_p\|_{\rm HS}\le2\sum_{j<k}\|w_{b_j}\|_{\rm HS}\|w_{b_k}\|_{\rm HS}$,
so the old $O(B\|w\|^2)$ bound is only a rough consequence.

When every prefix transport preserves a common Abelian tangent algebra, all these commutators vanish. In the actual compact Abelian Wilson model, $C_p$ is the ordinary curl, and choosing the complete harmonic extension in(1) proves the boundary upper bound by its Schur curl energy directly, without a near-flat approximation. This statement is an Abelian check of(4)--(5), not a replacement of the full $SU(N)$ integral by a Cartan model.

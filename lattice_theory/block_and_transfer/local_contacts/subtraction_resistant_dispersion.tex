\title{Two-link dispersion after arbitrary single-plaquette subtractions}\author{}\date{}
\begin{document}\maketitle
This strengthens the bare-source dispersion argument in \cite[Section~2]{ND}
using the two-link geometry of the orthogonal contact calculation. It concerns the original real positive
Wilson law and explicitly delimits the subtraction class. It changes no source prescription.

\begin{lemma}[Four-corner band deficit]
Let $Q$ be smooth on $[-r,r]^d\times[-r,r]^d$, and suppose
$\partial_{z_1}\partial_{w_1}Q\ge\kappa>0$ there.
For arbitrary measurable real functions $A(z),B(w)$ and every interval $I$ of length
$h_0=\kappa r^2/4$, the complement of
$\{Q-A-B\in I\}$ has Lebesgue volume at least one sixteenth of the whole product cube.
If the probability density is at least $m_0$ on that cube, its probability is at least
$p_0=m_0(2r)^{2d}/16$.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
Choose $z_1^-,w_1^-\in[-r,-r/2]$ and $z_1^+,w_1^+\in[r/2,r]$, with all four
coordinates independently uniform on their intervals; choose common transverse coordinates
$z',w'$ uniformly on their cubes. Write $F=Q-A-B$.
The alternating rectangle difference of $F$ equals that of $Q$, since both additive
terms cancel. Double integration of the mixed derivative shows this difference is at
least $\kappa r^2$.
If all four corner values of $F$ belonged to $I$, their alternating difference would have
absolute value at most $2h_0<\kappa r^2$. Hence at least one corner is outside the band.
Each corner is uniformly distributed on one of four disjoint subcubes of volume exactly
$(2r)^{2d}/16$. Taking expectations and summing the four failure probabilities proves
that the total outside volume in these subcubes is at least that amount.
No derivative, boundedness, or moment assumption on $A,B$ is used.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[Dispersion survives arbitrary plaquette counterterms]
Let $f$ be a fixed real bounded physical test equal to one on a positive-volume box, with
its original finite pointed completion. Let
\[
 \mathcal L_a(U)=c_a+\sum_p h_{a,p}(U_p)
\]
be any deterministic real single-plaquette subtraction, with finite measurable summands
and square-integrability under the original finite Wilson law. Coefficients and functions
may depend arbitrarily on the cutoff and coupling. For $G_a=O_H(f)$ or the specified
ordinary $r_H(f)$, there are uniform constants such that
\[
 \sup_c\Pr\{|G_a-\mathcal L_a-c|\le R\}
 \le\min\{1,C_f[\log(2+\beta)/\beta+a^2+a^4R]\},
 \qquad\beta\ge\beta_0, a<a_0, R\ge0.
\]
In particular, if $\beta(a)\to\infty$, no such subtraction makes the resulting bare
random variable tight. Tightness after an additional deterministic real multiplier $z_a$
requires $|z_a|=O(a^4)$. This is necessary only, not a sufficient normalization prescription.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Pack $m_a\ge c_fa^{-4}$ separated two-link blocks inside the plateau, with complete
pointed determining stencils in its interior. Condition on all other links. Every actual
source summand meets at most one block, and the original Wilson law factors over the
free links. No plaquette contains both free links of a block, so every summand of
$\mathcal L_a$ is either a bath term or a function of a single free link.

The good-bath construction of the orthogonal contact calculation and PD12 gives probability of a bad bath at each
block at most $C\log(2+\beta)/\beta$. Markov therefore bounds the probability that
more than half the blocks are bad by twice this number; no independence of bath events
is assumed. On a good bath use the exact conditional minima to write
$z=\sqrt\beta(u-u_*)$, $w=\sqrt\beta(v-v_*)$. The joint conditional density is bounded
below on a fixed product cube.
For the dimensionless local source $G_j^0$, its rescaling $Q=\beta G_j^0$ has mixed
coordinate derivative bounded below by a fixed $\kappa>0$: the flat values are $4/3$
for H and $2$ for D, and the Casimir single-plaquette companion has zero mixed derivative.
Continuity on the finite good-bath chart supplies the uniform lower bound.
The physical block variable is $\sigma G_j^0-\mathcal L_{j,-}-\mathcal L_{j,+}$,
$\sigma=g^{-2}a^{-2}$. Multiplying by $\beta/\sigma$ gives exactly
$Q-A(z)-B(w)$. The lemma proves a fixed concentration deficit $1-p_0$ at radius $h_0$,
uniformly in all subtraction functions and their sizes.

Apply the equal-radius Kolmogorov--Rogozin inequality to the at least $m_a/2$ good,
conditionally independent block variables. Convolution with the other blocks cannot
increase concentration. An interval of length $h$ has conditional probability at most
$C(1+h)m_a^{-1/2}$. Since $\beta/\sigma=c_\beta a^2$, a physical interval of radius
$R$ has scaled length $2c_\beta a^2R$. Average over the bath and use the bad-count bound.
This gives the theorem. The tightness and multiplier conclusions follow exactly by
applying the interval estimate along a subsequence with $|z_a|/a^4\to\infty$.
\end{proof}

\paragraph{Sharper bad-bath error on the physical regime.}
The same-measure local exponential moment of the odd torus transfer moment calculation bounds the probability that any plaquette
in a fixed determining cube exceeds its fixed near-flat threshold by $Ce^{-c\beta}$
when $n\ge c_*\log(2+N\beta)$. The same bath-measurable existence implication used
above therefore replaces the first error term by $Ce^{-c\beta}$ on that regime, including
original odd tori. No change to the conditional source or denominator is made.

\paragraph{Scope.}
This result includes the literal Casimir companion and arbitrary identity/action mixing,
but not arbitrary multi-plaquette operators or field-dependent coefficients multiplying
several plaquettes. In particular it does not rule out the explicit local degree-two
source extension of the local source transition calculation, whose terminal covariance subtraction changes the coincident
product rather than the individual bare random variable. It also does not prove that
all divergence is local or construct a continuum hierarchy. The full original scalar/current
pairs and their responses remain required in every later operation.

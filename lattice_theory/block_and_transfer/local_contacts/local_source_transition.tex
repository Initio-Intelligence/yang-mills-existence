Use the definitions and hypotheses of the orthogonal contact calculation (\texttt{orthogonal\_contact.tex}). Let $\mathcal B$ be
all links outside the packed pairs, and write $G_i=R_i+S_i$, where $R_i$ is the sum
of the double-centered pair residues and $S_i=G_i-R_i$. Here $G_i$ must consistently
be the physical $O_H(f_i)$ or the ordinary $r_H(f_i)$; these are different sources.
Let $\mathcal K$ denote the original normalized conditional integration of all free links.
This is a genuine finite Wilson integration with its original product one-link density.

\paragraph{Stronger conditional orthogonality.}
Conditioned on $\mathcal B$, the observable is a sum of two-variable block observables.
In each block, removing its double-centered part leaves a constant and two additive
single-variable functions. Thus, directly under the product conditional law,
\[
 \mathcal K R_i=0,\qquad \mathcal K(R_iS_j)=0,\qquad
 \mathcal K(R_1R_2)=V_{12}(\mathcal B).
\]
For the physical source the exact local coefficient is
\[
 V_{12}(\mathcal B)=\frac{16}{9}\sigma^2
 \sum_{j} (f_1)_{b_{0,j}}(f_2)_{b_{0,j}}\operatorname{tr}(C_{j,-}C_{j,+}).
\]
The ordinary source has factor $4\sigma^2$ in place of $16\sigma^2/9$.
All finite pointed completions require the original double average, not a common-root
replacement. Each coefficient depends on a fixed local bath and its actual one-link
conditional denominators. It is independent of total torus size once the local patch embeds:
this locality statement concerns the FUNCTION $V_{12}$, unlike its full-measure expectation.
For equal real tests it is pointwise nonnegative.

\paragraph{Exact, explicitly changed source.}
In the squarefree degree-two algebra, define
\[
 A^{\rm loc}=\eta_1G_1+\eta_2G_2-\eta_1\eta_2V_{12}(\mathcal B).
\]
Then the exact identity is
\[
 \mathcal K e^{A^{\rm loc}}=\mathcal K e^{\eta_1S_1+\eta_2S_2}.
\]
Proof: the constant coefficients are one, the linear coefficients agree because
$\mathcal K R_i=0$, and the mixed coefficients agree because
$\mathcal K(G_1G_2)-V_{12}=\mathcal K(S_1S_2)$.
This equality also holds for the unnormalized original Wilson integration, multiplied by
its actual bath density and conditional normalizer. Subsequent original linear operations
on the bath preserve it. A selector depending only on the bath factors unchanged; no
integration by parts has been used and no boundary partner has been discarded.
The identity need not hold for a selector, readout or kernel depending on the free links.
It is a verified transition for a specified full compact fiber, not identification with all
mixed maps in the manuscript. For the original XP3 source, $A_{12}=0$, the additional
$V_{12}$ is PRESENT in the output; the identity does not authorize deleting it.

\paragraph{Deterministic versus local field-dependent subtraction.}
Put $K_{12}=\E V_{12}$. A deterministic extension $A_{12}=-K_{12}$ produces the
same terminal scalar but differs before full integration. For any output $q$ measurable
with respect to the bath,
\[
 A_{12}^{\rm out,det}
 =\Cov(S_1,S_2\mid q)+\E(V_{12}\mid q)-\E V_{12},
 \qquad
 A_{12}^{\rm out,loc}=\Cov(S_1,S_2\mid q).
\]
The fluctuating zero-mean scalar in the first formula is a real generated coefficient.
It cannot be declared absent because its final expectation vanishes. If $q$ depends on
free links, all conditional mixed terms involving $R_i$ must be retained as well.
This gives a concrete limitation on transporting terminal covariance subtractions backward
through an arbitrary readout.

\paragraph{Actual real compact derivative bounds.}
For each fixed order $m$, and every ordered string $\mathcal D^m$ of invariant bath-link
derivatives in the finite determining patch,
\[
 |\mathcal D^m V_{12,j}|\le C_m\sigma^2(1+\beta)^m
 |(f_1)_{b_{0,j}}(f_2)_{b_{0,j}}|.
\]
These are real compact bounds without a small denominator assumption. To prove them,
write each conditional moment as a ratio of finite Haar integrals. A bath derivative obeys
\[
 D\langle H\rangle_b=\langle DH\rangle_b
 -\beta\{\langle H DS_b\rangle_b-\langle H\rangle_b\langle DS_b\rangle_b\}.
\]
Every fixed derivative of the finite plaquette action and force function is uniformly
bounded on the compact group. Repeatedly apply this identity and Leibniz: derivatives
of order $m$ are finite sums of normalized moments of bounded derivatives, with at most
$m$ factors of $\beta$. The same applies to the covariance matrices and their product.
This proof retains the derivative of the genuine conditional denominator explicitly as a
centered score. No complex zero-free domain or uniform inherited-score estimate follows.

On the good baths of the preceding note, conditional concentration implies
$\|C_\pm\|\le C\beta^{-1}$, hence
$|V_{12,j}|\le Ca^{-4}|(f_1)_{b_0}(f_2)_{b_0}|$.
Globally the weaker bound is $C\sigma^2=Cc_\beta^{-2}\beta^2a^{-4}$ per block.
The averaged bound proved previously is $Ca^{-4}\beta\log(2+\beta)$ per block.
None of these estimates supplies the required inherited graded MT36 norm.
A physical current acting on $V_{12}$ must differentiate this local function, including
its conditional denominators; it is not an independent field-free scalar. Its current
companion and all live-reference/readout responses must be retained by the manuscript's
full weak transport rules. No pair of boundary distributions is multiplied here.

\paragraph{Normalization and unresolved issue.}
The extension is constructed at the original mesh. Saved field-independent factors enter
as $\Gamma_1\Gamma_2$; subsequent output meshes never replace its original $\sigma^2$.
Raw numerators and independent absolute scalars remain unchanged except for the explicitly
added mixed-source coefficient. The extension supplies a local finite-cutoff prescription
for a particular positive contact component. It has not removed or bounded the remaining
$\Cov(S_1,S_2)$, supplied a universal continuum normalization, or proved reflection
positivity and higher-degree consistency. The original manuscript must still bound its
unchanged source, or explicitly adopt and analyze a renormalized prescription.

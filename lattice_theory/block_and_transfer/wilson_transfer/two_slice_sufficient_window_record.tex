\section*{An actual smaller sufficient record}
Original finite temporal range implies that conditional on $H$, the interior Wilson slab, including every interior temporal link, is independent of $X$ and of the exterior slab. Integrating this ORIGINAL interior bridge to the chosen partial window gives a probability kernel $R_H(d\omega)$ independent of $X$. Its normalizer is exactly $K_h(U,V)$, including all half-action factors and gauge averages. In particular
\[
 \nu_X(d\omega)=\int\gamma_X(dU,dV)R_{U,V}(d\omega),  \tag{1}
\]
where the normalized ORIGINAL two-slice conditional density is
\[
 \gamma_X(U,V)=
 \frac{K_s(X,U)K_h(U,V)K_s(V,X)}{K_m(X,X)}.           \tag{2}
\]
The semigroup identity proves its normalization. Equation(1) concerns a genuine conditional bridge, not a projection of the previous whole-path bath density that reverses an inequality.

Affinity increases under the COMMON probability kernel $R$, giving
\[
 \operatorname{Aff}(\nu_X,\nu_{X'})
 \ge\operatorname{Aff}(\gamma_X,\gamma_{X'}).         \tag{3}
\]
If instead the entire interior path together with its two boundary slices is retained, its affinity is EXACTLY the right side of(3): disintegrate over $H$ and the conditional bridge has affinity one with itself. Thus two full boundary slices are a sufficient statistic for the dependence of a complete interior path on the distant initial slice. This is an exact factorization of the original measure, not an approximation in physical window thickness.

The retained record now has two full spatial slices instead of $O(w)$ complementary spatial slices and temporal layers. For $w\asymp\log(1/a)$ this is a strict reduction in temporal coordinate count. It includes the selected colors at its endpoints and all global spatial modes. No spatial coarse-graining or second-color independence is claimed.

\section*{Explicit original two-slice overlap with all normalizers}
The true initial marginal is $p(X)=K_m(X,X)/Z_m$. Define
\[
 \mathcal P_{s,h}^{(2)}
 =E_{X,X'\ {\rm iid}\ p}\operatorname{Aff}(\gamma_X,\gamma_{X'})^2.
\]
The exact boundary denominators in(2) cancel against this initial marginal, yielding
\[
 \mathcal P_{s,h}^{(2)}=\frac1{Z_m^2}\int dX\,dX'
 \left[\int dU\,dV\ K_h(U,V)
 \sqrt{K_s(X,U)K_s(X',U)K_s(V,X)K_s(V,X')}\right]^2.   \tag{4}
\]
Every $K$ is the ORIGINAL kernel. A common scalar multiplying $A$ contributes the same power $2m$ to numerator and denominator in(4), and cancels only there. The short central bridge has not been replaced by a delta function or normalized reference density.

Combining(3) with the thin-window lower bound of the interior window affinity lift calculation gives the actual comparison
\[
 \mathcal P_{\rm full}
 \ge(1-16\delta^2)^{N_I}
       [\mathcal P_{s,h}^{(2)}-2\mathbb P(\mathcal G^c)].                \tag{5}
\]
The original sparse-record bound still controls $\mathbb P(\mathcal G^c)$; no new probability law was introduced. Importantly, (5) does not replace its bracket by an output midpoint purity.

For an equivalent operator expression, set
\[
 F_{X,X'}(U)=\sqrt{K_s(X,U)K_s(X',U)}.
\]
The inner integral in(4) is $\langle F_{X,X'},A^h F_{X,X'}\rangle$. This is a positive quadratic form in the ACTUAL central transfer and makes the still-needed comparison explicit. At the formal zero-width case $h=0$, (4) reduces by the semigroup identity to $Z_{4s}/Z_{2s}^2$, the original full-slice purity. For $h>0$ there is an additional boundary correlation to estimate; continuity at fixed cutoff is not uniform control as $a\to0$.

# Strict births, clean descendants and reverse conditioning

All probabilities are in the original positive lift. The analytic inputs RB9.20–21 and geometric inputs H8-A and H8.3 are used as stated in the companion notes in this directory. This note proves consequences of those inputs and gives a necessary and sufficient interface test for a reverse reveal. It does not establish the general multiscale estimate.

## 1. An arbitrary-base clean episode

Fix a deterministic step b and write t=b+1, q_b=p_0(g_b)^2. Use the event that the incoming carrier H is regular in the precise sense of RB9.1 and the next background W is in the strict set of RB9. No conditioning on microscopic past labels inside H is allowed. At time t select unresolved components all of whose geometric origins in this step are actual Q/R flags at step b. Components with an imported old region, P flag, or preparatory birth as a geometric origin are excluded. Other branches can be ignored when taking upper bounds. At j≥t keep components whose entire still-unresolved ancestry after t consists of clean descendants of those seeds, allowing their mergers. As in H8, an intervening new birth, failed preparation, successful reset, or import from an ineligible ancestor excludes that component. Denote the selected cells by A(b,j). This describes a class by events in the lift. It requires no identification of pre-b flags that were already summed into H. If an implementation coalesces labels, the selected post-b flags are retained from their defining decomposition exactly as in RB9.19.

**Proposition.** There are uniform c,C>0 and w_b=ceil(C_R R_t)+C (increased by the fixed H8 geometry constants), such that for any deterministic family S of distinct j-cells,

    Π(S ⊂ A(b,j)) ≤ exp(−c B(b,j)|S|),
    B(b,j) = q_b 2^{floor((j−t)/w_b)}.                         (1)

For empty S interpret the inequality trivially. This is a probability of membership in an actual unresolved component. It does not require that every birth leave such a component.

**Proof of the base estimate.** In CMP119 pp.264–269, (3.3), (3.5), (3.16), (3.20), the Q and R bad test cubes enter the large region by taking their intersecting current MR_t-cells and a fixed number of neighboring layers. Previous and P regions enter separately. The paragraph after (3.20) fills only the bounding rectangle of a component fitting in a 100-cell box. A newly covered cell of a selected component lies within a fixed number of t-cells of a Q/R flag. For a newly filled cell, pick an original point of the component within 100 cells and apply the same assertion there. Merging by unions of these sets preserves a witness near each individual cell. Deletion through processing only restricts the event. The Q/R test grids and active grid differ by fixed factors involving L,M,M_2, so there are at most a fixed number C_0 of candidate flags near one t-cell. Sources: [CMP119](https://doi.org/10.1007/BF01217741), [LFR II, pp.384–386](https://doi.org/10.1007/BF01238433).

Color the bounded-degree conflict graph of these deterministic candidate neighborhoods to extract at least |S|/C_1 disjoint neighborhoods. For each choice of one flag per retained cell, bound its joint probability with the strict carrier by RB9.21, averaged over H,W. There are at most C_0^{|S|} choices. RB9 already allows the geometric eligibility of an R cube to depend on the Q flags. Do not separately condition on the observed Q pattern, the later surviving component, or its individual posterior. The weak endpoint pays the candidate entropy. This proves (1) at j=t.

**Proof of later occupancy.** Apply H8-A on clean windows after t, as in H9.26–27. Fixed-fraction coloring of terminal roots gives disjoint leaf cones, with at least c 2^{floor((j−t)/w_b)} distinct t-cells per selected root. A remainder window of length less than w_b costs exp(C w_b) choices per leaf by finite propagation. All embeddings together cost exp(C w_b N) for N base leaves. Applying the preceding base estimate to those leaves and using q_b/w_b large proves (1). No product of probabilities at two different strict steps was used. In particular this argument never conditions RB9 on a later history label.

**Ordinary size and separation.** Let Y denote a selected actual component, ℓ_j(Y) its size in the ordinary fixed M-block convention, r the distance between the marked supports (up to the fixed support collars of H8). For each fixed λ≥0,

    E Σ_{Y containing x,y} exp(λℓ_j(Y))
      ≤ C exp[−c B(b,j)(1+r/(h_j R_j))].                      (2)

Indeed, a connected m-cell animal has ℓ_j≤C R_j^4 m, at most C^m embeddings from x, and m≥c(1+r/(h_j R_j)). Use (1). The trajectory gives R_j≤C R_b, q_b ≫ R_b^4 uniformly at the weak endpoint. Root collars only change fixed constants in this estimate. With y=x it is the ordinary-size moment.

## 2. Selected microscopic old marks with a depth budget

Use terminal units and the H8.12 bare bound |F_0(x)|≤A_x a^{-4}T(a)^s. Fix s≥0. Select pairs (b,j) obeying

    B(b,j) ≥ C_s (1+j),                                        (3)

with C_s chosen using the c in (2) and D_s in H8.14. This includes b up to a sufficiently small fixed multiple of q_b for every later j, since j=t+d and d≤w_b(1+floor(d/w_b))≤2w_b 2^{floor(d/w_b)}. It also includes arbitrarily old births after enough clean branching. It grants no rarity to the first short return after an arbitrary much later birth.

For admissible pairs, multiply (2) by the deterministic seed envelope. Converting a^{-8}T(a)^{2s} to h_j^{-8}T(h_j)^{2s} costs at most exp(D_s j). A fraction of (3) pays this and exp(j) for summation. A separate fraction of B absorbs R_j to replace the spatial decay by exp(−c r/h_j). Thus, with q_min the minimum q_b over the weak window, the nonnegative, possibly overcounting, sum satisfies

    Σ_{j,b admissible} E Σ_{Y containing x,y} |F_0(x)F_0(y)| exp(λℓ_j(Y))
      ≤ C A_x A_y e^{−c q_min} r^{-8} T(r)^{2s},   0<r≤1.     (4)

Indeed at most j possible births occur at j, and the residual exp(−j) pays that count. The geometric h_j sum above r is dominated by its finest scale; below r, r/h_j grows geometrically and pays its eighth power and the extra logarithm. Common deterministic subtraction is part of the seed envelope. Translation-averaging preserves the bound. For one root at terminal j=K (use (2) with y=x), the union of (3) events has selected second moment ≤C A_x² ℓ_0^{-8} exp(−c q_min) in physical units. Jensen bounds the squared norm of E[1_union F_0(x)|G] by the same value for every retained sigma algebra G. Equations (2)–(4) concern selected component masses. Their sum does not furnish the full absolute covariance or bound sector cross terms by itself.

At a chosen j one can replace (3) by B(b,j)≥C_s(1+K) when an application needs to pay the entire remaining microscopic/terminal ratio already at j. Subsequent normalized kernels only contract L² of the selected conditional mean. A bound uniform on cross terms still needs a bound on the other factor, or a separate allocation of the bare second-moment cost. Logarithmic factors are paid by increasing C_s.

## 3. Reverse reveal criterion

For one step let I=(H,W), let G be its strict event, and B a fixed-family birth event. Write q=exp(−c p_b² n), where n is the number of prescribed cubes. Let Y record the complete variables on which the later selected continuations depend. For fixed I define the subprobability and probability measures

    ν_I(D)=P(B, Y∈D | I),       μ_I(D)=P(Y∈D | I).

For a cost factor d≥1 the bound ν_I≤d q μ_I on the strict set is equivalent, by Radon–Nikodym, to P(B | I,Y)≤d q there, and to the tested estimate with every nonnegative function of I,Y on the right and left. This equivalence is at the specified interface. A restricted class of continuation tests can need less. If q_b(I)=P(B|I)>0, the distortion at this interface is the likelihood ratio dP(Y∈·|B,I)/dμ_I. Conditional independence of B and Y given I suffices with d=1, but need not hold. When Y is only an interface state, propagation to the whole future requires an actual factorization of the continuation through Y,I with the same tail kernel on both sides. Taking Y to be the entire tail always makes the criterion valid as an equivalence. Using only the physical coarse output as interface requires a proof of the corresponding screening for the selected full history.

At finitely many deterministic scales i, suppose these tested inequalities hold for all subsequent continuation indicators, using a reverse order. Integrate the earliest event against the full later continuation, then drop its strict indicator for an upper bound and iterate with the next event. This yields the product of the d_i q_i. Scalar carrier weights are included in the outer integration. If later factors use a label omitted from the test at an earlier scale, this induction does not apply. RB9 bounds the mass of ν_I; it does not bound its density with respect to μ_I. In a finite model take B_1=B_2={U=1} for a Bernoulli(q) variable retained after step 1, with both displayed I_i constant. Both one-step marginal bounds are q, while the joint probability is q. This demonstrates the nonimplication for resummed one-step information; it is not a model of the Yang–Mills action.

H8.28 supplies conditional independence of a fresh reset interior from an old mark given the *complete* retained E. Passing from I to E can make a selected old birth measurable. The independent draw therefore cannot by itself certify the required inequality for P(B|I,E). LFR I (1.76) also allows replacements on a branch that fails a subsequent selector. The missing input is the selected-to-full continuation likelihood at the interfaces actually retained by such branches, or a bound tested only on the required continuations with a summable joint error. Even a perfect reverse tower restricted to globally strict steps would leave the mixed steps reached while old unresolved components persist.

## 4. A joint bound requiring no new screening input

For deterministic specifications let A be any causal innovation family for which H9.8/H8.26 provides P(A)≤exp(−U), and B_k the strict RB9 birth family at deterministic scale k with weight W_k=c p_k² n_k. Include each required strictly regular carrier in B_k. For a nonempty list of these layers,

    P(A ∩ ⋂ B_k) ≤ exp[−max(U,max_k W_k)].

This is immediate from containment and the standalone normalized bounds. In particular when the sum of the physical-layer costs is ≤D max_k W_k for a fixed D, it yields exp[−(U+ΣW_k)/(D+1)]. The loss is independent of the number of layers in that restricted class. For one physical layer with many other predictable innovations one pays at most a factor two in the exponent. No independence across the classes is asserted, and random choices of witness locations still require an embedding or entropy count. Comparable physical costs on arbitrarily many scales remain untreated.

## 5. A local physical future observable with controlled replacement error

Here is a source-specific alternative to uniform domination by the whole future. Fix the RB9 step b and a finite collection of physical gauge-invariant plaquette tests at later deterministic levels. For a bounded nonnegative function f of these observations put f^0=f evaluated on the corresponding deterministic iterated block averages of W, using the same future frozen block maps. If W itself is subjected to a subsequent current-field operation, include that operation in the test below. Let P denote the event that every link in the complete backward stencil of these observations, from the indicated W onward, has experienced only the ordinary block averages and current-field pass-through up to gauge. Exclude any (1.76) current-coordinate integration in that stencil and every subsequent replacement. Then f(actual)=f^0 on P. This is H9 §1's fixed-current-coordinate fact started at W; earlier processing and operations outside the stencil are unrestricted. The equality concerns gauge-invariant physical tests, with their entire averaging stencil. An eligibility pattern or a mixed minimizing field generally also depends on history.

For any fixed birth family B of n cubes, with G denoting both the compatible regular carrier and the strict next background, q=exp(−c p_b² n), one therefore has

    E[1_G 1_B 1_P f(actual)] ≤ q E[1_G f^0]
                              ≤ q E[1_G f(actual)]
                                 + q ||f||∞ P(G ∩ Pᶜ ∩ {f^0>f(actual)}).     (5)

The first inequality follows by dropping the pass-through indicator after substitution, then testing RB9.21 with the nonnegative function f^0(W). The second follows by decomposing the difference and bounding it on disagreement. In the indicator case the additive term can be sharpened to q P(G ∩ {f^0=1,f(actual)=0}). If P is certain on G, (5) is a true same-law domination for this physical interface. For arbitrary histories (5) pays the *replacement-error probability* with the same q; it does not bound that probability itself. If the selected event on the left omits the restriction P, add the uncontrolled selected mass E[1_G 1_B 1_{Pᶜ} f(actual)]. In particular it is incorrect to assert (5) on arbitrary subsequent reset branches. The effective two-step test q E[1_G f^0] can also be used directly when the shadow observable is the desired test.

An exact unconditional special case is the thin interface consisting solely of gauge-invariant functions of the immediate block field W and its deterministic further block averages *as functions of W*, even with arbitrary other retained geometry unobserved (RB9.21). For a future observed after mandatory intervening transformations, (5) states precisely what must be checked to identify that interface with the actual observation. A deterministic extrapolation of W need not have the law of a later actual physical field when replacements occur. The inequality says nothing about a future Q/R cost that depends on the earlier flags.

## 6. Predictable later births can be combined with an old physical birth at full cost

There is a stronger result than the general containment bound in §4 when all additional innovations are later in forward time. At step b retain the actual strict birth event B together with G from their defining decompositions. Fix a chronologically later sequence of deterministic observed cube families S_i. Require all later Q/R events to belong to the pre-gauge canonical-small and P-admitted sector of H9.37. For discarded geometry interpret these events as false. If each later selection and the earlier event are recorded in the full chronological past to which H9.37 applies, then

    P(G ∩ B ∩ ⋂ B_i^{can}) ≤ exp(−c p_b² n − c' Σ_i p_{k_i}² |S_i|).       (6)

Indeed H9.37 removes the latest family against the earlier indicator, dropping its canonical-small selector on the right. Iterate to reach the b-step event and use RB9.21 once with multiplier one. The iteration is valid even when mixed exterior data have entered later physical inputs, as H9.37 is conditioned on that physical pre-draw state and ordinary gauge draws have the exact normalized innovation law. Equivalently first enlarge the chronological sigma algebra by the retained earlier flag in a forward lift with fresh innovations sampled by their specified normalized kernel; this leaves their law unchanged. The earlier flag must have the RB9 marginal at its own step. Equation (6) also allows later specified actual reset-innovation threshold events from H8-D, with their stated probabilities. Location choices based on subsequent draws still require a union or geometry bound.

In contrast, if the large canonical-*physical* fluctuation is allowed later, the H9.37 event implication is unavailable. A past B can then distort the later regular density if one reveals it to force a forward tower. An earlier gauge-innovation family with an arbitrarily later RB9 birth need not satisfy (6) in that order; §4 remains applicable there without a screening estimate.

## 7. An arbitrary-depth window allowing additional births and failed preparations

Fix t=b+1 and a deterministic restart level d≥t. At b use the strict Q/R incoming seed class of §1. Through time d select unresolved ancestries with no successful reset or ineligible import whose only subsequent ordinary creations are canonical-small P-admitted Q/R births as in H9.37; failed preparations may retain old geometry during this window. A root may first enter during this interval. Include G throughout as an event on the original b-step law. Set

    q_* = min_{b≤k<d} p_k²,          D = d−t.

The set of eligible components is defined subject to the following geometric anchoring property, as in H9-E: tracing any occupied cell back through covers, bounded fills and failed preparations ends at a b-step seed or at one of the later admitted births. This property holds when the ancestry has the prescribed origins and no processing event creates a new geometric component unconnected to those origins. In particular a post-(1.76) failed attempt can retain geometry without requiring unchanged current-field stencils for the *later canonical-small gauge test*. The old current field itself must still be the actual one in that test. For d=t, the displayed minimum consists of k=b.

For a deterministic family of d-cells, H9-E finite propagation supplies a witness of one of these types within distance C s_d per cell. Fixed-fraction coloring gives disjoint enlarged physical neighborhoods. A particular neighborhood contains at most exp(C(1+D+log(1+max_{b≤k≤d} R_k))) candidate labeled cubes in all levels from b onward, by the fixed grid sizes, geometric ratio h_d/h_t and the sum over levels. Witness choices in disjoint neighborhoods give distinct cubes at any fixed layer. For each assignment, (6) costs exp(−c q_* N) for N retained anchors; if it uses no b-step flag, simply ignore G and use H9.37 chronologically. No conditioning on subsequent failure labels is used. Thus, provided

    C(1+D+log(1+max_{b≤k≤d} R_k)) ≤ c q_*/2,

the actual d-cell occupancy satisfies exp(−c' q_* |S|). If this also pays the H8 post-d window and R_d^4, all subsequent clean descendants satisfy (1)–(2) with t replaced by d and q_b by q_*. Equations (3)–(4) (and the terminal budget with K) then apply for a fixed choice of d per b. If many possible restarts are summed separately at each j, there are at most (j+1)^2 choices and the existing exponential-in-j summation reserve pays this polynomial after a fixed enlargement of C_s. No entropy of all actual history labels is incurred. This gives a mixed finite-window exception to the prohibition on later births in §1 and to the prefix's unchanged-stencil restriction. Unrestricted later canonical-large physical births and persistent failed preparations without a certified recent geometric origin remain outside it.

If later ordinary P births are included through a separate actual reset-witness implication, the witness draw must occur chronologically after B for (6) to pay both costs without loss. Such births can also conflict with the no-successful-reset requirement on a chosen unresolved ancestry. They are not assumed in this window estimate.

## 8. Paying later replacements by the component that caused them

The following refinement combines §7 with the descending geometric observation of  One. It covers some actually canonical-large or P-bad later births. The unresolved term is a concrete function of the earlier coarse output W.

Use the strict full regular entry at step b, t=b+1, with the positive preclassification RB9 marginal. Start the physical comparison at the next field W. All active geometry at this starting layer used by the construction below must have the Q/R-only geometric origins of §1. For simplicity one can enforce this on **all starting components**. It is a selector on the left, never extra conditioning in RB9. Include in the comparison every subsequent replacement of a current variable, including a b+1 processing of W if it is attempted. There must be no separate mechanism for creating a new physical current-coordinate replacement at a location unassociated with an active component. In the source LFR I, the integration is on Λ⊂Z by (1.73),(1.76), where each processed component is contained in a 100-cell box. In (1.79) the physical extension outside Λ uses the original retained V. The following (1.82),(1.88) failures do not enlarge the physical support of that earlier replacement. Global changes of gauge do not change a gauge-invariant plaquette observation, and canonical comparisons must be made covariantly in compatible endpoint frames. Exponentially nonlocal dependence of the minimum or density is allowed. No local bound on that dependence follows or is needed for locating an integrated physical link.

Let S be specified d-cells and let A denote the actual unresolved d-cells under the starting selector. Between t and d allow actual Q/R/P creations, processing, failures and entry into different components. For each x∈S construct a deterministic ball of radius C s_d, large enough for the following searches. At each later source cube in this ball and each available step, use the exact copied P comparison and the canonical Q comparison on the *shadow fields* obtained by the deterministic block iterates of W. Use the full enlarged finite determining patch for the copied data and for the underlying physical gauge-covariant contours, enlarged beyond the ball if necessary. Call x shadow-admitted if every candidate P comparison has a strictly good result and every candidate canonical Q comparison satisfies the smallness in H9.37; an undefined candidate chart is recorded as nonadmitted. Tests are evaluated on the fixed cube lists, regardless of the actual later geometric eligibility. For R an actual eligible cube uses the P/Q-protected mixed-to-local estimate (3.17)–(3.19), as in H9-C; one does not generate a shadow copy of its global mixed minimum. This definition only uses W and the fixed future block maps. At W flat every such local margin holds, hence it holds on a neighborhood at fixed finite cutoff and window. No uniform lower bound on the probability or radius of such a neighborhood is claimed.

**Deterministic redirection.** Trace an actual occupied d-cell backward through the source's covers, fixed layers, bounded fills and component retention until reaching an ordinary birth (cf. H9-E). Trace one nearby *cell* at a time, including through a large merged component. A failed preparation can only keep or modify an existing active component. If a reset has removed an older component, an actual present unresolved component must have a later geometric origin or another surviving predecessor. This trace stops at a b-step Q/R seed if it reaches the starting layer. If the encountered later birth has an unchanged complete W-stencil, P is impossible at a shadow-admitted root, and an actual Q/R bad flag has a large ordinary gauge innovation, by the physical canonical margin and H9.16–18. To use H9.37 here one only needs the actual later canonical small test before that ordinary gauge draw, which follows from the shadow test on an unchanged physical stencil. The birth label is recorded when its defining cutoff is inserted.

If the stencil intersects a prior current replacement, select one such location at level i. Its distance from the birth tested at level j is at most C s_j by H9 finite determining supports and (H8.3). The processing domain intersecting that location lies in a previously active i-component, regardless of acceptance of the later selectors. From its nearby pre-processing cell trace geometry back to an ordinary birth at level at most i. Since the step to j had used only strictly preceding current-field operations, i<j. Here the step testing W at j occurs before a reset/potential replacement at level j. The b+1 base is handled at its pre-processing geometry even if W subsequently changes within that layer. Repeat. Each descent has strictly smaller birth level. Jumps from a replacement to the nearby pre-processing cell cannot cross the diameter of an arbitrary merged component; the replacement was confined to the eligible 100-cell component, and the geometry trace is cell based. Consequently the entire displacement is bounded by C Σ_{i≤d}s_i≤C' s_d, with constants independent of d. Repeated component membership after a failed preparation adds a finite propagation displacement, not a new independent trial. Coloring the original d-cell anchors with the resulting enlarged radius gives disjoint terminal witness neighborhoods.

**Explicit conditional bound.** On the strict entry, write S_good(W) for the set of shadow-admitted cells in S. Provided the entropy condition in §7 holds, the argument yields

    P(S ⊂ A, starting Q/R-only selector | H,W)
         ≤ exp(−c q_* |S_good(W)|).                                    (7)

To prove it, fix a fiber (H,W), color the good subfamily, and enumerate deterministic terminal witness choices in their disjoint neighborhoods. The other cells cost nothing. Each witness is an actual b-step strict Q/R flag, or a specified later large ordinary gauge innovation. The latter is an actual normalized fresh draw with the H9 threshold. Apply H9.8 chronologically, conditional also on the history through the b-step labels; then apply RB9.20 to the specified b-step family on its original H,W fiber. Even if some address was never used, its specified occurrence is false. No older labels inside H are revealed in this final RB9 application. Counts and entropy absorption are those of §7. Only final witnesses are counted; charging every redirection or a tie-breaking history would give a different and generally unwarranted cost.

This is a bound on the conditional probability of the *intersection*, without dividing by the posterior of the starting selector. Averaging yields E[1_G exp(−c q_*|S_good(W)|)]. In particular all anchors on the admitted shadow have the §7 cost, although their actual intervening canonical fields can be large. For mixed shadows the remaining problem has become a joint bound on the explicit nonadmitted W-cells; no such cost is asserted. Post-window H8 clean continuation, ordinary-size and age bounds apply when all required base leaves are shadow-admitted or their complement is separately paid. Keeping only (7) supplies neither the general full-history nor terminal estimate.

## 9. Literal early time before any LFR-I processing is possible

For the specific H9 cutoff J=floor(κ log log β), κ may be decreased in H9.20. Choose also κ<r/log L. In the literal LFR-I procedure, every attempt needs a preceding N-step history, with h=k−N≥0 (pp.177–178). The printed p.179 requires N>N_0, where L^{N_0−1}=R_{k−N_0+1}. If k≤J were eligible, its radius index would be nonnegative and at most J; H9’s trajectory estimate gives R_{k−N_0+1}≥c(log β)^r. Thus N_0≥(r/log L)log log β−C>J for sufficiently weak coupling, contrary to k≥N>N_0. This establishes that (1.76) cannot occur anywhere up to this choice of J. It uses the physical construction starting at its prescribed Wilson cutoff and no alternative initial short-history reset. CMP119's ordinary Q/R averaging and normalized soft-gauge insertions still occur. All H9 ordinary causal stencils in that prefix are consequently unchanged up to gauge and are covered by H9-C. Preparation failures in this shorter initial window cannot occur under the literal LFR-I eligibility rule.

LFR I, printed p.198, after (1.94), imposes an *upper* log-power restriction on N and a separate lower condition. An inference of a mandatory log-power lower bound from that page would be incorrect; the preceding argument uses p.179 instead. [LFR I, original paper](https://doi.org/10.1007/BF01257412). The geometric descending proof in §8 does not rely on this initial-clock observation and also applies at later absolute depths.

## 10. A clean-tree criterion allowing foreign leaves after a noisy interval

Let t be a deterministic cut and A_t a selected set of genuine preexisting occupied cells, measurable through t, with the annealed all-family rate exp(−q|S|). Let the next interval [t,d] have length D, and follow it by n complete H8 clean macrosteps of length w_d. At the final cut j=d+n w_d, select actual components all of whose cells admit the following certificate. An H8 admissible clean witness tree ending at that cell has N≥c_0 2^n distinct base leaves at d, and at least θN of these leaves, with a fixed θ>0, are each within H s_d of some A_t-cell. The trees must have the standard H8 separation and cone bounds. The other leaves may have genuinely new causes; this certificate is stronger than mere clean evolution but weaker than anchoring every post-noise cell. A sufficient causal reason for an anchored leaf is an actual redirection reaching A_t as in  One §6. A purely geometric near-cell check also suffices.

There are constants κ_H,c,C independent of q,D,n,w_d such that, for every deterministic set S at j and sufficiently large q compared to (1+D)+w_d/θ, the joint occupancy of these selected cells is at most

    exp(−c κ_H θ 2^n q |S|).                                         (8)

Indeed select a fixed separated fraction of final roots and enumerate their H8 trees, at cost exp(C w_d N_total), where N_total is the total number of d-leaves. Their leaf locations are distinct by the H8 cone separation. Enumerate the anchored subfamily at cost ≤2^{N_total}. Among its leaves a fixed fraction κ_H have disjoint enlarged anchor neighborhoods in the d-grid (or color their bounded-degree graph). For those leaves enumerate one t-cell per neighborhood at cost exp(C(1+D) times their number). Apply the original joint occupancy assumption only once to these distinct t-cells. If θ q is large relative to w_d, and q is large relative to 1+D, each enumeration cost is absorbed, giving (8). This is an existence/union-bound certificate over deterministic endpoint sets. Selection must never condition the law on those trees or divide by their posterior mass. Taking fixed n sufficiently large obtains rate ≥2q. The same induction at deterministic cycles is valid by treating the previous selected set as A_t at the next step. A finite terminal partial cycle, physical age and summation over alternate schedules require their own accounting.

A genuinely causal extension covers a noisy interval in which every shadow-admitted endpoint can instead redirect either to a selected A_t-cell or to a later specified large gauge innovation. This follows by the redirection of §8 provided its comparison starts from the actual cut physical field, all potential pre-cut origins in the selected trees belong to A_t, and births on unchanged stencils have the deterministic local shadow margin. Unlike the strict RB9 entry, this general A_t rarity is only annealed. For fixed distinct base cells T and later specified innovations z, the applicable inequalities are

    P(T⊂A_t, all later z occur) ≤ exp(−q|T| − Σ_z cost(z)).

This uses the tower only for the later fresh draws; no estimate conditional on the cut physical field is inferred for T⊂A_t. Shadow selectors are dropped after the pointwise implication, or retained as part of an explicitly estimated base probability. If an arbitrary fraction of the leaves may originate at new innovations and there is no lower bound on the fraction tied to A_t, the effective noisy-interval rate from this argument is bounded by a constant times min(q, min_z cost(z)). Additional clean steps can amplify that new rate for their endpoints, but unrestricted repeated fresh entries prevent automatic growth proportional to an *already arbitrarily large* prior q. An independent new Bernoulli source of probability e^{-u} after every cut illustrates the obstruction at the noisy cuts. The anchored fraction in (8) removes this obstruction for its selected class. Its verification for all real noisy/clean cycles is not supplied by RB9 or H9.

## 11. An actual finite interface of Q/R labels

On one strict RB9 fiber fix a family B of n required Q/R bad cubes and a disjoint finite list J of m further candidate cubes. Disjoint here means distinct labels in the family convention of RB9.20; no spatial gap beyond its uniform coloring loss is required. Write L⊂J for the actual set of bad labels in this list, with ineligible tests counted false as in RB9. Set q=exp(−c p_b²) with c from the joint-family theorem, weakening c once if necessary for the chosen finite test types. For every nonnegative function h(H,W,T) of the resulting label subset,

    E[1_{all B bad} h(H,W,L) | H,W]
      ≤ q^n Σ_{T⊂J} q^{|T|} h(H,W,T).                                 (9)

For the atom L=T, keep the required positives in T and discard the negative tests. The resulting event requires n+|T| distinct bad cubes in the same actual fiber. Apply RB9.20 and sum the atoms. The right-hand side is q^n(1+q)^m times expectation against independent Bernoulli(q/(1+q)) flags used solely as a majorizing finite sum. This identity neither samples a new physical field nor conditions on a rare pattern.

For the all-good thin interface the stronger same-law assertion is

    P(all B bad | H,W,L=empty) ≤ q^n/(1−m q),    whenever m q<1.         (10)

The union bound and RB9.20 give the denominator; the numerator is at most q^n. Thus a fixed or polynomial-size clean collar of same-step flags has a genuine bounded-cost posterior distortion in this precise sense. Even after specifying any pattern T of positive mass, the joint bound q^{n+|T|} remains valid with its posterior weight. Estimating the conditional law on a prescribed nonempty T by the corresponding power additionally requires a lower bound on P(L=T|H,W) or a suitable ratio. A retained continuous exterior field is a different interface. A future continuation can be inserted into (9) if it is measurably bounded above by one of these h on the actual lift, with source-specific justification. The complete mixed continuation is not in general a function only of W and finitely many binary flags.

## 12. Exact conditioning on continuous coordinates in a thin collar

Here the interface includes the actual values of independent fluctuation coordinates. It is a conditioning of one strict regular parent. No unidentified old boundary action is added. Write p=p_k, R=R_k on the RB9.6 fiber, let T be a deterministic set of m independent bonds with 1≤m≤C_0 R^4 for a fixed C_0, and write a for their pinned vector, with |a|_1=Σ_{b∈T}|a_b|. The following constants can depend on C_0 and fixed source choices. For sufficiently small fixed ε>0, every a with |a|_1≤εp has positive conditional density in the canonical integral version, and

    P(all n specified Q/R births | H,W,A_T=a) ≤ exp(−c p² n),  n≥1.      (11)

This also covers a cube containing some pinned witnesses. The event is the actual source birth under the conditional law; its geometric eligibility is unchanged.

**Uniform inputs.** Let V be the complete localized potential in RB9.8. For two configurations in the box differing only on T the potential difference is bounded by C m v_k, by summing the suprema of terms whose dependence supports meet T in the RB9 localized norm. The positive quadratic precision M has uniformly summable rows in these independent coordinates. The stated exponential kernel bounds in RB9.6 imply this directly for M. Alternatively the exponential bounds for C=M^{-1}, together with the uniform upper operator norm for M used in RB9.10a, suffice. For D_z(x)=exp(α dist(x,z)), exponential row and column sums of C give ∥D_z C D_z^{-1}−C∥≤C'α uniformly by Schur's test, using the torus metric. For α small invert this perturbation. Taking z=y yields |M_{xy}|≤C exp(−α dist(x,y)). A smaller exponential weight makes the row sum uniformly finite by polynomial lattice volume growth. With H_k=O(p) the outer box, for all allowed remaining coordinates u,

    |log f_0(a,u)−log f_0(a',u)|
       ≤ C p(|a|_1+|a'|_1)+C m v_k.                                   (12)

Here f_0 is the positive exponential density on the box, before the inherited indicator factors. The estimate follows by expanding the quadratic difference about the midpoint of the two fields. No holomorphic dependence of cutoffs on a is required.

**Comparison integral.** Delete only inherited factors η_α whose determining sets S_α meet T, and retain the outer box and all other factors exactly. Call the resulting normalized comparison law μ⁻ and its partition function Z⁻. The derivation RB9.9–15 is uniform after this omission: in the finite defect expansion remove the terms involving omitted labels; all absolute majorants, tilt margins and convergence thresholds are precisely the old ones, and the remaining support still contains the inner product neighborhood. Consequently for every P the comparison satisfies E_{μ⁻}exp(u Σ_{b∈P}|A_b|²)≤exp(C|P|). This assertion uses the connected (normalized) expansion, not a volume-dependent estimate of Z⁻/Z. Outside the inherited cutoff support, the regular integrand is evaluated on its existing analytic box RB9.5–8. Every remaining inherited factor is independent of T.

Let U be the union of the S_α touching T with T removed. By RB9.7, |U|≤C m R^4. Fix a sufficiently large K_0 and put D_0={a': |a'|_1≤K_0 m}. Under μ⁻ the mean of |A_T|_1 is at most C m, so μ⁻(A_T∉D_0)≤1/4. By the comparison one-bond tail, the probability that any b∈U has |A_b|>r_in=c_h p is at most C m R^4 e^{-c p²}<1/4. Since m≤C_0 R^4=o(p), D_0 is contained in the strict inner coordinate box at the weak endpoint. Choose ε<c_h and smaller than the actual single-bond witness constant in RB9.19. Thus all deleted factors at (a,u) equal one when |u_b|≤r_in on U, and a belongs to the outer box.

**Lower bound for the pinned denominator.** For a'∈D_0 and the specified a let Δ=Cp(εp+K_0 m)+C m v_k. On the stated good U-coordinates, integrate the lower comparison from (12) over u and average over a'∈D_0 with normalized Lebesgue measure. The lower bound on the full conditional partition integral at a is

    Z(a) ≥ |D_0|^{-1} e^{-Δ} Z⁻ μ⁻(A_T∈D_0, max_{b∈U}|A_b|≤r_in)
         ≥ (2|D_0|)^{-1} e^{-Δ} Z⁻.

Here |D_0| denotes its finite-dimensional Lebesgue volume; it cancels below.

**Upper bound for the selected pinned numerator.** On the support of the full integrand the specified births force the raw RB9.19 witness choices after the fixed fraction coloring of RB9.20. No witness coordinate can belong to T since |a_b|≤εp. Let E_U denote the union over these choices of the threshold events on the full *unpinned complement* of T (not merely on the neighborhood U). It depends only on the unpinned variables. The comparison moment and the same witness entropy give μ⁻(E_U)≤exp(−c_1 p² n). Deleting the touching factors gives an upper bound, and now (12) permits averaging over a'∈D_0 for all other coordinates. This yields

    Z_B(a) ≤ |D_0|^{-1} e^{Δ} Z⁻ μ⁻(E_U).

Thus Z_B(a)/Z(a)≤2 exp(2Δ−c_1 p² n). Fix ε sufficiently small and then the endpoint sufficiently weak that 2Δ≤c_1 p²/2. This proves (11) uniformly, for the kernel version given by ordinary disintegration of its finite-dimensional density.

**Cost of pin values outside this region.** For a family of N disjoint nonempty deterministic collars T_i, |T_i|≤C_0 R^4, the original RB9.15 tilt on their union and Cauchy–Schwarz give

    P( |A_{T_i}|_1>εp for all i |H,W)
      ≤ exp[−c ε² p² N/R^4 + C N R^4]
      ≤ exp(−c' p² N/R^4).                                            (13)

Indeed each offending collar contributes at least ε²p²/|T_i| to the sum of squares. Fixed bounded overlap only modifies the constants. The exponent p²/R^4 dominates R^4 since p²/R^8→∞. It can therefore pay an ordinary-size cost per such collar at this step. If several simultaneously pinned collars are to be held fixed, (11) as proved applies when their union obeys its size and total pin budget. Equation (13) is a joint exceptional-set estimate; it does not assert arbitrary-many-collar conditional (11).

This is a pointwise continuous pinning result on a strict parent and an annealed estimate for its exceptional coordinates. An arbitrary old mixed exterior may change the underlying potential, may supply active constraints different from this parent's η_α, or may reveal data beyond A_T,H,W. In those cases the comparison above requires additional identification. It neither erases an old boundary-dependent mean nor proves that a real multiscale branch is screened by this collar.

## 13. Reverse domination for a real next P or Q test on a mixed output

The following result uses the current-coordinate formulas, without asserting a new density formula for an arbitrary mixed parent. Consider a full strict entry at b with no old active components and globally P-good current output; impose these entry conditions explicitly. Write t=b+1. At t all new active regions arise from the b-step Q/R tests. In LFR I pp.177–178 a processing attempt on a component needs the last N one-step operations to contain no new large-field characteristic function on that component. A component just formed from Q/R flags fails this eligibility condition. Thus there is no current (1.76) integration on such a component in that same sweep. The current physical field entering the t→t+1 ordinary transition is W, up to gauge. In particular its physical next average is MW. The b-step old integration variables may still reside in a genuine mixed large-field region, but are not arguments of the next literal P test or the explicit Q comparison on an eligible cube. CMP119 pp.264–266, preceding (3.3) through (3.5), and H9's descriptions of the local copied P test and canonical comparison are the relevant source formulas.

Fix a deterministic finite list of next P-test cubes. Alternatively fix a finite list of next Q-test cubes, restricting W so that every next P test in the fixed finite neighborhoods needed for those Q eligibilities is strictly good on the copied data of MW. Denote by C the event that **all** of the chosen next tests are actually eligible and bad. For Q, its canonical comparison is allowed to be large. A P-family and a Q-family can be combined if their requirements are compatible, but no such combination is needed below.

For P write h_0(W) for the indicator that the prescribed local copied tests on MW would be bad if eligible. For Q let h_0(W,r) be the analogous indicator of the bond comparison, using its literal local copied background and the ordinary normalized soft-gauge coordinates of the next step. Generate the needed finite tape r with the product H9 law; unused coordinates may be added independently. Its law does not depend on the earlier fields and flags. By H9.14–16 the gauged comparison on an eligible cube is exactly its physical canonical comparison multiplied by the appropriate endpoint gauge coordinates. Thus always 1_C≤h_0. In the Q application all assertions below are restricted to the indicated P-good W-set. No assertion about the actual R minimizer after the Q split is needed.

Let J be the list of b-step Q/R labels possibly creating old active geometry in the entire fixed eligibility neighborhood of any prescribed next cube, including its added layers. This is a deterministic finite list. Its size m is at most C times the number of prescribed next cubes: H8.3 bounds the ratio of neighboring grid sizes, the CMP119 coverings add a fixed number of layers, and a post-cover rectangular fill has width at most 100 old active cells. A large component can only contribute its already covered individual cells; a small filled component must contain an actual initial flag within this fixed radius. Consequently if all J flags are good there is no old mask in any of the required neighborhoods. Every prescribed P test is then eligible. For Q the explicit next P-good requirement gives also its P collar and the full gauge-fixing blocks. In each case 1_C=h_0 on the all-good J event. No further negative Q pattern has to be specified to define a Q test. This use of local eligibility is special to P/Q at the next transition and does not apply verbatim to R or a preparation selector.

Now let B require n fixed bad Q/R cubes on the earlier strict RB9 fiber. Write q=exp(−c p_b²). The two comparisons just proved give, conditionally on H,W,

    P(B,C | H,W) ≤ q^n E_r h_0(W,r),
    P(C | H,W) ≥ (1−m q) E_r h_0(W,r).

The first uses independence of the next ordinary gauge tape from the entire earlier b-step outcome after conditioning on H,W and RB9.20 for B. The second uses the union bound for the finite list J and the same independence. It follows that for m q<1,

    P(B,C | H,W) ≤ [q^n/(1−m q)] P(C | H,W).                           (14)

The inequalities remain true after multiplication by any nonnegative function of H,W and integration on the stated entry set. The earlier B cubes can intersect J; the comparison does not spend them twice. On some B outcomes an eligible later cube may coexist with an unprocessed old large-field region elsewhere, so the later test can belong to an actual mixed history. Equation (14) gives a true relative bound against its own same-law next-event probability, without requiring strict-carrier RB9 at that later step. It does not independently estimate P(C). If m is large one needs a stronger all-good lower bound; marginal upper estimates alone do not provide one for m q≥1. A continuous future boundary or an R test whose near-identity comparison only provides one-sided inequalities is not covered by (14).

The full mixed-density bridge is still missing. LFR II (1.103)–(1.104), pp.391–392, explicitly isolates the old operation's scalar T'_k(Y)1 as a multiplier of V_k|Y and *also* produces new localized boundary terms B'(X) for domains containing Y; CMP119 (2.23) includes B_k(U_k,A) and a minimum depending on the mixed determining set. Fixing V_k|Y indeed removes the scalar from the next denominator. On a full physical conditional it does not remove the residual boundary action, turn the mixed minimum into the strict minimum, prove inherited-cutoff margins, or screen an old inserted observable. The argument above bypasses this only for the next explicit P/Q test once W is fixed and the local mask checked.

### 13.1 All-good collar probability without a cardinality restriction

The elementary union bound in (10) and (14) can be replaced by a much stronger bound for the actual strict RB9 law. Let J contain m specified b-step Q/R tests. By RB9.19 there is a deterministic set U_J of at most C m R_b^4 independent coordinates, given H,W and the list, such that a bad flag in J requires |A_z|≥c_0 p_b somewhere in U_J. Fix α∈(0,c_0). Enumerate U_J. At each stage append the singleton cutoffs 1{|A_z|<α p_b} for the previously enumerated coordinates. Up to null threshold sets these are additional inherited indicators with the same properties needed in RB9.7: range one, at most one extra support per coordinate, and common inner margin min(c_h,α/2)p_b. The original potential, outer box and all original inherited cutoffs remain exactly the same. The fixed-parameter defect expansion RB9.9–15 applies with these extra indicators: singleton defects require a coordinate of size αp_b, their support cost is O(1), and their extra weighted activity is O(exp(−c_α p_b²)). No constant depends on the number or location of singleton indicators. The same real connected expansion with quadratic tilt therefore yields the uniform one-coordinate tail

    P(|A_z|≥αp_b | H,W, every earlier singleton veto succeeds)
       ≤ C_α exp(−c_α p_b²).

Each conditional normalization is the actual nested restricted partition function, positive because the common strict inner box persists. Multiplying the exact successive conditional probabilities for all |U_J| vetoes gives

    P(L=empty | H,W)
       ≥ P(|A_z|<αp_b for all z∈U_J | H,W)
       ≥ exp(−C_α m R_b^4 exp(−c_α p_b²)).                            (15)

The last inequality uses log(1−x)≥−2x at the weak endpoint. Thus there is no restriction m q<1 in the sharpened versions of (10) and (14). Replace (1−m q)^{-1} there by exp(C_α m R_b^4 exp(−c_α p_b²)). This works for the actual all-good finite collar at any cardinality; the distortion may accumulate with m but its log per candidate cube is exponentially small. It supplies no lower bound for a specified nonempty pattern. This strengthening uses robustness of the RB9 cutoff expansion under the verified extra singleton factors. The abstract RB9.20 family upper inequality in isolation would be insufficient.

### 13.2 A deterministic physical trajectory after a globally empty cut

Suppose the incoming b-step geometry is globally empty, on the actual strict RB9 carrier. Older regions already resummed into H may not be re-revealed at this cut. Any component of the current geometry born after b must contain a geometric birth from a step B∈{b+1,…,l} at its level l prior to the first new preparation. Indeed, before a first processing, covers and bounded rectangular fills preserve at least one geometric origin under growth and mergers. The old empty set cannot create a nonempty component without a new flag. If l−b≤N(l), that birth belongs to the last N(l) ordinary transitions of the component's history and prevents the LFR-I clean eligibility, pp.177–178. LFR-I (1.2) includes exactly the last N operations; a new ordinary bad characteristic function in that interval is incompatible with the stated form.

A uniform age for this exclusion follows from the literal N_0 formula in §9. If processing were possible at l, put i=l−N_0+1≥0. Then

    N(l)>N_0=1+log_L R_i ≥ 1+log_L(R_l/C_R)

by H8.3 (including the trivial equal-index case with C_R≥1). On the standing trajectory x_s−x_{s+1}≤c_+, x_s proportional to g_s^{-2}, for l−b=O(log(2+R_b)) we have x_l≥x_b/2 at the weak endpoint. The fixed rounding and R_i≍(log x_i)^r give R_l≥c_R R_b for this interval. Thus the deterministic horizon

    a_b = max(0,floor(log_L R_b − C_*))

with a sufficiently large fixed C_* satisfies l−b<N(l) at every prospective eligible l≤b+a_b (truncated at K). By considering the first alleged preparation, none occurs throughout. This reasoning does not require distant future carriers to be regular and does not constrain the number of new ordinary P/Q/R flags. The globally empty *actual unresolved geometry* at b is essential. Resumming some old labels while retaining a nonempty old region would not restart its clock.

For a bounded nonnegative function f of the physical fields at levels b+1,…,d with d≤min(K,b+a_b), assume f is gauge invariant at each level (or invariant under the actual interscale changes of frame). These fields equal W,MW,…,M^{d−b−1}W up to gauge on every branch of the stated entry, by fixed-current pass-through and the absence of current-coordinate replacements. The exact actual equality, before taking probabilities, gives

    E[1_B f(actual trajectory) | H,W] ≤ q^n f(W,MW,…,M^{d−b−1}W)
                                       = q^n E[f(actual trajectory) | H,W].   (16)

Here B is the b-step bad family above. This is multi-step reverse domination on the entire physical-field observable algebra with the indicated gauge invariance and finite horizon. Any signed bounded test obeys the corresponding absolute bound by applying (16) to |f|. The Q/R eligibility flags, auxiliary gauge draws and old boundary responses are additional variables outside this algebra. Equation (16) alone does not multiply probabilities of arbitrary multiscale history labels; (14) treats the stated next P/Q labels by adding their distinct geometric and gauge checks.

### 13.3 A genuinely later label inside the return horizon

Let a deterministic finite family C of P tests (or of Q tests with their final P collar good on shadow) now be at a single depth d>b+1 within the no-processing horizon of (16). As usual C denotes their actual joint eligible-and-bad event. Build their complete backward *mask* neighborhoods recursively using the finite cover layers and 100-cell rectangular fills of H8, as well as the fixed margins of the actual tests. The total physical backward radius per final cube is ≤C Σ_{l≤d}s_l≤C' s_d. Include any lower-level flags capable of contributing to an old mask in the final eligibility tests. All candidate lists can be deterministic supersets (fixed H,W). From the generation immediately after the initial b-step through the last generation preceding the terminal tests, assume the deterministic physical iterates of W give good local copied P tests and the H9.37 canonical-small margin on all corresponding Q comparison cubes in these neighborhoods. Impose also the final P collar condition when targeting Q. This restriction is only on H,W; the final desired P or Q comparison need not be small. The empty set of intervening steps recovers §13.

At the starting layer require the old b-step Q/R candidate flags in the backward mask neighborhood to be good. Equation (15) charges this with a factor at least exp(−C m_b R_b^4 e^{-c p_b²}), where m_b counts the candidates. At each intervening level k require the finitely many potential ordinary-gauge tape coordinates in the enlarged neighborhoods to have |r−1|<c_*δ_k, decreasing c_* to fit H9.16–18. It can be realized with independent coordinates at every prospective location, including unused ones. Thus the conditional probability of success for all M_k specified coordinates is at least exp(−C M_k e^{-c p_k²}) at that level, even after imposing the earlier events.

On joint success there are no intervening P flags in those neighborhoods by the shadow condition. There are no eligible Q or R births there by H9.14–18, which use the physical canonical margin and the small gauge coordinates and, for actual R, its eligible P/Q protection. Inductively the earlier masks therefore cannot reach any specified final cube; each terminal test is eligible.     P(B,C | H,W) ≤ q^n exp[C m_b R_b^4 e^{-c p_b²}
                             + C Σ_{intervening k} M_k e^{-c p_k²}]
                    P(C | H,W).                                         (17)

All terms refer to actual probabilities on their stated initial/shadow event. At fixed source constants the backward candidate/tape count per final cube is polynomial in R_b for d−b≤log_L R_b−C_*: a cone has diameter O(h_d R_d), h_d/h_b≤R_b, and R_d≤C R_b. ## 14. Dependence of an actual old mean on other live interiors at a reset

Fix the retained real exterior e and the geometry in the second integration of LFR II (1.103), with all local one-body normalizations positive. On this exact bracket the remaining live variables ξ_i have conditional law

    μ_e(dξ) = Z_e^{-1} (⊗_i ν_{i,e}(dξ_i)) exp[Σ_{P: |I(P)|≥2} W_P(e,ξ_{I(P)})].

The first connected logarithm (1.98) supplies the functions W_P *inside* the next integral. Their incidence with the integrated old operations is actual: p.391 says the second Mayer expansion need apply the normalized operations only in the regions contained in the activity support. Single-incident terms have been absorbed into the normalized ν_{i,e}; their masses remain in the exterior normalization. This is the real product representation used in T1 §7.125 immediately before Lemma 7.194. A function localized in physical fine background links need not be local in exterior determining variables. The incidence used here is with the **integrated live variables** at fixed e.

For two disjoint live Y_i,Y_j at native M-cell distance D, freeze also every other live coordinate. A potential term reaching both must contain an exterior cell next to each, since the supports contain or exclude complete components and distinct components have no common wall. The first-stage relative tree of (1.67) is a single connected tree visiting those two exterior cells, so its length is at least D−C. Root the tree near one chosen component and apply the rooted domain count of  One §10 or T1 Lemma 7.194, retaining a smaller exponential in the distance. On the actual absolute convergence margin this gives

    δ_ij = Σ_{P: i,j∈I(P)} ||W_P||∞
         ≤ C min(v_i,v_j) exp(−γD),

where v_i counts the native cells of Y_i. Exterior boundary counts may replace v_i with the boundary-root proof. Terms depending only on i among the two unfixed variables, or only on j, go into their one-body reference probabilities, even when they previously also involved the fixed variables. The remaining conditional law differs from their product by a likelihood ratio between exp(−2δ_ij) and exp(2δ_ij). For bounded real old marks F_i(e,ξ_i),G_j(e,ξ_j), it follows that

    |Cov(F_i,G_j | e, all ξ outside {i,j})|
       ≤ C ||F_i||∞ ||G_j||∞ min(1,δ_ij).                              (18)

For δ≤1 this follows by comparing the two first means and the product expectation with those under the product reference; for δ>1 use the uniform bounded covariance estimate. This concerns old, possibly non-Gaussian, interiors. Integrating a third unfixed core can reintroduce mediated dependence.

There is a one-root version which does not pay for the number of other live components. Suppose the distance of Y_i to **every** other live Y is at least D_*. Root near Y_i and sum all multi-incident P containing i once, retaining exp(−γD_*) from its tree. Then δ_i≤C v_i exp(−γ D_*). Put m_i^0(e)=∫F_i(e,ξ_i)ν_{i,e}(dξ_i). For every realization of the other cores of positive conditional mass,

    | E[F_i | e, ξ_{≠i}] − m_i^0(e) | ≤ C ||F_i||∞ min(1,δ_i).            (19)

This is a deterministic oscillation bound on the cross-core part of the old conditional mean. It also bounds its discrepancy after integrating any subset or all of the other cores. The one-body reference retains the actual old kernel and its e-dependent normalization; no derivative or lower bound for that mass is taken. If the source function itself is a verified function of ξ_i and fixed e only, (19) gives a genuine decomposition of the actual posterior numerator. A physically nonlocal old source with unproved readout support does not meet the premise.

In particular select an exterior/geometry event A known at this cut on which v_i≤v and D_*≥D. For any later sigma algebra in the same physical continuation, conditional Jensen bounds the L² norm of the subsequently propagated cross-core discrepancy by C ||F_i||∞ min(1,Cv e^{-γD}) P(A)^{1/2}. Propagation here means that of the function on the original cut; it does not change the terminal physical kernel. The L¹ norm is bounded by the same expression with P(A) in place of its square root. With any bounded unrestricted second mark B_G, the terminal change in covariance due to this discrepancy is ≤2 B_G times the latter L¹ bound. For bare old amplitudes of order a^{-4}T(a)^s, a separation satisfying γD ≥ 8K log L+2s log T(a)+log(1+v)+u pays both bare factors at the terminal scale up to fixed constants. The selected class allows arbitrary future resets and failures. Successive applications at deterministic cuts permit summation of these residuals by linearity and contraction **only when the current mark at each usage has the indicated verified live-core readout**.

What remains in (19) is exactly m_i^0(e). Holding the other interiors apart does not estimate its variation with the exterior e, nor the effect of conditioning on a gauge-invariant observable that was already determined by e. Likewise (18) does not bound Cov_e(E[F_i|e],E[G_j|e]). These are genuine missing terms for the full terminal mean. Neither Wilson reflection positivity nor RP7's conditional blocking identities remove them; the R normalization need not factor across an OS reflection plane.

### 14.1 A one-root rarity factor and the first wave of eligible live cores

In (18) replace F_i by 1_A F_i, where A is measurable in (e,ξ_i) or in e alone. Freezing other live variables as in (18), write p_e for its probability on that two-body fiber (including the frozen variables among the parameters). For δ_ij≤1, the joint law relative to the product of its **actual two marginals** has density between exp(−6δ_ij) and exp(6δ_ij), by the one-body comparisons used in (18). Since the true product of marginals has the same P(A|e), the conditional covariance is at most C δ_ij ||F_i||∞||G_j||∞ p_e. For δ_ij>1 the direct L¹–L∞ bound gives 2||F_i||∞||G_j||∞ p_e. There is a concrete source interval on which the current *outer geometry alone* gives a rare-root factor. At the literal Wilson initialization fix the H9 cut J from §9, with whole-set occupied-cell rate q_J=cΓ_*. Observe a LFR-II second live integration at level k with

    h=k−N_hist ≤ J ≤ k,           N_hist ≤ C R_k.

The second bound is the native window bound used in O2 §12.3; retain it explicitly if varying the schedule. This implication concerns the original pre-split geometry. It is independent of the outcome of the last selector that distinguishes Y_i.

For fixed roots x,y the event E that at k they lie in distinct such Y_i,Y_j is measurable in the outer geometry of (1.103).

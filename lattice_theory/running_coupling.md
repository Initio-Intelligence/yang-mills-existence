# Running coupling and the finite-depth Gaussian endpoint

**Note.** [Native base and trajectory admission](native_trajectory.md), §§12–14, supplies the fixed-depth endpoint identification and a uniform incremental value modulus for its explicitly matched fixed-cutoff/readout/projector family. Statements below leaving EP open concern the general case; that result settles EP only for the same family. It supplies no coupling/source derivative. It also bounds the coarse Schur contribution. The signed fine-determinant plus gauge/coordinate comparison, actual admitted trajectory and universal Gaussian slope remain open; N2-F is not proved.

## 1. Target and interfaces assumed

The question is whether the inverse squared coupling of the prescribed block transformation has the same leading decrement at **each** step and a uniformly differentiable error. No upstream flow theorem is assumed. N0 is a source concordance. CMP 109, p. 259, Theorem 2 announces a small-coupling trajectory for SU(2), and its proof is deferred. LFR II, p. 355, makes its stability conclusion conditional on control of the trajectory. The present investigation concerns that gap in the literature and a stronger quantitative interface. It proves a shooting lemma and a reduction of the derivative estimate to identifiable properties of the scalar polarization. The absolute projected determinant at a single specified rank remains unevaluated. Transfer from the Gaussian endpoint to the physical native recursion at step two is conditional on EP in Appendix I.1. Appendix H proves a within-scheme obstruction for the SU(N) family using the color decomposition of the compact Wilson plaquette. Appendices D–G specialize its Bloch symbol and rigorously separate the infinite-volume one-loop coefficients of the two printed block maps at L=13.

Statements marked (L) describe primary results within their printed hypotheses, (P) deductions here, (C) implications with explicit hypotheses, and (O) outstanding claims. We use no source insertions. All estimates are in dimension four. [CMP 109](https://doi.org/10.1007/BF01215223) · [CMP 116](https://doi.org/10.1007/BF01239022)

## 2. Definitions and conventions

Use SU(N) in the fundamental representation, with Hermitian generators satisfying Tr(TᵃTᵇ)=δᵃᵇ/2. Let S be the sum over μ<ν of N−Re Tr U_p. In four dimensions the native action is A=S/N, since the native trace is normalized and one elementary square is counted per orientation plane (CMP 109 p. 252 (0.2), CMP 119 p. 253, and CMP 95 p. 18 (1.2)–(1.3)). Introduce H=(gᴮ)⁻², X=(g_program)⁻² and Y=(g_standard)⁻². Equating the exponents gives

**(P)** H A = 2N X S = 2Y S, hence H=2N²X=2NY and Y=NX. Equivalently, one may recover the factor 2 in 2Y by expanding S for U_p=exp(i a²FᵃTᵃ) and matching the continuum action (Y/4)∫Σ_{μ,ν,a}(Fᵃ_{μν})². In particular the coefficient of S in the program action is 2NX and the coefficient of A is H. A threshold for the native coupling must be converted using g_program=√(2N²)gᴮ. The notation β_j below means Bałaban’s scalar step increment, distinct from the bare Wilson exponent β_a.

**(L)** Equations (0.18) and (0.20), pp. 255–256, define H_{k+1}=H_k−β_{k+1}(gᴮ_k). The scalar extraction (1.20)–(1.22), p. 264, takes the external-field Hessian of the generated term evaluated at the constrained minimizer for exp(iB). The invariant off-diagonal polarization component gives a spatial second moment on the **infinite** lattice after passing to that limit. Thus H_k is the parameter in the prescribed action decomposition involving the fine minimizer, not an arbitrary coefficient obtained by fitting a coarse Wilson action. Vacuum constants are fixed separately at external identity. Finite torus polarization kernels and log-normalizers can depend on the box even when the prescribed scalar does not. For physical sides T, a=L⁻ᴷℓ₀ and ℓ_k=Lᵏa, the fine spacing in step-k units is L⁻ᵏ. We use the periodic lattices compatible with the construction and its sufficiently large odd L, as specified in N0 §2 and Appendix C.2. Extension to all preamble geometries needs separate justification.

**(P)** Set λ=2N². The step function for the program variable is D_k(X)=β_{k+1}((λX)⁻¹ᐟ²)/λ. The equation for X has decrement D_k. Even just k=0 in N2-F requires β₁(0)=λ b₀ log L, provided continuity at zero. The same equation applies to every fixed k. It is a finite-step matching test.

The same decrement in H is 11N²/(12π²), and in the specified X it is 11/(24π²). These are positive; coarse graining decreases inverse squared coupling in the perturbative comparison. An identification of the third number with *every* D_k(∞)/log L requires an additional evaluation. The proof and the finite-scale qualification appear in §4.

**Proposition 3.3 (P, shooting for specified step maps).** Fix d>0 and real functions r_k of one real variable on [γ₀⁻²,∞), genuinely specifying all the step maps, with |r_k(x)|≤C/x and |r′_k(x)|≤C/x² there. **Proposition 3.4 (P, a criterion for the C¹ estimate).** If the scalar functions β_j of the physical homogeneous recursion extend to [0,δ] as functions of their stated single argument, have β′_j(0)=0 and sup_j sup_[0,δ]|β″_j|≤M, then in X the decrement equals β_{k+1}(0)/λ plus a remainder bounded by M/(2λ²X), whose derivative has absolute value at most M/(2λ²X²). **Proposition 3.5 (P, native first-step scheme difference, computer-assisted).** At L=13 use the same fixed physical small-field cutoff and the same axial gauge for both transforms. **Proposition 3.6 (P, fixed (0.12) scheme and the SU(N) family).** Let C_j(N) be the cumulative projected one-loop log density in H.1. ## 4. Proofs

### 4.1 The standard logarithm and the matching condition

Lüscher–Weisz compute the background two-point function in lattice perturbation theory and match α_MS(s/a)=α_std+d₁(s)α_std²+O(α_std³), α_std=g_standard²/(4π). In (7.1), the coefficient of log s in d₁ is −11N/(6π); all finite terms independent of s cancel in a difference between s and s/L. Inverting gives Y_MS(s/a)=Y−d₁(s)/(4π)+O(1/Y). Coarsening by L therefore subtracts (11N/(24π²)) log L from this inverse coupling at one loop. Applying §2 gives Proposition 3.2. This computation pertains to the standard background momentum matching in the infinite-volume weak-coupling expansion. [Lüscher–Weisz, matching calculation, (1.1), (7.1)](https://arxiv.org/pdf/hep-lat/9505011) · [Background-field action and conventions, §2](https://arxiv.org/pdf/hep-lat/9504006)

**(P) Exact transient test.** Suppose the leading conversion to a reference coupling at resolution k has a finite matching term c_k, i.e. X_k=Y_ref,k/N+c_k+o(1) in the weak-coupling limit at fixed k. Then X_k−X_{k+1}=d+c_k−c_{k+1}+o(1), where d=11 log L/(24π²). Consequently a uniform bound C/X around d implies c_{k+1}=c_k for every k. Conversely the constant difference survives at arbitrarily weak coupling if nonzero. Example: c₀=0, c_j=c≠0 for j≥1. The total leading decrement differs from Kd by a bounded amount for all K, yet the first decrement differs from d. This is an example about the inference from a universal coefficient, not an evaluation of the native c₁. CMP 109 subtracts the vacuum value, which removes only the degree-zero term, and takes a polarization moment, which fixes the allocation of the degree-two term. Neither operation is an identity equating successive finite matching terms.

### 4.2 What must actually be integrated

Use the classical minimizer U_{k+1}(exp(iB)), with B an external field on coarse links. For fixed B, expand the action on the blocked fiber. The constrained first variation vanishes, including the correction from solving the nonlinear constraint. Rescale fluctuation coordinates by g=gᴮ_k. A term of degree n in that fluctuation in the bare action then carries g^{n−2}. Thus at order g⁰ the entire quadratic Hessian contributes. The cubic and quartic fluctuation vertices carry powers g and g² respectively. Two external-field derivatives can act on the Hessian without changing this power count. In particular vertices with two quantum legs and one or two external legs are already part of the one-loop Hessian. Terms surviving at zero from the constrained coordinate Jacobian must be retained. Normalizing at external B=0 removes all field-independent factors. The inherited action evaluated at the minimizer is accounted for in (1.3), while its *variation* in the rescaled fluctuation enters (2.13).

More explicitly, the precision on independent real coordinates is D_k(B)=C(B)ᵀΔ⁽ᵏ⁾(B)C(B). Here C solves the linearized block constraint; Δ⁽ᵏ⁾ includes the nonlinear constraint's second variation times the minimizer current and the actual gauge-fixing quadratic term. CMP 109 (1.5), (2.7), (2.11) expressly uses exponential gauge fixing in this formula. Replacing it by just the free CMP 99 propagator, by the unconstrained covariant vector Laplacian, or by a free ghost quotient requires a matching proof. Write J_k(B) for any leading coordinate density remaining outside det D, including delta elimination; its identity value cancels. For real external link jets u,v the normalized leading log Gaussian has mixed Hessian

**(P, finite-dimensional identity)**

    ∂uv log Z_G = ∂uv log J_k
                  − (1/2) Tr[D⁻¹ D_uv − D⁻¹ D_v D⁻¹ D_u],

evaluated at B=0. This follows by differentiating log det D, using ∂D⁻¹=−D⁻¹(∂D)D⁻¹. The two terms include both background derivatives of C, the constrained Hessian and the minimizing field. The evaluation of β_j(0) calls for the *combined* E in (1.6), with the explicit Gaussian of (1.3) included once. Any subtraction carried by the inductive prescription must be applied before the moment. The zero-coupling vanishing in the integrand of (2.13) removes fluctuation-interaction powers at leading order in the fixed-chart, untruncated Gaussian limit; it does not remove the explicit background determinant. The numerical work remaining is to construct D_u,D_v,D_uv,J for the chosen group average and gauge, pass the polarization to infinite volume, and take the (1.22) moment. Up to this point no scalar integral giving its value has been established. The residual local terms consist of the identity part, the projected Wilson part, and the difference from that projection, which can contain higher curvature or derivative terms. No coefficient of the latter is supplied to N6.

A spatially constant commuting link field in a trivial periodic bundle has zero local curvature. Uniform nonzero Abelian flux on the torus is quantized; its amplitude has no continuum of choices near zero at fixed side. One alternative to flux matching is to use two periodic infinitesimal external link modes, reconstruct the infinite-lattice off-diagonal Fourier kernel and differentiate at p=0. A numerical extrapolation needs cutoff, momentum-discretization and finite-size errors. The T1 charged finite-flux calculation (§7, Lemma 7.189 and the following prefix comparison) measures a metric derivative across a window, proportional to k up to bounded endpoint terms. Its geometry, observable and endpoint precision do not determine the fixed-step second moment above.

### 4.3 Derivative estimates, parity and the cutoff

**Proof of Proposition 3.4.** For 0≤g≤δ, Taylor integration gives |β_j(g)−β_j(0)|≤Mg²/2 and |β′_j(g)|≤Mg. Substitute g=(λX)⁻¹ᐟ² and |dg/dX|=g/(2X). Division by λ proves both bounds, for X≥1/(λδ²). The sign is reversed for the error in the update rather than decrement; its bounds remain valid.

**(P, parity at fixed finite step and frozen inherited data).** In the real physical integral with cutoff (2.9) fixed before the dilation, all explicit inverse couplings in the bare quadratic prefactors and gauge penalties occur as g⁻². On using signed dilations of the Lie-algebra variables the measure contributes |g| to the power of the dimension, canceled by the same normalization at the identity. The simultaneous change (g,b)↦(−g,−b) fixes the original unscaled field gCb, every nonlinear constraint correction evaluated there, the group measure in that coordinate and the physical cutoff. This yields evenness of the normalized integral on the punctured signed interval when inherited coefficients are kept fixed or themselves extended evenly. At a nondegenerate regular constrained saddle with a neighborhood inside the cutoff, the fixed finite-dimensional Laplace coefficient of order g is zero: every term at that order has odd degree in the Gaussian fluctuation (linear Taylor term from the density/inherited action or cubic term from the action), and the centered Gaussian is symmetric. Discarding the fixed-chart Gaussian tail for this coefficient is justified in a fixed dimension by a positive local quadratic lower bound and by exponential suppression on the compact remainder of the localized chart with a unique minimum. These hypotheses refer to the localized saddle; the argument estimates neither complete-field probabilities nor constants uniform in the dimension. Field derivatives are permitted on smaller regular charts with uniform nondegeneracy at the fixed volume. An even punctured extension alone allows |g| and therefore does not prove β′_j(0)=0.

**(C, route to a uniform bound).** CMP 109 p. 264, after (1.22), describes smoothness on the closed coupling interval with uniformly bounded derivatives. Theorem 3 there constructs the small-field inductive family while the couplings are admitted. CMP 116 p. 22 identifies the previous theorem as completed. For the interpretation that this supplies a uniform second-derivative bound for the physical one-variable family, it suffices to identify its first derivative at zero by the preceding Gaussian calculation **after** projection and the infinite-volume limit. One sufficient quantitative condition is a summable bound, uniform near zero and across k, on the first two coupling derivatives of the localized polarization kernels, including inherited terms evaluated on the same scalar family, weighted by 1+|z|². Under this condition dominated convergence allows the differentiated limit, and termwise Laplace parity gives β′_j(0)=0, provided the inherited linear-in-g jets vanish recursively. If the derivative holds only with older coefficients frozen, differentiating the physical family also gives their derivatives via the chain rule. Their increments are those of the earlier recursion. Holding the past fixed and varying g is a different operation. The displayed bound (1.18) on the fields does not exhibit these differentiated kernels. This paper does not verify the uniform physical interpretation plus the parity passage. Real scalar smoothness alone, without the zero first derivative, permits an error proportional to X⁻¹ᐟ².

A useful sufficient alternative is a uniform bound |R_k(z)|≤A/X on a holomorphic scalar disk |z−X|≤cX for the subtracted increment; Cauchy's formula then gives |R′_k(X)|≤A/(cX²). The native choice of the real indicator in (2.9) does not itself supply such a complex disk. For derivative estimates with a moving cutoff one must keep the differentiated boundary contribution or return to fixed physical coordinates. In particular a positive-axis smallness bound cannot be differentiated as an inequality. For example e^{−(log X)²}sin(e^X) is smaller than every inverse power yet has large derivatives along suitable positive X. If p₀=A₀(log H)^p with p>1, powers of H times exp(−p₀) are O(H⁻ᵐ) for any fixed m by elementary comparison of logarithms. This only bounds magnitudes. More fundamentally CMP 119 uses the couplings defined by the *small-field* recursion as parameters of the complete large-field representation. Its R operations control residual densities. Adding a further R contribution to β_j defined by (1.22) would require a new definition and a proof of equivalence. The term of order g² comes from two cubic insertions, quartic insertions, the measure, inherited vertices and constraint/gauge contributions; decay of the undifferentiated activities in CMP 116 alone does not quantify their differentiated sum.

### 4.4 Backward shooting and the interscale comparisons

**Proof of Proposition 3.3.** Choose γ=min(γ₀,1,√(d/(2C)),(2C)⁻¹ᐟ⁴) for C>0, omitting the last two restrictions for C=0. Write A=γ⁻². Then d/2≤x−f_k(x)≤3d/2 for f_k(x)=x−d+r_k(x) and f′_k≥1/2. Put x_K=g_*⁻²≥4A and successively set x_k=f_k⁻¹(x_{k+1}). This constructs the unique admitted trajectory with that endpoint, gives x_k≥x_K+(K−k)d/2, and proves continuity in the endpoint by finite composition. Summing the recurrence yields

    |x₀−x_K−Kd| ≤ C Σ_{n=1}^K (x_K+nd/2)⁻¹
                  ≤ (2C/d) log(1+Kd/(2x_K)).

Part (iv) follows for K≥1, with the endpoint included in its constant. Changing units of length or the torus introduces no dependence in this scalar argument. **(P, weaker conditional implementation).** If d is replaced by step constants d_k in [d₋,d₊] with d₋>0 and |Σ_{j<K}(d_j−d)| bounded independently of K, the same inverse argument with d₋ gives admission, continuity and x₀=Kd+O(log(K+1)). ## 8. Summary

Bałaban extracts the running coupling from a second spatial moment of the infinite-lattice vacuum polarization. The complete finite-volume construction then uses that sequence. Its Gaussian term depends on the constrained minimizing field, the nonlinear block condition and the axial gauge. Normalizing at the identity removes the vacuum constant.

The program inverse coupling equals the standard Wilson inverse coupling divided by N. Consequently the reference one-loop decrement in program units is 11 log L/(24π²). Two block maps expressly permitted by Bałaban yield different projected determinants at L=13. The infinite-volume reduced difference integral lies between −0.017584 and −0.003378. Its sign was certified by interval integration over the full Brillouin zone.

There is also an obstruction within a single fixed block map. In its cumulative one-loop curvature coefficient the fourth-order fundamental Wilson plaquette contributes a term proportional to 1/N. Its size is fixed by the rank of the fine curl on the constrained Gaussian fiber. All remaining contributions are proportional to N. The first two increments therefore cannot agree at both SU(2) and SU(3); at most one rank among all N can be exceptional. The argument does not identify that rank or evaluate its absolute coefficient. The printed-recursion inference assumes the matched-history endpoint condition stated in Appendix I; the Gaussian conclusion is unconditional.

The rank-dependent transient decays geometrically and is compatible with a bounded cumulative error. Backward shooting still works with that weaker hypothesis even when individual step decrements have either sign. The unknown adjoint contribution must satisfy a uniform aggregate estimate, and the finite-coupling remainder still requires control for the physical scalar family. The paper proves the shooting implications and color obstruction for the chosen readout. It does not establish an admitted native trajectory for a designated rank, a source-dependent extension, or an unconditional constant-coupling stability theorem along the proposed terminal trajectory.

## Appendix A. Numerical normalization check for the reference determinant

For a constant Abelian magnetic field F₁₂=BH, normalize the color generator by Tr H²=1/2. A component of charge q has scalar heat trace per unit four-volume (4πs)⁻² z/sinh z with z=sqB. Indeed its Landau levels (2n+1)|qB| have degeneracy |qB|/(2π) in the first plane and the free heat trace in the other plane is (4πs)⁻¹. In background Feynman gauge the vector trace multiplier is 2+2cosh(2z), from the two spectator components and the two opposite spin shifts ±2qB. The vector determinant enters with weight 1/2 and the complex ghost determinant with weight −1. Thus the combined heat trace multiplier is (z/sinh z)cosh(2z); its quadratic coefficient is 11/6. ## Appendix B. A finite-volume matrix formula retaining the nonlinear block constraint

**(P, regular constrained saddle).** Here is a way to form the one-loop trace of §4.2 without differentiating a chosen basis C or estimating an unspecified delta-function Jacobian. Work in a fixed smooth chart for the incoming group links, with all redundant gauge directions fixed or penalized as in the chosen native integral. Denote these real coordinates by y and the external coarse link parameters by t. Let F(y,t)=0 express the group block condition in logarithmic coordinates centered at the identity of the target group, e.g. F=log(M(y)V(t)⁻¹) in a fixed Lie-algebra basis. Write P(y,t) for the part of the physical action multiplied by g⁻², including the exponential gauge penalty where applicable. Write h(y,t) for the physical smooth coordinate density at leading order, with Haar and all other smooth measure factors included. Scalar constants independent of t cancel at identity. The following calculation applies whenever the constraint derivative J=F_y has full row rank and the Hessian on the constrained tangent space at the stationary point is positive definite.

The extremum solves F=0 and P_y+Jᵀλ=0. Define H=(P+λ·F)_{yy} at that solution. If there are m real constraints introduce

    K = [ H   Jᵀ ]
        [ J    0 ].

In a Euclidean orthonormal tangent/normal basis T,N, JN is square invertible, and |det(JN)|²=det(JJᵀ). Expansion of det K along the constraint rows and then the constraint columns gives det K=(−1)^m det(JJᵀ)det(TᵀHT). At the saddle the delta-function volume on the fiber contributes 1/√det(JJᵀ). The Gaussian integral along that fiber contributes (det TᵀHT)⁻¹ᐟ². It follows that, up to an external-field-independent constant, the one-loop log prefactor of the integral is

    ell(t) = log h(y(t),t) − (1/2) log |det K(t)|.

When a positive inherited amplitude occurs at the saddle, its logarithm may be included in log h and must then be subtracted according to the previous-action convention. The expression applies directly to the Gaussian piece with inherited action accounted separately. The Haar delta at the identity in group logarithmic coordinates differs from the Euclidean delta by a fixed density at zero; the use of M(y)V(t)⁻¹ captures the t dependence before taking the derivative. If a parameterization uses a moving gauge slice instead, its own coordinate factor must enter h.

For two source directions i,j this gives

    ell_ij = (log h)_ij − (1/2) Tr(K⁻¹ K_ij − K⁻¹ K_j K⁻¹ K_i).

All derivatives are *total* along the constrained solution. For a concrete evaluation define w=(y,λ) and G(w,t)=(P_y+F_yᵀλ,F). Since G_w=K, first and mixed second derivatives of the required background are obtained by solving with the same base matrix:

    K w_i  = −G_i,
    K w_ij = −G_ij − G_iw w_j − G_jw w_i − G_ww[w_i,w_j].

Here derivatives on the right are partials with w fixed except for the displayed w factors. These formulas follow by differentiating G(w(t),t)=0 twice and require only derivatives of the specified native action and average on a fixed regular chart. In particular omitting the λ·F_{yy} part of H changes generally both K_i and K_ij even when λ=0 at the identity. This construction provides a finite-matrix test for the first-step determinant: evaluate mixed jets for two external bond directions, assemble the translation-covariant kernel and apply the second moment only after passing to the infinite lattice. At later steps P itself uses the previously constrained minimum and hence requires its derivatives as well. No finite-volume identity shown here cancels the differences between the resulting matrices at successive resolutions.

## Appendix C. A finite-volume route to the first derivative

The passage of parity to the scalar limit in §4.3 needs less than uniform higher coupling derivatives of *all* local terms. For fixed k, let B_R(g) be the finite-volume polarization moment with an origin-centered representative of each separation in a periodic box of coarse side R, or a local truncated version if periodic images are controlled separately. Assume on a compatible geometric sequence R=R₀Lⁿ, for all sufficiently small g≥0, the following two estimates:
    
    |B_R(g)−β_{k+1}(g)| ≤ A_k exp(−c_k R),
    |B_R(g)−B_R(0)| ≤ A_k R^p g²   when g R^q ≤ ε_k,

with positive c_k, ε_k and finite p,q. Constants here may depend on k. Then β′_{k+1}(0)=0. Indeed pick the least compatible R above (3/c_k)log(1/g). Since R is at most L times this lower bound for small g, gR^q≤ε_k. Subtract at g and zero; the two finite-size errors are O_k(g³), while the fluctuation error is O_k(g²(log(1/g))^p)=o(g). This proves the required derivative for that k. A uniform single-variable second-derivative estimate for the limiting scalar, as discussed after Proposition 3.4, would then give the uniform inverse-coupling bound. No uniformity of A_k, c_k or ε_k is required for the parity step itself.

The first proposed bound calls for comparing **the same native local activities** on two geometries; a bound on each separately does not by itself bound their difference. The second calls for a shrinking-coupling estimate with polynomial growth in the finite size. At first step the Laplace expansion supplies a starting calculation; at later steps its coefficients include the previously generated terms. These two displayed estimates have not been established here. They isolate a possible route avoiding differentiation of the large-field R integrals or a complex disk for real cutoffs.

### D.1 Flat operator

For a fine plane wave A_b=v_σ exp(iθ·x) based at the beginning x of positive link b=(x,σ), define its path row t_r(θ)v as follows. For each coordinate ordering of the nonzero entries of r follow the shortest coordinate-segment path from 0 to r.            [ Q(q)                                      0    ].

Here constant normalizations of the Lie-algebra basis can equivalently be kept in the upper-left block. It is often preferable to evaluate T as the finite path operation in position coordinates and use a within-block Fourier transform only for the curl. The multiplier row has four entries in position-component count per coarse site. The axial operator has L⁴−1 rows per coarse site and should not be discarded in a four-row constraint update.

**(P) Invertibility in every fiber.** Q has a right inverse. ### D.4 A cubic difference between the two printed block maps

**(P) The first differing jet can be expressed using the variance of the endpoint contour sums.** For a fixed r and μ let a and c be the mean *linear* contour sums on the left and translated right endpoint paths, b the middle bridge sum, and R the sum on the reference center bridge. ### H.1 Collapsing successive regular Gaussian integrals

Let M=Lᵏ. At fixed k, write C_k(N) for the coefficient of F² per terminal site in the **cumulative one-loop log density**, after subtracting the bare classical action. Test the external 12-plane curvature F times a Hermitian traceless generator T with Tr T²=1/2. As in Appendix E this means the zero-momentum Taylor coefficient tested on an affine Cartan potential in an exponentially local kernel. At each fixed finite depth exponential locality follows in the Gaussian problem from regular constrained inversion in each of the finitely many Bloch fibers; constants in this statement may depend on k. The finite-box route is to take the increasing-box limit of the regular polarization and then take the momentum derivative at zero. A periodic flux at one fixed side is not being differentiated.

**(P, cumulative Gaussian identity).** The one-loop increments at fixed depth sum to the one-loop coefficient of the original fine Wilson measure pushed forward by the composed readout. The difference C_j(N)−C_{j−1}(N), with C₀=0, is the new jth coefficient at its own scale. Here are details of the identification used in this statement.

1. At finite volume integrating the intermediate group fields against the Haar deltas fixes them successively to the block images. On the regular chart the fiber parametrization is nonsingular; the stationary point at one stage is the image of the stationary point at the next by constrained minimization in stages. Algebraically the Gaussian determinants of the conditional fibers multiply to the determinant on the composite fiber, including their coarea factors. This can be seen by applying ordinary changes of variables successively to the *same* finite-dimensional pushed-forward density; in Hessian coordinates the identity is the Schur complement formula. Originally imposed gauge factors must be included in this calculation, as described below. For a fixed number of steps, lower powers than the Gaussian saddle term disappear in the common bare weak-coupling limit.
2. In rewriting the original exponent, replacing H₀ by H_j subtracts from its correction exactly the same scalar multiple of A as it adds to the explicit classical factor. It is an algebraic reallocation. CMP 109 pp. 256–257, (0.22)–(0.23), explicitly states that older terms reappear on the changed background. Its p. 265, (2.2)–(2.3), identifies that background. The residue of a previous local action after its polarization subtraction has zero quadratic-curvature projection. Inserting that residue into the next saddle preserves this vanishing: its first variation at identity is zero by global color invariance, and the pullback of a quadratic-curvature term on affine data depends only on the linearized stationary field. At fine scale 1/M the stationary plaquette curvature is F/M² and there are M⁴ fine sites per terminal site. This scales its F² coefficient by exactly one. For the affine calculation one can choose a Cartan fine potential with uniform curl. Its Wilson current vanishes (the neighboring plaquette contributions cancel); every linear block gives the straight coarse curvature times L², modulo a coarse gradient from its endpoint paths. A fine gauge fixes the axial data. The transverse minimizer has that same curl by uniqueness at zero. Thus an inherited Gaussian term contributes its previous projected value and the new projection is the *difference* of the cumulative coefficients. This argument only uses the action through second order in the external field. The classical coefficient of F² in A on the minimizer is 1/(4N) per terminal site at every depth.
3. Equivalently, one may remove internal gauge by normalized orbit integration. Repeat for the subgroup fixing the next centers and then for the gauges acting on those centers but fixing the final ones. Locally this computation can also be made on nested internal tree sections: product Haar is invariant under the triangular tree changes, and normalizers evaluated at the identity are background independent. The saddle is in the interior of the fixed physical cutoffs; Gaussian jets are unchanged on shrinking the regular chart around it. For the color argument below it is sufficient to keep the original soft gauges: choose at the nested saddle their axial variables exactly identity. The quadratic form then comes from the quadratic Taylor term of 1−Re tr U(y,x); the fourth-power Taylor term has **zero Hessian** at that axial variable for every background. ### H.2 Isolating the color coefficient

**(P) For the specified Wilson action and Lie-series readout there are real numbers u_k independent of N, with u₀=0, such that**

    C_k(N) = N u_k − (1−L⁻⁴ᵏ)/(16N).

**Proof.** Write X_p for the Hermitian logarithm of a fine plaquette. The action density on that plaquette through degree four in X_p is Tr X_p²/(2N)−Tr X_p⁴/(24N). In a right-invariant link chart the plaquette logarithm, the logarithmic readout (0.12), and the operations giving the implicit mean (0.10) have expansions in nested commutators. On the Cartan background all background entries commute. Expanding any of these Lie series around that background to second order in its amplitude gives only adjoint commutators with T. In the action Hessian terms built using the quadratic invariant Tr X_p², invariance of the trace moves a bracket to the other slot. Thus the monomials with two quantum indices and two equal background generators reduce to multiples of ad_T² on the real color space (in the anti-Hermitian convention use ad_{iT}); the terms with one external generator are multiples of ad_T. This also holds for the constraint and gauge jets; their flat inverses are identical for every color. The determinant traces at order F² then contain Tr_ad(ad_{iT}²)=−N. The overall metric factor 1/(2N) in the flat quadratic action is canceled by its covariance; field-independent normalization factors cancel at identity. This accounts for all Lie-series contributions as N times geometrical coefficients independent of N. The argument uses the **specified Lie-series mean** and the fundamental Wilson action, not an arbitrary group-equivariant readout.

The remaining quartic term begins at fourth order in all fields. For two external and two quantum legs it uses only the linear plaquette field. On the affine Cartan background this field is F T/M² on each fine 12 plaquette, up to a harmless color rotation. The quantum plaquette field on the flat constrained fiber is the ordinary linear curl. The quartic term in the log density has a plus sign from the minus in the Wilson action. Let σ_p be the variance of that linear curl for **one** real color with unit Maxwell precision (quadratic action one half the squared curl on the gauge quotient). With the generator metric Tr(TᵃTᵇ)=δᵃᵇ/2 and normalized trace, the actual variance in the fluctuation scaled by gᴮ is 2Nσ_p. The six cyclic word positions in the quartic trace give

    4 Σ_a Tr(T² TᵃTᵃ) + 2 Σ_a Tr(T Tᵃ T Tᵃ)
         = N − 3/(2N).

Indeed completeness of the traceless Hermitian basis gives Σ Tᵃ Y Tᵃ=(Tr Y)1/2−Y/(2N), where 1 denotes the identity matrix. This identity follows by taking the kernel of the orthogonal projection onto the traceless matrices and using the basis normalization. With Y=1 or T and Tr T²=1/2 it gives respectively (N²−1)/(4N) and −1/(4N) for the two unweighted color sums. The quartic contribution to C_k(N) is therefore (N/12−1/(8N)) times the mean of σ_p over fine sites for the 12 orientation.     |ā_{μ,M}(q)−ā_{μ,∞}(q)| ≤ C M⁻².

The constant is numerical. For each retained n, the factors in the exact summand after scaling differ from the continuum factors by the multipliers (p_i/(2M))/sin(p_i/(2M)). Each lies between 1 and π/2 and differs from 1 by at most C p_i²/M². Moreover M²D(p/M)=Σ p_i² sinc²(p_i/(2M)) lies between (4/π²)|p|² and |p|². The change of reciprocal relative to |p|⁻² is at most C |p|²/M² in relative units. Thus each absolute summand error is at most C M⁻²∏_i(1+|n_i|)⁻². On the omitted tail |p|⁻²≤C M⁻²; its terms satisfy the same summable majorant. These estimates prove the bound uniformly even arbitrarily close to q=0. The principal alias also bounds each positive coarse covariance below by c/|q|². Hence the inverse quadratic forms on the coarse transverse subspace differ by at most C|q|⁴/M².

The error is real, not just an upper-bound artifact. For q on one coordinate axis and μ perpendicular to it, the alias sum evaluates to

    ā_{μ,M}(q) = 1/(4 sin²(q/2)) − (1−M⁻²)/6.

The inactive alias factors force zero aliases off the q axis. On that axis use Σ_{n=0}^{M−1}csc²(z+nπ/M)=M² csc²(Mz), obtained by differentiating the log of the sine product. Differentiating twice and using (csc² z)″=6 csc⁴ z−4 csc² z gives the necessary fourth-power sum. This verifies an O(M⁻²) transient of the flat coarse covariance, while its leading quadratic-curvature normalization is scale independent.

The irreducible missing estimate for even bounded one-loop cumulative drift concerns the *two background derivatives* of the adjoint fluctuation determinant, including those of the composed readout and of its coarea factors. Convergence of its value at the flat background, proved here for the covariance, cannot be differentiated with respect to an unestimated background variable. A quantitative bound |2u_k−dk|≤B would already suffice for the zero-coupling part of H.4. A sufficient stronger estimate is a constant m and |2u_k−dk−m|≤C L⁻ᵋᵏ, ε>0, which explicitly implies summable increment errors. None is assumed in proving H.2–H.3. Appendix I.2 controls local curved readout representatives, and I.3 isolates the stronger determinant estimate that remains open.

## Appendix I. Endpoint bounds, a uniform curved readout estimate and the interface conditions

### I.1 Which limit is being evaluated

The Gaussian calculation in H instead computes π lim_{T↑∞}P^G_{j,T}, where the superscript means the fixed-cutoff Gaussian endpoint in that torus. Each limit is at fixed j=1 or 2. The comparison is about the value, not its derivative.

### I.2 A uniform estimate on the curved readout, with the gauge gradients removed

This is a bound for **the readout** in the adjoint part of the determinant. It concerns fixed terminal spacing one, scales δ_j=L^{j−k}, a constant Cartan flux F, and Lie-algebra link variations measured as connection components (left-trivialized link variation divided by its spacing). Give these components norm squared δ_j⁴Σ|a_b|², and quotient by the infinitesimal fine gauge gradients when comparing physical outputs.

**(P) Real bound.** For fixed N,L and sufficiently small |F|, the operator induced on gauge quotients by the derivative of the scale-j readout has norm at most 1+C_{N,L}|F|δ_{j+1}². The composed norms are bounded independently of k. Moreover one can choose representatives of the composed output, modulo a terminal infinitesimal gradient, whose localized rows have uniformly bounded first and second F derivatives in a cell-centered affine gauge.

**Proof.** In one layer write P_r,P′_r for the two endpoint contour means, B_r for the parallel bridge, H_r=P_r B_r(P′_r)⁻¹, C for the center bridge and X_r=log(H_r C⁻¹). The output is M=exp(average X_r)C. At a constant Cartan flux each relative loop in this formula bounds a fixed number of plaquettes in step-j units. Its logarithm has size O_{N,L}(|F|δ_{j+1}²). The endpoint contour holonomies likewise differ only by such closed loops. Let a variation of a group element be written as dot U U⁻¹. Differentiating exp and log at this small relative loop replaces each corresponding left-trivialized differential by I+O_{N,L}(|F|δ_{j+1}²), as follows directly from the convergent Lie series. The implicit endpoint mean has a bounded differential in its inputs: in the equation for the mean the unknown left variation has derivative −I plus an error of the same size after dividing by the number of paths. Therefore no scale-dependent inverse enters it.

In dot H_r H_r⁻¹ the endpoint contributions are dot P_r P_r⁻¹ and −Ad_{H_r}(dot P′_r(P′_r)⁻¹); the bridge contributes Ad_{P_r}(dot B_r B_r⁻¹). Replace Ad_{H_r} in the endpoint term by Ad_M, paying O_{N,L}(|F|δ_{j+1}²) times the endpoint derivative. In differentiating M, the two unperturbed center-bridge derivatives cancel. The average endpoint terms are now exactly the coarse gradient of the average endpoint variation at each center. Subtract that gradient. The linearized readout is represented by the physical-scale average of the parallel-transported bridge variations plus an error. For real fields each adjoint transport is an isometry. The bridge contains L successive bonds and one averages L⁴ choices of r. After dividing by coarse spacing and multiplying by fine spacing, the weights sum to one; each fine bond occurs with total multiplicity L in the sum over starting positions on the coarse tiling for its orientation. Jensen’s inequality with the lattice volume factors gives norm at most one. The error consists of finitely many local path rows with at most a fixed L-dependent number of entries; each carries the extra |F|δ_{j+1}² factor. The same finite bound applies to their column multiplicities. The row/column sum estimate gives operator norm at most C_{N,L}|F|δ_{j+1}². Gauge covariance of the exact derivative sends fine gradients to coarse gradients; this remains true after subtracting any coarse gradient. Thus this representation induces the stated quotient map. Multiplication over j gives exp(C_{N,L}|F|Σδ_{j+1}²), uniformly bounded.

For the localized derivative statement use the **same gradient-subtracted representative** at each step before composing. It preserves the quotient action because each later map carries gradients to gradients. Ancestors of a terminal cell lie inside a fixed physical enlargement: the added radii form a geometric sum of the δ_{j+1}. Use an affine Cartan gauge centered on this enlargement, of bounded physical radius R independent of k. For complex |F|≤r sufficiently small the closed-loop relative logarithms still have size O_{N,L}(|F|δ_{j+1}²) and the same inverse for the implicit mean exists. Open transports in the cell need no unitarity estimate in the complex disk: their norms are at most exp(C_{N,L}|F|(R+1)δ_{j+1}), by their path lengths and the affine potential. Thus on the required local rows the bridge bound is at most that factor, and the relative-loop error is bounded by C_{N,L}|F|δ_{j+1}² times that factor (changing the constants if necessary). For intermediate projections restrict to the full determining patch; row and column bounds there are the same as with zero padding. Composing these analytic representatives gives a uniform bound because Σδ_{j+1} and Σδ_{j+1}² are finite independently of k. Cauchy’s estimate on the fixed F disk gives the asserted first and second derivatives at zero. No global holomorphic constant-field family on an arbitrarily large periodic torus is needed. ∎

This estimate controls local rows modulo coarse gradients, a stronger statement than merely differentiating the flat limit in H.5. A determinant needs an actual gauge/coarea convention, the inverse Wilson Hessian and its curvature jets as well; the quotient norm bound alone cannot control their traces.

### I.3 A determinant comparison isolating the open estimate

At a fixed finite depth, on regular independent gauge coordinates let H be the fine stationary Hessian, J the differential of the composite hard readout, and h the smooth coordinate density. Use the constrained stationary Hessian when a multiplier is nonzero. For the affine Cartan calculation the fine Wilson current vanishes. For any positive coarse penalty S for which D=H+J*SJ is invertible, the bordered matrix in B satisfies

    |det K| = |det D| · |det(J D⁻¹ J*)|.

Indeed adding J*SJ to the upper-left block amounts to a determinant-one row operation on K; take its Schur complement. This identity includes the coarea factor. Combined with the gauge measure, the hard log prefactor is log h minus half the sum of the two log determinants. A replacement of the soft fine operator D by a reference background-gauge regulator must account also for the gauge determinant of that reference. The formula identifies two required comparisons: a bounded curvature coefficient per terminal site for the fine determinant **difference** from the chosen reference, and a bounded curvature coefficient for the coarse Schur determinant and coordinate/gauge terms. Fixed-k invertibility and I.2 do not furnish a depth-uniform trace bound on either one. In particular no uniform massive-to-hard Wilson background determinant comparison, including its two external derivatives, has been proved here. A single bound on the combined difference would suffice, without a separate sign estimate on each determinant. With a reference calculation giving dk+O(1) in program units, such a combined bound would give the remaining |2u_k−dk|≤B in H.4. The uniform local readout estimate eliminates one possible growth with the number of block paths, but it does not give this missing comparison.

The sum is bounded independently of K by comparison with an integral using the above lower bound. ### I.5 A per-volume sufficient estimate for endpoint identification

For fixed j≤2, there is also a route that needs no uniform scalar derivative in g. The following elementary lemma specifies what to estimate if a direct use of the closed-interval statement is unavailable. Let b∈ℝⁿ have a centered Gaussian measure with covariance of norm at most K. Assume the true change of variables writes the regular torus integral, divided by the prescribed Gaussian factor, as E[χ_g exp V_g(b)] with 0≤χ_g≤1. Suppose the row sums of the absolute entries of the covariance square root are bounded by K₁. Set R=g⁻¹ᐟ⁶. On the allowed support assume

    V_g(b) ≤ δ_g(n+||b||²) + c Σ_i b_i² 1_{|b_i|>R},

with δ_g→0 and c<1/(8K); outside the support set χ_g=0. On the cube ||b||_∞≤R assume χ_g=1 and V_g≥−ε_g n, ε_g→0. These are conditions on the *actual* regular fluctuation integral, not on a replacement gauge law. Then, uniformly in its dimension,

    n⁻¹ |log E[χ_g exp V_g]| → 0.

Indeed absorb δ_g||b||² into a tilted Gaussian of covariance bounded by 2K for g small, at an O(Kδ_g)n log-normalization cost. Expand the remaining exponential as a product of 1+(exp(c b_i²)−1)1_{|b_i|>R}. Choose t>0 with c+t<1/(4K). For any set S of marked indices, its expectation is at most exp(−tR²|S|) E exp((c+t)Σ_{i∈S}b_i²) ≤ [exp(−tR²)(1−4K(c+t))⁻¹ᐟ²]^{|S|} by the finite Gaussian determinant. Summing subsets gives the upper per-coordinate error. For the lower bound write b=C¹ᐟ²z with independent unit Gaussian z and restrict each |z_i|≤R/K₁. The product probability costs o(n) in its logarithm, the required b cube holds, and the exponent loses at most ε_g n. This proves the lemma.

If these estimates are verified uniformly over real small external plane-wave amplitudes, per-volume convergence extends to their field Hessians using a uniform holomorphic bound in those amplitudes and the normal-family theorem. At fixed j a uniform exponential spatial bound on the resulting polarization kernels also makes their Fourier transforms a normal family on a strip; pointwise convergence on real momenta then passes to the p-derivatives at zero on a smaller strip. These implications require neither C¹ in g nor derivatives of moving cutoff indicators. The lemma does **not** verify for the second native step the marked-tail estimate above with its inherited action and solved nonlinear constraints. It therefore does not remove the EP qualification.

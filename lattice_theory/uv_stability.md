# Constant-coupling ultraviolet stability

## 1. Target and interfaces assumed

This paper compares the pointwise density estimate of T. Bałaban, *Large field renormalization II*, CMP **122** (1989), 355–392, with the program's finite-torus Wilson integral. No upstream interface is assumed. A theorem conditional on keeping the couplings small is usable at specified steps. Existence of a bare-coupling trajectory reaching a chosen terminal value is a separate question. Labels (L), (P), (C) and (O) distinguish primary statements, deductions here, hypotheses and outstanding verifications.

## 2. Definitions and conventions

In physical coordinates the box has side T, lattice spacings a and ℓ_k=L^k a, and N_k=(T/ℓ_k)^4. In step-k coordinates the initial spacing is η=L^{-k}, the coarse spacing is one, and the fine lattice T_η and coarse lattice T₁⁽ᵏ⁾ occupy the *same* geometric box. V is the coarse integration variable. U_k(V) is a fine, regular, constrained minimizer modulo gauge, characterized by CMP 119 (2.11)–(2.13), pp. 256–257. Different determining sets in a large-field history give different minimizers.

**(L) Geometry.** CMP 109 §0, pp. 251–252, uses centered lattices on a periodic cube with half-side a power L^m and a large odd L. Taking odd L>11 is sufficient for the displayed size restriction there. CMP 119 p. 245 further requires mutually compatible partitions, including the original partition π₀ and those at future steps. In the coordinates used for the relevant partitions the auxiliary sides include MR_j and LM₂R_j. The corresponding physical lengths must tile T (at step j multiply by ℓ_j). M₁ is the background-field block scale, M₂ an intermediate power of L (example L²M₁), M a sufficiently larger power of L. R_j is the least power of L at least (log g_j⁻²)^r, r≥2. Choose the centered-grid shift consistently through all steps. A sufficient family is the original boxes made large enough for all these divisibilities at the finite depth under consideration. An arbitrary even terminal side or arbitrary odd L≥3 is an extension. CMP 109 (0.19)–(0.21), pp. 255–256, makes the endpoint a chosen unit of length.

**(L/P) Action dictionary.** CMP 109 (0.2), p. 252, uses normalized trace, and counts elementary squares (p. 251). The Wilson plaquette orientation is explicit in CMP 119 p. 253, immediately before (1.27): p=p_{μν}(x) with μ<ν. Independently, the printed CMP 95 p. 18, (1.2)–(1.3), equates one half the plaquette sum of |F(p)|² to one quarter the sum over all ordered μ,ν. Thus reversing a loop conjugates/inverts its holonomy and leaves its real trace unchanged, but does not create another summand in the native Wilson action. In four dimensions A=Σ_p(1−Re Tr(U_p)/N)=S/N. Write xᴮ_k=(gᴮ_k)⁻². To match β=2N x_program in front of S requires xᴮ=2N² x_program. In particular β-j⁽⁰⁾ corresponds to xᴮ−N j⁽⁰⁾. When applying a printed threshold, radius or recursion below g always means the published coupling gᴮ. Numerical AF coefficients also transform under this conversion. The clover tensor source in the program is an additional interaction.

## 3. Main statements and dictionaries

### D0. Statement B(0.1), with its arguments retained

**(L)** On the construction's compatible tori, let the sequence of effective couplings be contained in (0,γ] for γ sufficiently small. LFR II Theorem 1 and (0.1), pp. 355–356, give, at each constructed step, for the complete R-renormalized density,
   
$$\chi_k(T_\eta;V)e^{-x_k^B A(U_k(V))-E_- N_k}
\ \leq\ \rho_k(V)\ \leq\ e^{E_+N_k}.$$

The pointwise bound is also a bound almost everywhere for the product group measure. LFR II states independence of k, T_η and U_k. CMP 119 Corollary 3, p. 264, states independence of η and T while permitting dependence on g_k. Keep this distinction when using an *integrated* estimate and when taking g_* small. The papers prescribe a sufficiently small γ and inequalities among construction constants, without giving numerical functions E_±(N,L,M,γ). The theorem has no source radius, test-function order, or insertion count. Physical ℓ₀ enters only via rescaling. There is no terminal shooting assertion in B(0.1).

**(L/P) Volume.** CMP 119 p. 263 defines scale-j counts as numbers of sites of the corresponding lattice and p. 264 applies the stability counting in η-scale coordinates. LFR II p. 355 explicitly identifies the earlier superrenormalizable estimate as the template. In that template, CMP 102 (1985), 255–275, p. 256, (3),(5) and the equality immediately after (5) give the convention: the volume of the fine torus in rescaled units is the sum of the lattice volume element. Here it gives |T_η|=Σ_{x∈T_η}η⁴=η⁴(T/a)⁴=N_k. This also agrees with the unit sublattice in CMP 95 (1.18). The fine-site count would insert an extra factor L^{4k} and would fail to give finite terminal exponents per unit physical volume. Counts of free bonds in the Jacobian in LFR II (1.2) have a different meaning from |T_η|.

**(L) Outer measure and selector.** The outer integral is ∫dV_k ρ_k(V_k), CMP 119 p. 264. Equation (3.1) there integrates the incoming field against dV_k with the block-average group delta. This is product Haar on one positively oriented bond per geometric link: integrating the output against Haar removes the delta. CMP 119 (1.5)–(1.7), p. 247, inserts normalized gauge identities *inside* this integration. LFR I (1.76),(1.99)–(1.100), pp. 195–201, reorganizes terms into a representative having equal integral; LFR II p. 356 omits internal graph averages from the displayed formula only. The representative in B(0.1) is the one after R. These graph integrals cannot impose a delta function on the *external* coarse field.

**(L)** CMP 119 (2.4),(2.5), p. 255, sets ε_k=g_k p₀(g_k), p₀(g)=A₀(log g⁻²)^{p₀}; CMP 119 p. 246 specifies p₀≥5r and sufficiently large A₀. The action in the lower bound is evaluated on the global all-small determining-set minimizer, while the selector uses a family of local minimizers. The crude coarse-ball deduction is given in §4.

### D2. The density carried through the induction

**(L)** CMP 119 (2.1)–(2.23), pp. 254–258. A history consists of nested Ω_j and Λ_j, with Λ_j between Ω_{j+1} and Ω_j, and associated fluctuation sets S_j⊂Ω_j∩Λ_jᶜ, empty or unions of LM₂R_j cubes. ## 4. Proofs of the conversions and the coarse Haar bound

**(P) Counting.** T/ℓ_k is the side in coarse lattice units. Each coarse site corresponds to L^{4k} fine sites of volume η⁴. The sum of these volumes over that block is one. Summing over coarse sites gives §3. For a unitary holonomy W one has 1−Re tr W=(2N)^{-1}‖I−W‖²_HS. Both the action conversion and the estimate below follow with constants depending only on the chosen matrix norm and N.

**(P) A terminal coarse Haar ball.** Use operator norm for the elementary estimates (equivalence constants can be absorbed into B₃). Put all coarse links in the open ball ‖V_b−1‖<δ around 1. An oriented product of q links in this ball differs from 1 by at most qδ by unitarity and telescoping; inverses have the same bound. On the global all-small coarse determining set, (7) of CMP 102, 277–309, holds with 4δ. Its Theorem 1 (8), pp. 278–279, gives regularity (2) with constant B₃·4δ: on the terminal layer ‖U_k(V,∂p)−1‖<4B₃δη². Here the factor is L^{-2k}=η², not an unscaled bound.

The same argument applies to the *local* data in (2.16), with the following interface check. The section Q_k^{s*} replaces bonds within each k-block by identity and those crossing a k-block face by the corresponding coarse bond, by (1.3) and p. 257. At every intermediate block level the averages of this lift have that same form on the coarser subblocks. Indeed the paths inside an averaging block have trivial transport and each parallel straight crossing of two adjacent subblocks has the same transport, either identity or that face's V. Thus the logarithmic corrections relative to the reference transport in the contour average vanish. In (7) a plaquette at a change of scales uses averaged finer links on any missing sides. For this lift those averages equal the straight transports, as Appendix C1 verifies. Each such straight side has length at most one in terminal units and crosses at most one k-block face. Its transport is identity or a single V or inverse. Hence every mixed plaquette of the prescribed determining data also satisfies the 4δ bound. This argument uses *link* smallness and does not assert the analogous criterion from coarse plaquette smallness alone.

By CMP 119 pp. 256–257, the minimal determining set based on □̃⁴ has a coarsest-scale interior that contains the region left after removing a 2M₁ boundary strip. The tested enlarged cube □̃ lies in this inner portion because the remaining three layers have combined thickness at least 2M₁ (M₂>M₁). Thus (2),(7),(8) of the variational paper apply there at j=k and yield 4B₃δη² on every tested plaquette. Choose 4δ strictly below a₁, a₀/B₃ and ε_k/B₃ with the group norm conversion if needed. Here a₀,a₁,B₃ denote the constants of that variational theorem, not the lattice spacing. Every factor of χ equals one. On the global minimizer, summing the squared curvature bound over 6η^{-4}N_k fine plaquettes gives A(U_k)≤C_N B₃²δ² N_k ≤ C ε_k² N_k.

Finally the normalized Haar mass m_G(δ) of this ball is strictly positive. There are 4N_k independent outer links, so the exhibited event has measure at least exp(−b(δ)N_k), b(δ)=−4log m_G(δ). The gauge normalization identity LFR I (0.4), p. 176, and the reverse Faddeev–Popov step LFR II p. 378 legitimize evaluation with this external product measure. This yields

**(P)**

$$-E_- - b(δ) - C x_k^B ε_k^2
\;\leq\; N_k^{-1}\log\big(e^{-E(T)} Z[0]\big)\;\leq\; E_+.$$

For fixed published terminal g_K=g_* the same δ works for all compatible depths and volumes. At earlier steps tending to g_k=0 the Haar cost may grow; no uniform positive lower bound on intermediate radii was used. The event restricts only the final links of the complete density. At k=0 the elementary plaquette test and link product bound apply directly whenever a step-zero version is used; the scalar E(T) was chosen for the full finite trajectory.

## 8. Summary

Bałaban’s published four-dimensional ultraviolet stability theorem applies to complete effective densities when the effective couplings stay in a sufficiently small interval. The exponent counts the volume in units of the current block spacing. The earlier three-dimensional primary paper explicitly identifies that volume as the sum of the fine volume elements and as the number of coarse sites. The later four-dimensional paper uses this scale for the same combinatorial estimate.

Two field arguments matter in applying the lower bound. Its action uses the fine constrained minimizer determined by the coarse links. Its characteristic function tests localized minimizers on enlarged cubes. A product Haar ball for the terminal coarse links satisfies those tests. The lifted determining data are identity inside blocks and have one coarse transport at each crossed face. The variational regularity bound therefore gives a fine curvature bound with the required factor of the squared fine spacing. The terminal Haar cost per coarse site is finite at fixed terminal coupling. Internal gauge fixing preserves the integral.

The homogeneous scalar subtraction has a specified sign and an inductive recipe. It includes coordinate normalization and identity evaluations of localized regular and large-field terms. Interior terms on a given torus can be independent of the conditional integration domain. Equality of the complete coefficient on covering tori and locality with respect to an external coupling profile require further arguments.

The printed coupling recursion identifies the scalar coefficient that a flow calculation must follow. The assertion that a choice of bare coupling reaches a prescribed terminal value was announced for SU(2), and the final stability paper explicitly retained the small-coupling hypothesis. The program must also convert the normalized trace and restrict to compatible tori. The constant-coupling ledger records where variable coefficients would change the stationary point, fluctuation measure, cutoffs, geometry, component quotients and vacuum normalization.

Project a fine site to its containing k-block. Each nesting level r<k partitions each k-block into whole r-blocks. The lift is the identity on edges with both endpoints in one k-block and V_c on an edge crossing its c-face, with the inverse on the reversed edge. On averaging r-blocks to (r+1)-blocks, all paths in either averaging block stay in one k-block. Thus endpoint transports in the two blocks are identities. All parallel straight bridge paths in the averaging formula give identity or the same V_c as the center bridge, since they cross the same face. Each logarithm of the transported bridge *relative to the reference bridge* is therefore zero. The coarse output equals the reference bridge. This proves the same statement successively for M, …, M^k and also for missing-edge averages in (7). The group-average formulation (CMP 109 (0.5)–(0.12)) gives the same result since the average of coincident transports equals that transport. Any of the four oriented boundary factors in the mixed plaquette hence has distance <δ from 1 on the coarse link ball. Their product has distance <4δ. No assumption about rough data outside Q is required: this determining set uses the specific lift in (2.16) everywhere in its own support.

For global U_k(V) the all-terminal determining set only uses ordinary coarse plaquettes. CMP 102 Theorem 1 gives |∂U−1|<4B₃δs² throughout the terminal region in both applications. The schematic slice does not assert that the fine curvature in *outer lower layers* has this same s² factor; the old layer there has its own L^{-2j}. This distinction is necessary to obtain the correct power of η in (2.17).

### C2. Explicit compatible cofinal tori

**(P; conditional only on supplying each finite coupling trajectory).** Let u=log_L M, v=log_L M₂ and r_j=log_L R_j, all integers. Choose the background scales as L-powers as above. For each finite depth K let a_K=ℓ₀L^{-K}. Evaluate R_j for its admitted scalar sequence 0≤j≤K. Define

$$d_K=\max_{0\le j\le K}\{ j-K+u+r_j,\ j-K+1+v+r_j,\ 0\},\qquad
h_K=\max\{K,\ d_K+q,\ h_{K-1}+1\},\qquad T_K=2\ell_0L^{h_K}.$$

Here h_{−1}=0 and q is any fixed integer with L^q>1000. In native units the side of each published centered cube is thus 2L^{h_K} terminal lattice spacings. The elementary k-blocks, π_j (Mℓ_j), MR_jℓ_j and LM₂R_jℓ_j all divide T_K: their side in units ℓ₀ is L to the exponent j−K, j−K+u, j−K+u+r_j or j−K+1+v+r_j, respectively. All auxiliary **partitions** into smaller M₁ or M₂R_j cubes divide the larger corresponding scale; the fixed native M_v,R_vM_v grids also divide Mℓ_j by the choice of constants. Any partitions from prior stages retained by the R operation already appear among these powers. Intersections, unions, rectangular hulls, boundary collars, local cubes of side 100M, and tree paths in regions are not additional tilings requiring divisibility by their sizes. The extra q gives room for neighborhoods, including the finite-layer enlargements of the partition cubes and the bounded component boxes used in LFR II. Cubes can cross the torus identification using the periodic continuation of the same grid.

For compatibility as *partitions*, take all cube corners on the grids of powers of L with a common origin. If s divides s′ and s′/s is odd, centers of s′-cubes lie on the s-center lattice. After translating the original centered-lattice convention by −(ℓ₀/2,…,ℓ₀/2), the sites at level j have the offset (ℓ_j−ℓ₀)/2 in each coordinate, an integer multiple of ℓ_j since L^{K−j} is odd. Thus the single translation identifies every level's site lattice with the P2 lattice. Set inclusion of the grid boundaries and periodicity follow in each coordinate. Since K→∞ and h_K≥K, a_K→0 and T_K→∞. The terminal side is even, all stated divisors are satisfied, and the boxes form successively larger covers in terminal units. The construction is an arithmetic existence result. It makes no claim that a specified g_* can be reached or that the resulting estimates extend to arbitrary even sides. For example a terminal side strictly smaller than M R_K in ℓ₀ units cannot be tiled by a single such cube; an even side not divisible by L M₂ R_K is also outside that specified partition family. The scalar β_j in the source-free recursion is defined via the infinite-lattice polarization (CMP 109 (1.20)–(1.22), p. 264), so the choice of T_K here does not enter that definition.

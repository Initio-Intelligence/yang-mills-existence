# Minimizing-field response and the two-insertion cluster formula

## Part I. Actual minimizing-field response and the regular Gaussian calculation

**Status:** Two estimates for the actual minimizing fine field are proved below. They apply on the exact current-unit regular Gaussian carrier admitted by the cited Balaban results. They do **not** identify a positively transported old microscopic insertion with an insertion evaluated on a newly sampled minimizer, and therefore do not complete full-law S2.

The new input is the last section of Bałaban's variational paper, CMP102 (1985), 277–309. Proposition 9 and its estimates (177), (190) are **imported published results**, not reproved here. They concern the derivative of the actual nonlinear minimizing field; the plaquette cancellation and covariance composition below are the new derivation. Consequently the proof below uses the correct interpolation derivative rather than substituting a nearest-neighbor coarse curl.

### 1. Precise source scope and notation

Fix a scale $k$, a real admitted background $U_0$, the source nested domains $\{\Omega_j\}_{j=0}^k$, and the gauge and Dirichlet conventions in the cited papers. In current-scale coordinates let

\[
\eta=L^{-k},\qquad a=\eta h,
\tag{LR1}
\]

where $a$ is the original fine physical mesh and $h$ is the physical size of a current-unit block. All distances in source formulas below are in current-scale coordinates. All constants are independent of $\eta$, cutoff depth and volume; they may depend on the fixed group, $L$, and the fixed geometric admissibility constants.

The carrier requires the following actual source conditions.

1. The nested domains obey the source separation and block conditions. The background satisfies CMP99 (3.35) and (3.36), with $M\alpha_0$ sufficiently small. These are local gauge-potential, first-difference and second-difference conditions, not merely a bound on plaquette traces. For complex analyticity one also retains the source (3.37)–(3.38) or the equivalent conditions cited in CMP102 Proposition 9.
2. The fluctuation domain $\Lambda\subset\Lambda_k=\Omega_k^{(k)}$ is a union of the admitted current-unit big blocks, with the buffer from $\Lambda_k^c$ stipulated on CMP99 printed p.427. The constrained fluctuation field vanishes off $\Lambda$.
3. Every marked fine plaquette stencil lies in the current stratum on which the source derivative bounds apply. In particular all 24 signed leaves, and the bonds entering their covariant differences, must belong to that admitted part of $\Omega_k$. No assertion is made for a marked stencil crossing an excluded region.
4. The minimizing field is represented in the Landau gauge relative to $U_0$ used in CMP102 (173)–(175). Exact gauge-invariant leaf quadratic observables can be evaluated in this representative. A derivative of the axial representative would additionally differentiate its field-dependent gauge transformation; it is not silently identified with the derivative used here.

These conditions define a possibly smaller carrier than the carrier needed only for the background plaquette estimate in [Positive transport, nonlinear remainders and admissible curvature](positive_transport.md), Part III. They are stated explicitly rather than inferred from that plaquette estimate alone.

#### The actual minimizer and its derivative

Write the Landau representative of the minimizing configuration as

\[
U(B)=\exp\{i\eta\mathcal H(B)\}U_0,
\qquad U_0=U_k(V_0),\quad V=\exp(iB)V_0.
\tag{LR2}
\]

CMP102 printed pp.305–306, (174)–(177), gives

\[
\mathcal H(B)=\mathcal A_1(B)+H_1B
 -HD\bigl(\mathcal A_1(B)+H_1B\bigr),
\qquad \mathcal A_1(B)=O(B^2),\quad D(A')=O((A')^2).
\]

It follows, for the **actual minimizer**, that

\[
D\mathcal H(0)=H_1.
\tag{LR3}
\]

The correct $H_1$ includes the quadratic correction generated by linearizing the nonlinear averaging constraint. CMP99 printed p.421, (3.127)–(3.129), gives

\[
H_1=G_1Q^*(QG_1Q^*)^{-1},
\tag{LR4}
\]

with the source averaging normalization and gauge constraints. It is not permissible to replace $H_1$ by an unrelated interpolation operator or by a fine free Green function.

Most importantly, CMP102 printed p.308, (190), proves an estimate for the derivative at every admitted small $B$, not only at zero. The first two entries of that estimate are

\[
\begin{aligned}
\left\|\frac{\delta\mathcal H(B,x)}{\delta B(y')}\right\|
&\le C(L^j\eta)^{-1}(L^{j'}\eta)^{-d}
 e^{-\delta_0 d(y,y')/8},\\
\left\|\nabla_{U_0,x}
\frac{\delta\mathcal H(B,x)}{\delta B(y')}\right\|
&\le C(L^j\eta)^{-2}(L^{j'}\eta)^{-d}
 e^{-\delta_0 d(y,y')/8},
\end{aligned}
\tag{LR5}
\]

for $x\in\Delta(y)$, $y\in\Lambda_j$, $y'\in\Lambda_{j'}$. The derivative norm is covariant: CMP99 printed p.397, (3.39), defines the first-difference norm using $D_{U_0,\mu}A_\nu$. The weighted metric and its convolution estimates are in CMP96 printed pp.231–234, (2.46) and Lemma 2.1. CMP99's reference to “(2.36)” at this point is a source cross-reference typo; the displayed metric definition in CMP96 is (2.46).

For the current-unit output and input strata, $j=j'=k$, both scale factors in (LR5) are one. With $z_x$ a current-unit block center near the marked fine site, this gives

\[
\|H_1(x,b)\|+\|\nabla_{U_0}H_1(x,b)\|
\le C e^{-\gamma |z_x-b|},\qquad b\in\Lambda,
\tag{LR6}
\]

and the corresponding estimate for $D\mathcal H(B)$. To pass from the source weighted metric to this weaker current-unit estimate, use that each source length weight is at least one, and absorb the bounded block-center offsets into $C$. The same statement uses periodic distance on a torus.

#### Volume-measure normalization

The source kernels use the natural measure of their input lattice. A $j'$-stratum has volume weight $(L^{j'}\eta)^d$; this is why a functional kernel estimate may display its inverse in (LR5). On the current input stratum that weight is **exactly one**. Thus the sums below are sums over current-unit bonds and finitely many Lie algebra components. They are not sums over all fine $\eta$-bonds, and no factor $\eta^{-4}$ is introduced by the covariance composition.

For a multiscale source $j'<k$, the kernel powers, integration weights, covariance and admissible domain must all be reconsidered together. The present argument does not identify the multiscale $B_0$ in LFR I (1.82) with the current-unit domain $\Lambda$.

### 2. The constrained covariance really is the one in the regular Gaussian integral

The current-unit fluctuation space in CMP99 printed p.428 is

\[
\mathcal V_\Lambda=
\{B:B|_{\Lambda^c}=0,\; B|_{\cup_{y\in\Lambda'}\mathrm{Ax}(y)}=0,\;Q_1B=0\},
\tag{LR7}
\]

with exactly the source axial trees and averaging constraints. Let $C$ be the source coordinate embedding from the remaining independent bonds into this space. Thus $B=C A$; $C$ is **not** a square root of a covariance.

The quadratic form is the full source form (3.156), including the terms produced by the nonlinear constraint,

\[
\langle B,\Delta_k B\rangle
=\langle B,(QG_1Q^*)^{-1}B\rangle
-a_{\rm gf}\langle B,B\rangle
-2\langle H_1\widehat D^{(2)}(B),J\rangle.
\tag{LR8}
\]

Here $a_{\rm gf}$ denotes the parameter called $a$ in that source formula, to avoid confusion with the physical fine mesh in (LR1). The independent Gaussian variable has precision and covariance

\[
M_\Lambda=C^*\Delta_k C,
\qquad \operatorname{Cov}(A)=M_\Lambda^{-1},
\]

and the covariance of the full constrained variable is exactly

\[
C^{(k)}(\Lambda)=C(C^*\Delta_k C)^{-1}C^*.
\tag{LR9}
\]

This is CMP99 (3.158). Its Theorem 3.15, printed p.432, proves

\[
\|C^{(k)}(\Lambda;b,c)\|
\le C_Ce^{-\delta_C|b-c|}.
\tag{LR10}
\]

The group/color normalization only changes fixed constants: the source uses normalized matrix trace, whereas the leaf space of [Positive transport, nonlinear remainders and admissible curvature](positive_transport.md) uses Hilbert–Schmidt trace. Fix an orthonormal Lie algebra basis once and absorb the resulting $N$-dependent norm conversion into $C_C$.

There is also an exact match to the nonlinear coordinate map actually written in the regular fluctuation integral. CMP119 printed p.272, (3.28), contains

\[
\exp\{-\tfrac12\langle A,C^*\Delta^{(k)}CA\rangle\},
\qquad
U_k\left(\exp i\,[g_{B,k}CA-\mathfrak hD(g_{B,k}CA)]V^{(k)}\right).
\tag{LR11}
\]

The letter $\mathfrak h$ here denotes the source linearizing operator, not physical scale. The correction $D$ starts in degree two; the same local coordinate map is displayed in CMP116 printed p.6, (1.19)–(1.20). Therefore differentiation at $A=0$ gives

\[
D_A\mathcal H\bigl(g_{B,k}CA-\mathfrak hD(g_{B,k}CA)\bigr)\big|_{A=0}
=g_{B,k}H_1C.
\tag{LR12}
\]

Thus (LR9) is precisely the constrained Gaussian reference covariance needed for the linearization of the fine minimizer occurring in the regular integral. The hard characteristic functions, Jacobians and effective interaction in that integral are additional factors; (LR11) does not call the full normalized conditional law Gaussian.

### 3. Exact fine-plaquette cancellation

Let $P=UVW^{-1}Z^{-1}$ be a positive oriented fine plaquette of $U_0$, with $U=U_{0,\mu}(x)$, $V=U_{0,\nu}(x+\eta e_\mu)$, $W=U_{0,\mu}(x+\eta e_\nu)$, $Z=U_{0,\nu}(x)$. Vary positive links by

\[
U_t(e)=e^{it\eta a(e)}U_0(e),
\]

and impose the inverse-link relation on reversed links. Put

\[
\eta\,(D_{U_0}a)_{\mu\nu}(x)
=a_\mu(x)+\operatorname{Ad}_U a_\nu(x+\eta e_\mu)
-\operatorname{Ad}_Z a_\mu(x+\eta e_\nu)-a_\nu(x).
\]

Directly differentiating the four factors gives the exact based-loop identity

\[
\begin{aligned}
\dot P={}&i\eta^2(D_{U_0}a)_{\mu\nu}(x)P\\
&+i\eta(I-\operatorname{Ad}_P)
\bigl(\operatorname{Ad}_Z a_\mu(x+\eta e_\nu)+a_\nu(x)\bigr)P.
\end{aligned}
\tag{LR13}
\]

The second term is important for a based, matrix-valued loop. A trace-only expansion should not be used to delete it. Unitarity and $\|I-\operatorname{Ad}_P\|\le2\|I-P\|$ imply, if $\|I-P\|\le\alpha\eta^2$,

\[
\|\dot P\|
\le\eta^2\bigl(2\|\nabla_{U_0}a\|+4\alpha\eta\|a\|\bigr)
\tag{LR14}
\]

on the finite plaquette stencil. Changing signs or base orientation only introduces unitary parallel transports and the same fixed-size bounds, so (LR14) holds for all 24 signed leaves.

Recall the actual leaf coordinate used in [Positive transport, nonlinear remainders and admissible curvature](positive_transport.md), Part III,

\[
L_{\mu\nu,\epsilon\delta}(U;x)
=\frac{\epsilon\delta}{2a^2}\,
\pi_0\frac{P_{\epsilon\mu,\delta\nu}(U;x)-P_{\epsilon\mu,\delta\nu}(U;x)^*}{2i}.
\tag{LR15}
\]

The Hermitian-part map and $\pi_0$ are contractions in Hilbert–Schmidt norm. Consequently (LR14) and $a=\eta h$ yield

\[
\|D L_h(U_0)[a](x)\|
\le C h^{-2}\bigl(\|\nabla_{U_0}a\|+\alpha\eta\|a\|\bigr).
\tag{LR16}
\]

This is the required cancellation of the two fine powers of $\eta$. Bounding the four differentiated links separately before taking the plaquette cancellation would lose this improvement.

### 4. The proved Gaussian response covariance

Define the actual fine-leaf response row

\[
\mathcal R_h(x,b)=D L_h(U_0)[H_1(\cdot,b)](x).
\tag{LR17}
\]

It is a map from one coarse bond's Lie algebra to the $24\dim\mathfrak g$-dimensional leaf space. Combining (LR6) and (LR16), with the regular background plaquette bound, proves

\[
\|\mathcal R_h(x,b)\|\le C_Rh^{-2}e^{-\gamma|z_x-b|}.
\tag{LR18}
\]

For $A$ distributed by the reference Gaussian in (LR11), the linearized leaf fluctuation is

\[
\xi_h(x)=g_{B,k}\mathcal R_h(x,\cdot)CA,
\]

so its covariance is **exactly**

\[
K_h(x,y)=g_{B,k}^2\,
\mathcal R_h(x,\cdot)C^{(k)}(\Lambda)
\mathcal R_h(y,\cdot)^*.
\tag{LR19}
\]

#### Theorem LR-A: current-unit Gaussian covariance of the actual minimizing-field response

On the carrier in Section 1 there are constants $c_K,c>0$, uniform in fine mesh, depth and volume, such that

\[
\boxed{\displaystyle
\|K_h(x,y)\|\le c_Kg_{B,k}^2h^{-4}
e^{-c|x-y|/h}.}
\tag{LR20}
\]

Here $x,y$ on the right are physical coordinates. This proves the previously required covariance estimate for this actual minimizing-field Gaussian reference, with $\mathcal D_h(x,y)=e^{-c|x-y|/h}$.

**Proof.** Equations (LR10), (LR18) and (LR19) bound the left side by

\[
Cg_{B,k}^2h^{-4}
\sum_{b,c\in\Lambda}
e^{-\gamma|z_x-b|}e^{-\delta_C|b-c|}e^{-\gamma|c-z_y|}.
\]

Choose $\lambda=\tfrac12\min(\gamma,\delta_C)$. The triangle inequality extracts $e^{-\lambda|z_x-z_y|}$. The two remaining endpoint sums are bounded by the absolutely convergent unit-lattice sum of $e^{-\lambda|z|}$; the middle residual factor is at most one. Bond directions and color dimensions only multiply the fixed constant. The block-center offsets are uniformly bounded and are absorbed into the constant. This gives (LR20). On a periodic lattice use periodic distance and the corresponding uniform shell-counting bound. $\square$

#### The two-point quadratic-leaf consequence

Let $B_h(x)=L_h(U_0;x)$, and let $M_\alpha$ be the fixed symmetric matrices representing the $Q^{\rm leaf}_\alpha$ of [Positive transport, nonlinear remainders and admissible curvature](positive_transport.md), so $\|M_\alpha\|\le2$. Put

\[
O_\alpha(x)=\tfrac12(B_h(x)+\xi_h(x))^TM_\alpha(B_h(x)+\xi_h(x)).
\]

For a fixed admitted history $Y$, the exact Gaussian identity is

\[
\operatorname{Cov}_{\gamma_Y}(O_\alpha(x),O_\beta(y))
=\tfrac12\operatorname{tr}(M_\alpha K_h(x,y)M_\beta K_h(y,x))
+B_h(x)^TM_\alpha K_h(x,y)M_\beta B_h(y).
\tag{LR21}
\]

The generic regularity hypotheses used for (LR20) do not themselves supply a factor of $g_{B,k}$ in the background. They only give $|B_h|\le C\alpha_0h^{-2}$, with the fixed geometric factors absorbed into $C$. On that generic carrier, (LR21) therefore proves only

\[
\left|\operatorname{Cov}_{\gamma_Y}(O_\alpha(x),O_\beta(y))\right|
\le C\bigl(g_{B,k}^4+g_{B,k}^2\alpha_0^2\bigr)h^{-8}
e^{-c|x-y|/h}.
\tag{LR21a}
\]

The stronger running-smallness bound must concern the **same background** used in the Gaussian response. It can be supplied directly for the regular background $U_0=U_{k+1}$ in CMP119, without identifying that background with the different LFR R minimizer.

Specifically, CMP119 printed pp.266–267, (3.10)–(3.12), expands about this regular minimizing background. On each admitted source cube $\Pi'\subset\Omega_{k+1}$, with the stipulated field restrictions, (3.12) gives

\[
\|U_{k+1}(\partial p)-I\|
<2B_3L^2(1+\beta_0)\,\epsilon_k
\bigl(L^{-1}\eta_{\rm src}\bigr)^2,
\qquad p\subset\widetilde{\Pi'}.
\tag{LR21b}
\]

Here $\beta_0$ is the source's fixed regularity slack parameter. Keep the two coordinate meshes distinct: $\eta_{\rm src}=a/h$ is the fine mesh in current-response units, while the displayed next-scale background uses $\xi=L^{-1}\eta_{\rm src}=a/(Lh)$. The factor $L^2$ in (LR21b) exactly compensates $\xi^2$ when expressing the bound in current-response units. Consequently

\[
\|U_0(\partial p)-I\|
\le C_0\epsilon_k(a/h)^2,
\qquad C_0=2B_3(1+\beta_0),
\]

up to the fixed conversion from the source matrix norm. This is a bound on the same physical fine plaquette; it is not a claim that the old and next-scale coordinate meshes coincide.

CMP119 printed p.255, (2.4), identifies $\epsilon_k=g_{B,k}P_k$. If all 24 signed leaves at every mark satisfy (LR21b), the elementary leaf domination $|L_h(U_0;x)|^2=q_{\rm leaf}(U_0;x)\le q_W(U_0;x)$ gives

\[
|B_h(x)|^2
\le C_{\rm bg}g_{B,k}^2P_k^2h^{-4}.
\tag{LR21c}
\]

Indeed each plaquette action is at most $\frac12 C_0^2\epsilon_k^2(a/h)^4$, and the factor $1/(2a^4)$ and 24 leaves in $q_W$ give $6C_0^2\epsilon_k^2h^{-4}$ before fixed norm conversions.

For the following bound, define $\mathcal A$ to be the intersection of the full covariance/response carrier from Section 1 with the carrier on which the **same $U_0=U_{k+1}$** has all 24 marked leaves satisfying (3.12). Then (LR21c) holds pointwise, hence under any normalized history average,

\[
E[\mathbf1_{\mathcal A}|B_h(x)|^2]
\le C_{\rm bg}g_{B,k}^2P_k^2h^{-4}.
\]

Alternatively, the following result holds on any common carrier where this same-background moment bound has been separately proved. Applying Cauchy–Schwarz at the two endpoints of (LR21) now proves

\[
E\left[\mathbf1_{\mathcal A}
\left|\operatorname{Cov}_{\gamma_Y}(O_\alpha(x),O_\beta(y))\right|\right]
\le Cg_{B,k}^4(1+P_k^2)h^{-8}e^{-c|x-y|/h}.
\tag{LR22}
\]

Here $O_\alpha$ is quadratic in the **linearized leaves** $B_h+\xi_h$. It is not the full nonlinear insertion $Q^{\rm leaf}_\alpha(U(A))$. In the latter insertion, second derivatives of the leaf map already supply additional second-order Taylor terms involving the background leaf and the second leaf variation. These terms and the remaining nonlinear terms have not been included in the Wick calculation.

The previously proved all-order Gaussian cycle/path theorem now also has its actual minimizing-field response hypothesis on this stricter source-admitted carrier. The history expectation in (LR22) averages a **reference Gaussian covariance**. It does not assert that this reference covariance equals the shell $D_k^{FG}$ of the full positive transport.

If prescribed finite mixing matrices have operator norms $z_x,z_y$, multiplying the observables by them multiplies the right side of (LR22) by at most $z_xz_y$. This elementary observation does not establish the existence, correct normalization or scale growth of the renormalization matrices required by the observable route.

### 5. Nonlinear minimizing-field observable derivatives

The uniform $B$-dependent estimate (LR5) yields more than a linearization at zero.

Let $B$ be in the real analyticity and regularity domain of Proposition 9, with $\|\mathcal H(B)\|+\|\nabla_{U_0}\mathcal H(B)\|\le C\varepsilon_{\rm reg}$ on the current stratum. For a variation $\dot B$, the left tangent of $U(B)$ is

\[
a_B=\int_0^1\operatorname{Ad}_{e^{it\eta\mathcal H(B)}}
\bigl(D\mathcal H(B)\dot B\bigr)\,dt.
\tag{LR23}
\]

Unitary conjugation is an isometry. A covariant finite difference of (LR23), and the change of the connection from $U_0$ to $U(B)$, give

\[
\|a_B\|\le\|D\mathcal H(B)\dot B\|,
\quad
\|\nabla_{U(B)}a_B\|
\le C\left(\|\nabla_{U_0}D\mathcal H(B)\dot B\|
+\varepsilon_{\rm reg}\|D\mathcal H(B)\dot B\|\right).
\tag{LR24}
\]

For completeness, the first difference of the conjugating exponential is bounded by $C\eta\|\nabla_{U_0}\mathcal H(B)\|$; division by the lattice step has already been included in this covariant difference. Replacing $U_0(e)$ by $e^{i\eta\mathcal H_e(B)}U_0(e)$ contributes at most $C\|\mathcal H_e(B)\|$, because $\eta^{-1}\|\operatorname{Ad}_{e^{i\eta\mathcal H_e(B)}}-I\|\le C\|\mathcal H_e(B)\|$. These estimates justify (LR24) without a hidden negative power of $\eta$.

Apply the exact plaquette identity (LR13) at $U(B)$, whose admitted plaquettes satisfy $\|P(B)-I\|\le C\varepsilon_{\rm reg}\eta^2$, and then use (LR5).

#### Theorem LR-B: nonlinear leaf and Wilson/clover insertion derivatives

Uniformly throughout that admitted real domain, for a current-unit source bond $b$,

\[
\boxed{\displaystyle
\|D_B L_h(U(B);x)[b]\|
\le C h^{-2}e^{-c|z_x-b|}.}
\tag{LR25}
\]

For the actual Wilson/clover matrix $Q^W$ constructed in [Wilson tensor witnesses and large-field suppression](tensor_witnesses.md),

\[
\boxed{\displaystyle
\|D_B Q^W_{AB}(U(B);x)[b]\|
\le C\varepsilon_{\rm reg}h^{-4}e^{-c|z_x-b|}.}
\tag{LR26}
\]

The same estimate applies to $Q^C$ and $Q^{\rm leaf}$.

**Proof of the insertion statement.** The curvature-leaf magnitude is $O(\varepsilon_{\rm reg}h^{-2})$, so differentiating a leaf quadratic and applying (LR25) yields the claimed bound for $Q^C,Q^{\rm leaf}$. For the exact scalar Wilson term, use

\[
s(P)=\tfrac12\|P-I\|_{\rm HS}^2,\qquad
|\dot s(P)|\le\|P-I\|_{\rm HS}\,\|\dot P\|_{\rm HS}.
\]

The factors are $C\varepsilon_{\rm reg}\eta^2$ and $C\eta^2e^{-c|z_x-b|}$. Dividing by $a^4=\eta^4h^4$ in $q_W$, and summing its 24 leaves, gives $C\varepsilon_{\rm reg}h^{-4}e^{-c|z_x-b|}$. Finally $Q^W=Q^C+I_6(q_W-q_C)/6$. $\square$

This closes a derivative bound for the **actual nonlinear observable evaluated on the minimizer**. A spatial covariance estimate under the interacting, truncated conditional measure still requires a valid covariance inequality for that measure, including its exact domain and effective-action derivatives. An exponentially localized observable gradient alone is insufficient.

### 6. Logarithmic localization: what has actually been proved

The exact source operators on the carrier above have the exponential bounds used in (LR20). No logarithmic localization length was deleted from those source inequalities. For clarity one can make a controlled *input localization* of the response explicit. Define

\[
\mathcal R_h^{[\rho]}(x,b)
=\mathbf1_{\{|z_x-b|\le\rho\}}\mathcal R_h(x,b),\qquad \rho\ge1,
\]

and keep the same positive constrained covariance $C^{(k)}$. Let $K_h^{[\rho]}$ be the covariance obtained from (LR19) with these truncated rows. It is still a covariance; no possibly nonpositive truncation of $C^{(k)}$ was introduced. The same exponential convolution gives, for a fixed $c_1>0$,

\[
\|K_h(x,y)-K_h^{[\rho]}(x,y)\|
\le Cg_{B,k}^2h^{-4}e^{-c_1\rho}e^{-c_1|x-y|/h}.
\tag{LR27}
\]

Indeed each difference term has at least one endpoint tail of length $>\rho$. Split the exponential path length between that tail, the endpoint separation and two summable residual endpoint factors. This absorbs the polynomial counting factor uniformly.

Taking

\[
\rho_k\ge\frac{q}{c_1}\log(1/g_{B,k})
\]

gives an error bounded by $Cg_{B,k}^{q+2}h^{-4}e^{-c_1|x-y|/h}$. The physical input-localization radius is **$\rho_k h$**; it is not $h$. A source radius involving a higher power of $\log g^{-2}$ can be retained directly in (LR27).

Input truncation does not by itself localize the dependence of the coefficients on the external history or background. CMP99 Theorem 3.14 and the random-walk expansions, and CMP116's subsequent decoupling of the nonlinear minimizer, address that separate dependence. Nor does (LR27) establish the actual profile of the complementary R histories. If those histories have a logarithmically enlarged decay profile, their true profile must still be proved and summed; (LR20) only settles the displayed regular Gaussian reference estimate.

### 7. Exact interface still missing for full S2

The statements above must not be substituted for a theorem about the marked numerator of positive transport.

LFR I printed p.201, (1.100), introduces a fresh normalized reference distribution in the reset region. At fixed exterior and selector history the old transported insertion is an **old marked integral divided by the old unmarked integral**. It is independent of the new interior variable sampled by that reset distribution. Therefore it cannot in general equal $Q^W(U_k(V_{ \mathrm{fresh}});x)$, even though both are legitimate observables associated with that region.

To use (LR20) or (LR26) for the full-law shell one still needs an identity or a controlled expansion of the actual transported mark,

\[
T_kF_x
=\mathcal O_{k,x}^{\rm min}+\mathcal E_{k,x},
\tag{LR28}
\]

on the *same normalized history law*, with estimates for both the marked numerator defining $\mathcal O_{k,x}^{\rm min}$ and the error $\mathcal E_{k,x}$. The preceding analysis only provides sharp derivative and reference-Gaussian estimates for the explicit minimizing-field observable that could occur in such an expansion; it does not prove (LR28).

The precise remaining requests at this interface are:

- Identify the old marked numerator through every T and R step, including the kernel derivative/score terms if a source is represented by changing a coupling in a normalized kernel.
- Bound the nonlinear effective conditional law with the hard characteristic functions and Jacobians retained. CMP119 (3.28)–(3.29) explicitly contains these extra factors.
- Cover the full multiscale $B_0$, boundary-crossing marked stencils and complementary R histories; the current-unit covariance theorem alone does not do so.
- Control the conditional-mean cumulants and the terminal covariance uniformly as the original cutoff and volume change.

Thus the correct progress statement is: **the actual current-unit minimizing-field Gaussian covariance and the nonlinear minimizing-field insertion derivative are now proved on their source-admitted carrier. The corresponding estimates for the positively transported old insertions, and hence full S2, remain open.**

## Part II. Nonlinear action stability and the actual hard cutoff

### Disposition

This note closes a **normalized comparison for the actual classical cubic remainder on an admitted multiscale R component**. The comparison retains the actual cutoff, the actual gauge-fixed multiscale integration variables, and every other positive factor of the conditional law. It therefore does not replace that law by a Gaussian. It also gives exact formulas for the cutoff boundary terms and identifies the source estimate that controls a failed cutoff.

These are useful completed statements, but they do **not** complete S2. A spatially localized estimate for the full transported insertion under the full effective law is still needed. In particular, a small oscillation of one interaction does not prove a spatial decay estimate, and an unnormalized small factor for a failed cutoff does not bound a normalized marked conditional expectation.

The two relevant kinds of variables must be distinguished:

* The ordinary current-scale small-field Gaussian carrier in CMP119 (3.28) has a constraint embedding and a covariance furnished by CMP99.
* The R-component cutoff in LFR I (1.82) acts on the **multiscale** set \(\mathcal B_0\). It is not a single current-scale cube and is not automatically the preceding Gaussian carrier.

All source statements below concern the cited papers, in particular the equations on LFR II printed pp.361 and 383 and the cutoff definition on LFR I p.196.

### 1. The actual carrier and a classical remainder estimate

Fix an admissible component, its exterior field, and the retained history, denoted collectively by \(Y\). Impose all the geometric, regularity, and small-coupling conditions of LFR I–II. Write \(n_Y\) for the number of preparatory RG steps associated with this component. The source denotes this number by \(N\); it is distinct from the gauge rank in \(\mathrm{SU}(N)\). In the notation of LFR I, pp.195–196,

\[
U_0=U_{k,Z}(V_\Lambda),\qquad
V''=V'V_0,\qquad V'=\exp(iB')=\exp(ig_kB).
\tag{NC1}
\]

The fluctuation variables occur on

\[
\mathcal B_0=\mathcal B'_k\cap\Lambda_0,
\]

with the source's boundary convention and tree gauge \(T_0\). On tree bonds the fluctuation is fixed; the independent variables lie on \(\mathcal B_0\setminus T_0\). The actual cutoff is

\[
\chi'=
\prod_{b\in\mathcal B_0}
\mathbf1_{\{|B'(b)|<\delta'_k\}},\qquad
\delta'_k=g_k A_1p_1(g_k),\quad
p_1(g)=(\log g^{-2})^{p_1}.
\tag{NC2}
\]

The definition of \(\delta'_j\) is on LFR I p.183; the actual \(\chi'\) is (1.82) on p.196. Thus the radius in the rescaled variable \(B\) is \(A_1p_1(g_k)\).

LFR II p.361, (1.20), expands the localized classical action as

\[
\begin{aligned}
A(\zeta,U''_{k,Z})
={}&A(\zeta,U^0_{k,Z})
+g_k\langle DH''_{1,k,Z}B,\zeta\eta^{-2}\operatorname{Im}\partial U^0_{k,Z}\rangle\\
&+\frac{g_k^2}{2}
\langle H''_{1,k,Z}B,\Delta_1(\zeta)H''_{1,k,Z}B\rangle
+V(\zeta,g_kH''_{k,Z}B).
\end{aligned}
\tag{NC3}
\]

The paper's estimate immediately after (1.20) is

\[
\begin{aligned}
|V(\zeta,g_kH''_{k,Z}B)|
&\le C|\mathcal B_0|(\delta'_k)^3\\
&\le C(100MR_k)^dn_Y^2(\delta'_k)^3\\
&\le Cg_k^3 M^dR_k^{d+2}A_1^3p_1(g_k)^3,
\end{aligned}
\tag{NC4}
\]

where the last step uses the stated assumption \(n_Y\le R_k\). Constants may depend on the fixed source parameters, including the factor \(100^d\), but not on ultraviolet cutoff, torus size, or the depth of this admitted component.

Two details matter. First, the multiplicity in (NC4) is the actual multiscale support estimate

\[
|\mathcal B_0|\le C(100MR_k)^dn_Y^2,
\tag{NC5}
\]

as used in the source, not a guessed count of bonds in one current-scale cube. Second, the term entering the probability density is the action divided by \(g_k^2\). Consequently the corresponding dimensionless cubic potential obeys

\[
\boxed{
|\mathcal R_Y(B)|
:=g_k^{-2}|V(\zeta,g_kH''_{k,Z}B)|
\le r_k,
\quad
r_k=Cg_kM^dR_k^{d+2}A_1^3p_1(g_k)^3.
}
\tag{NC6}
\]

In four dimensions the factor is \(R_k^6\). With the source's polylogarithmic \(R_k\), \(r_k\to0\) as \(g_k\to0\). This conclusion is on the admitted component and cutoff support. It is not a uniform assertion on excluded histories or on arbitrary group configurations.

The source separately estimates small corrections to the quadratic form and the linear term in (NC3). Only the cubic term explicitly bounded in (NC6) is removed in the comparison below. No unverified smallness of the other terms is used.

### 2. Closed theorem: normalized stability under the actual cubic remainder

#### Theorem NC-A

On the fixed admitted carrier above, write the actual positive conditional law in the form

\[
\nu_Y(d\omega)
=\frac{e^{-\mathcal R_Y(\omega)}w_Y(\omega)\,
\mathbf1_{D_Y}(\omega)\,d\lambda_Y(\omega)}
{\int e^{-\mathcal R_Y}w_Y\mathbf1_{D_Y}\,d\lambda_Y}.
\tag{NC7}
\]

Here \(D_Y\) includes the **same** \(\chi'\), all other retained characteristic functions, and the gauge constraints. The measure \(d\lambda_Y\) can include auxiliary variables integrated in the component. The nonnegative factor \(w_Y\) is the assembled positive effective density after removal of the single displayed cubic potential. It retains the other classical terms, the Haar density, the coordinate Jacobian, the assembled effective-action contribution, and the retained history factors. This is a comparison of positive integrals; no individual signed polymer activity is treated as a probability density. We assume the finite-volume normalization is positive and finite, as required for this conditional law to exist.

Define the reference law by omitting only that cubic potential:

\[
\mu_Y(d\omega)
=\frac{w_Y(\omega)\mathbf1_{D_Y}(\omega)d\lambda_Y(\omega)}
{\int w_Y\mathbf1_{D_Y}\,d\lambda_Y}.
\tag{NC8}
\]

This reference law need not be Gaussian, log-concave, or a product law. If (NC6) holds, then, for every admitted \(Y\),

\[
e^{-2r_k}\le\frac{d\nu_Y}{d\mu_Y}\le e^{2r_k}.
\tag{NC9}
\]

Set \(\delta_k=e^{2r_k}-1\). For integrable \(f\),

\[
|\nu_Yf-\mu_Yf|
\le\delta_k\,\mu_Y|f-c|
\quad\text{for every constant }c.
\tag{NC10}
\]

For real \(f,g\in L^2(\mu_Y)\),

\[
\boxed{
|\operatorname{Cov}_{\nu_Y}(f,g)
-\operatorname{Cov}_{\mu_Y}(f,g)|
\le(\delta_k+\delta_k^2)
\sqrt{\operatorname{Var}_{\mu_Y}f\,
\operatorname{Var}_{\mu_Y}g}.
}
\tag{NC11}
\]

For every real \(f\in L^2(\mu_Y)\),

\[
e^{-2r_k}\operatorname{Var}_{\mu_Y}f
\le\operatorname{Var}_{\nu_Y}f
\le e^{2r_k}\operatorname{Var}_{\mu_Y}f.
\tag{NC12}
\]

The inequalities are independent of the dimension of the integration space once \(r_k\) is fixed. The actual support cardinality has already been paid for in (NC6).

#### Proof

Let \(r=d\nu_Y/d\mu_Y\). By definition,

\[
r(\omega)=\frac{e^{-\mathcal R_Y(\omega)}}
{\mu_Ye^{-\mathcal R_Y}}.
\]

The numerator and denominator both lie in \([e^{-r_k},e^{r_k}]\), proving (NC9). Moreover \(\mu_Y(r-1)=0\) and \(|r-1|\le\delta_k\). Thus

\[
\nu_Yf-\mu_Yf=\mu_Y[(r-1)(f-c)],
\]

which proves (NC10).

Put \(F=f-\mu_Yf\) and \(G=g-\mu_Yg\). Exact centering gives

\[
\operatorname{Cov}_{\nu_Y}(f,g)
-\operatorname{Cov}_{\mu_Y}(f,g)
=\mu_Y[(r-1)FG]-\nu_YF\,\nu_YG.
\tag{NC13}
\]

Cauchy–Schwarz bounds the first term by
\(\delta_k\|F\|_{2,\mu_Y}\|G\|_{2,\mu_Y}\), and (NC10) bounds the second by the same product times \(\delta_k^2\). This proves (NC11), including the normalization and changed conditional means.

For the upper variance bound, use

\[
\operatorname{Var}_{\nu_Y}f
=\inf_c\nu_Y(f-c)^2
\le\nu_Y(f-\mu_Yf)^2
\le e^{2r_k}\operatorname{Var}_{\mu_Y}f.
\]

Interchanging the measures gives the lower bound. ∎

#### All finite connected orders

The same comparison controls connected finite jets, not only moments. For \(f_1,\ldots,f_m\in L^m(\mu_Y)\), the moment–cumulant formula and telescoping products give

\[
\begin{aligned}
|\kappa_{\nu_Y}(f_1,\ldots,f_m)
-\kappa_{\mu_Y}(f_1,\ldots,f_m)|
&\le\delta_k
\sum_{r=1}^m r!S(m,r)e^{2r_k(r-1)}
\prod_{i=1}^m\|f_i\|_{m,\mu_Y}\\
&\le\delta_k m!(1+e^{2r_k})^{m-1}
\prod_{i=1}^m\|f_i\|_{m,\mu_Y}.
\end{aligned}
\tag{NC14}
\]

Here \(S(m,r)\) are Stirling numbers of the second kind. To verify the first line, for a partition into \(r\) blocks, each moment changes by at most \(\delta_k\) times the corresponding absolute \(\mu_Y\)-moment. The other \(r-1\) moments are bounded by \(e^{2r_k}\) times their \(\mu_Y\)-counterparts. The cumulant coefficient is \((r-1)!\), and there are \(r\) possible changed factors. Generalized Hölder bounds the product of the block absolute moments by \(\prod_i\|f_i\|_m\). Finally,

\[
r!S(m,r)\le m!\binom{m-1}{r-1},
\]

because an ordered set partition can be represented by a permutation and \(r-1\) cut positions. Summing the binomial expression proves the second line. For \(m\ge2\), each \(f_i\) may be replaced by \(f_i-\mu_Yf_i\), by invariance of cumulants under addition of constants.

The comparison survives any common measurable projection or common probability kernel: integrate (NC9) against that kernel. This observation permits marginalizing auxiliary variables without losing the estimate. It does not identify two different RG transports whose kernels themselves change with the conditional law.

#### What has and has not been removed

The theorem is a genuine statement about a portion of the actual nonlinear action on the actual cutoff support. It closes the passage from a pointwise cubic action remainder to a comparison of **normalized** conditional means, covariances, and finite connected jets.

It does not estimate the reference variance in physical units. The reference in (NC8) retains the effective interactions and all cutoffs. It does not compare the full regular law to the Gaussian law in CMP99. It also does not prove that either measure's mark is the exact old-field insertion transported by the observable route ([Yang–Mills existence through observable derivatives](../observable_route.md), §3); the theorem can be applied to that mark once it has been identified, but its reference moments must still be bounded.

### 3. Why the comparison alone cannot be S2

Two independent, centered Rademacher variables \(X,Y\), assigned to sites at an arbitrarily large distance, have zero covariance. Under

\[
\frac{d\nu}{d\mu}=\frac{e^{\varepsilon XY}}{\cosh\varepsilon},
\]

the potential has magnitude at most \(\varepsilon\), while

\[
\operatorname{Cov}_{\nu}(X,Y)=\tanh\varepsilon.
\tag{NC15}
\]

Thus a small global oscillation bound gives a small covariance error with **no** spatial decay. This example is not a model of the Balaban action; it proves the logical inadequacy of that type of bound by itself. The locality of the actual action has to enter quantitatively in a connected marked expansion or a weighted derivative estimate.

In addition, applying (NC9) to a union of many components gives an exponent bounded by \(2\sum_C r_{k,C}\). A global comparison would then pay a volume factor. The route needs an anchored estimate tied to the supports of the two marks, with the components that connect them explicitly controlled.

### 4. The actual hard cutoff has boundary terms

The product cutoff in (NC2) is a hard indicator. It cannot be retained in an integral and simultaneously ignored in integration by parts.

For clarity, fix the exterior/history, eliminate the tree variables, and write the remaining logarithmic coordinates as \(B=(B_b)\). Suppose the resulting positive density is \(w(B)=e^{-S_{\mathrm{eff}}(B)}\), including the Haar and coordinate Jacobians. Let

\[
D_\rho=D_{\mathrm{other}}\cap
\prod_{b\in\mathcal B_0\setminus T_0}\{B_b:|B_b|<\rho\},
\qquad
\mu_\rho(dB)=Z_\rho^{-1}\mathbf1_{D_\rho}w(B)dB.
\tag{NC16}
\]

The actual radius is \(\rho=A_1p_1(g_k)\). The fixed set \(D_{\mathrm{other}}\) retains other constraints, which can create additional boundary terms. The integration-by-parts functional below includes the entire boundary; its \(\chi'\) portion is the sum over bond faces. One can state the identities first for a piecewise smooth domain, smooth norm balls, and a smooth density, and then use an appropriate divergence theorem or regularization when available. The actual source domain's regularity is a separate hypothesis for that use; no global convexity or smoothness is asserted here. A different source norm is not silently replaced by the Hilbert–Schmidt norm.

For one independent coordinate \(i\), define the boundary functional

\[
\mathcal B_i(f)
=\frac1{Z_\rho}\int_{\partial D_\rho}
f(B)w(B)n_i(B)\,d\sigma(B).
\tag{NC17}
\]

The divergence theorem gives

\[
\mu_\rho(\partial_i f)
=\mu_\rho(f\partial_iS_{\mathrm{eff}})+\mathcal B_i(f),
\tag{NC18}
\]

and hence the exact centered formula

\[
\boxed{
\operatorname{Cov}_{\mu_\rho}(f,\partial_iS_{\mathrm{eff}})
=\mu_\rho(\partial_i f)
-\mathcal B_i(f-\mu_\rho f).
}
\tag{NC19}
\]

The right-hand boundary functional is generally nonzero. Even for a Gaussian density, the usual untruncated Gaussian identity acquires this term. In the purely quadratic case \(S_{\mathrm{eff}}=\frac12 B^TQB\), (NC19) reads in vector form

\[
Q\,\operatorname{Cov}_{\mu_\rho}(B,f)
=\mu_\rho(\nabla f)-\mathcal B(f-\mu_\rho f).
\tag{NC20}
\]

This is also consistent with CMP119 p.273, (3.32), where differentiating the cutoff yields a sum with one characteristic factor differentiated in each term.

#### Exact covariance variation at the cutoff

For a common moving radius and a fixed density and fixed marks, the coarea formula gives

\[
\frac{d}{d\rho}\mu_\rho f
=\frac1{Z_\rho}\sum_{b\in\mathcal B_0\setminus T_0}
\int_{\partial_bD_\rho}(f-\mu_\rho f)w\,d\sigma_b,
\tag{NC21}
\]

for almost every \(\rho\), with the appropriate normal-speed factor for a non-Euclidean norm. For Euclidean balls the normal speed is one. Thus

\[
\boxed{
\frac{d}{d\rho}\operatorname{Cov}_{\mu_\rho}(f,g)
=\frac1{Z_\rho}\sum_b
\int_{\partial_bD_\rho}
\left[(f-\mu_\rho f)(g-\mu_\rho g)
-\operatorname{Cov}_{\mu_\rho}(f,g)\right]w\,d\sigma_b.
}
\tag{NC22}
\]

If the marks or density also depend on \(\rho\), their ordinary derivative terms must be added. Formula (NC22) displays the required **normalized, connected, marked boundary estimate**. It cannot be replaced by a bound on an unmarked exterior probability.

There is one geometric face family per independent bond. The count to retain in a crude sum is

\[
\#(\mathcal B_0\setminus T_0)
\le |\mathcal B_0|
\le C(100MR_k)^dn_Y^2
\le CM^dR_k^{d+2}.
\tag{NC23}
\]

Lie-algebra components and boundary surface factors must be handled in the integrand or in the constants; they do not license replacing the multiscale count by a fixed number.

A bound on \(\int_{|B_b|\ge\rho}w\) controls an integral of surface masses over radii by coarea. Without an additional density or trace estimate it does not control a surface mass at the particular prescribed radius, and without a lower normalization estimate it does not control (NC21) or (NC22).

### 5. What the published failed-cutoff estimate actually supplies

LFR II pp.381–383 explicitly treats \(1-\chi'\) from LFR I (1.82). This is not merely a generic Gaussian tail argument. If a bond violates \(|B'(b)|<\delta'_j\), the proof distinguishes a bond in the final axial-gauge annulus from a bond in the interior multiscale determining set. In the first case a plaquette must be large and supplies a Wilson-action penalty. In the second case the argument uses the localized quadratic form and the estimates (1.77), (1.78).

The relevant interior estimate (1.78), printed p.383, is

\[
|B(b)|^2
\le6(d+3)\bigl(100M(L+1)n_Y^{\beta_0}R_j\bigr)^{d+2}
\sum_{p'}|(\partial B)(p')|^2.
\tag{NC24}
\]

Together with (1.77) and \(|B(b)|\ge g_j^{-1}\delta'_j=A_1p_1(g_j)\), it yields the source's failure factor

\[
\begin{aligned}
\exp\!\left\{
-\frac{\gamma_0}{2}
\bigl[6(d+3)(100M(L+1)n_Y^{\beta_0}R_j)^{d+2}\bigr]^{-1}
A_1^2p_1(g_j)^2
\right\}
&<\exp\{-R_j^{-d-5}p_1(g_j)^2\}\\
&\le\exp\{-p_0(g_j)\}.
\end{aligned}
\tag{NC25}
\]

The last comparison uses the source's parameter condition

\[
2p_1-(d+5)r_0>p_0.
\tag{NC26}
\]

The source states that this is the weakest of the small factors arising from the preparatory large-field tests. It is then included in (1.79), an **unnormalized** bound on the positive source-free operation \(T'_k(X)1\), together with all of the volume and sequence-counting factors. The last product in (1.79) contains one factor \(e^{-p_0(g_j)}\) for each relevant component in which a preparatory large field is created. It is not a factor independently supplied for every one of the bonds in \(\mathcal B_0\).

Two distinct issues remain after (NC25):

1. A boundary flux at the exact radius in (NC19) or (NC22) is a surface insertion, not the volume integral bounded in (1.79).
2. A transported old observable is a marked numerator divided by its matching conditional normalization. The source-free bound controls neither that marked ratio nor the covariance of such ratios after histories are averaged.

LFR II p.380, (1.75), already makes the normalization issue visible. Its analytic normalized-operation bound contains \(\sup|F|\) for an inserted function \(F\). Taking a microscopic field's elementary supremum supplies its microscopic power of \(a^{-1}\); it does not produce the required coarse-shell power of \(h^{-1}\).

Thus (NC25) is a real large-field estimate to retain and use. It is not the missing marked conditional covariance theorem.

### 6. The full nonlinear-law estimate still needed

#### What the variational paper proves

CMP102, Proposition 3, pp.285–289, constructs the nonlinear constraint-linearizing map

\[
A=A'-HD(A'),\qquad D(A')=O(\|A'\|^2),
\]

and gives exponential localization of its first functional derivative in (73). Proposition 4, pp.292–293, proves analyticity and the weighted first-derivative bound (97), (98) for the **classical** higher-order action \(V(A')\). Section G, pp.305–309, derives the nonlinear minimizer expansion and the localized derivative estimate (190). These facts can control derivatives of a nonlinear leaf observable evaluated on an admitted minimizer; they are not estimates for a probability distribution.

In particular, (186) on p.308 is a useful small Hessian estimate in the variational weighted regularity norms. Its use is to solve the minimizer's derivative equation by a Neumann series. A norm contraction after composition with the source propagator is not, by itself, a lower Hessian bound for the full normalized density in independent fluctuation coordinates.

#### The actual regular probability law is larger

CMP119 p.272, (3.28), (3.29), contains, in addition to its quadratic reference form, the function \(P^{(k)}(g_k,A)\), the nonlinear constrained/minimizing argument in the effective action \(E_k\), and the hard characteristic function. The other parts of (3.26)–(3.29) retain external-field, boundary, and R terms. The logarithmic density also contains the Haar and coordinate factors absorbed in those constructions.

A route based on weighted Brascamp–Lieb or a Helffer–Sjöstrand identity therefore needs a statement about **this** density, in the actual independent coordinates, of the following kind:

\[
\nabla^2S_{\mathrm{eff}}(B)\succeq\lambda I,
\qquad
\sup_b\sum_c e^{\alpha d(b,c)}
\|\nabla_b\nabla_c V_{\mathrm{eff}}(B)\|
\le\varepsilon,
\tag{NC27}
\]

with \(\varepsilon\) below the relevant reference coercivity margin, uniformly on the actual domain and histories in question. It also needs the appropriate convex-domain/boundary theorem or an explicit control of the boundary terms. Formula (NC27) is a **required verification**, not a result of this note.

CMP119 (2.27)–(2.42), printed pp.259–262, supplies source-free localized analytic activity bounds. Their analyticity can certainly be used to derive derivative estimates inside their declared domains. However, obtaining (NC27) still requires keeping the radius available in each independent variable, the constraint/minimizer chain rule, all scale weights, the activity sum anchored at a bond, and all Jacobian and boundary terms. No such complete uniform calculation has been supplied for the actual multiscale conditional law here. It would be incorrect to infer (NC27) just from the word “analytic,” or to assert that published analyticity is categorically incapable of supporting such a calculation.

The primary paper CMP116, *Renormalization Group Approach to Lattice Gauge Field Theories II*, Lemma 2, printed pp.10–11, gives a finer decomposition of the regular small-field action into localized quadratic-coefficient terms and a bounded analytic remainder. Its Sect.2 explicitly expands the hard cutoff by subsets of bonds before its cluster expansion. This is a promising starting point for the regular-sector calculation. It does not itself identify the two exact old-observable insertions of this route, and its formulas must not be imported with guessed constants when the scan or the stated domains have not been checked.

### 7. The precise insertion interface

The exact transported old field has the structural form

\[
F_{k,n}(x;Y)
=\frac{\mathcal N_{k,n}[F_{0,n}(x)](Y)}
{\mathcal N_{k,n}[1](Y)},
\tag{NC28}
\]

with the same positive operation in numerator and denominator, including any retained histories and R operations. A fresh minimizing field \(Q^{\mathrm{leaf}}(U_k(Y);x)\) has useful deterministic and Gaussian response estimates, but equality between it and (NC28) has not been proved and generally should not be expected. The numerator carries the fluctuations already integrated out.

For an anchored marked cluster approach, the missing input can be made explicit. One needs a source-deformed operation with the **actual** marks,

\[
Z_Y(t,s)=\mathcal N_{k,n}
\left[e^{tF_{0,n}(x)+sF_{0,n}(y)}\right](Y),
\]

or the corresponding already-transported marks at one shell, together with a uniformly convergent expansion whose first and mixed source derivatives have norms tied to \(x\) and \(y\). The derivative

\[
\partial_t\partial_s\log Z_Y(0,0)
\]

is the desired connected object. Connected-cluster algebra removes all terms not joining both roots once the requisite marked expansion and norm bounds exist. A source-free activity norm at \((t,s)=(0,0)\) does not bound those derivatives without the source dependence and its analytic radius or insertion estimates.

The missing physical normalization is substantial: the goal is a shell bound of order \(h^{-8}\), with permitted powers of \(\log(\ell_0/h)\) and a summable spatial profile, for the exact renormalized transported field. The finite microscopic moment estimates and positive transport theorems currently give bounds retaining the microscopic scale. Neither (NC11) nor (NC14) changes that scale on its own.

## Part III. Actual R transport, history means and the terminal obligation

### 1. Finding

The actual normalized R replacement identifies a specific remaining quantity: the **marked numerator divided by the old unmarked numerator**, on each positive carrier. Neither the fresh minimizing field nor the new small-field Wilson action is that quantity. A coupling derivative of the source-free transformed density contains an additional reset-kernel score, which cancels precisely the fresh-field term in the simplest case.

The source-free R bounds and normalization therefore do not complete the terminal estimate. They bound carrier weights and fresh-field distributions; the terminal estimate still requires the local old-insertion response of those weights, with one common deterministic subtraction and the prescribed running normalization. This note gives the exact formulas, a sufficient terminal criterion, and counterexamples to the proposed shortcuts. These counterexamples concern logical inferences, not Yang–Mills measures.

The supplied sources do support the exact reset identification below. It is more specific than an abstract conditional-expectation rewrite, but it is not a new physical-scale moment estimate.

### 2. Source identification

In Balaban, LFR I, pp.192–194, (1.73)–(1.76), the domain \(\Lambda\) is a rectangular parallelepiped contained in a cube of side \(100M\), in the current scale. The operation first integrates **all old field variables on \(\Lambda\)**. The paper expressly says the resulting function is independent of those interior variables, and that equality of densities has been replaced by equality of their integrals.

At p.201, (1.100), the corresponding fresh-field factor is

\[
r_{\gamma}(v\mid b)
=\frac{q_{\gamma}(v;b)}{\mathcal Z_{\gamma}(b)},\qquad
q_{\gamma}(v;b)
=\delta_{G_\gamma}(v)\,\chi_{\gamma}(v)
\exp\{-g_k^{-2}A(\zeta_\gamma,
 U_{k,X_\gamma}(vV_{\Lambda_\gamma}(b)))\},
\tag{R1}
\]

\[
\mathcal Z_{\gamma}(b)=\int q_{\gamma}(v;b)\,dv.
\tag{R2}
\]

Here \(b\) denotes the unintegrated exterior data. The label \(\gamma\) includes the admitted domain sequence, graph and symmetry averaging choices, and other positive history information. The gauge delta in (R1) denotes integration on the corresponding gauge-fixed slice; it is not a Lebesgue density to be differentiated as an ordinary function. Formula (1.101) fixes the fresh interior cutoff. Products of the normalized factors in (R1) treat disjoint reset components. Formula (1.102) is the integral-preservation identity.

This representation must be used before the later signed polymer expansion. It does not turn each signed activity into a probability.

LFR II, p.358, (1.10)–(1.11), identifies the denominator more precisely:

\[
\mathcal Z_\gamma^{-1}
=\exp\{g_k^{-2}A(\zeta_0,U_0)+I(U_0)-E_k(\Lambda)\},
\qquad |I(U_0)|\le C\log M\,|\Lambda|\le CM^5.
\tag{R3}
\]

The paper proves the relevant analytic continuation in the **background field** and a lower bound for the remaining rescaled integral. It emphasizes that the large Wilson term in (R3) must cancel the corresponding term from the old numerator. This is not a bound for a source derivative of that numerator.

The effective action in CMP119, pp.258–259, (2.23), contains the Wilson term, regular terms, R terms, boundary terms and vacuum scalar. Its subsequent analyticity statements concern the stated background variables. A uniformly small effective coupling and bounded source-free terms do not, by themselves, provide uniform derivatives with respect to a local observable source or the initial coupling.

### 3. Exact old-mark transport through the actual reset

First fix the reset geometry, so that all carriers being summed use the same interior/exterior decomposition \((u,b)\). The carriers may still differ in their earlier history, gauge or symmetry choices. Within this sector write the old unnormalized density as

\[
\rho(u,b)=\sum_\gamma\rho_\gamma(u,b),\qquad
N_\gamma(b)=\int\rho_\gamma(u,b)\,du.
\tag{R4}
\]

All earlier positive integrations can be included in \(u\); the exterior restrictions and the carrier indicators belong in \(\rho_\gamma\). Sums below may equally be integrals over a carrier space. Zero-mass carriers are omitted. Within the fixed-geometry sector let \(Z=\sum_\gamma\int N_\gamma(b)\,db\). The extension to arbitrary geometries and the full partition function is given below; the fixed-geometry sector is not asserted to have full-law mass one.

The actual reset gives

\[
\rho'(v,b)=\sum_\gamma N_\gamma(b)r_\gamma(v\mid b).
\tag{R5}
\]

The positive joint law is

\[
d\Pi(\gamma,u,v,b)
=Z^{-1}\rho_\gamma(u,b)\,du\,db\,
r_\gamma(v\mid b)\,dv.
\tag{R6}
\]

Thus, conditional on \((\gamma,b)\), the old interior and the fresh interior are independent. For an old insertion \(F\), define

\[
N_\gamma^F(b)=\int\rho_\gamma(u,b)F(u,b)\,du,
\qquad f_\gamma^F(b)=N_\gamma^F(b)/N_\gamma(b).
\tag{R7}
\]

The transport into a state retaining the carrier is exactly

\[
Q_R F(\gamma,v,b)=f_\gamma^F(b).
\tag{R8}
\]

In particular, it is independent of the freshly sampled interior \(v\). If the carrier is marginalized in the physical output state, put

\[
w_\gamma(v,b)
=\frac{N_\gamma(b)r_\gamma(v\mid b)}{\rho'(v,b)}.
\tag{R9}
\]

Then

\[
\boxed{\quad Q_R F(v,b)
=\sum_\gamma w_\gamma(v,b)f_\gamma^F(b).\quad}
\tag{R10}
\]

**Consequence.** A bound for a fresh coarse plaquette observable, or for the curvature of \(U_{k,X}(vV_\Lambda)\), is not yet a bound for (R8) or (R10). Such a bound becomes applicable only after proving a marked comparison between \(f_\gamma^F\) and the proposed coarse local operator.

#### 3.1 The history-mean covariance that survives

Let \(\nu_{\gamma,b}(du)=\rho_\gamma(u,b)du/N_\gamma(b)\). For the physical output state, the one-step conditional covariance is

\[
\operatorname{Cov}(F,G\mid v,b)
=\sum_\gamma w_\gamma(v,b)
\operatorname{Cov}_{\nu_{\gamma,b}}(F,G)
+\operatorname{Cov}_{w(v,b)}
   (f_\gamma^F(b),f_\gamma^G(b)).
\tag{R11}
\]

The second term is exactly

\[
\frac12\sum_{\gamma,\delta}w_\gamma w_\delta
(f_\gamma^F-f_\delta^F)(f_\gamma^G-f_\delta^G).
\tag{R12}
\]

This formula states the missing estimate in terms of **differences of marked numerator ratios**, rather than an unspecified extra cumulant. Small positive carrier weights alone do not bound these differences.

At the terminal scale, if the carrier is retained, the transported mark is \(f_\gamma^F(b)\) and its covariance is taken under

\[
d\nu_K(\gamma,b)=Z^{-1}N_\gamma(b)\,db.
\tag{R13}
\]

Marginalizing carriers can reduce the variance but does not automatically make it bounded. An argument must specify the terminal sigma-algebra: keeping a fine-scale selector is additional information, and can retain an ultraviolet-sized conditional mean even when the coarse-field marginal has bounded local variables.

Identity subtractions may be common deterministic constants. Subtracting a different conditional mean on every history removes (R12) artificially and changes the covariance being estimated.

#### 3.2 Different reset geometries in the same full law

When the reset domain depends on \(\gamma\), there is generally **no common exterior coordinate \(b\)**. Let \(V\) denote the full output field and write \(b_\gamma(V)\) for its unchanged exterior projection and \(v_\gamma(V)\) for its reset interior coordinates. Define

\[
a_\gamma(V)=N_\gamma(b_\gamma(V))
r_\gamma(v_\gamma(V)\mid b_\gamma(V)),
\qquad \rho'(V)=\sum_\gamma a_\gamma(V).
\tag{R13a}
\]

Coordinate Jacobians and the stipulated gauge-slice measures are included in these expressions. More generally these equations are identities of positive measures; posterior weights are Radon–Nikodym derivatives with respect to their positive sum, without requiring a common Lebesgue density for different gauge slices. Set

\[
w_\gamma(V)=a_\gamma(V)/\rho'(V),\qquad
\widetilde f_\gamma^F(V)=f_\gamma^F(b_\gamma(V)).
\tag{R13b}
\]

The global version of (R10) is
\(Q_RF(V)=\sum_\gamma w_\gamma(V)\widetilde f_\gamma^F(V)\).
Equations (R11)–(R12) remain valid with conditioning on \(V\), with the conditional old-interior law appropriate to each \(\gamma\), and with \(f_\gamma\) replaced by \(\widetilde f_\gamma(V)\). This applies the ordinary mixture identity to one actual global output field; it never compares carriers at an artificially shared exterior configuration.

For a terminal state retaining the carrier, (R13) is interpreted on the **disjoint union** of the carrier-specific exterior spaces, using the full partition function
\(Z=\sum_\gamma\int N_\gamma(b_\gamma)\,db_\gamma\).
All subsequent summed marked estimates, including (R21) and (R31), have that same disjoint-union interpretation. Their identity subtraction is common across this entire union.

### 4. The source derivative and the missing kernel score

The distinction can be proved without any asymptotic argument. Suppose a parameter \(\theta\) changes both an input density \(\rho_\theta(x)\) and a forward probability kernel \(P_\theta(x,dy)\). Assume a common dominating measure, positive differentiable densities and sufficient local domination for the displayed derivative. Let \(p_\theta(y\mid x)\) be the kernel density and

\[
\rho'_\theta(y)=\int p_\theta(y\mid x)\rho_\theta(x)\,dx.
\]

Then

\[
\boxed{\quad
\partial_\theta\log\rho'_\theta(y)
=Q_\theta[\partial_\theta\log\rho_\theta](y)
+\mathbb E_\theta[
 \partial_\theta\log p_\theta(Y\mid X)\mid Y=y].
\quad}
\tag{R14}
\]

**Proof.** Differentiate the integral, write each derivative as its density times its logarithmic derivative, and divide by \(\rho'_\theta(y)\). Both resulting normalized integrals are expectations under the same conditional input law. \(\square\)

For a normalized input/output family, the same equation holds with normalized density scores. The kernel-score correction has total expectation zero because the forward kernel integrates to one; it need not be pointwise zero or have a uniform local \(L^2\) bound.

In the simplest reset kernel, hold the domain, cutoff and minimizing map fixed, set \(\lambda=g_k^{-2}\), and write \(r_\lambda(v\mid b)\propto e^{-\lambda A(v;b)}\). Then

\[
\partial_\lambda\log r_\lambda(v\mid b)
=-A(v;b)+\mathbb E_{r_\lambda(\cdot\mid b)}A.
\tag{R15}
\]

If the old density has score \(-S\), (R14) gives

\[
Q_\lambda S
=-\partial_\lambda\log\rho'_\lambda
+\mathbb E[\partial_\lambda\log p_\lambda\mid\text{output}].
\tag{R16}
\]

On a fixed retained carrier, \(\rho'_\lambda=N_{\gamma,\lambda}r_{\gamma,\lambda}\). Substituting (R15) in (R16) cancels the fresh action and gives

\[
Q_\lambda S=-\partial_\lambda\log N_{\gamma,\lambda}.
\tag{R17}
\]

This is exactly the old marked numerator ratio. It is not the fresh Wilson action.

For the actual coupling-varying construction there are further responses from the coordinate map, the running parameters, the carrier decomposition, and the cutoffs. A hard-cutoff family need not admit the dominated smooth score assumed in (R14); boundary derivatives require their own control. Formula (R14) is therefore a valid smooth-case identity and an explicit warning against silently differentiating the actual hard-cutoff construction. It does not assert those derivatives already exist.

#### 4.1 What a constant scalar source does and does not give

For the normalization fixed in [Positive transport, nonlinear remainders and admissible curvature](positive_transport.md),

\[
a^4\sum_zq_W(z)=2S,
\qquad \rho_\beta=e^{-\beta S}.
\]

Consequently a constant source \(t\) for \(q_W\) changes \(\beta\) to \(\beta-2t\), up to the deterministic identity subtraction. A frozen scalar normalization \(z_a\) changes this to \(\beta-2z_at\).

In the source convention \(g_{B,0}^{-2}=N\beta\), the corresponding shift is \(g_{B,0}^{-2}\mapsto g_{B,0}^{-2}-2Nz_at\). A derivative of a later Wilson coefficient therefore carries the derivative of the running coupling map as well; keeping all running couplings small does not bound that derivative.

This identity identifies the **spatial integral** of the trace. It does not identify each local insertion at terminal scale. A derivative of the homogeneous source-free effective density would also need (R14)'s kernel response, derivatives of the running parameters, and the chosen normalization. Local terminal covariance requires local marked information; it cannot be deduced merely from a bound on the sum over all insertion positions.

#### 4.2 The legitimate frozen-source formula

For an individual mark at \(x\), keep the actual source-free kernels and carriers fixed and set

\[
N_{\gamma,t_x}(b)
=\int\rho_\gamma(u,b)e^{t_xF_x(u,b)}\,du.
\tag{R18}
\]

Then

\[
\left.\partial_{t_x}\log N_{\gamma,t_x}(b)\right|_{0}
=f_\gamma^{F_x}(b).
\tag{R19}
\]

For fixed cutoff the marks are bounded, so this derivative exists; formal finite jets also suffice. This is the local derivative that needs the physical-scale estimate. The supplied source-free theorem has not established a uniform bound for it.

### 5. A sharp terminal criterion and what finite coarse volume supplies

Write \(T_KF_x\) for the actual renormalized mark in the actual terminal state. The required terminal covariance follows from

\[
\sup_{n,T,x}\|T_KF_x-\mathbb E(T_KF_x)\|_{L^2(\mu_K)}
\le C\ell_0^{-4}.
\tag{R20}
\]

If the final reset carrier is retained, a concrete sufficient condition, in the preceding notation, is the existence of **deterministic** \(c_{a,x}\) such that

\[
\frac1Z\sum_\gamma\int N_\gamma(b)
|f_\gamma^{F_x}(b)-c_{a,x}|^2\,db
\le C^2\ell_0^{-8}.
\tag{R21}
\]

If only the physical output is retained, its exact necessary-and-sufficient version replaces \(f_\gamma\) inside the squared norm by (R10), and centers by the actual expectation. Condition (R21) is stronger and sufficient in that case.

There is a particularly simple way an established operator-matching theorem would finish this step. Suppose it proves

\[
T_KF_x=c_{a,x}+\sum_{\alpha=1}^{21}
z_{a,K,\alpha}Q^W_{\alpha,\ell_0}(x)+\mathcal E_{a,K,x},
\tag{R22}
\]

with

\[
\sup_a\sum_\alpha|z_{a,K,\alpha}|\le Z_*,\qquad
\sup_{a,T,x}\|\mathcal E_{a,K,x}\|_2\le E_*\ell_0^{-4}.
\tag{R23}
\]

The already proved compact-group inequality
\(|Q^W_{\alpha,\ell_0}|\le24N\ell_0^{-4}\) gives

\[
\|T_KF_x-\mathbb ET_KF_x\|_2
\le(24NZ_*+E_*)\ell_0^{-4},
\]

\[
|\operatorname{Cov}_{\mu_K}(T_KF_x,T_KF_y)|
\le(24NZ_*+E_*)^2\ell_0^{-8}.
\tag{R24}
\]

**Proof.** Centering is an orthogonal projection in \(L^2\), so it is contractive. Apply the triangle inequality to (R22), then Cauchy–Schwarz to the covariance. No factor depending on the total torus volume appears. \(\square\)

Thus compact local variables and finite physical block size would finish the terminal estimate **after** (R22)–(R23). They do not prove that representation, its running coefficients, or its remainder estimate. Source-free stability alone does not give any of them.

### 6. Counterexamples to the shortcuts

These finite probability models are not Yang–Mills counterexamples. They show exactly which inference is unavailable from the proposed abstract hypotheses.

#### 6.1 A constant smooth output density can have an arbitrarily large transported mark

Take \(U,V\in\{-1,1\}\), fix \(0<c\le1/4\), and let

\[
\mu_\theta(u)=\frac{e^{\theta A u}}{2\cosh(\theta A)},
\quad m_\theta=\tanh(\theta A),\quad
P_\theta(v\mid u)=\frac{1+c v(u-m_\theta)}2.
\tag{R25}
\]

This is a strictly positive smooth Markov kernel for every \(A>0\) and every real \(\theta\), since \(|u-m_\theta|\le2\). Its output law is the uniform measure on \(\{-1,1\}\) for every \(\theta\). In particular every output density derivative vanishes and the output state space is fixed and compact.

At \(\theta=0\), for \(F_A(u)=Au\),

\[
Q_0F_A(v)=Acv,
\qquad\operatorname{Var}(Q_0F_A)=A^2c^2.
\tag{R26}
\]

The kernel-score term in (R14) is \(-Acv\), cancelling the input-score contribution \(Acv\). Letting \(A\to\infty\) proves that source-free terminal stability, smoothness and compactness do not bound the transported mark. If a nonnegative microscopic observable is desired, replace \(Au\) by \(A(1+u)\); its centered transported variance is unchanged.

#### 6.2 Pure reset plus a retained selector can retain all of a large conditional mean

Let \(U\in\{-1,1\}\) be uniform, let the carrier be \(\gamma=U\), and independently reset \(V\) to a fresh uniform sign. The physical output \(V\) contains no information about \(U\). The extended output \((V,\gamma)\) does. For \(F_A=AU\),

\[
\mathbb E[F_A\mid V]=0,
\qquad\mathbb E[F_A\mid V,\gamma]=A\gamma.
\tag{R27}
\]

Therefore the terminal variance is zero for the first sigma-algebra and \(A^2\) for the second. This is why the terminal state must be specified and why history means cannot be discarded.

#### 6.3 Integrated source information does not bound local covariance

On a two-site periodic system let \(V\) be a uniform sign and set \(F_1=AV\), \(F_2=-AV\). Translation by one site acts by \(V\mapsto-V\), so the law is translation invariant. The sum \(F_1+F_2\) vanishes identically, but both local variances equal \(A^2\). Adding a common constant makes the uncentered observables nonnegative. Thus a homogeneous coupling derivative, even when exactly identified, cannot replace local marked estimates.

### 7. What the actual R penalty controls

The actual hard-cutoff failure mechanism appears in LFR II, pp.377–383. In particular p.383 derives, after the stated exponent inequalities, a factor

\[
e^{-p_0(g_j)},\qquad
p_0(g)=A_0(\log g^{-2})^{p_0},
\tag{R28}
\]

inside the source-free, unnormalized bound for the large-field operation. It is a real source result, not an assumed Wilson microscopic event. But it is still a bound for an unmarked integral. It is not itself \(\mathbb P(\text{that carrier})\), because normalized probabilities include the full remaining partition sum, and it does not bound (R7).

Even granting a normalized event estimate \(\mathbb P(B)\le e^{-\kappa}\), a fourth-moment bound only at the microscopic scale gives at best

\[
\mathbb E[\mathbf1_B|F_a|^2]
\le\|F_a\|_4^2\,\mathbb P(B)^{1/2}
\le C a^{-8}L_a\,e^{-\kappa/2},
\tag{R29}
\]

where \(L_a\) denotes the already tracked microscopic coupling/logarithmic factors. To turn this into \(C h^{-8}\) by this estimate alone requires

\[
\kappa\ge16\log(h/a)+2\log L_a-O(1).
\tag{R30}
\]

For \(g_a^{-2}\asymp\log(1/a)\) and a fixed finite exponent in (R28),
\(p_0(g_a)=O((\log\log(1/a))^{p_0})=o(\log(1/a))\).
That bare rare-event factor cannot absorb arbitrarily many powers of \(h/a\) by (R29). This is not a failure of the R construction. It says that its scale-by-scale renormalization and marked response estimates are essential. LFR I's introduction, p.175, already explains the analogous source-free difficulty: the initial small factor controls only a fraction of the total ultraviolet steps.

For the actual R-history bound, the missing object is a normalized marked estimate such as

\[
Z^{-1}\sum_\gamma\int
\mathbf1_{\mathcal B_\gamma}\,N_\gamma(b)
|f_\gamma^{F_x}(b)-c_{a,x}|^2\,db
\le C h^{-8}\,\text{(the required carrier/profile factor)},
\tag{R31}
\]

together with the cross-history oscillations in (R12), or a directly proved bound on their marginalized combination. A bound on \(T'_k(X)1\) is the degree-zero version of this requirement and does not imply (R31).

## Part IV. Summing the actual localization profile

The previous shell lemma covered a pure exponential in \(r/h\). The source geometry has an additional scale-dependent radius. That loss can be summed explicitly; it need not be suppressed in the statement of S2.

### Theorem P.1 — geometric shells with logarithmic and iterated-logarithmic radii

Let \(h_j=\ell_0L^{-j}\), \(L>1\), and define
\[
 t(h)=1+\log(\ell_0/h),\qquad w(t)=1+\log t.
\]
Fix \(s>0\) and \(b,d,q,p\ge0\). Suppose a radius satisfies
\[
 1\le R(h)\le C_R t(h)^q w(t(h))^p.
\]
Then, for \(0<r\le\ell_0\),
\[
 \sum_{j\ge0}h_j^{-s}t(h_j)^b w(t(h_j))^d
 \exp\!\left[-\frac{cr}{h_jR(h_j)}\right]
 \le C r^{-s}t(r)^{b+sq}w(t(r))^{d+sp}.
 \tag{P1}
\]
The constant depends on the displayed fixed parameters, but not on a lower cutoff, the number of shells, or the torus volume. A finite subset of the shells satisfies the same bound.

**Proof.** Put \(T=t(r)\ge1\), \(W=w(T)\), and \(A=T^qW^p\). Choose a geometric scale \(h_*\) such that
\[
 \frac{r}{LA}\le h_*\le\frac rA.
\]
It exists because \(r/A\le\ell_0\). The definitions imply
\[
 t(h_*)\le T+\log L+q\log T+p\log W\le C_0T,
 \qquad w(t(h_*))\le C_1W.
\]
For coarser scales \(h=L^m h_*\le\ell_0\), discard the exponential. Both logarithmic factors are bounded by their values at \(h_*\), while \(h^{-s}=h_*^{-s}L^{-sm}\). Their sum is at most
\[
 C r^{-s}A^sT^bW^d.
\]
For finer scales \(h=h_*L^{-m}\), \(m\ge1\),
\[
 t(h)\le C_2T(1+m),\qquad
 w(t(h))\le C_3W(1+m).
\]
Consequently
\[
 R(h)\le C_4A(1+m)^{q+p},\qquad
 \frac r{hR(h)}\ge c_0\frac{L^m}{(1+m)^{q+p}}.
\]
The fine-shell sum is bounded by
\[
 C r^{-s}A^sT^bW^d
 \sum_{m\ge1}L^{sm}(1+m)^{b+d}
 \exp\!\left[-c_1\frac{L^m}{(1+m)^{q+p}}\right].
\]
The numerical series converges: its negative exponential dominates every fixed exponential and polynomial in \(m\). Adding both estimates proves (P1). \(\square\)

### Application to the radius in the supplied source

On printed p.255, (2.5), CMP119 defines \(R_j\) to be the smallest power of \(L\) satisfying
\[
 R_j\ge(\log g_j^{-2})^{\mathfrak r}.
\]
Thus \(R_j<L(\log g_j^{-2})^{\mathfrak r}\), up to an immaterial lower bound by one. If the admitted trajectory supplies
\(g_h^{-2}\le C\,t(h)\), then
\[
 R_h\le C' w(t(h))^{\mathfrak r}.
\]
This uses the specified trajectory bound as a hypothesis; it does not prove the missing standalone N2-F input. Fixed geometric factors such as \(M,L,M_2\) are absorbed into \(C_R\).

An actual shell estimate of the form
\[
 \mathbb E|D_h(x,y)|\le
 B h^{-8}t(h)^b w(t(h))^d
 \exp\!\left[-\frac{c|x-y|}{hR_h}\right]
\]
would therefore sum to
\[
 C|x-y|^{-8}t(|x-y|)^b
 w(t(|x-y|))^{d+8\mathfrak r}.
 \tag{P2}
\]
For a larger bound \(R_h\le Ct(h)^q\), the loss instead is \(t(r)^{8q}\). More generally, a fixed polynomial prefactor \((1+r/(hR_h))^v\) can be absorbed by decreasing the exponential rate, since \((1+u)^v e^{-cu}\le C_{v,c}e^{-cu/2}\).

**What is closed.** The deterministic summation for these radius profiles is proved. **What is not closed.** No actual-history estimate for \(D_h\) is inferred from a radius definition. The bound must first be proved with the same normalized transported insertion on every relevant carrier.

## Part V. The exact two-insertion cluster formula

CMP119 already contains a normalized one-insertion construction: printed pp.277–278, (3.44)–(3.47), separates the distinguished numerator polymer from the denominator and cancels denominator clusters not linked to it. Its inserted functions there are the particular localized expressions generated in the source-free effective-action calculation. That scope does not automatically include the transported physical scalar.

The following algebra makes the additional two-insertion obligation precise without requiring a cutoff-uniform finite-source analytic function.

### Proposition C.1 — two labeled insertions in a polymer expansion

Let a finite polymer gas have activities \(H(X)\) and incompatibility factor \(f(X,Y)\), equal to \(-1\) for incompatible polymers and zero otherwise. Put
\[
 H_{s,t}(X)=H(X)+sH_x(X)+tH_y(X)+stH_{xy}(X),
 \qquad s^2=t^2=0.
\]
For the ordered-list convention,
\[
 Z(s,t)=\sum_{n\ge0}\frac1{n!}\sum_{X_1,\ldots,X_n}
 \prod_{i<j}(1+f(X_i,X_j))\prod_iH_{s,t}(X_i).
\]
Let
\[
 \varphi^T(X_1,\ldots,X_n)
 =\sum_{G\text{ connected on }\{1,\ldots,n\}}
   \prod_{\{i,j\}\in E(G)}f(X_i,X_j),
 \qquad \varphi^T(X_1)=1.
\]
Whenever the unmarked and marked cluster sums are absolutely convergent, the coefficient of \(st\) in \(\log Z\) is
\[
 \begin{aligned}
 [st]\log Z={}&
 \sum_{n\ge1}\frac1{(n-1)!}\sum_{X_1,\ldots,X_n}
 \varphi^T(X_1,\ldots,X_n) H_{xy}(X_1)
       \prod_{i=2}^{n}H(X_i)\\
 &+\sum_{n\ge2}\frac1{(n-2)!}\sum_{X_1,\ldots,X_n}
 \varphi^T(X_1,\ldots,X_n)H_x(X_1)H_y(X_2)
       \prod_{i=3}^{n}H(X_i).
 \end{aligned}
 \tag{C1}
\]
At finite volume the identity is also an identity of formal activity series. To identify its infinite activity sum with the actual numerical covariance, the stated convergence is required.

**Proof.** Expand each compatibility product into graphs. Every graph decomposes uniquely into connected components, and its activity product factors over those components. The exponential formula therefore identifies \(\log Z\) with the connected-graph sum. At degree \(st\), either both labels occur in one activity or they occur in two distinct activities. There are respectively \(n\) and \(n(n-1)\) ordered choices. Multiplying these counts by \(1/n!\) yields the two factorials in (C1). Absolute convergence permits the rearrangements at the numerical activities. \(\square\)

If this gas is an exact localized representation of
\[
 Z(s,t)=\int \rho(\omega)(1+sF_x(\omega))(1+tF_y(\omega))\,d\omega,
\]
then
\[
 [st]\log Z=\operatorname{Cov}_{\rho/Z(0,0)}(F_x,F_y).
\]
In particular, \(H_{xy}\) cannot be deleted: two labels can enter one localized integral, including at separated observation points. Nilpotent labels remove any demand for a cutoff-uniform radius in \(s,t\); they do not remove the need to estimate the displayed coefficients.

If the exact localized representation has a scalar prefactor \(e^{E(s,t)}\) outside the gas, then \([st]E\) must be added to (C1). A source-independent vacuum scalar, or a scalar linear in the two labels, contributes nothing at degree \(st\). A mixed source coefficient cannot be discarded on that basis.

### The spatial estimate required of the marked sums

Suppose the localization domains of a nonzero \(H_x\) contain a prescribed anchor region \(A_x\), and similarly for \(H_y\); \(H_{xy}\) contains both. For a local input mark, its original support can be used as that anchor. A transported mark needs a proved anchored expansion: its generally enlarged functional dependence is not silently declared to have microscopic support. Equip domains with an additive length \(\mathcal L\) whose sum along a connected incompatibility cluster dominates the distance between the anchors, divided by the actual localization scale \(hR_h\). For ordinary connected block polymers one may take their total number of blocks, with fixed geometric constants.

If the sum of the two absolute weighted series from (C1), after multiplication of each summand by \(\exp(\lambda\sum_i\mathcal L(X_i))\), is at most \(A_h\), then
\[
 |\operatorname{Cov}(F_x,F_y)|
 \le A_h\exp\!\left[-c\lambda\,
 \frac{\operatorname{dist}(A_x,A_y)}{hR_h}\right].
 \tag{C2}
\]
Indeed, connectedness supplies the required length in every nonzero summand; extract its exponential before summing absolute values. If the chosen anchors have physical radius \(O(a)\) and \(h\ge a\), changing their distance to the distance between centers only changes the fixed constant. Applying this observation to an exact transported mark requires the anchored expansion just specified.

This formulation identifies the missing analytic work, rather than hiding it in a cancellation: prove the two weighted marked sums for the **actual** transported old numerator, including the mixed activity, under its normalized conditional law. Source-free activity bounds apply to the factors \(H\). They do not by themselves give the physical \(h^{-4}\) size of \(H_x,H_y\), the \(h^{-8}\) size of \(H_{xy}\), or the corresponding R-history estimates.

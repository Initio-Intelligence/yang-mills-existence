### 2. The remaining normalization issues are now narrower

#### 2.1 Bounded scalar seeds versus normalized tensor point seeds

The actual regular phase recursion has the all-order bound

\[
\operatorname{Av}_z\|f_{k,z}\|^m
\le C^{m+1}(m!)^{d_{\mathrm{ph}}}\|f_0\|^m.
\tag{V9.1}
\]

The scalar and pseudoscalar mean normalizers have bounded inverses, so their commonly normalized microscopic seeds satisfy the hypothesis. The full 21-dimensional phase recursion is used in this conclusion: scalar phase noise may enter anisotropic directions, and the mean metric controls those directions.

Stress and Weyl inverse mean factors can instead grow with the inverse-coupling ratio with powers \(1\) and \(18/11\). Applying (V9.1) to such a seed retains those powers on its right-hand side. Dissipativity of the unnormalized mean map does not erase them. S8's general matrix counterexample explains why that issue is substantive.

There are two honest routes forward. One is the full point-source endpoint correction SP37 in the actual marginal/stable space. The other is to build the complete smeared source hierarchy directly from T9-G's density lift, where the physical mesh factor multiplying a source derivative pays inverse-normalizer growth. The second route already works for one smooth source in the regular reference. To use it for M one must prove it for all generated multi-source responses and collision terms, with one linear Schwartz-order budget and one factorial exponent.

#### 2.2 Actual forcing is not an independent new phase sample

The rare regular action correction, a birth term, or a reset mark can depend on the blocking environment and later digits. Its phase word cannot be declared independent of the source term. The established deterministic N7 arbitrary-profile propagator remains available:

\[
\|\mathscr L_{j,k}\|
\le C\left(\frac{x_j}{x_k}\right)^{p_{\mathrm b}}.
\tag{V9.2}
\]

Apply it to the actual forcing, together with its maturity, rare-factor, or source-gradient estimate. For pure regular comparisons A8 and N7 already supply these inputs on their common chart. Extension to a mixed boundary requires admission of that same boundary-dependent potential; it cannot be obtained by changing a retained function into a scalar vacuum subtraction.

### 3. The next normalized history target

The H7 normalized majorant remains the correct full-history target. Schematically, for anchored connected families of roots and unresolved components, it requires

\[
\frac1Z\sum_{\mathcal C\ \mathrm{anchored}}

The numerator must be constructed from an exact absolute covariance or reveal expansion. It must retain the posterior carrier weights, reset normalizers, source cutoffs, boundary potentials, old fields, and conditional means. An unnormalized polymer activity does not establish (V9.3).

#### 3.2 First remaining branch: outputs outside the strict carrier

#### 3.3 Second remaining branch: a mixed conditional boundary law

RB9's argument can be repeated on a finite regular conditional domain if the actual conditional covariance has the required gap/localization, the complete conditional potential is admitted, and every inherited cutoff has a strict local margin for the same fixed exterior. Thus the next analytic theorem must either establish these three properties with an annealed boundary budget or integrate the bad boundary into the same normalized ancestry expansion. #### 3.4 Third remaining branch: joint histories and conditional means

RB9 conditions on a next coarse output. Such a conditioning is not automatically predictable for a forward multiscale tower. To turn its one-step estimate into an ancestry bound, construct a reveal order in which the relevant conditional estimates apply, or derive a joint normalized expansion that retains all intervening coarse fields. Those terms must enter the exact full decomposition.

### 4. The exact remaining S2 exit

Use the same commonly normalized microscopic scalar field and deterministic subtraction. Through the original source-free positive kernels, put

\[
F_{k+1,z}=Q_kF_{k,z},\qquad
D_{k,z}(x,y)=Q_k(F_{k,z}(x)F_{k,z}(y))
-F_{k+1,z}(x)F_{k+1,z}(y).
\tag{V9.4}
\]

S8-A gives

\[
\begin{aligned}
|\operatorname{Cov}_{\mu_0}(F_0(x),F_0(y))|
\le{}&\sum_{k<K}\operatorname{Av}_z\mathbb E|D_{k,z}(x,y)|\\
&+\left[\operatorname{Av}_z\operatorname{Var}F_{K,z}(x)\right]^{1/2}
\left[\operatorname{Av}_z\operatorname{Var}F_{K,z}(y)\right]^{1/2}.
\end{aligned}
\tag{V9.5}
\]

Only the original microscopic law must be translation invariant. Each translated observable goes through the original kernels. There is no extra covariance from a globally randomized block origin.

The two sufficient inequalities still to be established for the complete law are

\[
\sum_{k<K}\operatorname{Av}_z\mathbb E|D_{k,z}(x,y)|
\le Cr^{-8}\left(1+\log\frac{\ell_0}{r}\right)^b,
\tag{V9.6}
\]

and

\[
\sup_x\operatorname{Av}_z\operatorname{Var}_{\mu_K}F_{K,z}(x)
\le C\ell_0^{-8}.
\tag{V9.7}
\]

N9/P9 remove the regular phase-moment obstruction to these estimates. S7, H8, H9, R9, and RB9 supply specified portions of their history cost. To finish (V9.6), place the regular, protected-core, clean, early-birth, general later-birth, failed-preparation, boundary, and exterior terms in one exact normalized decomposition. To finish (V9.7), bound every remaining old conditional mean and its cross terms. A new reset fluctuation's Gaussian moment does not replace the terminal old field's boundary-dependent mean.

**Neither complete inequality is claimed here.** Consequently full S2 is still open even though its regular normalization subgate is closed.

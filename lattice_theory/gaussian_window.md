This note uses the original one-step Haar readout CMP109 (0.12)/(0.10), its zero-source fixed-cutoff Gaussian endpoint, the calibration and fixed-depth endpoint identification in O0 §§1 and 12, and the compatible weak regular classical charts. No differentiability of a running coupling or independence of past counters is assumed. ## 1. The stationary hard integral with Haar coarea

Take constant real Cartan flux on a compatible period as in T1 7.187, and separately take zero flux. The backgrounds and their native block averages are stationary, including for the composite readout. Use raw right-invariant link coordinates with the same per-link invariant metric and ordinary product site-generator metrics. Let d and d' denote fine and coarse infinitesimal gauge action, Q the derivative of the actual readout, E evaluation of site generators at retained centers. Thus Qd=d'E, E is an orthogonal coordinate projection, and K denotes the isometric inclusion of ker E. Based internal gauge integration is free. T1 and N2 H.1 use the same normalized tree-orbit insertion at the regular saddle. At these fields the equation-of-motion multiplier in the stationary Hessian vanishes, and H d=0. The two stabilizer Lie algebras W=ker d and W'=ker d' consist of parallel sections. Pick the quantized flux of T1 with all nonzero charged plaquette phases nontrivial on the coarse lattice, trivial neutral holonomies up to the designated Cartan, and sufficiently small magnitude there. E maps W isomorphically onto W', including at zero. The calculations below can all be done with finite-dimensional tangent volumes.

Write V=dK and T for an isometric inclusion of ker Q. Up to fixed metric constants independent of the two backgrounds the native one-loop density relative to coarse link Haar is

    D_hard = det(V*V) /
             [det(QQ*) det(T*(H+VV*)T)]^{1/2}.                         (1)

Indeed the hard delta has coarea det(QQ*)^{-1/2}, internal group volume in the kernel gives det(V*V)^{1/2}, and the positive hard physical Hessian is on ker Q orthogonal to V. Introducing VV* in that kernel multiplies its determinant by det(V*V). This proves (1) without slicing the full singular gauge action. Reintroducing the coarse directions by Schur with a constant σ gives

    D_hard = det(V*V) det(H_c+σI)^{1/2} /
             det(H+VV*+σQ*Q)^{1/2}.                                   (2)

Here H_c is the Hessian of the constrained minimum on coarse links. In computing its stationary extension one may shift by V to impose V*a=0. This leaves Qa and the original quadratic form unchanged. The Schur-coordinate coarea is included in (2). With a single blocking step of fixed L, the scalar V*V has an anchored Poincaré gap, and the stabilized vector has a gap on all links: use the native uniform hard gap, the bounded classical extension on coarse data, a bounded local right inverse in the longitudinal subspace and σ larger than the fixed negative part of H_c. These properties are only required in one-step units.

There is a second exact expression appropriate for a whole prefix. Use the full physical quotient in the orthogonal link metrics, let Q̄ be the induced readout there, and H_f,H_c now denote the fine/coarse physical Hessians. The density is

    D_hard = c_vol |det(E:W→W')|
          × [det′(d*d)/det′(d'*d')]^{1/2}
          × [det(H_c+σI)/det(H_f+σ Q̄*Q̄)]^{1/2}.                      (3)

The quotient coarea det(Q̄Q̄*)^{-1/2} cancels the coordinate determinant in the hard-to-soft Schur formula. To see the stabilizer determinant directly, the formal Euclidean density of link space is the physical density times det′(d*d)^{1/2} times the density of site generators divided by the density on W. This is the elementary orthogonal kernel-and-range change of variables for d, with densities interpreted in determinant lines. Divide the fine density by the coarse density and by the density on ker E. The site densities cancel because E is coordinate restriction. The remaining W' density pulled back to W is |det(E|W)| times the W density. This argument uses (1) on the free internal group; it does not postulate a smooth probability density on singular orbit strata. For prefix spacing h=L^{-k} in terminal units all parallel sections preserve their pointwise norm, so |det(E|W)|=h^{2r} in four dimensions, r=dim W. The factor c_vol contains only background-independent original Haar/gauge normalization. If the overall elementary Gaussian constants are retained, their exponent is the fine minus coarse physical dimension; the common r cancels there.

## 2. Removing the cumulative constraint at finite real flux

Use the actual regular tangent extension M from terminal fields to the fine constrained minimum. In raw orthonormal link coordinates,

    ||Q̄|| ≤ C_R h,       ||M|| ≤ C_R/h,       Q̄ M=I.                 (4)

The first bound is the physical readout estimate of N2 I.2. In terminal units, dividing a fine raw link variation by h and taking the h⁴-weighted connection norm gives h times its raw norm.

CMP102 §G, printed pp.305–309, constructs the relative field for the entire pair U_k(V'V₀), U_k(V₀). The base V₀ must satisfy the plaquette bound (7) on the determining set. The smallness in (172) is on the relative input V', so a fundamental-domain seam in a periodic representative of V₀ does not violate this restriction. Both tested V₀ satisfy (7) at sufficiently small q_R. Equations (172)–(174) give a single Landau-gauge relative connection exp(iη H(B)); the transformation back to the axial representative is described just before (181). Proposition 9 identifies the derivative of this same H on the full determining domain with the derivative estimated in (190). In the homogeneous all-terminal case L^jη=L^{j'}η=1 for both determining layers. The normalized potential bound in (190) consequently gives a single global tangent representative h D H with entries at most Ch exp(−c dist_terminal), in terminal units. Orthogonal projection onto the whole fine physical space discards only a gauge tangent, including any derivative of the transformation between representatives. It follows that the second inequality of (4) holds on the repeated compatible tori with a volume-independent constant. Independent local choices of fine gauges need not be assembled.

By (190), the absolute row sum of this representative is O(h) and its column sum is O(h^{-3}); the square root of their product bounds its ℓ² norm by C/h. At zero and the selected real flux the physical hard Hessian on ker Q̄ has lower bound c_R h² by T1 7.188. The coarse H_c has uniform operator norm C_R by O0 §13.2 and the same regular physical extension. Choose σ>C_R+1. Write a=t+Mb with Q̄t=0. The stationary H-orthogonality yields

    (a,(H_f+σQ̄*Q̄)a) ≥ c_R h²||t||²+||b||² ≥ c'_R h²||a||².

T1 7.188 gives a spectral projector P_low of rank ≤C_R such that the stabilized physical vector H_s=H_f+c_R' h²P_low is ≥c_R''h². The difference of the two stabilized matrices has rank ≤C_R and norm ≤C_Rh² by (4). Interpolating the positive matrices and bounding the trace of inverse times this difference gives an O_R(1) difference of log determinants. No comparison with a negative unconditioned Wilson Hessian is used. The coarse factors in (3) have fixed dimension, gap on their positive scalar spectrum, and bounded logarithms, uniformly in k at each of the two explicit coarse fields.

At a stationary field H_f annihilates fine gauge gradients. Add dd* in the longitudinal direction and extend the physical stabilizer by zero there. Denote this full stabilized Wilson Feynman vector by L_s. Its longitudinal determinant is det′Δ, Δ=d*d. Therefore the fine factors of (3) in the log density become

    −½ logdet L_s + logdet′Δ + 2r log h + O_R(1)
      = −½ logdet L_s + logdet(Δ+h²P_W) + O_R(1).                     (5)

The cancellation is pointwise in the two backgrounds, whose stabilizer dimensions need not be equal. The fixed Wilson Hessian normalization is converted to unit Maxwell kinetic coefficient for the T1 Feynman heat operator. A fixed positive metric rescaling of the transverse Hessian and its stabilization changes its log determinant by the dimension times a constant; after flux subtraction only the bounded stabilizer-rank difference can remain. The analogous scalar-gauge normalization has the same property. Formula (5) uses the native affine chart only at the two stationary fields and keeps the full physical coarea. Omitting h^{2r} would create an artificial logarithmic drift.

## 3. Extraction using the native Gaussian activities

Fix one sufficiently large compatible R, an integral regular Cartan generator T, and q_R=2π/R². Use the zero and magnetic fields of T1 7.187 on the same torus, followed by their repetitions on sides mR in terminal units. At each fixed depth k, take m→∞ before varying k. One block step reproduces this homogeneous Cartan curvature up to endpoint gauge, so in earlier scale-t units q_t=q_R L^{-2(k−t)}. N2 H.1 identifies the product of the Gaussian densities including the native coareas with the cumulative hard density. Prior quadratic-curvature terms retain their coefficients under the stationary substitution.

CMP109, printed p.261 following (1.7), expressly includes the separately constructed Gaussian log normalizer in the localized expansion. Its coefficient on X depends on the restriction of (U,J) to X. On pp.262–263, just before (1.18), the smaller bounds (i)–(iii) imply the synthetic-domain condition (iv); (1.18)–(1.19) then give a uniform analytic bound and local gauge invariance. O0 §12.4 uses these bounds for the Gaussian log itself, and (12.13) identifies the exact increment and its subtracted vacuum. 

On a nonwinding carrier of current diameter D choose lifted centered Landau gauge with potential q x₁T in direction 2. Each fine link is its exponential with the actual finer spacing η; the normalized potential is O((1+D)|q|), its first differences O(|q|), and J=0 for each real or complex affine Cartan plaquette field. For |q|≤c/(1+D) these are the original strict native small chart bounds near zero. The compatible minimum for the real uniform flux is the genuine affine fine field up to gauge, so its restriction is the value of this same local function, without constructing a nonquantized flux on the finite torus. Taylor through degree two with local error C(1+D)³|q|³ times the native support weight is justified for D≤|q|^{-1/2} at sufficiently small q. At larger D the real regular field itself is an admitted minimal configuration, as stated immediately before (1.18); its local value and the zero-field Taylor terms have an exponential tail O(exp(−c |q|^{-1/2})), after summing with the spare rooted support rate. Winding carriers vanish per cell at fixed depth and m→∞. These estimates are uniform in t.

For the projection, test the zero-field Hessian with the two direction-2 waves sin(p x₁)/p and cos(p x₁)/p in the direction T. A local gauge removes the constant at each support home. The affine slopes are respectively cos(p x₁) and −sin(p x₁); their squares add to one. On each bounded support the Taylor error tends to zero with p, and the decay of the tangent response and of the supports gives dominated convergence for the full sum, as in O0 (12.8)–(12.10). The affine fine tangent has the uniform Cartan curl by stationary reproduction. Take m→∞ at fixed depth and nonzero wavevector, then p→0. ### 3.1 Heat calculation on the repeated torus

Divide by the terminal volume V=(mR)⁴. The low vector rank in (5) is at most C_R V, from the magnetic form comparison and return bound at s=h^{-2} of T1 7.188. The finite-rank logdet error in §2 is O_R(1) per terminal cell. The massive terminal Hessian has uniformly bounded logarithm per cell, as does the terminal nonzero scalar ghost by the ordinary Fourier integral at zero field and on neutral colors and by the magnetic gap on charged nonzero-flux colors.

For the full stabilized determinants in (5), T1 7.175–176 and 7.189 provide the actual Wilson vector and ghost heat diagonal at metric σ_metric=0, prior to the optional anisotropic derivative. On proper times 1≤s≤h^{-2} the finite-hop Wilson remainders beyond the continuum magnetic coefficient contribute an integrable O(s^{-1/2}) error to the quadratic term per root. The actual finite-flux Taylor remainder of the heat diagonal is bounded by C s^{-2}(|q_R|h²s)³, and the winding terms obey (7.249). Their normalized integrals cost O_R(1) on this window. This remains true when the stabilizers in (5) are inserted: Duhamel on their finite-dimensional spectral subspaces costs C_R s h² exp(C_R s h²) per terminal cell, integrable against ds/s on the window uniformly in h. For s≤1 the flux-minus-zero difference and its short windings obey T1 7.190, and the stabilization costs O_R(h² s). For s≥h^{-2} the stabilized physical vector, the raised scalar zero modes, and the charged longitudinal modes at nonzero flux have gap c_R h². Their trace per terminal cell at s=h^{-2} is O_R(1) by T1 7.188–190. The remaining ordinary scalar tails on tiled boxes are estimated below. This order of limits avoids a thermodynamic derivative of an unstable massless vector at fixed homogeneous curvature.

The commuting sector is included when diagonalizing the magnetic background. Choose a regular integral Cartan generator, so the neutral adjoint sites lie in its Cartan. The Wilson curl form there reduces to the ordinary plaquettewise diagonal Hessian with the 12-plane weight involving cos(h²q_R T). Its relative form change from flat is O_R(h⁴q_R²). Adding the identical divergence square and stabilizing the flat harmonic modes at h² preserves that relative estimate. A dimension bound O((mR/h)⁴) on the resulting logdet change gives O_R(q_R²) per terminal cell. The charged and neutral components must both be included before asserting the time-window sign.

The charged scalar gap at nonzero flux is c_R h² by the magnetic-translation commutator in T1 7.188. Neutral nonconstant longitudinal vector modes have exactly the ordinary scalar spectrum, also valid for every color at zero flux. For these modes the nonzero periodic return trace is bounded by C (mR/h)⁴s^{-2} up to s=(mR/h)² and by C exp(−c s/(mR/h)²) thereafter; these are the direct discrete Fourier sums. Integration on s≥h^{-2}, followed by division by (mR)⁴, gives O(1) independently of m,h. The lifted scalar zero modes and all stabilized physical vector modes have the already stated h² gap. Thus both proper-time tails in (5) are uniform per cell even in the tiling limit.

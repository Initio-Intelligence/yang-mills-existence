# Plaquette insertions through constrained boundary integration

## 1. Physical mark and frozen geometry

The claims below concern the first actual conditional integration B119 (1.26). They apply on the weak interval and domains for the printed B119/B116 expansion. The connection to a prescribed physical running trajectory retains the R0 qualification. This calculation supplies a restricted contribution to the complementary density of (MR.26). Its original plaquette has a link integrated over the *regular constrained fluctuations*. This distinguishes it from the strict-original-group-core calculation.

Fix the first-step geometry, the actual exterior fluctuation coordinates and the background field in B119. Write $\mathcal I$ for the ordinary free bonds integrated over $\Lambda_1$ in (1.26), and $\mathcal O$ for the ordinary bonds retained in $\Omega_1\setminus\Lambda_1$ in the actual bond-set convention. Consider an original fine plaquette $p$ meeting both sets such that

* all four bonds are ordinary free bonds in $\Omega_1^*$, and none is a distinguished $b_0(c)$;
* none of those four bonds is subject to the coordinate switch (1.22) or its constant-switch version on $S_1^*$.

This is a historywise, exact decomposition of the original source. A source value attached to a plaquette that fails either test remains in the complementary part still to be estimated. We use the original positive-link convention and the physical plaquette weight $z_p=j^{(0)}(x_p)$ multiplying $s_p=N-\operatorname{ReTr}U_p$.

The class includes marks at planar faces of the regular domain. On a face between coarse blocks, take a fine plaquette in the normal and one tangential direction. Its two tangential bonds can run inside their respective coarse blocks, one on either side. There is only one distinguished normal crossing bond per coarse bond $c$, and there are $L^3$ possible transverse rows. The transverse coordinates can be chosen to avoid it for both normal crossings. With the admitted large widths such plaquettes occur away from $S_1^*$: before rectangular filling (1.20) places the cut at least a block layer away from $S_1$, and filling enlarges the set excluded from $\Lambda_1$. A simple geometry is an isolated initial large-field core with a planar side of its enclosing rectangular buffer and a nonempty exterior regular region. A fine configuration equal to identity outside a single nontrivial link generates initial large plaquettes if that link is chosen above the strict (1.1) threshold. On a sufficiently large torus its surrounding buffers leave unaffected regular cubes; away from the defect all local coarse boundary values there are identity. The geometric criterion above, rather than a property of a typical history, is the hypothesis of the estimate.

The regular block average and axial background choice are the ones of (1.11)–(1.12). The relation $U=e^{iA'}U_1$ is (1.13). Immediately after (1.14), B119 p.249 states that the change linearizing the group delta functions differs from identity only on $b_0(c)$. The subsequent linear solution $C$ also fixes every ordinary free coordinate. Thus at each of the four **positive** links in this class the exact original field after the coordinate changes is
\[
 W_b=e^{ig_0 A_b}\mathcal U_b , \tag{C.1}
\]
where $\mathcal U=U_1$ at the physical background. Reverse occurrences mean $W_b^{-1}$ in the proper order. Both integrated and retained $A_b$ in this expression are actual surviving first-step coordinates. In particular (C.1) does not use $e^{ig_0 A_b}\mathcal U_b$ as an approximation on distinguished bonds. If (1.22) acts, a factor involving $H_{1,\Lambda_1}$ and its switch must be inserted and this proof does not apply.

## 2. Admission of the localized source before conditional integration

Use $s^{\rm hol}(P)=N-\tfrac12\operatorname{Tr}(P+P^{-1})$. Let $P_p(A,\mathcal U)$ be the ordered plaquette of (C.1). Split its mark as
\[
 c_p=s^{\rm hol}(P_p(A|_{\mathcal O},\mathcal U)),\qquad
 \psi_p=s^{\rm hol}(P_p(A,\mathcal U))-c_p, \tag{C.2}
\]
where precisely the coordinates in $\mathcal I$ are set to zero in the first term. The exterior factor $c_p$ stays in the density and in its field-valued derivatives. It is not a prescribed flat vacuum or a conditional mean.

On the first-step chart (2.34)–(2.39), (3.43), use the displayed representative $\mathcal U=e^{i\eta A'}U_{\rm r}$ with bounded $\eta A'$ and $U_{\rm r}$ unitary. Thus the four background factors and their inverses are bounded. Real retained $A_b$ exponentiate to unitary matrices; their complex small-cutoff extensions also have bounded exponentials if used. On the integrated small-cutoff chart $g_0\max_{b\in\mathcal I\cap p}|A_b|\le\vartheta$, where $\vartheta=\epsilon_1$ in B116 (1.34), or the corresponding $C_1g_0p_1(g_0)$ in B119 (3.43). Subtract the two ordered products by telescoping over the integrated bonds, and likewise their inverse products. The bounds for matrix exponentials give
\[
 |c_p|\le C_N,\qquad |\psi_p|\le C_N g_0 \sum_{b\in p\cap\mathcal I}|A_b|
 \le C'_N\vartheta . \tag{C.3}
\]
The constants are uniform on these chart representatives, in the real retained variables, volume, and coupling on the weak interval. The simultaneous real gauge law (1.17) acts by conjugation at each start site on $A_b$; the two plaquettes in (C.2) each transform by conjugation. In particular each difference is gauge invariant in the boundary sense of (2.41)–(2.42). For analyticity we only vary $(\mathcal U,J)$ on these background charts, or the integrated small-chart coordinates specified in (3.43). The seed is independent of the auxiliary $J$.

Assign $p$ to a connected union $D_p$ of a bounded number of first-scale cluster cubes containing a one-cube neighborhood of its full support. Keep this same label through the expansion, including the exterior bond, so $D_p$ meets $\Lambda_1^c$. Its tree size and its volume in cluster units are bounded. Each such seed carrier has boundedly many original plaquettes since this is the first step, with $L,M$ fixed independently of the cutoff. Thus
\[
 \delta V(Y)=\sum_{p:D_p=Y} z_p\psi_p,\qquad
 \sup_Y e^{\kappa' d_0(Y)}\|\delta V(Y)\|
       \le C_{N,L,M,\kappa'}\,r\vartheta,\quad |z_p|\le r . \tag{C.4}
\]
This bound concerns the physical source at its original anchor. It entails no bound on how many fine plaquettes have influenced a coarse carrier after many further steps.

Here is how (C.4) enters the actual integral. B116 (1.41)–(1.43) splits the local potential into a quadratic form, estimated by (1.43), and a bounded analytic part, estimated by (1.36) at size $E_0\epsilon_1 C_1 M^4 e^{C_2\kappa_1}$ times tree decay. Add (C.4) to the bounded part only. On the B119 boundary version use the same cutoff in the bounded part and in (C.3). The harmless fixed ratio of first-step cutoff conventions can be included in $C_{N,L,M,\kappa'}$. For a sufficiently small *fixed* $r$, the numerical majorant is increased by an arbitrarily small fixed fraction; equivalently enlarge its bounded-part constant by a fixed factor before choosing the weak coupling ceiling. In (2.18) of B116 the inverse interpolation radius is linear in this majorant, so $\tau(Y)\delta V(Y)$ changes only the bounded volume allowance of (2.20). The resummation smallness in (2.28) is proportional to $\epsilon_1$ and is retained under this change. The quadratic form, covariances, tail estimate (2.22), and retained history mass are the original ones. For a crossing root the support label carries the exterior stencil, but the Gaussian variables on that carrier are only its integrated bonds. More explicitly, retain $\chi$ on the integrated bonds in the bounded cube neighborhood of $p$, regard the other factors in (C.2) as exterior parameters, and attach the adjacent exterior cubes to the output support tag. On a planar face the interior seed neighborhood can be chosen wall-connected. More generally include only roots admitting such a bounded connected interior neighborhood. In (2.1)–(2.3) this integrated seed is included in $Y_0$ and its small cutoff stays in the integrand. No new cutoff on a retained exterior variable is imposed. The change of variables by a covariance square root in (2.5) acts on the complementary Gaussian variables; the rooted factor remains a function of the original integrated coordinates on its seed. Consequently (C.3) is required on precisely those small-chart coordinates; it is never used to assert a small field throughout an unbounded Gaussian tail. An interpolation parameter attached to a localized covariance does not enter (C.2) when $(\mathcal U,J)$ are the local background arguments.

B119 p.252 identifies the conditional regular integral as the regular fluctuation integral with its actual background. The mixed-background potential localization is (3.43), p.276. B119 (3.44)–(3.47), pp.277–278, uses one localized inserted numerator inside the same restricted integration. The denominator clusters disjoint from the distinguished carrier cancel; clusters intersecting it stay attached. B119 p.279 applies these steps also to the expectation values with boundary measure (3.27). Equivalently insert (C.4) as an additional bounded summand in the first Mayer expansion, before its exponentiation, and subtract the resulting connected expansion at $z=0$. Every surviving connected term now contains at least one seed $D_p$. Tagging that seed requires no independent sum over translations in an anchored estimate: one can use the same anchored polymer majorant with the prescribed cube kept in the carrier. The number of physical sources in each input summand is already included in (C.4). For multiple marks the same joint polydisk computes mixed jets, including terms in which several marks occur in the same rooted cluster.

## 3. Conditional output and the direct receiving kernel

Let $I_1(z)$ denote (1.26) with the source on precisely these plaquettes inserted before regular integration. At positive real mass and real exterior define $G(z)=I_1(z)/I_1(0)$. Let $K_z(X)$ be the difference of the connected fluctuation logarithms with and without (C.4), with carriers enlarged to contain their full physical seeds. The output is
\[
 G(z)=\exp\!\left[\sum_p z_p c_p+\sum_X K_z(X)\right]. \tag{C.5}
\]
The exponential is the continuation of the ratio from the source-zero normalized branch; the statement does not require a separate lower bound on an old large-field mass. For each source $p$, its differentiated activities have carriers containing $D_p$. The usual connected estimate on the B119 boundary domains gives, with fixed spare decay and smaller source radius and any $m\ge1$,
\[
 \left|D_z^m K_0(X)[f^1,\ldots,f^m]\right|
 \le C m!(C/r)^m e^{-\kappa_{\rm out}d_1(X)}
              \prod_{\nu=1}^{m}\|f^\nu\|_\infty . \tag{C.6}
\]
Here $\kappa_{\rm out}$ is an admissible B119 boundary decay after a fixed margin for the $O(1)$-cube augmentation; the parameters of the weak construction are chosen with that reserve. Cauchy uses direction radii $r/(2m\|f^\nu\|_\infty)$ and $m^m\le e^m m!$. If a single mark is differentiated its anchor must occur in the carrier. At finite source $K_z$ vanishes at $z=0$; the same bound on an outer polydisk gives a factor proportional to $\|z\|_\infty$ on a smaller one. Each $c_p$ has the same finite carrier and its elementary bound (C.3). These outputs are boundary activities, since their required anchor crosses the first regular cut. B119 p.279 sends touching terms to the boundary family without the regular identity/Wilson projections. No additional vacuum subtraction is made for this specific family.

For a single unprojected physical insertion on the first real constrained history, differentiate (C.5) to identify the boundary logarithmic coefficient with its actual conditional mean. At second order the logarithmic coefficient is the conditional covariance and the density coefficient includes the product of means:
\[
 b_f=\mathbb E_{\rm reg}(V_f),\qquad
 b_{fg}=\mathbb E_{\rm reg}(V_fV_g),\qquad
 b_{fg}-b_fb_g=\operatorname{Cov}_{\rm reg}(V_f,V_g). \tag{C.7}
\]
The conditional law is exactly (1.26), with the selected geometry and the source-zero factors held fixed. Equations (C.2)–(C.6) control the localized complex-background extensions of this *restricted* boundary logarithm; they give more information than the real inequalities $|b_f|\le 2N\sum_p|f_p|$. The first-stage unnormalized output retains $I_1(0)G(z)$.

### 4. The copy of this boundary term at a receiving R-operation

There are two separate statements about the subsequent history. The unchanged old boundary summand can be bounded at a mixed kernel. Its replacement under *all* intervening conditional integrations also includes newly generated boundary summands.

Take one fixed plaquette as above. Define the analytic old boundary summand $L_{p,X}$ by the corresponding coefficient of (C.5), including $c_p$ on its own carrier. Its first and second logarithmic coefficients are bounded on the B119 boundary background domain by (C.3) and (C.6). The old input argument $A$ is the retained actual old fluctuation and the complex argument is the printed background $(\mathcal U,J)$. The sum of these bounds over old $X\supset D_p$ is finite uniformly in the volume. Indeed a connected set of $m$ fixed-scale cubes containing a fixed cube has at most $C^m$ possibilities and the tree size controls $m$ up to fixed constants. Choose the available decay with the usual spare entropy margin. This argument is for one fixed anchor, or for a boundedly charged family. It does not extend by replacing the number of anchors of an arbitrary previous E activity by the number of current cubes.

The destination of the old *copy* is explicit. B119 (3.26), p.271, rewrites the old term as its value at the next background and a difference evaluated inside a conditional expectation; the first term passes on as a summand of $B_k$, while the differences create additional terms. The analogous split in the preliminary large-field integrations leaves the old action and creates new boundary terms, as seen in LFR I, pp.182–183 around (1.25) and LFR II p.369, (1.49), which displays old $B_k$ and newly created $B''_k$ separately. No identity of the *entire* source derivative with the unchanged old part is implied by these splittings.

At a receiving operation where this copy is present in the old $B_k$ with its old coordinates, its field-domain check is the one for an old boundary term. LFR II p.372 specifically applies the simple subtraction (1.60) to boundary terms; its estimate uses B119 (2.42). LFR II pp.373–375 keeps the boundary gauge transformation in (1.61) and maps the new localized field, by (1.65), into the quoted old analyticity domain. The paragraph following (1.65) verifies the domain for terms connected with the old determining set. Consequently applying this field substitution to $L_{p,X}$ is bounded by its old norm on that image. This statement is about copies using the same old fluctuation coordinates as in the printed $B_k$; no substituted complex argument for a real switched fluctuation is inferred. A different historical path on which an old coordinate has itself been integrated gives a new conditional term and is outside this copy bound.

For precision, fix one completely component-owned copy contribution $b$ at the integration (1.71): its old coordinates are in that operator, and other component integrations are not needed to evaluate it. For a copy directly evaluated in the admitted image, let $M_b$ be its old sup bound; for a localized summand after the field substitutions use the corresponding bound with the factors of LFR II (1.66). The latter estimate is linear in the input norm for a single boundary insertion, since (1.60) and the successive differences act linearly on the test function; each Cauchy estimate uses its old domain, and (1.66) charges the localization. The subsequent sum over such labeled interactions still obeys the original distinction between (1.68) and the component-local cost (1.69). For each such bounded old readout, the printed complex-to-real domination (MR.13) gives the unnormalized statement
\[
 |T'_{k,c}(X)[bF]|
       \le e^{\varepsilon_c}M_b\, [T'_{k,r}(X)1]\|F\|_\infty . \tag{C.8}
\]
Here $X$ denotes the receiving component, and the norm of $F$ is taken on its real old integration coordinates. If a controlled raw mark also appears, apply the same inequality before the source-dependent old integrations, inserting the strictly raw exponential and using (MR.14) with coefficient $M_b$. Cauchy in that raw source gives the corresponding mixed raw/copy coefficient while preserving the original mass accounting. Equations (C.5) and (C.8) identify one generated boundary readout and the termwise receiving bound on its copy. No claim that every first-step large-field component is immediately eligible for erasure is needed; LFR I p.177 imposes an age and birth condition before selecting the components.

**Remaining restriction at (MR.27).** The complete coefficient also contains the conditional differences generated in each middle step as the old $B$ is evaluated before and after integration, the other plaquettes including distinguished and switched bonds, and old E/R and intercomponent terms. The first constrained calculation and the copy estimate do not sum these branches through arbitrarily many transitions in one invariant physical-source norm. A real positivity bound or a fresh choice of conditional mean cannot supply that sum. The full $T'_k B_f$ and second mixed kernel remain open.

### 5. The distinguished bond and the location of the smooth switch

The ordinary-bond restriction can be removed when the two blocks determining each distinguished bond stay in the small chart and miss the switched region. The following derivation verifies the necessary inverse map; it does not treat the eliminated coordinate as a free one.

For one positive coarse bond $c$ put $D_c=B(c_-)\cup B(c_+)$ and include its common crossing corridor. In B109 (0.11)–(0.12), pp.253–254, each internal path defining $U(c_-,x)$ or $U(x',c_+)$ stays in its endpoint block. Each straight length-$L$ path $[x,x']$ crosses only the corridor between these two blocks, and the path $c$ in the formula also stays in them. Thus the group average for $c$ sees the distinguished crossing $b_0(c)$ and no $b_0(c')$ for another unoriented coarse bond. This remains true for the symmetric contour averages within each block. Reverse occurrences impose the inverse equation and add no independent unknown.

In a local real gauge for the two blocks, let $\mathcal U$ be the background links and $W_b=e^{ix_b}\mathcal U_b$ on the ordinary bonds. Give the distinguished bond the temporary value $W_{b_0}=e^{iy}\mathcal U_{b_0}$. Define the analytic equation
\[
 \mathcal F_c(y,x;\mathcal U)
    =\frac1i\log\!\big[\overline W(c)\overline{\mathcal U}(c)^{-1}\big]=0.
 \tag{C.9}
\]
At $x=y=0$ it vanishes for every background in the chart. At the flat background its derivative in $y$ is $L^{-3}I$. Indeed the derivative of the reference path $-c$ in (0.12) cancels that of the final outer $c$. The paths within endpoint blocks never traverse $b_0$. Exactly $L$ out of the $L^4$ transverse-translated paths traverse this bond. The argument applies to each Lie algebra component. This calculation uses the group average, without a spurious $1/L$ normalization of its logarithm.

There are finitely many local bonds and path words since $L$ is fixed. The matrices in (0.11)–(0.12) are analytic on a fixed small complex neighborhood of the flat chart, using the analytic symmetric contour average of (0.5)–(0.10). On the admitted first-scale regular representatives, (2.38) makes the real background close to identity in a local unitary gauge; (2.39) bounds its complex perturbation. Choose these regularity ceilings sufficiently small once for fixed $N,L,M$, as allowed by the weak construction. Then $\|D_y\mathcal F_c-L^{-3}I\|<L^{-3}/2$ throughout a fixed neighborhood of zero. For completeness the map $y\mapsto y-L^3\mathcal F_c(y,x;\mathcal U)$ is a uniform contraction there and sends a fixed ball into itself after shrinking the $x$-ball, since $\mathcal F_c(0,0;\mathcal U)=0$. Its fixed point $y_c(x;\mathcal U)$ is holomorphic, with
\[
 y_c(0;\mathcal U)=0,\qquad
 \|y_c(x;\mathcal U)-y_c(x';\mathcal U)\|
       \le C_{N,L}\sum_{b\in D_c\setminus\{b_0\}}\|x_b-x'_b\|
 \tag{C.10}
\]
on a fixed smaller ball. This follows by subtracting the two fixed point equations and bounding the $x$ derivative on the outer ball. The real solution is the actual fine link in the eliminated coordinate. B119 (1.14)–(1.15) leaves the other links unchanged and imposes the same group average; hence local uniqueness identifies it with (C.9). B109 pp.267–268 likewise constructs the supported $h$, solves the analytic nonlinear correction and then removes the linear delta function. There is no additional Jacobian in the marked input: it is already in the actual unmarked integration density.

For a source loop containing such distinguished bonds, take the union of the required $D_c$ and the ordinary loop support. In (C.2) now set its **independent integrated** coordinates to zero, recomputing every dependent $y_c$ with that choice. Real retained coordinates on this small-chart neighborhood satisfy the first-step good-fluctuation cutoff; that condition is checked before using the extension. Equations (C.9)–(C.10) and the same product telescoping prove (C.3) with this bounded enlarged set in place of $p\cap\mathcal I$. Thus (C.4)–(C.7) hold, with new constants depending on $L$, for these distinguished crossing marks as well. A block touching the integration boundary is used in full; the small-chart bound on its interior is protected by the $\chi_{Y_0}$ of §2.

The smooth switch of (1.22) has a different location. Before rectangular filling, $\Lambda_1=\Omega_1^{\sim-2}\cap(R'_1{}^{\sim})^c$ and $S_1=(\Omega_1\setminus\Omega_1^{\sim-1})\cup R_1$ in B119 (1.20), p.251. Thus an exterior block layer separates $\Lambda_1$ from $S_1$; rectangular filling only shrinks $\Lambda_1$. A true fine crossing loop and the bounded two-$L$-block neighborhoods used above miss $S_1^*$ provided this separation exceeds their diameter plus the incident-bond convention. This holds at an admitted buffered face for $MR_1$ sufficiently large, and can equivalently be tested on the specified finite stencil. The occurrence of a switched retained variable cannot be inferred merely from the fact that the *source* crosses the regular-domain boundary.

For an ordinary bond where the switch actually acts, (1.22) gives the exact product, with $t_b=g(A'_b)$ or $1$ as specified on p.252,
\[
 W_b=e^{ig_0 A'_b}\,e^{-it_b H_{1,\Lambda_1}(b)}\mathcal U_b . \tag{C.11}
\]
All coordinates on $S_1^*$ in this formula remain outside the integral in (1.26); $A'_b$ is real, and $H_{1,\Lambda_1}$ depends on the background data in (1.21). For an ordinary loop completely in the real retained region, (C.11) therefore pulls through that integral exactly as multiplication. The C-infinity switch is not to be analytically continued in $A'_b$. Fixing the real $A'_b$, the factor is entire in $H_b$. Comparing with $H_b=0$ costs $C\sum_{b\in p}\|H_b\|e^{C\sum\|H_b\|}$ on bounded background-link representatives, by product telescoping; there is no factor $g_0^{-1}$. B119 p.251 supplies exponential decay of $H$ on the separated real chart and p.252 describes analyticity in its background argument. Holomorphic *localization on a later mixed chart* requires the actual background argument and the inherited layer norms. Formula (C.11) alone does not supply those for all switched loops.

### 6. The first newly generated middle-step term

This paragraph concerns the next ordinary $T$ integration on a history carrying the boundary family in (C.5), before an intervening erasure of its supporting component. Write $L_z$ for its full source-dependent boundary logarithm, zero at $z=0$, including all exterior factors. Older fluctuation coordinates $A_{\rm old}$ are held where the unmarked $B_1$ holds them. Denote the next background by $U_2$, the actual previous background reconstructed from the new fluctuation by $U_{\rm pre}$, and the **source-zero** conditional measure of this next regular integration by $\Pi_2$. Use the actual boundary coordinate changes in $U_{\rm pre}$, including (1.22)'s current-scale counterpart where applicable. Pulling out the carried value gives the exact identity
\[
 \log G_2(z)=L_z(U_2,A_{\rm old})
   +\log\Pi_2\exp\Delta L_z,\qquad
 \Delta L_z=L_z(U_{\rm pre},A_{\rm old})-L_z(U_2,A_{\rm old}).
 \tag{C.12}
\]
Any exterior factors in the conditional integrand which are independent of the integrated variable may be left inside $\Pi_2$ without changing the identity. At $z=0$ no measure is perturbed. In particular the newly generated first and mixed second coefficients in the second term are
\[
 \Pi_2(\Delta L_f),\qquad
 \Pi_2(\Delta L_{fg})+\operatorname{Cov}_{\Pi_2}(\Delta L_f,\Delta L_g).
 \tag{C.13}
\]
The use of the normalized *regular* integral in this calculation does not divide out a rare old R mass.

It applies the field variations (3.6)–(3.7) of B109 directly to each old localized boundary activity. The changes act on the background, holding its old fluctuation arguments fixed. The layer-dependent margins in (2.36)–(2.39) and exponential decay of $H_k$ are the ones checked on p.277. The result there is (2.42)-type decay with the additional $O(\epsilon_k)$ from the fluctuating field, and the output preintegration domain is (3.43). Those estimates are linear in the old term: the dependence on its value enters through a difference or first Cauchy derivative; the background reconstruction and its interpolations are independent of that term. Kernel decay, labeled carrier summation and the original geometric constants are unchanged. If the transformation acts on $S_{k+1}$, the change of this same term is included in $V^{(k)}(S_{k+1})$, explicitly treated on p.277 with an $S_{k+1}$ localization. Its switch acts on a real retained *current* fluctuation; the change is a background change for $L_z$. It does not require differentiating $L_z$ in an old real-coordinate cutoff. Apply this linear input argument at $k=1$ to the family actually constructed in §§1–2, on a smaller source disk. It satisfies precisely the old B sup and locality bound with size $C\|z\|_\infty$, with no older E or R insertion in this restricted incoming source family. The resulting new boundary activities have a finite outer-domain sup bound and exponential boundary decay as in (C.6) with the constants for this one extra step. Products in (C.13) are controlled by source Cauchy on the resulting logarithm. This is the first *new* regular conditional readout for this restricted old B family. It does not assume that source-free Jacobians change with $z$ or that old E/R terms have become source-free in the entire physical insertion.

## 7. Full compensation on a retained shell

There is a real-coordinate case where the switched product in (C.11) has no residual dependence on a distant complex background at all. Use ordinary bonds in the portion of $S_1^*$ where the change (1.22) takes $t_b=1$ by the geometric prescription immediately below (1.23), B119 p.252. For instance, the boundary shell of a first blocked plaquette defect has such bonds when there is no separate fluctuation defect $R_1$ there. Choose a fine plaquette strictly within this shell, off all distinguished corridor bonds. The coarse shell width permits such plaquettes. Their gauge-invariant plaquette values are generally functions of retained fluctuations. The original four links are in $\Omega_1$ and are outside the strict original group core of MR §1.

Write $U_{\mathrm{loc}}=U_1(\Omega_1\cap\Lambda_1^c,V)$ as on B119 p.251. The dimensionless bond field $H_b$ used in (1.22) has $\exp(iH_b)U_{\mathrm{loc}}(b)=U_1(b)$. This normalization follows directly from the transformed product in (1.23). The general-step definition (3.22), B119 p.269, writes the same relative bond as $\exp(iH^{(k)})=V^{(k)}(V_{\mathrm{loc}}^{(k)})^{-1}$. Thus the following cancellation occurs before taking either a logarithm or a bound:
\[
 e^{ig_0A_b}U_1(b)
 =e^{ig_0 A'_b}e^{-iH_b}U_1(b)
 =e^{ig_0 A'_b}U_{\mathrm{loc}}(b).
 \tag{C.14}

# Stability with spatially varying Wilson couplings

## 1. Target and assumed interface

This paper tests the sourced extension of Bałaban’s ultraviolet bound on each compatible torus. The exact constant-source change of variables gives a useful control, but even scalar stability for nonconstant sources requires a new spatial response estimate. The letter (P) denotes a proof here, (L) a checked published result in its stated setting, (C) a conditional implication and (O) an open estimate. We assume the following statement, including its normalizations, for the unsourced trajectory only. Its proof is not part of this note.

> **(N2-F) Coupling flow.** There are constants γ ∈ ]0, 1], b_0 > 0, C_r < ∞, depending only on N, L and Balaban's fixed construction constants, such that for every K ∈ N and every g_* ∈ ]0, γ/2] there is a bare coupling g_0 = g_0(K; g_*) with the following properties. Let x_k = g_k^{-2}, k = 0, …, K, be Balaban's effective couplings for the theory on Λ_{a,T}, a = L^{-K} ℓ_0 (x_k is the coefficient of the Wilson term in the inductive representation of ρ_k, in the normalization in which x_0 = g_0^{-2} = β_a/(2N); by N0's dictionary D2 it does not depend on the torus T). Then: (i) x_{k+1} = x_k − b_0 log L + r_k(x_k) with |r_k(x)| ≤ C_r/x for all 0 ≤ k < K and all x ≥ γ^{-2}; (ii) g_k ≤ γ for all 0 ≤ k ≤ K; (iii) g_K = g_*; (iv) x_0 = b_0 K log L + O(log(K+1)) with the O depending only on (N, L, g_*); (v) g_* ↦ g_0(K; g_*) is continuous on ]0, γ/2]; (vi) each r_k extends to a function of x that is C^1 on [γ^{-2}, ∞) with |r_k′(x)| ≤ C_r/x². Here b_0 is the one-loop coefficient in the normalization x = g^{-2} (expected value 11N/(24π²) for SU(N); the value in the preamble's conventions, with its derivation, is to be supplied with N2).

## 2. Definitions and conventions

Write $h_k=L^k a$ for the step spacing, $h_K=\ell _0$ for the physical cell side, $N_T=(T/\ell _0)^4$ for the number of cells, and $P_k$ for averaging a step-$k$ field over a single (L)-block. Aligned blocks give $P_k j_k=j_{k+1}$. Bałaban's step-$k$ units assign spacing $1$ to that coarse lattice and $L^{-k}$ to the original one. All sources here are real and have the componentwise scaled $C^5$ bound of P2. A cell-supported source vanishes on the band of width $\ell _0/8$ at its boundary. General sources in the stated interface may be nonzero there. We distinguish an enlarged cell on which the source is actually supplied from a source supported strictly inside a cell.

The normalized coordinate is $x=\beta/(2N)$. The actual plaquette coefficient in the P2 action is $2Nx$. Thus a Wilson term with coefficient $x(y)$ multiplies $2Ns_p$ when expressed in the normalized coordinate. In §3 we retain the wording of N1-S and record its necessary conversion in §6. At step zero the sourced normalized coordinate equals $x_0-j^{(0)}(x_p)/(2N)$. The bare scalar insertion is $a^{-4}\sum _{\mu <\nu }s_{p_{\mu \nu }(x)}$, using the same sampling point as P2. At later steps extraction of the coefficient requires a specified local projection of the complete effective action; bare substitution alone defines no such projection.

## 3. Main theorems

### Theorem 3.1. Requested N1-S (O)

> **(N1-S) Base with sources.** There is r_0^* > 0 depending only on (N, L, γ) such that for every r_0 ∈ ]0, r_0^*], every j ∈ 𝒥(r_0), every K ∈ N with a = L^{-K} ℓ_0 and g_0 = g_0(K; g_*) as in N2-F, and every compatible torus T, there are a functional E_{a,T}[j] ∈ R and constants b_+, b_−, b_0, C_L, C_e, C_Z < ∞ depending only on (N, L, ℓ_0, γ, g_*, r_0) — never on a, T, j — such that:
> (i) Z_{a,T}[j] = exp(−E_{a,T}[j]) ∫ ρ_K[j] dν_T, where ν_T is the reference measure on step-K configurations identified in N0 (Haar or Balaban's gauge-fixed measure) and ρ_K[j] ≥ 0; ρ_K[j] ≤ e^{b_+ N_T} ν_T-a.e.; and ρ_K[j] ≥ e^{−b_− N_T} on a ν_T-measurable set of measure ≥ e^{−b_0 N_T}. Hence |log Z_{a,T}[j] + E_{a,T}[j]| ≤ (b_+ + b_− + b_0) N_T.
> (ii) (Locality of the counterterm in the source.) E_{a,T}[j] − E_{a,T}[0] = −Σ_{cells c ⊂ Λ_{a,T}} L_a(j|_c) − R_{a,T}[j] with |R_{a,T}[j]| ≤ C_L N_T, where L_a maps restrictions of sources to a single cell (with margin) to R, does not depend on T or on the cell (covariance under the translations of the cell grid), satisfies L_a(0) = 0 and L_a(Θj) = L_a(j) for every reflection Θ in a face of the cell (with the tensor transformation of j^{μνρσ}), and for every finite linear family j = Σ_i λ_i f_i of cell-supported sources the map λ ↦ L_a(Σ_i λ_i f_i) is real-analytic on {Σ_i |λ_i| ‖f_i‖_{C^5} ≤ r_0}.
> (iii) Consequently W^{ren}_{a,T}[j] := log(Z_{a,T}[j]/Z_{a,T}[0]) − Σ_c L_a(j|_c) satisfies |W^{ren}_{a,T}[j]| ≤ C_W N_T for all j ∈ 𝒥(r_0), with C_W := 2(b_+ + b_− + b_0) + C_L, uniformly in a and T.
> (iv) For the chessboard replication Wj of a cell-supported source j with margin (Wj ∈ 𝒥(r_0)), L_a((Wj)|_{c′}) = L_a(j) for every cell c′.
> (v) (Inductive form with sources.) For every k ≤ K, ρ_k[j] has Balaban's inductive form (N0 dictionary D2) with Wilson term Σ_p x_k(y_p) s_p(U_k), where x_k(y) = x_k − j^{(0)}_k(y)/(2N) + e_k(y), |e_k(y)| ≤ C_e r_0, and with the clover term Σ_y (Z^{(k)} j_k)^{μνρσ}(y) Q_{μνρσ}(U_k; y), Z^{(k)} an H_4-covariant linear map of the dimension-4 tensor space with ‖Z^{(k)}‖ + ‖(Z^{(k)})^{-1}‖ ≤ C_Z; all other terms (E-, R-, B-terms) obey Balaban's bounds with constants independent of a, T, j, k. The sourced couplings satisfy x_k(y) ≥ γ^{-2}/2 for all y.

## 4. Proofs and tests of the proposed estimates

### 4.1 The exact initial step (P)

Substitution in the finite integral gives $Z[t]=Z_{\mathrm{Wilson}}(\beta _a-t)$ for a constant scalar source and the Wilson integral with plaquette coefficients $\beta _a-j^{(0)}(x_p)$ for a general scalar source. Every plaquette satisfies $0\le s_p\le 2N$. Every clover is bounded on a compact product of groups. The finite-integral exponential series and all its parameter derivatives converge uniformly on bounded parameter sets, by domination by the exponential of a finite constant. The integral at each real parameter is strictly positive. The power series for its real logarithm therefore converges in some neighborhood of that real parameter. This argument supplies no radius uniform in the lattice.

Write $U-I=D$ for one oriented plaquette. Then $|D|_F^2=2s_p$, and $|U-U^{-1}|_F\le 2|D|_F$. Conjugating to the common base point preserves these norms. The traceless anti-Hermitian projection is contractive. Cauchy–Schwarz for four terms in the clover yields $|\widehat F_{\mu \nu }(x)|_F^2\le \tfrac12 \sum _{p\in C_{\mu \nu }(x)}s_p$. Consequently $|Q_{ij}(x)|\le \tfrac14 (\sum _{p\in C_i(x)}s_p+\sum _{p\in C_j(x)}s_p)$, where $i,j$ label the antisymmetric index pairs. Summing, using the bounded multiplicity of the clovers, gives $|\sum  jQ|\le C(N)r_0 S$. This proves a compact-law domination and permits fixed small real perturbations at the bare level. It does not estimate the marginal tensor mixing. A lattice reflection through a coordinate plane permutes the four transported clover paths and inserts the orientation signs of their index pair. The traced product therefore has the tensor transformation law. An anchored scalar plaquette needs separate care: under reflection reversing direction μ, its lower corner shifts by one lattice spacing in μ relative to the reflected old corner. Sampling the source at plaquette centers intertwines this reflection; sampling at the lower corner with the site-scalar law of P2 does not in general intertwine it. Thus a proof of reflection covariance for the extracted cell functional must specify sampling, or bound the discrepancy after subtraction.

### 4.2 What the constant flow can control (P, C)

Let $d=b_0 \log L>0$, $F_k(u)=u-d+r_k(u)$. For $u,v\ge \gamma ^{-2}$, $u,v\ge x_k/2$, N2-F gives
$$
 |F_k(u)-F_k(v)-(u-v)|≤4C_r x_k^{-2}|u-v|.
$$
The backward estimate requires control of a possible upward jump from small x. A sufficient explicit regime is $x_*>C_r\gamma ^2+2C_r/d+2d+2\gamma ^{-2}$. Then $x_k\ge x_{k+1}-C_r\gamma ^2$ by N2-F(i),(ii); induction backward gives $x_k\ge x_{k+1}+d/2$. Indeed the first inequality gives $x_k>2C_r/d$ whenever $x_{k+1}\ge x_*$; the recursion then gives the asserted improvement. Hence $\sum  x_k^{-2}\le \sum _{n\ge 0}(x_*+dn/2)^{-2}<\infty $. Always $\sum _{k<K} g_k^2 h_k/\ell _0\le \gamma ^2/(L-1)$. This regime gives a uniform admission margin. Equivalently one can choose a smaller terminal interval in advance using the constants of the original N2-F estimate. Its coverage of every terminal coupling in the originally stated interval would need a uniform summability argument there; the allowed $O(\log K)$ endpoint error alone gives no such bound. The printed interface gives $x_k\ge \gamma ^{-2}$; subtracting a signed perturbation from a point at this endpoint need not keep it in the admitted interval. The lower bound $\gamma ^{-2}/2$ in (v) itself would only imply $g_{max}\le \sqrt{2}\gamma $. Constants in these inequalities have precisely the dependence of N2-F and on $g_*$ as indicated.

**Constant sources on genuinely shot trajectories (P, assuming N2-F).** Choose a terminal inverse coupling $X=x_*$ strictly inside the sufficiently weak interval just established, with a fixed interval $[X-s,X+s]$ still inside it. Let $b_K(X')=g_0(K;(X')^{-1/2})^{-2}$ be the shot bare inverse coupling. For any two terminal values in this interval their trajectories stay above $X-s+(K-j)d/2$ at step $j$. All intermediate points in the mean-value theorem do as well. Taking $X-s$ larger if needed, $F'_j(u)=1+r'_j(u)>0$ there and $|r'_j(u)|\le C_r/u^2$. Therefore the secant slopes from the two bare values to their terminal values are products of positive factors lying between $C^{-1}$ and $C$, uniformly in $K$, by the square summability above. Distinct terminal values must have distinct bare values, and their order agrees. In particular the two bare endpoints bracket $[b_K(X)-s/C,b_K(X)+s/C]$. N2-F(v) and the intermediate value theorem show that every shifted bare value $b_K(X)-t/(2N)$ in that bracket is an actual shot homogeneous theory, with terminal inverse coupling differing from $X$ by at most $C|t|/(2N)$. This proves the homogeneous finite-shift identification rather than extrapolating an arbitrary scalar recursion. The exact bare identity then permits application of the published complete homogeneous bound to that theory, on tori compatible with the **shifted** partitions. For a whole interval of shifts at fixed $K$ one can choose common divisible tori, but a torus compatible only with the original profile need not tile all changed auxiliary blocks. The terminal Haar-ball argument in N0 can use the minimum radius over this compact weak-terminal interval: the positive cutoff $g p_0(g)$ has a positive infimum there, and the coefficient of the minimizing action has a finite supremum. This uses the two-sided pointwise stability constants with their stated N0 scope; it supplies neither the cell-uniform vacuum subtraction nor a spatial trajectory.

**Scalar induction lemma (C).** Suppose a normalized extracted field $u_k$ obeys, on its admitted domain,
$$
 \lVert u_{k+1}-F_k(P_ku_k)\rVert_\infty\le A r_0 g_k^2 h_k/\ell_0 .
\tag{4.1}
$$
Then with $u_0=x_0-j_0/(2N)$ and $D_k=|u_k-x_k+j_k/(2N)|_\infty $, the preceding mean-value inequality gives $D_{k+1}\le (1+4C_rg_k^4)D_k+2C_rg_k^4r_0/N+Ar_0g_k^2h_k/\ell _0$. Iterating bounds $D_k/r_0$ by $\exp(4C_r \sum  g_k^4)(2C_r \sum  g_k^4/N+A\gamma ^2/(L-1))$. This closes a bootstrap if the constant trajectory has enough admission margin and the displayed sums are uniform. An error $Ar_0h_k/\ell _0$ without $g_k^2$ is already summable and also suffices. For the genuinely shot constant shifts above this recurrence has $A=0$. The bare identity itself holds for every real t. A sup bound on $D_k$ alone gives no Lipschitz bound on successive values of $u_k$.

**Spatial response (O).** Write $\Phi _{k,y}$ for the projection of the full one-step action onto its normalized Wilson coefficient, including the dependence on previously generated terms. Even on the manifold of constant profiles, the scalar flow specifies only the derivative in the direction of the constant function. It gives no bound on the first spatial moment of $D\Phi _{k,y}$, on its sign or $\ell^1$ norm, or on dependence on previous irrelevant terms. For example adding $M_k[u(z+e)-u(z)]$ to any trial local map changes none of its constant-profile values or derivatives along constants; $M_k$ is arbitrary. A reflection-invariant second difference gives the same test if discrete symmetries are imposed. These examples demonstrate underdetermination by the scalar recursion and say nothing against the physical map. At fixed cutoff an exact test is differentiation of a positive source-independent block integral. For its normalized log density at coarse field V relative to V=1, the derivative in a scalar plaquette coefficient equals the difference of the two conditional plaquette expectations (with the sign fixed by whether one differentiates j or β). The elementary bound is 2N per varied plaquette. It has no decay in the distance from that plaquette to a coarse-field variation. A quadratic coarse-field projection requires differentiating these conditional expectations with respect to the coarse field as well. Computing the actual second variation and projecting its local part with the constrained response of the minimizing background is the outstanding step in (4.1). One may alternatively prove an all-scales uniform row bound for the derivative of $u_k(y)$ with respect to the original bare profile, uniform on the complete sourced interpolation. Its direct integral along $tj$, $0\le t\le 1$, yields $O(r_0)$; translation covariance applied to differences of shifted profiles yields an $O(r_0 h_k/\ell _0)$ neighbor bound. Both are real-parameter arguments. Neither requires moment generating functions at complex sources.

### 4.3 Weighted quadratic forms and the determinant (P, C)

A fixed finite-dimensional gauge slice with a nonsingular positive Hessian admits a local implicit solution after perturbation of the weights; this follows by applying the inverse function theorem to the projected gradient with the constraint chart held fixed. This statement allows its neighborhood and its inverse norm to depend on the slice and on scale. The full Hessian contains the curvature terms and second derivatives of the constraint map as well as its covariant derivative energy. Uniform weighted minimizer regularity would require estimates for the projected inverse in Bałaban's scaled norms and for the residual force when a derivative falls on the weight. A sup bound on a scalar multiplier does not establish these estimates.

For a controlled quadratic calculation restrict to a fixed linear space of fluctuation variables and let $H_0>0, H_w=H_0+D^*M_wD$, where $M_w$ is plaquette multiplication by $w$. Let $P=DH_0^{-1}D^*$. Suppose
$$
 B=\sup_p\sum_q\lVert P_{pq}\rVert\exp(\mu\,\mathrm{dist}(p,q))<\infty,\qquad B\lVert w\rVert_\infty\le q_0<1 .
\tag{4.2}
$$
Distances are in step units. Matrix submultiplicativity and the determinant identity yield
$$
 \log\frac{\det H_w}{\det H_0}
 =\sum_{n\ge1}\frac{(-1)^{n+1}}{n}\operatorname{Tr}[(M_wP)^n].
$$
This follows by integrating the convergent resolvent series along real interpolations $tw$. Anchor a trace at its first plaquette $p$. Each differentiated $w(z)$ lies on a closed walk through $p,z$; summing all walks with a marked vertex and the weight $\exp(\mu  \mathrm{dist}(p,z))$ costs at most $n B(B|w|_\infty )^{n-1}$ times the bounded internal matrix dimension. The $1/n$ cancels the marked-vertex count. Thus each anchor's derivative has exponential row norm at most $d_{\mathrm{int}}B/(1-q_0)$, uniform in volume under (4.2). This also proves finite fifth moments at any smaller rate. In a periodic model whose local kernel pieces agree on lifts, the same walk sum loses an exponentially small tail when it reaches the cut. The common-pieces hypothesis is necessary to identify lifted contributions. Flat fixed constraints and a curvature covariance satisfying (4.2) give a verified prototype; for variable constrained backgrounds the Hessian, constraint subspace, and $D$ itself change. The formula does not count their derivatives.

On independent fluctuation bonds the substitution $V'_b=\exp(i g_b B'_b)$ contributes $-d(G)\sum _{b \mathrm{free}}\log x_b/2$ to the logarithm of the leading Haar Jacobian, besides its smooth density at $g_b B'_b$, with $d(G)=N^2-1$. For $|x_b-x_k|\le Cr_0\le x_k/2$, each difference is bounded by $d(G)Cr_0/x_k$. The sum is over free bonds after tree fixing, not all sites. The nonlinear Haar density, the scaling of axial and block constraints and the kernel of the resulting quadratic form require their own comparison. The determinant calculation above treats just the indicated fixed linear space.

### 4.4 Vacuum energy and a cell extension lemma (P, C)

For each cell use a closed interior box whose two endpoints along every axis are the first and last strictly interior grid layers. On sufficiently fine grids it differs from the physical cube by at most one fine spacing per face. Its choice is covariant under translations and reflections of the grid. On its interval keep the supplied source. Across either end, let $t$ denote the outward distance. Choose the six odd integers $m=1,3,\ldots,11$ and the unique coefficients $b_m$ satisfying $\sum_m b_m(-m)^q=1$ for $0\le q\le5$ (the coefficient matrix is Vandermonde). Evaluate the sum of $b_m$ times the source at inward distance $mt$ from that end. Multiply by a fixed rescaled smooth cutoff equal to one near $t=0$ and zero for $t\ge\ell_0/32$. For a sufficiently fine lattice all reflected points lie in the box. Take the product of the four commuting coordinate extension operators. Across a face, the six moment equalities match the derivatives through order five. Across edges and corners, applying each matching condition in its own coordinate matches all mixed derivatives of total order at most five. The cutoff and the finite dilation factors bound the scaled $C^5$ norm of the extension by $C_{\mathrm{ext}}r_0$. Odd dilations preserve compatible integer or half-shifted meshes across a face. In particular the extension at sampled points uses only the original interior samples, even if a continuum representative of these samples is nonunique. Its compact support in a small enlargement of the cell allows evaluation on the lifted lattice; translating a lift translates every argument. Reflections interchange the two ends of each interval, and tensor reflection signs commute with this scalar linear map.

On the shrunken box the global representative and extension agree through order four up to its boundary. Expand at the coordinatewise projection of an exterior point onto this box. Taylor's integral remainder and the $C^5$ bounds give
$$
 |j(z)-\mathrm{Ext}_c j(z)|\le C r_0((\mathrm{dist}(z,c)+a)/\ell_0)^5 .
 \tag{4.3}
$$
This also covers fine sites on the geometric face and configurations crossing several faces. Away from the collar the estimate follows from boundedness with a larger absolute constant. If the mesh is too coarse for the shrunken box, $K$ itself is bounded in terms of $L$ and the fixed cutoff. One may use the zero extension for the cell rule in these cases: its error is $O(r_0)$ per anchor under (4.4), and there are only $O(1)$ anchors per cell summed over these scales. For fine meshes the straight-line interpolation with the actual source lies in a scaled $C^5$ ball of radius $\max(1,C_{\mathrm{ext}})r_0$. Choose $r_0$ small enough that this larger ball is admitted by the hypothesized estimates. This is a bounded smooth extension of sampled restrictions, with no rough intermediate source.

**Cell lemma (C).** Suppose the increment $-E[j]+E[0]$ has a sum of scale-anchor functionals $v_{k,y}$, with at most $C(\ell _0/h_k)^4$ anchors per cell, and that the anchored rule has a volume-independent lift. More precisely suppose replacing the actual torus evaluation by its common lifted rule costs at most $C \exp(-c\ell _0/h_k)$ per anchor; only the real source profile needs to be continued and the calculation is performed with the same local factors. Suppose on the interpolations between $j$ and $\mathrm{Ext}_cj$ the row of derivatives of the lifted functional satisfies
$$
 \sum_z |D_z v_{k,y}|\,(1+\mathrm{dist}(z,y)/h_k)^5 \le C .
\tag{4.4}
$$
Here distances are physical, and if $z$ is an original lattice site each $D_z$ is the derivative with respect to that sampled value with its correct discrete measure included. Indices for source components can be summed. Require the same rule under cell translations/reflections and real-analytic versions on finite source families with a common convergent majorant for each fixed cutoff. Define $L_a$ as the sum over the cell's anchors of these lifted increments evaluated on $\mathrm{Ext}_cj$, subtracting their values at zero. This defines a single cell functional with the specified symmetries and analyticity. The fundamental theorem of calculus, (4.3), and $a\le h_k$ bound the extension error per anchor by $C r_0(h_k/\ell _0)^5$. The total per cell over all scales is bounded since $\sum _{k\le K}h_k/\ell _0\le L/(L-1)$. The lift error is bounded by $\sum _{n\ge 0} L^{4n}\exp(-cL^n)<\infty $. The latter follows by the ratio test. This proves (ii) under the stated hypotheses. For a cell-supported source with the prescribed margin, zero extension and exponential copying suffice when the derivative row has exponential decay; a dependency reaching from another cell to the support traverses the margin. For arbitrary smooth sources a face-straddling term may move just one fine step. Counting a strip of thickness $h_k$ alone can cost $O((\ell _0/h_k)^3)$ anchors per cell, so exponential suppression at distance $\ell _0/8$ cannot be used generically. Equation (4.4) is proved by §4.3 in its fixed-kernel model, but is missing for the interacting subtractions and varying constraints.

### 4.5 Complete large-field terms, terminal measure, and logical consequences

For the same plaquette configuration an identical weighted action cancels algebraically in an exponent quotient. The two integrals of LFR II (1.1) contain different configurations: (1.10) extracts the denominator's action at its constrained background; (1.14)–(1.15) split a numerator term to retain both its Wilson piece and the rare factor. Estimating their difference at distinct backgrounds is indispensable. For two coefficients $u,\tilde u$ on a region $D$ the elementary bound is $|\sum _{p\in D}(u_p-\tilde u_p)s_p|\le |u-\tilde u|_\infty \sum _{p\in D}s_p$; proximity alone omits the extensive curvature sum. A weighted saddle estimate must account for this factor, the factors $x_k$, and all constants in the large-field reserve. Once a positive reference integral $T$ and an integrand $e^{\sigma}$ have $|\Re\sigma|\le h, |\Im\sigma|\le \theta <\pi /2$, one has $|T(e^{\sigma} F)/T(e^{\sigma})|\le \sec\theta\,|F|_\infty $ by taking the real part of the denominator. This permits complex **field** intermediates of the published argument while every input source here is real. The requisite weighted bounds on $\sigma$ are open. The rare factor needed in exponentiation is $\exp[-p_0(g_{\max})]$.

In a product Haar terminal measure, restricting every independent terminal link to a fixed identity ball of Haar mass $m>0$ gives a set of measure at least $m^{4N_T}$, with equality for exactly that many independently restricted links. For a use in (i) one still needs to place these terminal configurations in the true cutoff and bound the action of the **fine constrained minimizer** there. The restriction is solely at the last scale. Global holonomies/flat modes belong to the coarse link configurations when retained; gauge trees and constraints occur inside the transformation, and full small-field one-step determinant constants occur in $E$. Any separate treatment of a projected zero mode requires the actual projection and determinant identified in the construction. A uniform two-sided density estimate on all advertised tori cannot be concluded from this measure count.

Given (i), put $B=b_++b_-+b_0$. Then $d[j]=\log Z[j]+E[j]$ has $|d[j]|\le B N_T$. By (ii), $W^{ren}=d[j]-d[0]+R[j]$; this proves (iii) with $2B+C_L$. By composition of the cell symmetries in (ii), all reflected translates of the supported source give the same $L_a$, proving (iv). These are implications; their hypotheses remain open.

### 4.6 A genuine first-step weighted saddle on the all-regular torus (P)

Here is a cutoff- and volume-uniform nonlinear case. Use a compatible periodic lattice with sufficiently many complete $L$-blocks for the fixed patches below to embed as rectangles. Fix the first-step coarse links $V$ and take the actual constrained unsourced saddle $U_*$ of CMP 119 (1.12), p. 248, in the all-small region. For this lemma assume it has a representative with every bond in a single sufficiently small identity chart, and its coarse links in that chart. Put $\epsilon=\sup_p\|I-U_{*,p}\|$. This is a restriction on the field, not a conclusion from a bound on the plaquettes. It avoids assuming that all flat connections admit this global representative with small coarse links. No assumption on the torus volume enters the chart radius. The lemma is stated on the all-regular torus; holes with multiscale determining data are outside its scope.

CMP 119 p. 243 allows either CMP 109 (0.4) or (0.12), pp. 252–254. The proof applies to both. For (0.12) the endpoint transports are the analytic group averages (0.11) of finite path transports. Their derivatives through order three are bounded on a smaller fixed identity chart by analyticity; equivariance permits local gauge rotations, and (0.8) gives the arithmetic linearization. For (0.4) the finite path sums and analytic logarithms give the same bounds directly. To compute the minimum modulo gauge, fix an internal spanning tree rooted at each block center. Gauge transformations used here are the identity at every center, hence preserve this coarse constraint exactly. In this chart the chosen tree bonds are the identity. One may return to the symmetric axial representative of CMP 119 (1.12) using its local covariant transport $U(y,x)$ inside each block: use the transformation equal to the identity at the center that sets all these transports to one. It has bounded first and second derivatives on the small chart, uniformly in the number of blocks, because its paths have bounded length and stay in their block. This use of a different local section for the orbit of the **same** constrained minimum does not replace the published gauge factor inside the fluctuation integral. Reaching the tree section from the assumed small representative changes bond sizes by at most a constant depending on $L$.

For each coarse bond $c$, solve $M_c(U)=V_c$ for a selected crossing bond $b_0(c)$ at its block interface. This is the nonlinear version of the independent coordinates of CMP 119 (1.14)–(1.16), p. 249. At the identity the linearized average is the average of the sums along the paths from one center to the other. The endpoint segments lie inside the two blocks. A translated straight segment in the coarse direction crosses their common face once; a fixed face bond is crossed for exactly $L$ of the $L^4$ starting sites. Thus its derivative is $L^{-3}I$ in the oriented Lie algebra. The reference path in the logarithmic expression (0.4) cancels at first order against its prefactor. A solved crossing bond belongs to just one coarse constraint. By finite-dimensional inversion on this one patch, for data near the identity there is a unique local analytic solution for the selected bond as a function of the remaining links and $V_c$, with derivatives through order three bounded by $C_{N,L}$. All constraints can be solved at once with the same bounds. Denote the resulting link chart by $U(q;V)$, for independent non-tree, nonsolved bond coordinates $q$. A derivative in $q_i$ affects only a bounded patch, and the number of affected coordinates per link is bounded in terms of $L$.

**Gap for the projected Green operator.** In flat coordinates $A=D_q U(0;1)v$, the tangent field has zero tree bonds and zero linearized coarse averages. On the union of the two adjacent rectangular blocks supporting a given bond, a curl-free tangent field is a discrete gradient: define its potential by path integration, with elementary squares giving path independence on the rectangle. The tree bonds force the potential to be constant inside each block; the average along any center-to-center path is then the difference of the two constants. Their difference is zero by the coarse constraint. In particular the chosen bond value vanishes. Interior bonds need just one block. Finite-dimensional norm equivalence on these finitely many patch types gives $|A_b|^2\le C_L\sum_{p\subset\mathrm{patch}(b)}|dA(p)|^2$ for every tangent field. One way to see the inequality is to quotient by the kernel of the patch curl; evaluation at $b$ annihilates that kernel. The patches have bounded overlap. Since the free components of $A$ include $v$, the flat Hessian of $\sum s_p$ restricted to this chart satisfies $m_L I\le H_{\mathrm{flat}}\le M_{N,L}I$. The upper bound uses finite range and bounded chart derivatives. Both constants are independent of the number of blocks. Curl and step coordinates here are in fine lattice units; the normalized trace only rescales the constants.

Let $H=D^2_q\sum s_p(U(q;V))|_{q_*}$. It includes the second derivative of the constraint chart against the full Wilson current. Its entries are finite-range local functions with bounded third derivatives. Thus small chart fields and coarse data give $\|H-H_{\mathrm{flat}}\|_{2\to2}\le C_{N,L}\delta$, where $\delta$ bounds all link/coarse chart sizes; bounded row and column sums imply this operator bound uniformly. With $\delta$ small the positive gap is at least $m_L/2$. On the actual constrained coordinates the inverse has an exponential kernel. Indeed for $R$ above the uniform upper eigenvalue use $H^{-1}=R^{-1}\sum_{n\ge0}(I-H/R)^n$. The $n$th power has propagation at most $nC_L$, and its entries are bounded by its $\ell^2$ operator norm $(1-m_L/(2R))^n$. Summing from $n\ge\mathrm{dist}(i,j)/C_L$ proves exponential decay. The polynomial growth of grid balls implies a uniform exponential row bound at a smaller exponent. This explicitly supplies the projected Green estimate in this one-step chart. CMP 99 Thm. 3.13, p. 426, uses $QA=0,RD^*A=0$ for its own projected operator, and Thm. 3.15, p. 432, bounds $C^{(k)}(\Lambda)$ on a buffered domain via (3.183)–(3.185). Neither can be substituted for the tree-section Hessian without identifying the slice and the second-constraint term. CMP 102 Thm. 1, pp. 278–279, supplies the unweighted minimizing orbit under its own determining-data hypotheses. These domain checks are why the local gap calculation above is necessary.

**The source force.** Write the normalized weights $1+\omega_p$ with $\omega_p=-j^{(0)}(x_p)/(2N x_0)$ in P2. In left-trivialized link coordinates $A_b=\delta U_b\,U_b^{-1}$, let $D_{U,pb}$ be the signed adjoint transport from a link variation to $\delta U_p\,U_p^{-1}$. For an oriented boundary this is the sum over occurrences of $b^{\pm1}$, with the inverse-link derivative and prefix transport for reversed occurrences. With the positive inner product $-\mathrm{Re\,Tr}(AB)$, let $F_p$ be the traceless anti-Hermitian part of $U_p$. The plaquette variation is $-\mathrm{Re\,Tr}((\delta U_p U_p^{-1})U_p)=\langle\sum_b D_{U,pb}A_b,F_p\rangle$. Therefore if $B=D_q U\,U^{-1}$, the constrained force is $B^*D_U^*((1+\omega)F)$. The adjoint $D_U^*$ is the signed covariant plaquette divergence. The exact Hessian contains derivatives of $B,D_U,F$; dropping $D B$ would drop the constraint-curvature term. Since $B^*D_U^*F=0$ at $U_*$, the $i$th forcing component replaces $\omega_p$ by $\omega_p-\omega_{p_i}$ for any plaquette $p_i$ within its patch. With $\mathrm{osc}_{C_L}\omega=\sup_{d(p,q)\le C_L}|\omega_p-\omega_q|$,
$$
 \|D_q f_{1+\omega}(q_*)\|_\infty
 \le C_{N,L}\epsilon\,\mathrm{osc}_{C_L}\omega .
 \tag{4.5}
$$
Here $f_{1+\omega}$ is the genuine constrained weighted Wilson functional divided by its common positive coefficient. The product rule for the weighted divergence contains both the weighted connection current and a difference of $\omega$; the projection and cancellation above treat both.

**Nonlinear conclusion.** There are constants $\delta_0,\omega_0,c,C>0$ depending only on $N,L$ and the fixed chart so that if $\delta\le\delta_0$, $\|\omega\|_\infty\le\omega_0$, a unique stationary/minimizing branch exists near $q_*$ and obeys
$$
 \|q(\omega)-q_*\|_\infty\le C\epsilon\,\mathrm{osc}_{C_L}\omega
 \le C_{N,L}\epsilon\,r_0 g_0^2\,a/\ell_0 .
 \tag{4.6}
$$
Indeed $q\mapsto q-H^{-1}D_q f_{1+\omega}(q)$ has derivative in the sup norm at most $C(\|\omega\|_\infty+\|q-q_*\|_\infty)$, by the local third-derivative and inverse row bounds. The ball of radius twice the inverse row norm times the right side of (4.5) is invariant and the map contracts there if $\delta_0,\omega_0$ are sufficiently small. The Hessian stays positive there by the same row bound for its perturbation. This proves uniqueness in a uniform chart neighborhood and identifies the branch as a strict local minimum on the constraint slice. In particular a spatially constant change leaves the classical constrained saddle exactly fixed.

Returning to the symmetric axial section costs only $C_{N,L}$ in (4.6). The difference in fine plaquette curvature and in the signed transported plaquette current has the same sup bound, by differentiating finite link products; a fixed number of finite differences has the analogous bound. In units of the first coarse lattice $\eta=L^{-1}$, corresponding powers $\eta^{-1},\eta^{-2},\eta^{-3}$ cost constants depending only on $L$, including in the regularity norms (2),(9),(10) of CMP 102. Thus this is a regular first-step estimate uniform in physical cutoff and torus volume. This conversion would cease to be uniform if repeated at $\eta=L^{-k}$ by the same argument.

Haar density belongs to the subsequent fluctuation integral. In the fixed tree section, Haar invariance eliminates the internal gauge volume; the independent link chart carries the product of its smooth Haar factors and the inverse Jacobians of the solved coarse constraints, one matrix determinant per crossing constraint. They are positive and smooth with bounded log derivatives per patch because the solved matrices stay near $L^{-3}I$. Hence the change of an individual log factor on the two saddles is at most $C\|q(\omega)-q_*\|_\infty$. The exponential symmetric gauge-fixing representation has its own orbit normalizers as in CMP 109 (0.14)–(0.16), pp. 254–255. These are internal to the group integral. Neither a log Haar factor nor a log constraint Jacobian is part of the classical stationarity equation (1.12). CMP 119 (1.14) explicitly retains both log densities in the fluctuation calculation. The estimate just proved controls their evaluations at the shifted saddle, not the extracted Wilson coefficient of the integrated density.

**Extension to regular first-step backgrounds with unrestricted holonomy (P).** The global near-identity hypothesis in the preceding proof can be replaced by small plaquette curvature on the all-regular first-step torus, provided $U_*$ is the unweighted constrained minimizing orbit in its regular branch. The published variational theorem provides such an orbit when its coarse data satisfy its stated hypotheses. The following local argument supplies the uniform bounds with no smallness assumption on the coarse links themselves.

First put the whole field in the tree section using gauge transformations equal to one on the coarse sites. Parametrize free links as $\exp(q_b)U_{*,b}$; the saddle is at $q=0$. On any fixed rectangular patch consisting of a bounded number of complete blocks there exists a gauge in which every link differs from identity by $C_L\epsilon$. To see this, choose a tree from a base vertex along ordered coordinate lines. Its assignment makes tree links identity. A remaining link closes a loop made of two coordinate paths and one edge. Commuting successive coordinate steps fills the loop by at most $C_L$ elementary squares; the norm of its product of transported plaquettes minus identity is at most $C_L\epsilon$ by unitarity and telescoping. Restore the prescribed internal trees in this patch, at cost at most another $C_L$ in the bound. Because the original section and final section both have those trees equal to identity, the net gauge transformation is constant on each complete block. Such local gauge changes conjugate the free tangent coordinates orthogonally and transform the fixed coarse data covariantly. We use them only to estimate a local matrix, never to replace the background by a globally flat torus. On the all-regular compatible boxes in this claim the bounded patches are embedded rectangles; one may, in particular, take arbitrarily large compatible tori with more than the fixed patch diameter in each direction. This removes any assumption about the topology or holonomy of the global field on that class.

Consequently the solved-bond equations and their derivatives are uniformly well-conditioned on every coarse interface: in its patch gauge they are $O_{N,L}(\epsilon)$ perturbations of their identity values. This proves the claimed chart bounds around $U_*$, regardless of the size of individual coarse holonomies. To check coercivity, let $A=Bv$ be any constrained tangent at the saddle. The tree bonds vanish. On the two-block patch for an interblock bond, finite-dimensional norm equivalence gives, for **any** such tree-zero field,
$$
 |A_b|^2 \le C_L\left(\sum_{p\subset{\mathrm{patch}}(b)}|dA(p)|^2+|Q_{\mathrm{flat}}A(c)|^2\right).
$$
Indeed the joint kernel of the two terms on the right is the set considered in the preceding path argument, on which $A_b=0$. For an interior bond the one-block bound omits the coarse term. In the patch gauge the derivative $D M_c(U_*)$ differs from the flat average by $O_{N,L}(\epsilon)$ in the local norm and annihilates $A$. Likewise the signed curvature derivative $D_{U_*}A$ differs from $dA$ by $O_{N,L}(\epsilon)|A|_{\mathrm{patch}}$. Here the output is trivialized by the plaquette's base point as above. Absorb these two errors after summing the patch estimates:
$$
 \sum_b |A_b|^2\le C_{N,L}\sum_p |D_{U_*}A(p)|^2+
 C_{N,L}\epsilon^2\sum_b |A_b|^2 .
$$
The left side and the covariant curvature term are invariant under the local orthogonal changes of gauge, and overlaps are bounded independently of the number of patches. For $\epsilon$ small the last term is absorbed.

For completeness, along $q=tv$ write $W_p(t)=U_p(q)$, $C_p=\dot W_p W_p^{-1}$. Twice differentiating the true action gives
$$
 \frac{d^2}{dt^2}s_p(W_p)=-\mathrm{Re\,Tr}\big[(\dot C_p+C_p^2)W_p\big].
$$
At $t=0$ the contribution of $C_p^2$ differs from $|D_{U_*}A(p)|^2$ by at most $C\epsilon |D_{U_*}A(p)|^2$. The $\dot C_p$ term contains the second derivatives of the solved links and the transported link velocities. Its trace against $I$ vanishes since it is anti-Hermitian; its remaining trace is bounded by $C_{N,L}\epsilon\sum_{i\in\mathrm{patch}(p)}|v_i|^2$ by the local chart bounds. Summing, using $\|Bv\|_2\ge\|v\|_2$ on the free components at $q=0$, and taking $\epsilon$ small proves a two-sided gap for the **actual** constrained Hessian. The finite-range inverse proof, (4.5), and the contraction proving (4.6) now apply unchanged on these regular backgrounds. Gauge-covariantly measured link, curvature, and current differences obey (4.6) also in the symmetric axial representative: the transformation from the tree chart uses only smooth block transports, and its variation is uniformly bounded in the patch gauge. Every constant in this first-step statement depends on $N,L$, the fixed choice of section and the curvature admission radius, and is uniform in the cutoff and torus volume. The field must stay a fixed distance inside the chosen regular branch if a specified hard cutoff is to remain satisfied. This is a bound for the classical saddle in the full one-step all-small region; it does not integrate out the fluctuations or identify their quadratic marginal coefficient.

**Test at the first R-return.** There is a second finite-domain inequality that does not identify the numerator and denominator backgrounds. For either of two fixed admissible domains of configurations, suppose normalized action $A_0$ is perturbed to $A_\Delta=A_0+\sum_p\Delta_p s_p$, with a real perturbation supported on $P$. For any positive base measure the log integral changes by at most $2N\sum_{p\in P}|\Delta_p|$; the same bound holds for the change of each infimum, by testing the two problems on each other's minimizing sequences. Thus the difference of the **two** saddle values, or the log of the **two** integrals' quotient, changes by at most twice this bound for identical supports and sup bounds. On a component contained in the $100M$ cube of LFR II (1.6), one clean first step has only $C(L,M)$ original plaquettes. This gives an $O_{N,L,M}(r_0)$ real Wilson-part bound there even when the two saddles differ. It assumes the same domains/measures within each individual comparison and a disturbance supported on the indicated plaquettes. For a regular fine field at a later scale a useful replacement is $\sum_{p\subset D}s_p\le C\epsilon^2|D|$ if $\|I-U_p\|\le\epsilon\eta^2$, by counting fine plaquettes and using the quadratic relation to the plaquette norm.

The decisive remaining difficulty at the actual return appears already on LFR II p. 357, (1.3)–(1.6). The current is orthogonal to variations preserving data on $Z\setminus\Lambda$, not to variations satisfying a fixed coarse constraint on the entire all-regular torus. A weighted version must bound the field extension $H_{1,k}B'$ reaching the support of $1-\zeta_0$ for these mixed constraints and control the change of the integrated action $A_k''$ of the numerator. Positivity on fixed real domains and the all-regular estimate (4.6) establish neither of these bounds. In particular, applying the elementary Wilson comparison to the entire support in fine variables would cost order $\eta^{-4}$ on each coarse component unless uniform fine curvature or an actual reserve is proved there. The large-field constraints, analytic background continuations and history-dependent terms cannot all be replaced by the fixed patch calculation.

### 4.7 The source-dependent Gaussian vacuum term at the actual first step (P)

At coarse field $V=1$, fixed positive scalar weights have the same constrained minimum $U=1$ on the regular branch: every plaquette action vanishes there and every coefficient is positive. Thus the coarse constraint, its linearization and the Haar density at the saddle are independent of the source. In the independent tree coordinates of §4.6 the normalized precision of the Wilson Hessian is
$$
 H(\omega)=B_0^*d^*M_{1+\omega}dB_0 ,
$$
up to the fixed trace factor; $B_0$ is the derivative of the solved-bond chart at identity. In first-step fine units this is an actual finite-range matrix with range $R_L$ and spectrum inside fixed $[m,M]$ for $\|\omega\|_\infty\le\omega_0<1$, by the patch estimate of §4.6. These bounds also hold on the infinite block grid by testing on finitely supported vectors. Use the same global $g_0$ to rescale both integrals. This avoids a source-dependent Jacobian from rescaling the variables.

To identify this calculation with the Wilson-dependent part of (1.15), retain the soft gauge term of CMP 119 (1.12)–(1.16). On the linearized coarse constraint split a tangent vector into its tree-section part plus $d\varphi$, where $\varphi$ vanishes at the coarse centers. This decomposition is invertible: integrate the tangent vector along internal tree paths to determine $\varphi$, and remove those tree components. The flat coarse average annihilates $d\varphi$. The Wilson quadratic form annihilates it as well. The symmetric gauge condition is a center-to-site transport, so its linearized value on $d\varphi$ is the signed endpoint potential. The Gaussian integral over $\varphi$, including its cross term with the tree-section field, is therefore independent of that field by translation. The linear decomposition and the solved-constraint Jacobians are identical for all $\omega$. This proves that the change of the full untruncated first-step Gaussian log normalizer equals $-\tfrac12\log(\det H(\omega)/\det H(0))$. The cutoffs and higher-order fluctuation factors in the compact integral are outside this identity.

For each free bond index $i$ take its color-traced diagonal entry in the matrix logarithm as an anchor. The derivative with respect to $\omega_p$ of $H$ is a uniformly bounded matrix supported on links within $R_L$ of $p$. The positive matrix logarithm gives
$$
 D_{\omega_p}(\log H)_{ii}
 =\int_0^\infty\big[(H+sI)^{-1}D_{\omega_p}H(H+sI)^{-1}\big]_{ii}\,ds .
$$
This follows by differentiation of the real resolvent formula for $\log H$; the gap bounds the integrand at zero and gives $O((1+s)^{-2})$ at infinity. Choose $R>M$. The Neumann expansion of $(H+sI)^{-1}$ in $(RI-H)/(R+s)$ gives a kernel bounded by $C(1+s)^{-1}\exp[-c\,d(i,j)]$, because the power has finite propagation and contraction factor at most $(R-m)/R<1$. Substitution gives $|D_{\omega_p}(\log H)_{ii}|\le C\exp[-c\,d(i,p)]$, uniformly in the background weights. For the original source the derivative has the extra fixed-step factor $g_0^2/(2N)$ in absolute value; summing over plaquettes that use the same site costs bounded multiplicity. This proves (4.4) for this first-step term with $h=a$.

For periodic weights lift the tree/bond choices periodically by block. The diagonal of $\log H$ on the torus agrees with that on the lift up to $C\exp[-cT/a]$. Indeed expand $\log H=(\log R)I-\sum_{n\ge1}(I-H/R)^n/n$. For $nR_L<T/a$ every closed path in the torus kernel with this range lifts to a closed path in the grid and the coefficients agree exactly. The tails in both operators are bounded by contraction to the $n$th power. The infinite-grid rule uses no reference torus. Its restrictions to a finite family of sufficiently small weights are real-analytic by the locally normally convergent logarithm series (on a sufficiently small neighborhood the same strict contraction persists). Thus §4.4 gives an $O(N_T)$ localization error for this genuine first-step Gaussian term. Translation covariance is with respect to its aligned block grid. A cell-reflection claim for the separately anchored pieces requires compatible reflection choices or averaging equivalent representations, and the scalar sampling convention of ICR-N1-3. This construction fixes the anchor (the color trace of the coordinate-diagonal matrix logarithm), the tree chart, and the cell extension of §4.4. Retain that explicit choice when using the derivative bound; the numerical bound (ii) by itself permits arbitrary bounded cell-local changes. For example at fixed real sources add $1-\cos(M_a\int_c j)$ to each cell subtraction and absorb the opposite sum in the remainder. This is bounded by $2N_T$, but its second mixed derivative on two disjoint supports in the same cell can grow as $M_a^2$. Locality on a cell of fixed side alone therefore gives no cutoff-uniform derivative or contact-local subtraction. For the first-step log determinant the exponentially localized derivative comes from its anchored definition; for higher mixed source derivatives at vanishing physical separation use higher differentiated kernels. No construction of that specified full interacting counterterm has been proved here.

### 4.8 A scalar derivative on a native returning component (P, O)

Fix the real exterior data of one component from LFR I p. 177 conditions (i),(ii). Its surrounding region $Z$ lies in a cube of side $100MR_k$; the free coarse region $\Lambda$ lies in a rectangle of side $100M$. The two are different. The denominator in LFR II (1.1) integrates $V'$ on $\Lambda$, with the internal graph $G_0$, the cutoff (1.101), and the exponent $-x^{\mathrm B}_k A(\zeta_0,U_{k,Z}(V'V_\Lambda))$. Its center $U_0=U_{k,Z}(V_\Lambda)$ minimizes the **full** fine action with exterior averages fixed on $Z\setminus\Lambda$, (1.3). The numerator instead integrates the old $h$-scale variables and the variables on $\mathbb B_0$ with graph $T_0$, its own characteristic functions, and the full exponent $A_k''$ of LFR I (1.71),(1.76),(1.99)–(1.100). This exponent includes boundary terms from the preliminary integrations. The prefactor that reconstructs the new density uses the same kernel as its denominator. The two integrals do not have identical fibers, cutoffs, actions, or measures.

**Full stationary value, including coarse motion.** Prescribe an interior coarse variation $B$ in $\Lambda$ vanishing on $G_0$, leaving all exterior determining data fixed. For fixed $B$, let $U_t(B)$ be the regular solution in the fine fiber for $A+t A_v$, where $A_v=\sum_p v_p s_p$ and $v$ is a real scalar modulation (trace normalization can be absorbed in $v$). At $B=0,t=0$, $U_0(0)=U_0$. Take $B$ in the finite chart of LFR II (1.12), before rescaling by $g_k$. For a derivative at $t=0$, a finite-cutoff local branch suffices. Its existence for regular data in the interior of the chart follows from the positive constrained quadratic inverse in the unweighted saddle expansion; the inner fiber has its gauge freedom removed and the additional free coarse Hessian has the lower bound (1.9) on $G_0$. No uniform radius for $t\ne0$ is asserted by this argument.

Let $m_t(B)=(A+t A_v)(U_t(B))$. Stationarity in the fine fiber gives $\dot m_0(B)=A_v(U_0(B))$. At the mixed saddle, the variation of its true coarse force is
$$
 D_B\dot m_0(0)[b]=
 \sum_p v_p\,D s_p(U_0)\,[H_{1,k} b] .
 \tag{4.7}
$$
Here $H_{1,k}$ is the derivative of the unweighted fine background with respect to the interior coarse data in LFR II (1.2),(1.4); $b$ is a unit coarse bond vector off $G_0$. Gauge changes between the Landau derivative in CMP 102 (190) and the axial representative add an infinitesimal gauge variation, which each $s_p$ annihilates. The factor $g_k$ in (1.2) appears when the coarse variation is $g_k b$. For $v_p\equiv v_b$ the sum in (4.7) vanishes by (1.4); thus one may subtract $v_b$ from every coefficient.

Here is a scale-uniform estimate with explicit field hypotheses. Use a regular mixed determining set of the type admitted by CMP 102 Prop. 9 on $Z$, with its block separation satisfying CMP 96 (2.59) for the reduced decay exponent used below. Require the unweighted regular bounds on its layers: on a block of mesh $s_y=L^j\eta$, $\|I-U_{0,p}\|\le C\epsilon_{\mathrm{reg}}\eta^2/s_y^2$ and the local regular charts for (190). These are CMP 102 (2),(7)–(10) for sufficiently small data $\epsilon_{\mathrm{reg}}$. In particular this subclass includes the native mixed exterior equal to identity and its sufficiently regular perturbations with the stated determining data. LFR I (1.80) by itself bounds only $p\in\Omega_k$; it cannot replace the layerwise hypothesis for every history. The estimates involve no power of the number of underlying steps.

For the source bond $b$ at the top mesh $s_b=1$, the gradient row of (190), CMP 102 p. 308, gives $C s_y^{-2}\exp[-c\,d(y,b)]$; the input cell-volume factor is one. The site output also satisfies $Cs_y^{-1}\exp[-c\,d(y,b)]$. In a regular block gauge these imply
$$
 \left|D s_p(U_0)[H_{1,k} b]\right|
 \le C\epsilon_{\mathrm{reg}}\eta^4s_y^{-4}\exp[-c\,d(y,b)] .
$$
Indeed $\delta U_b U_b^{-1}$ on a fine bond is $i\eta H_b$. In the plaquette its four oriented terms cancel their constant part to first order; differences cost $\eta|\nabla H|$, and conjugations contribute at most $C\eta s_y^{-1}|H|$ in the local regular gauge. Multiplication by the plaquette curvature gives the displayed bound. Equivalently one can differentiate the plaquette trace in a local gauge using the covariant exterior difference. A bounded number of adjacent blocks covers plaquettes meeting a face, with comparable neighboring mesh sizes by the determining-set hypotheses.

A block contains at most $C(s_y/\eta)^4$ plaquettes. In dimension four its total contribution to (4.7) is therefore $C\epsilon_{\mathrm{reg}}\exp[-c\,d(y,b)]$. The input $(L^{j'}\eta)^{-4}$ in (190) is exactly one for $j'=k$; for a lower-scale input one would have to use the corresponding discrete input volume in applying the kernel. The sum remaining here is counting measure, one contribution per determining-set center, including all layers. In particular one does **not** separately sum an $O(1)$ bound over the $k$ layers. For a tail $d\ge D$ retain half the exponential, then use (2.61) for the remaining half; the factor $1+d$ for the gradient similarly costs only a smaller exponent. CMP 96 Lemma 2.1 (2.59)–(2.63), pp. 233–234, bounds the sum of this exponential independently of depth. Physical distance between a contributing plaquette and $b$ is bounded by $C h_k(1+d(y,b))$, since every scaled step has physical length at most $C h_k$. Use the Lipschitz bound on $v$, subtract $v_b$, and absorb the first moment into a smaller decay exponent. This proves
$$
 |D_B\dot m_0(0)[b]|
 \le C\epsilon_{\mathrm{reg}}\min\{\|v\|_\infty,\;h_k\|\nabla_{\mathrm{phys}}v\|_\infty\}.
 \tag{4.8}
$$
For a restriction of the sum with $d(y,\Lambda)\ge D$ after subtraction, the bound acquires an extra factor $C e^{-c'D}$. Thus the part supported where $1-\zeta_0$ is separated from the varying bonds genuinely has the expected decay for this *direct insertion*; if subtracting a constant on just that part, retain its unweighted current term, bounded separately by LFR II (1.5)–(1.6). The constants depend on the regularity margin, $N,L,M$, the admissible separation and decay rates.

If the **physical** action is $x_k^{\mathrm B}A+tA_v$, apply the implicit equation to the remaining free coarse variables $B_t$ for the full mixed saddle. Its derivative equals $-(x_k^{\mathrm B})^{-1}(D_B^2m_0)^{-1}D_B\dot m_0$, with $m_0,\dot m_0$ in the normalized notation of (4.7). By (1.9), its $\ell^2$ inverse norm is bounded by a constant depending on $M$; at most $C(100M)^4$ free bonds appear in this graph. Equation (4.8) gives a depth-independent sup bound for $B'_0$. This proves the first derivative for the **actual freed interior graph** at regular exterior. It is stronger than estimating the bare change by the count of fine links and is invariant under the choice of internal tree representative.

**Nonzero-curvature real fine-saddle response (P, first derivative).** The full fixed-data inner fiber permits a stronger calculation than a generic undifferentiated inverse. Base the CMP 102 Landau chart at its own unweighted minimizer $U$, with $B=0$ in that chart. CMP 102 (74)–(83), pp. 289–290, give the linearized constraints $Q a=0,RD^*a=0$. The map from a linearized fluctuation to the link chart is $a-HD(a)$, with $D(0)=D'(0)=0$. Its Hessian on the tangent slice includes the quadratic term $-\langle HC^{(2)}(a),J\rangle$; it is exactly the restriction of CMP 99 (3.127)–(3.129), not the gauge-added Laplacian on all links. CMP 102 (111),(116),(170)–(171) and CMP 99 (3.139)–(3.153) therefore identify its zero-data inverse as $\mathfrak G$. The minimizer satisfies $\mathfrak GJ=0$. At zero source the infinitesimal fine response in this section is
$$
  a_v=-\mathfrak G J_v,\qquad J_v=D_U^*(vF),\qquad F=\eta^{-2}\operatorname{Im} U_p ,
 \tag{4.10}
$$
up to the fixed Lie algebra, orientation and normalized-trace conventions. The formula follows by testing the linearized stationarity equation on precisely this tangent slice. Products in the covariant plaquette divergence include oriented transports as in CMP 99 (3.7)–(3.9). Gauge-conjugated current conventions may add a fixed factor; the Wilson variation defining the sign is that of §4.6. The section, rather than an arbitrarily fixed unprojected propagator, matters here.

For an output block $y$ of step mesh $s_y$, subtract the real constant $v(y)$ in (4.10), using $\mathfrak GJ=0$. In every input block $z$, the product rule gives a part $(v-v(y))J$, plus transported neighboring differences of $v$ times $F$. Use the layer current and curvature bounds of CMP 102 (2), p. 278, and the CMP 99 chart regularity (3.35),(3.36) for the inverse on $U$: in step units $|J|\le C\epsilon_{\mathrm{reg}}s_z^{-3}$, $|F|\le C\epsilon_{\mathrm{reg}}s_z^{-2}$.  C\epsilon_{\mathrm{reg}}s_z^{-3} h_k\|\nabla_{\mathrm{phys}}v\|_\infty(1+d(y,z)).
$$
Here the difference of neighboring scalar weights has a factor $\eta h_k$ canceled by the $\eta^{-1}$ in the divergence, and $s_z\le1$. CMP 99 Thm. 3.13, p. 426, gives the undifferentiated and one-gradient estimates of (3.42) for $\mathfrak G$, with output factors $s_y^2,s_y$ and exponential decay in the mixed distance. Split the input into the determining blocks. To sum, absorb $(s_y/s_z)^3$ by spending part of the exponential: for $y,z$ on layers $j,j'$ this ratio is at most $L^{3|j-j'|}$, whereas CMP 96 (2.60) gives decay at least exponential in $RM(|j-j'|-1)_+$. Require the stage constants large enough for the surviving exponent to satisfy (2.59). Apply its counting-measure bound (2.61) after absorbing the first distance moment. The result is
$$
 s_y\|a_v\|_{\infty,\Delta(y)}+
 s_y^2\|\nabla_U a_v\|_{\infty,\Delta(y)}
 \le C\epsilon_{\mathrm{reg}} h_k\|\nabla_{\mathrm{phys}}v\|_\infty .
 \tag{4.11}
$$
A sup norm on $v$ alone gives no uniform bound for its discrete derivative in $J_v$; (4.11) is stated for the original smooth profile. This uses only the zeroth and first gradient bounds of (3.42), not its exception concerning the covariant Laplacian. For a physical perturbation of absolute size $v$ to $x_k^{\mathrm B}A$, the fine shift acquires $(x_k^{\mathrm B})^{-1}$. The full relaxed mixed saddle also moves in its interior coarse variables; add its linear lift $H_{1,k}B'_0$, controlled by (4.8),(1.9),(190). Infinitesimal gauge terms can change the representative, but drop out of each differentiated plaquette action. This proves the native fine response on the regular differentiable branch at zero source. The $\mathfrak G$ in (4.10) may be based anew at each **unweighted** minimizer, including those with real nonzero fixed $B$ satisfying the same conditions.

There is a consequent first derivative of the actual localized denominator integrand on a top-scale cutoff core. On any part of $\operatorname{supp}\zeta_0$ covered by $V$ top blocks with the regular bounds, differentiating the localized action adds $D A_{\zeta_0}(U)[a_v]$ to the direct term. Per block the two fine curvatures in this variation have sup norms $C\epsilon_{\mathrm{reg}}\eta^2$ and $C\epsilon_{\mathrm{reg}}h_k\|\nabla_{\mathrm{phys}}v\|_\infty\eta^2$ by (4.11); counting plaquettes cancels the two $\eta^2$ factors. Thus the variation on these blocks costs at most $CV\epsilon_{\mathrm{reg}}^2(\|v\|_\infty+h_k\|\nabla_{\mathrm{phys}}v\|_\infty)$. The same calculation charges $O(\epsilon_{\mathrm{reg}}^2)$ per **native** block on any lower layer. If changing charts shifts $V_\Lambda$, its first derivative is the coarse motion in (4.8) and the fine response includes its lift. This accounting both uses the unchanged underlying fine depth and exposes exactly where a count of uncontrolled lower-layer blocks would still enter.

**Fine derivative needed beyond the stationary value.** In a finite smooth gauge section write $q_t(B)$ for the fine independent coordinates, $s(q,B)$ for the unweighted Wilson functional, $f(q,B)$ for the modulated one, and $G=s_{qq}^{-1}$ on the constrained fine slice. At $t=0$, $\dot q=-G f_q$, $q_B=-G s_{qB}$, and differentiation on the same chart gives
$$
 \dot q_B=-G\{f_{qB}+f_{qq}q_B+
           (s_{qqB}+s_{qqq}q_B)\dot q\}.
 \tag{4.9}
$$
For the actual link derivative add the derivatives of the coordinate map $U(q,B)$; if $B$ itself has been recentered at $B_t$, also add $D_B^2U\,B'_0$. This is the derivative of the weighted extension $H_{1,k}b$, not the already bounded insertion (4.7). In particular for the localized saddle action one gets
$$
 \partial_t A_{\zeta_0(1+t v)}(U_t(B))\big|_0
   = A_{\zeta_0v}(U_0(B))+D A_{\zeta_0}(U_0(B))[\dot U] .
$$
Since $\dot U$ preserves the fine data, full-action stationarity rewrites the second term as $-D A_{1-\zeta_0}(U_0(B))[\dot U]$. Its $B$-derivative now requires $\dot H b$, the variations of the gauge/constraint chart and the fine Hessian appearing in (4.9). CMP 102 (188)–(190) estimates differentiation with respect to $B$ and small lower-order analytic terms in the unweighted equations. CMP 99 (3.153) is for $QA=0,RD^*A=0$; (3.185) is a distinct coarse fluctuation covariance. The regularity estimates for the constrained inverse identify the linear force (4.10), but do not by themselves identify the mixed-principal insertion $G D^*M_vD H b$ with a lower-order kernel of (189). In particular applying an unrestricted sup norm bound to $D^*M_vD$ incurs two fine derivatives. Differentiating the integrated numerator also does not reduce to (4.8).

**Flat mixed exterior, all depths (P).** With exterior identity, $U_0=1,V_\Lambda=1$ remains the mixed saddle for every positive scalar weight; the actual cutoff background satisfies its strict curvature conditions. The current and every pure-background variation vanish at this saddle, so the first-order term in the second exponential of LFR II (1.2) is exactly zero for $v$ as well. To calculate the weighted $H b$ on the native graph at this point, quotient out infinitesimal gauge transformations. In physical step units use the $\eta^4$-weighted fine plaquette energy and let $d$ be its linearized curl. Write $H_t b$ for the minimizer of $\|dA\|_{1+t v}^2$ with the same linearized determining-set data as $H_0 b$. These constraints are fixed even for varying weights at identity. Let $\mathcal P$ be the orthogonal projection in plaquette energy onto the curls of zero-data fields. Then, exactly,
$$
 d(H_t-H_0)b=-t(I+t\mathcal P M_v)^{-1}\mathcal P M_v\,dH_0 b
$$
on that closed curl subspace, by variation against every zero-data field. In particular its derivative at zero is $-\mathcal P M_v\,dH_0 b$; since $\mathcal P dH_0 b=0$, a constant can be subtracted from $v$ on the right. The inverse has norm at most $(1-|t|\|v\|_\infty)^{-1}$. The block argument giving (4.8), now applied to the squared gradient in (190), gives $\|dH_0b\|_2\le C_M\|b\|_2$, independent of depth; there are only $C_M$ input coordinates. It also gives $\|(v-v_b)dH_0 b\|_2\le C h_k\|\nabla_{\mathrm{phys}}v\|_\infty$ for a single input bond. The cutoff quadratic action changes by $O_M(|t|\|v\|_\infty)\|b\|_2^2$ for $0\le\zeta_0\le1$, by Cauchy–Schwarz and the preceding identity. The lower bound (1.9) on the actual $G_0$ graph consequently persists for small relative scalar modulation, uniformly in depth. In fact the weighted response has an exponential **block energy** tail here. At flat background $\mathcal P=d\mathfrak G d^*$, because the zero-data curvature projection solves the constrained variational equation on $Q A=0,Rd^*A=0$; infinitesimal gauge variations have zero curl. CMP 99 (3.46), p. 398, and Thm. 3.13 give $\|\mathbf1_{\Delta(y)}\mathcal P\mathbf1_{\Delta(z)}\|_{2\to2}\le C e^{-c d(y,z)}$ in the fine-volume $L^2$ norm. With $\lambda<c$ and the required separation, CMP 96 (2.61) bounds the exponential row norm of these block operator norms weighted by $e^{\lambda d}$. It is a submultiplicative norm, by the triangle inequality for the mixed distance. Multiplication by $v$ has norm $\|v\|_\infty$. The Neumann expansion above converges in this norm for $|t|\|v\|_\infty$ sufficiently small. Together with (190) for $dH_0b$ this proves exponential block-energy decay of the full flat weighted lift and of its derivative in $t$. Unlike an absolute sup-kernel estimate, (3.46) controls two derivatives with no fine cutoff loss. Summing its squares on $d(y,\Lambda)\ge D$ retains an exponential in $D$. This proves a genuine nonlinear-in-weight quadratic comparison at flat mixed exterior. For nonzero small-log exterior this is extended below by the nonlinear construction.

**Same-exponent Hölder estimate at flat mixed exterior (P).** At flat exterior there is a better estimate than energy alone for the *specified regular forcing*. We work on the actual linearized determining sets satisfying (2.1)–(2.4) of CMP 96. Dirichlet and gauge restrictions are as in CMP 99 (3.109)–(3.112),(3.139). Write $P=d\mathfrak G d^*$ and $S=\mathfrak G d^*$; $P$ is the orthogonal projection onto zero-data curls, and $dS=P$. All fine norms in this argument are in $k$-step units. Fix $0<\beta<\beta+\varepsilon<1$, away from the endpoints.

For a plaquette two-form $\xi$ use the mixed norm $X_\beta$ given by the supremum over output blocks of $s_y^2$ times the local sup plus the $C^\beta$ seminorm in coordinates divided by $s_y$. Use fixed slightly enlarged blocks for these local tests, and smooth cutoffs equal to one on the test block. At layer interfaces adjacent mesh sizes are comparable, by the determining-set separations. Smooth partitions subordinate to doubled blocks have bounded scaled derivatives through order two, bounded overlap, and the same properties after normalization by their sum. The exterior Dirichlet layer has $s_y=\eta$, so zero extensions there cost a bounded factor in these scaled norms.

Two consequences of the **unweighted** estimates will be used. First (3.42),(3.44) and Thm. 3.13 give, for $A=S\xi$,
$$
 \sup_y\left(s_y\|A\|_{\infty,\Delta(y)}
            +s_y^2\|\nabla A\|_{\infty,\Delta(y)}\right)
 \le C_\beta\|\xi\|_{X_\beta}.
$$
For the first term use the one differentiated-input kernel; for the second use (3.44) with input Hölder exponent at most $\beta$. Secondly (3.44)–(3.45) give $\|Pf\|_{X_\beta}\le C_{\beta,\varepsilon}\|f\|_{X_{\beta+\varepsilon}}$. To pass from a single input block to these bounds, split by the preceding smooth partition so that the Hölder input is preserved, transfer powers $s_y/s_z$ into the mixed distance by (2.60), then sum over centers by (2.61). These estimates hold also with a fixed sufficiently small exponential weight $e^{\lambda d(y,b)}$ on the block norms when the input is localized near $b$; spend that exponent in the local kernel before summing. The split requires the same fixed separations as in (4.11).

Here $w$ is a scalar function sampled at the base sites of forms of either degree. For cochains the following difference identity is exact:
$$
 d(wA)_{\mu\nu}(x)=w(x)dA_{\mu\nu}(x)+(C_w A)_{\mu\nu}(x),\quad
 (C_w A)_{\mu\nu}(x)=\nabla_\mu w(x)\, A_\nu(x+\eta e_\mu)
                   -\nabla_\nu w(x)\, A_\mu(x+\eta e_\nu).
$$
If $\xi=P\xi$, take $A=S\xi$, so $dA=\xi$, $QA=0$ and $Rd^*A=0$. For any link field $Y$ in the Dirichlet domain, $(I-P)dY=dH(QY)$: the unweighted constrained minimal lift at the same data has curvature orthogonal to all zero-data curls. A gauge transformation may be needed to impose the Landau condition on $Y-H(QY)$; its curl is zero and the resulting representative has the prescribed zero data, as in the gauge fixing of CMP 96 (2.98)–(2.103) and CMP 99 (3.109)–(3.112). Equivalently take the minimization on the orbit with the coarse data fixed. For $Y=wA$ this proves the commutator formula
$$
 P(w\xi)=w\xi+(I-P)C_w A-dH\big([Q,w]A\big).
 \tag{4.12}
$$
The $Q$ inside $H$ here means the actual *log-bond datum*, namely $s_j Q_j A$ on layer $j$. There is no derivative of a coarse gauge choice in (4.12).

For completeness the row norm of this coarse datum is uniform over the number of blockings. At flat background each step of either allowed geometric average is its straight-segment average plus the difference of endpoint potentials: the two within-block transports in each path contribute their averages at the two ends with opposite signs. For the normalized potential average the end term is a coarse gradient. The end potential contributed at scale $s_n$ has sup norm bounded by $C_L s_n$ times the input sup norm on its neighboring blocks. Straight-segment averaging is an average of parallel oriented segments with norm one for a potential in inverse-length units (the normalized $Q$ of CMP 95 (1.11)). Geometric averaging carries an exact gradient to the gradient of the potential evaluated at the centers by endpoint covariance. Telescoping the $j$-fold composition against the straight averages, each earlier gradient remains a gradient in the final mesh. The straight averages to its right have norm one, and its final norm is bounded by $C_L s_n/s_j$ times the original sup norm. Since $\sum_{n<j}s_n/s_j\le1/(L-1)$, the normalized row norm is bounded independent of $j$; the geometric sums of the path ranges stay within $C_Ls_j$ of the two endpoint blocks. For the straight average used in the Landau formulation the row bound holds directly. This also proves the flat instance of the commutation rule CMP 99 (3.102), p. 414. In particular the log-bond datum of $[Q,w]A$ is bounded by $C s_j^2\|\nabla w\|_\infty\|A\|_{\infty,\mathrm{patch}}$, using $QA=0$.

By (190), or the flat specialization of (3.133) with the log-bond factor in (3.110), $dH$ maps bounded log-bond data to $X_\beta$ uniformly. In this application the kernel's factor $s_z^{-4}$ is paired with the **input lattice measure** $s_z^4$ (CMP 96 (2.69); CMP 99 (3.109),(3.110),(3.126),(3.133)); the extra inverse length from converting a log bond into a normalized potential can be placed at the output by (2.60). This gives at most $C s_y^{-2} e^{-c d(y,z)}$ times the log-bond value per input center for the gradient, with the additional $s_y^{-\beta}$ for the unscaled Hölder seminorm. Consequently the last term of (4.12) is bounded in $X_\beta$ by $C\|\nabla w\|_\infty\|\xi\|_{X_\beta}$, since $s_j\le1$. The middle term is controlled as follows. The first bound for $S\xi$ makes $A$ Lipschitz on its native blocks. Thus $C_wA$, which uses **undifferentiated** $A$, is bounded in $X_{\beta+\varepsilon}$ by $C(\|\nabla w\|_\infty+\|\nabla^2 w\|_\infty)\|\xi\|_{X_\beta}$. This pays the loss in (3.45) while returning the same exponent $\beta$. No such estimate for a general rough plaquette multiplier was used. We have proved
$$
 \|P(w\xi)\|_{X_\beta}
 \le C_{N,L,\beta,\varepsilon}\|w\|_{C^2({\mathrm{step}})}
             \|\xi\|_{X_\beta},\qquad P\xi=\xi .
 \tag{4.13}
$$
The same proof with exponentially weighted block norms retains a smaller rate. In particular the commutator $P(w\xi)-w\xi$ is controlled by derivatives of $w$ alone.

Apply (4.13) to the unknown zero-data correction $\xi=d(H_t-H_0)b$ in the flat equation above. The known $dH_0 b$ has a uniform $X_{\beta+\varepsilon}$ bound and exponential localization by (190), taking the larger exponent strictly below one. Thus $\|P(w\,dH_0 b)\|_{X_\beta}\le C\|w\|_{C^2}\|b\|$. For a single bond subtract the constant $w(b)$ first, since $P dH_0 b=0$; the resulting distance moment costs only decay and the source variation bound is proportional to $\|\nabla w\|_\infty+\|\nabla^2 w\|_\infty$. For $|t|\|w\|_{C^2}$ small, the Neumann series now converges in the **same** $X_\beta$ on the zero-data subspace, uniformly over all fine depths. The corresponding zero-data Landau-potential correction is $S\xi$, so (3.42),(3.44) give its native sup and sup-gradient bounds as well. This proves the finite-real-source regularity and tail of the flat linearized lift, including on the mixed graph needed at identity exterior. It handles the smooth-forcing case that energy embedding alone cannot decide.

For a curved non-Abelian saddle, the covariant curl of an infinitesimal gauge change need not vanish; it is proportional to background curvature, and the constrained Hessian includes the current term (79). Consequently $D_U\mathfrak G D_U^*$ is no longer the exact curl projection used in (4.12). To perturb this argument one must bound the resulting defect in the same $X_\beta$ operator norm, including the variation of $Q(U)$, the gauge restoration and the chart's quadratic current. Smallness in the mixed energy norm alone does not prove that assertion. Equations (4.10),(4.11) remain valid at nonzero regular curvature for the first source derivative.

**Non-Abelian nonlinear branch about the identity determining data (P).** There is a finite-source statement at every fine depth beyond the exactly flat saddle. Take determining sets satisfying the geometric hypotheses just used. Require all prescribed group averages in a single compatible gauge to have small logarithms, with $\sup_c|B(c)|=\sup_c|\log V(c)|\le b$. This is stronger than a local curvature bound on the coarse data. It includes small nonzero real exterior data on the returning graph, and it need not hold for a generic large-field history. For any scalar $1+w$ with $\|w\|_{C^2({\mathrm{step}})}\le w_0$ there is a unique local branch in the identity chart of the **genuine constrained weighted Wilson functional**, for $b,w_0$ sufficiently small independent of depth. Its Landau chart satisfies
$$
 \|A\|_Y:=\sup_y\big(s_y\|A\|_\infty+
                      s_y^2\|\nabla A\|_\infty\big)_{\Delta(y)}
 \le Cb .
 \tag{4.14}
$$
Constants here and below can depend on $N,L,M$, the construction margins and Hölder exponents. The proof uses the nonlinear Wilson action on the group and the **original**, rather than a source-modified, nonlinear constraint chart.

In detail, set $Y_0=A'-H D(A')$, where $D$ is the analytic coarse correction of CMP 102 (47)–(56), Prop. 3. It transforms $U=\exp(i\eta Y_0)$ into coordinates whose constraints are $s_jQ_j A'=B$, $Rd^*A'=0$; see (74)–(77). Here $H$ is the unweighted flat minimal lift and $\|D(A')\|_\infty\le C e^2$ for $e=\|A'\|_Y$. Its derivative kernel with respect to the fine-volume measure is at most $C e s_y^{-3}\exp[-c d(c,y)]$ by (73). These estimates are independent of $w$, since the constraint is unchanged. The full weighted action in these coordinates is a constant plus the flat quadratic form $\tfrac12\|dA'\|_{1+w}^2$ and a nonlinear remainder. Retain in that remainder the cross term $-\langle dA',(1+w)dHD\rangle$, the square $\tfrac12\|dHD\|_{1+w}^2$, and the cubic-and-higher Wilson functional $V_{0,1+w}(Y_0)$ from the plaquette expansion. There is no unweighted background current at identity. The factor $s_p=N-\operatorname{ReTr} U_p$ changes only the fixed positive normalization of these formulas.

We claim its gradient has the form $J+d^*F$ with
$$
 \sup_y s_y^3\|J\|_{\infty,\Delta(y)}
                 +\|F\|_{X_{\beta+\varepsilon}}\le C e^2 .
 \tag{4.15}
$$
For the cross term, differencing its first argument gives $F=-(1+w)dHD$; the Hölder row of (190) bounds this by $Ce^2$ in $X_{\beta+\varepsilon}$. Differencing $D(A')$ in either term yields a current $D'(A')^*H^*d^*f$ with $f=(1+w)dA'$ or $f=(1+w)dHD$, up to sign. On input block $y$, $|f|\le Ce s_y^{-2}$. Pair $f$ against $dH(\,\cdot\,,c)$ before taking the $H^*$ adjoint: the measure of the block is $O(s_y^4)$, the gradient kernel is $Cs_y^{-2}s_c^{-4}\exp[-c_0 d(y,c)]$. Hence $|(H^*d^*f)(c)|\le Ce s_c^{-4}$. In the next adjoint the coarse measure $s_c^4$ cancels this factor; (73) and (2.61) give the required $Ce^2 s_y^{-3}$. This bypasses integration of two successive fine derivatives in the principal perturbation.

For the local Wilson remainder before substitution of $Y_0$, CMP 102 (29)–(40),(90)–(96), pp. 282–284, 291–292, give a direct plaquette estimate. At identity the term of cubic order containing a derivative is $dY_0$ paired with a sum of commutators of neighboring $Y_0$'s. Varying the undifferentiated fields costs $C e^2s_y^{-3}$; varying $dY_0$ differentiates that commutator by summation by parts. The pointwise bound uses first differences of the fields, or uses the explicit fine factor $\eta$ in (94) against one difference. Inserting $1+w$ into this calculation leaves exactly an extra difference of $w$ times $O(e^2s_y^{-2})$. All other local Taylor terms have the derivative bound of (90) times $\|1+w\|_\infty$. Thus the gradient at $Y_0$ is a current $J_0$ bounded by $Ce^2s_y^{-3}$. Composing with $A'\mapsto Y_0$ gives $J_0-D'(A')^*H^*J_0$. The same block calculation as above uses the $H$ kernel of order $s_y^{-1}s_c^{-4}$, producing at most $Ce^3 s_y^{-3}$ for the extra term. These estimates hold on a sufficiently small complex **field** chart, while $w$ stays real; the source terms depend linearly on $w$.

On the zero-data slice consider the inverse of the flat weighted Hessian applied to $J+d^*F$. For the unweighted problem $\mathfrak G(J+d^*F)$ has native bounded potential/gradient and curvature in $X_\beta$, by (3.42)–(3.45) with the same block summation. For the weighted problem the curvature obeys the equation $(I+P M_w)\xi=d\mathfrak G(J+d^*F)$. The solution in $X_\beta$ exists by (4.13), and the potential is $S\xi$ on the Landau section. This gives a uniformly bounded inverse from the two forcing norms of (4.15) to $Y$. The weighted affine lift $H_w B$ has $Y$-norm at most $C\|B\|_\infty$, by applying the flat result to the data superposition (190),(2.61). Now solve for the zero-data field $A'-H_wB$ by applying this inverse to the negative remainder gradient. On a ball $e\le e_0$ the nonlinear contribution is $Ce^2$. Its Lipschitz constant on the smaller ball is $Ce_0$: expand the analytic gradient along the line between two chart fields and use Cauchy's inequality with the available field radius, or differentiate the bounded local terms above. Choose $e_0$ so that $Ce_0<1/2$, then $b$ so that the affine lift and nonlinear term fit in the ball. Contraction gives (4.14). At fixed cutoff the constrained Hessian is nonsingular by the differentiated contraction. Continuity from $B=0$, where it is positive, shows it stays positive on this connected chart branch, proving strict local minimality. This does not assert uniqueness among remote branches where the gauge chart fails.

The linearized lift of a localized change in $B$ on this branch has an exponentially decaying $Y$ bound. Here it is essential to check localization of the differentiated remainder, not just its global Lipschitz constant. For a tangent variation near a native center $b$, give the $Y$ blocks the additional factor $e^{\lambda d(y,b)}$ with $\lambda>0$ sufficiently small. Local plaquette derivatives preserve this weight; the products in (90)–(96) have one perturbed field and the others bounded by $Ce$. $H,H^*d^*,D'$ have the block tails already checked. To verify localization of the derivative of $D'^*$, use the actual local implicit equation (49),(56), $D=C(A'-HD)$, with the scale factors included as in (68)–(73). Differentiating gives $D'=(I+C'H)^{-1}C'$. The matrix inverse has kernel (71) with its coarse measure. $C'$ acts only on the blocks of its coarse bond and obeys the local (72). Its derivative along a field $\delta Y_0$ is bounded by the same scale factor as (72) times the local scaled sup norm of $\delta Y_0$, by the local analytic chart bound and Cauchy's inequality within the fixed larger chart. Differentiating the displayed inverse gives $(I+C'H)^{-1}(\delta C')(I-HD')$. Thus the two derivatives of $D$, including the transpose term against an unlocalized $f$, localize at the block of $\delta C'$; each remaining convolution has exponential kernels and the native measures already accounted for in (4.15). A smaller exponent and (2.61) absorb the convolutions. This yields weighted $Ce$ for the derivative of the nonlinear fixed-point map, uniformly in $b$; local Cauchy estimates are used only for the fields within their admissible neighborhood. The inverse for the differentiated contraction and the affine lift therefore give
$$
 s_y|\delta A(y)|+s_y^2|\nabla\delta A(y)|
 \le C e^{-\lambda d(y,b)}|\delta B(b)|
 \quad (s_b=1).
 \tag{4.16}
$$
This uses a single top-bond data variation; the lower-layer input has its own log-bond normalization as in (190). Varying $w=t v$ as well gives $\|D_t\,D_B A\|_{Y,\lambda,b}\le C\|v\|_{C^2({\mathrm{step}})}|\delta B(b)|$ on a fixed smaller ball. To see this, differentiate the convergent weighted inverse series (4.13) and the contracted equation. The unlocalized $D_t A$ is bounded in $Y$ by $C b\|v\|_{C^2}$ from (4.14),(4.15); the mixed terms contain one localized $D_B A$. **Classical quadratic part of the sourced Wilson projection (P).** There is also an actual spatial-response calculation for the quadratic coarse term at identity. Write $K_w[b,c]=\langle dH_w b,(1+w)dH_w c\rangle$ for the Hessian of the reduced weighted classical action; $b,c$ are two top-mesh coarse bonds. This includes the same weighted fine problem that supplies the tree term in the effective action. For a real profile perturbation $v$,
$$
 D_v K_w[b,c]=\langle dH_w b,\,v\,dH_w c\rangle .
$$
Both other derivatives of the displayed energy pairing vanish: the varying lifts have zero coarse data and the weighted lifts are stationary against all such variations. By (4.12)–(4.13) each fine curvature has local norm $Cs_y^{-2}\exp[-c_0d(y,b)]$, respectively with $c$. The measure in one native block cancels $s_y^{-4}$ exactly. Consequently the contribution of a block $y$ to the derivative has absolute value at most $C\|v\|_{\infty,\Delta(y)}\exp[-c_0(d(y,b)+d(y,c))]$. Interpolate from the constant profile $w(b)$ to $w(\cdot)$ and use the first distance moment of their difference in step units. The same estimates hold along the interpolation. For a smaller decay rate this proves
$$
 \left|K_w[b,c]-(1+w(b))K_0[b,c]\right|
 \le C h_k\|\nabla_{\mathrm{phys}}w\|_\infty
                           e^{-c' d(b,c)} .
 \tag{4.19}
$$
The frozen coefficient could instead be the average on the adjacent block, at the same order. For normalized weights with $w=-j^{(0)}/(2Nx_k)$, restoring the common $x_k$ gives an absolute error $O(r_0 h_k/\ell_0)$, summable in $k$ for a uniformly smooth normalized profile. This does not identify $K_0$ with a nearest-neighbor coarse plaquette form; it is the Hessian of the fine constrained Wilson action, as required by D2. Formula (4.19) gives the classical contribution to the requested spatial estimate, including all off-diagonal bonds. Projection of the integrated compact fluctuation action and the scalar beta correction are separate.

**Flat reference with nontrivial torus holonomy (P).** The nonlinear conclusion (4.14)–(4.16) also holds for data in a uniform small *relative* chart about the averages of any flat reference connection on the same lattice. This removes a global-holonomy restriction without asserting that every regular exterior is near such a reference. On the universal cover, parallel transport of the flat field sets all reference links to identity. The deck transformations act by fixed group holonomies; Lie algebra perturbations then obey the corresponding adjoint twisted boundary conditions. One can equivalently perform the estimates directly on the periodic flat background without untwisting: CMP 99 (3.35),(3.36) hold in flat local gauges and its uniform bounds apply to that periodic background. The covariant exterior derivative satisfies $d_U^2=0$ because the plaquette transports are identity, so a pure gauge change still has zero linearized curvature. The product rule (4.12) holds in each parallel chart with unitary transports inserted in shifted link variables; scalar $w$ commutes with transition functions. The constraints are gauge covariant, Theorem 3.13 has the same constants, and the normalized block estimates for the flat geometric averages are unchanged in norm. In the CMP 102 action expansion centered at this flat $U$ the current vanishes, and each local cubic or higher term equals the parallel conjugate of the identity-background expression because its plaquettes are flat. The constraint estimates (55),(73) apply at this admissible reference. All terms in the contraction preserve the twisted periodic conditions by equivariance. Its uniqueness gives a solution on the original torus. The same proof applies to a flat reference restricted to an admissible mixed domain, with the corresponding fixed exterior constraints. The estimates for the actual return denominator below extend to this class. It still excludes locally curved regular data that cannot be placed in a small chart around any single flat reference.

**The real normalized return denominator on this nonflat class (P).** Apply (4.14)–(4.16) with determining data exterior to $\Lambda$ in the preceding small-log chart, and use the actual $G_0$ graph of LFR II (1.8),(1.9). There are at most $C_M$ free coarse coordinates even for arbitrarily fine $\eta=L^{-k}$. Fix the prescribed mixed constraints on $Z\setminus\Lambda$. With the interior coarse coordinates $q$ temporarily fixed, let $U_w(q)$ be the fine branch just constructed and let $m_w(q)=A_{1+w}(U_w(q))$ be its full weighted Wilson value. The first derivative of $m_w$ in any interior coarse coordinate is bounded by $C_M b$ on the small-log ball: one fine derivative has the localized bound (4.16), the background curvature is $O(b)$ in its scaled norms, and the block count cancels as in (4.7). Hence all fixed-order further derivatives in the finite interior variables are bounded independently of the number of fine variables by Cauchy's inequality. This argument estimates derivatives of the energy, not the potentially extensive energy itself.

At identity data the full reduced Hessian is bounded below uniformly on the graph. For the unweighted cutoff form this is precisely (1.9); the full flat form is at least as large because $0\le\zeta_0\le1$. The energy comparison for $H_w$ above leaves a positive gap for the cutoff form as well, for $\|w\|_{C^2}$ small, with constants allowed to depend on $M$. The exterior-only full critical point exists in the same chart. Indeed fix its coordinates using the flat reduced Hessian as the inverse in an implicit contraction for $D_qm_w=0$. The force at $q=0$ is $O_M(\|B_{\mathrm{ext}}\|_\infty)$, since it is zero when the exterior is identity and its directional derivative is bounded by the preceding estimates. The derivative of the contraction is $O_M(b+\|w\|_{C^2})$. Thus the displacement of the new center is at most $C_M\|B_{\mathrm{ext}}\|_\infty$. Call it $V_\Lambda^w$. The group products used to recenter the graph are analytic changes of the bounded number of coarse coordinates. This is the released interior on the native graph; we have not imposed the inner coarse averages while solving its outer minimum.

Let $\Phi_w(q)=A_{\zeta_0(1+w)}(U_w(\exp(iq)V_\Lambda^w))$, with the exterior fixed as above. The full saddle has zero derivative for every interior variation. Therefore $D_q\Phi_w(0)$ is exactly the negative of the same current pairing with $1-\zeta_0$, including **both** the weighted fine minimizer and its weighted lift. Every scale block of this last sum costs at most $C b e^{-\lambda d(y,\Lambda)}$ by (4.16) and the regular curvature bound. The cutoff geometry in LFR II (1.5)–(1.6) puts the support at the stated separation; on a component with $d(\Lambda,\operatorname{supp}(1-\zeta_0))\ge cR_k$, (2.61) yields
$$
 |D_q\Phi_w(0)|\le C_M b\,e^{-c'R_k}.
 \tag{4.17}
$$
This proves the real weighted analogue of the linear-term estimate on this regular class. The separation is an explicit hypothesis; if a different cutoff or component fails it, (4.17) has the actual distance in the exponent. Multiplying by $1/g_k$ still gives a uniform bound because $R_k\ge(\log(g_k^{-2}))^r$, $r\ge2$, on the admitted weak interval. This is distinct from the large-field rare factor $\exp[-p_0(g_k)]$.

Keep the reference step coupling $x=x_k^{\mathrm B}=g_k^{-2}$ in the denominator outside the normalized profile $1+w$; $g_k$ here is in Bałaban's units. With fixed real graph cutoffs as in (1.101), let $D_w$ be the actual denominator integral just described and $n=d(G)|\Lambda^{(k)}\setminus G_0|$ the number of independent real coordinates (free bonds, including their Lie algebra dimensions). Provided $\gamma$ is small enough that the cutoff $M_0\epsilon_k$ lies in the positive-Hessian chart and its rescaled radius $M_0\sqrt{x}\epsilon_k$ contains a fixed ball, the following bound holds:
$$
 c_M \le e^{x\Phi_w(0)}x^{n/2}D_w\le C_M .
 \tag{4.18}
$$
Indeed the Haar density of the independent coarse links is bounded above and below in their small exponential charts. Put $y=\sqrt{x}\,q$. Equation (4.17) bounds $\sqrt{x}|D_q\Phi_w(0)|$; the Hessian on the graph is bounded above and below. The lower integral over a fixed $y$-ball is bounded below by a positive constant, and the upper integral is dominated by $\exp(C_M|y|-c_M|y|^2)$ on all of Euclidean space. The two constants may deteriorate with the fixed construction constants but not with $\eta,T$, or the number of steps below $k$. This proves a real single-return estimate at arbitrarily fine depth without equating the two backgrounds in (1.1). It also makes clear that $x^{-n/2}$ is a coarse **free-coordinate** normalization, not a factor on all underlying fine sites. Only the assigned real regular branch and the stated cutoff, not all histories or analytic continuations of their quotients, are covered.

The finite-component estimate gives a **compact integrated** derivative as well. Define $I(w)=\log D_w+x\Phi_w(0)+(n/2)\log x$ and take a smooth profile variation $v$, in step units, with the real exterior data fixed. Use the same cutoff on $q=\log V'$ throughout, so it is not differentiated. Haar is independent of $w$ in these coordinates. Differentiating the positive finite integral gives
$$
 D_v I(w)=-x\,\mathbb E_{D_w}\big[D_v\Phi_w(q)-D_v\Phi_w(0)\big].
$$
At the mixed center $D_v\nabla_q\Phi_w(0)$ still has the distant-support factor in (4.17). Indeed differentiate the exact full-current cancellation *before* estimating: the explicit $v F$ term, the change of the background and of its lift, and the change of $V_\Lambda^w$ all remain inside the $1-\zeta_0$ pairing. From (4.14)–(4.16) the nonlocalized field derivative is $O_M(\|v\|_{C^2})$ in native norms, the derivative of the center is $O_M(\|v\|_{C^2})$ by the finite coarse inverse, and the mixed lift derivative still decays from its interior input. $$
 |D_v I(w)|\le C_M \|v\|_{C^2({\mathrm{step}})}.
 \tag{4.21}
$$
This estimate integrates the actual restricted positive compact return denominator, including its hard cutoff. It requires subtracting the **moving** localized saddle and counting only the free graph bonds. Bounds for a numerator with previous $R$-history terms do not follow from (4.21).

**Why the available weighted energy bound cannot close the nonlinear chart.** This can be tested on the native tangent space itself. In a flat top-mesh block away from the determination interfaces, place $m+1$ disjoint tiny plaquette circulation fields of one fixed Lie algebra color. Each is a discrete co-boundary $d^*$ of a plaquette field, so $d^*A=0$, including the gauge restriction $Rd^*A=0$. Take them separated by a few fine mesh units. Only a bounded number $m$ of coarse linear constraints $QA=0$ meet this interior patch: the support of an iterated block average is in a bounded neighborhood in top units (the lengths of its constituent paths form a geometric sum). Choose the interior patch and a compatible box with a fixed top-mesh buffer so that no finer determining layer meets these supports. A nonzero combination of the $m+1$ circulations annihilates all those constraints; normalize its maximum coefficient to one. Each circulation has link amplitude $\eta^{-1}$ on a fixed number of bonds and a covariant difference of order $\eta^{-2}$ on its support. With disjoint neighborhoods the fine-volume $\|dA\|_2$ is bounded uniformly but $\|\nabla A\|_\infty$ on this unit-mesh block grows at least as $c\eta^{-2}$. This disproves a cutoff-independent embedding from the weighted energy estimate just proved into the native small-field sup-gradient test. It does not say these spikes occur for smooth forcing of the weighted Wilson problem. To exclude them in that problem one needs a regularity estimate that uses the source regularity. In CMP 99 (3.44)–(3.45) the sup-gradient bound for a differentiated inverse uses a positive Hölder exponent on the input, and (3.45) at output order $\beta$ assumes input order $\beta+\varepsilon$. Thus unrestricted iteration of the double-gradient perturbation in a fixed Hölder norm is not supplied by the stated inequalities. For flat regular forcing (4.12) overcomes this problem, and (4.14)–(4.18) treat nonlinear small-log data about identity. Arbitrary patchwise regular exterior data and the full numerator still require separate estimates.

**Two saddle values and the integrated numerator.** For any fixed real admissible fiber $\mathcal F$ with nonnegative unweighted Wilson energy $A$, if $|\Delta_p|\le\varepsilon w_p$ and $w_p>0$, then
$$
 (1-\varepsilon)\inf_{\mathcal F} A_w\le
 \inf_{\mathcal F}A_{w+\Delta}\le(1+\varepsilon)\inf_{\mathcal F}A_w .
$$
Apply this separately to the fixed-fine-data fiber and the released-interior fiber (1.3), using the corresponding unweighted minimizing configuration as competitor. This requires one **total** energy estimate per fiber. On sets of coarse volume $V_c$ satisfying (1.80) throughout, the count gives $A(U_0)\le C_M\epsilon_k^2 V_c$. Even $V_c\le(100MR_k)^4$ gives a finite uniform bound because $\epsilon_k=g_k p_0(g_k)$ and $R_k$ is polylogarithmic. On arbitrary mixed layers the correct bound instead sums $C\epsilon_j^2$ **per scale-$j$ block**. The near-boundary fine blocks in $B_k(Z)$ may be numerous, and (1.80) is expressly restricted to $p\in\Omega_k$. Terms outside the cutoff may cancel between fibers, but bounding their absolute total by the volume of $\Lambda$ alone is unjustified. On the flat-exterior fibers at $B=0$ the comparator has zero action at every scale; for general exteriors, (4.8) is a signed localized estimate bypassing this unproved total-energy bound.

There is also a nonlinear **real direct-factor** estimate for the numerator on its active regular layers. In the printed domains carrying $\mathbb B_0$ of (1.81), take whole mixed determining blocks whose oriented variables are included in that set, with bounded multiplicity. Let $D$ be any union of these blocks where LFR I (1.96),(1.98) give the displayed layer curvature control for the real unweighted $U''_{k,Z}$. This includes individual returning regular layers without requiring all of $Z$ to have the same scale. On a layer of mesh $s_j=L^{j-k}$, the bound is $C_M\epsilon_j(\eta/s_j)^2$; hence every mixed block contributes at most $C_{N,M}\epsilon_j^2$ to $A(D,U''_{k,Z})$, regardless of $\eta$. By the first paragraph of LFR II p. 361 the number of variables $|\mathbb B_0|$ is at most $C(100MR_k)^4 N_{\mathrm{hist}}^2$; $N_{\mathrm{hist}}$ is the number of preceding clean-history steps, called $N$ there, not the gauge group rank. The hypothesis $N_{\mathrm{hist}}\le R_k$ on that page and the original interscale comparison CMP 119 (2.8) bound $\max_{k-N_{\mathrm{hist}}\le j\le k}\epsilon_j$ by $C\epsilon_k(1+N_{\mathrm{hist}})^P$ for a fixed $P$. On this class of returning histories,
$$
  \sup_{\text{real allowed fields}}\left|\sum_{p\in D}v_p s_p(U''_{k,Z})\right|
  \le C\|v\|_\infty \epsilon_k^2 (1+R_k)^{P'}
  \le C'\|v\|_\infty .
$$
The last bound uses $g_k^2$ times a fixed power of $\log g_k^{-2}$. Each block counted here must actually belong to the active set; charges in the older region retained under $T_h$, or plaquettes outside the checked regular layers, are not covered by this count. Exponentiating this pointwise bound and taking positive integrals controls adding a **direct Wilson factor evaluated on the same background and cutoff** at any real interpolation parameter. This is useful even for different real numerator and denominator competitors; neither a change of the generated $A_k''$ nor the change of the fine minimizer inside a cutoff is a direct factor.

There is a separate pointwise statement for a **direct real Wilson defect**, including configurations outside the convex chart. If a fixed event in a common fiber has a certificate $\alpha x\sum_{p\in D}s_p(U)\ge B$ from a fraction $\alpha\le1$ of the nonnegative Wilson energy, then replacing every coefficient by $x+\Delta_p$, $|\Delta_p|\le\varepsilon x$, preserves a certificate $(1-\varepsilon)B$. This uses positivity, with no fine-volume factor and no evaluation of the new minimizing curvature. The same conclusion holds for infima on the same fixed fiber if the lower bound applies to every trial configuration. At the bare step, a cell with a plaquette satisfying $\|I-U_p\|_F\ge g_0p_0(g_0)$ costs at least the corresponding fixed multiple of $p_0(g_0)^2$ in the exponent by $\|I-U_p\|_F^2=2s_p$ and the positive coefficient bound; disjoint charged plaquettes add. Higher-step real estimates in LFR II pp. 380–381 obtain a reserve including $2p_0(g_j)$ for the indicated large-field regions; any portion proved by a deterministic Wilson-energy lower bound on an unchanged event retains at least $p_0(g_j)$ under $\varepsilon\le1/2$. Terms relying instead on fluctuation-form coercivity, moving background cutoff membership, or previously integrated $A''$ must be checked separately. This argument addresses how a small direct Wilson tilt can use the original positive reserve; it cannot subtract a wrongly normalized prior vacuum term.

There is also a volume-free **marked** version of this reserve estimate. For an integer $m\ge1$, $0<\alpha'<\alpha$, $E_D=\sum_{p\in D}s_p\ge0$, and arbitrary real profiles $v_1,\ldots,v_m$ supported in $D$,
$$
 \prod_{i=1}^m\left|\sum_{p\in D}v_i(p)s_p\right|\,e^{-\alpha xE_D}
 \le \left(\frac{m}{e(\alpha-\alpha')x}\right)^m
       \prod_i\|v_i\|_\infty\, e^{-\alpha' xE_D}.
 \tag{4.26}
$$
This follows by maximizing $u^m e^{-(\alpha-\alpha')xu}$ over $u\ge0$; the maximization is scalar and does not count fine plaquettes. Thus a marked Wilson part of a positive defect integrand can retain a fixed fraction of its deterministic suppression and pay $g_j^2$ for each absolute coefficient derivative. For a localized profile difference $v(p)-v(p_0)$, the same bound uses the physical oscillation on $D$. The estimate survives integration against any remaining positive measure or after taking absolute values of separately bounded factors. It requires an actual **available** share of the Wilson energy on that same support. Replacing an entire conditional Boltzmann coefficient by half without leaving the normalization budget intact can introduce the unrenormalized fluctuation-volume cost discussed below. No such share has been verified here for source derivatives of all older non-Wilson or vacuum terms. The retained $T_h$ in (1.76),(1.99) can still contain an older large-field complement; the set $\{|A|\ge\delta\}$ in a real bond variable contains opposite deviations but excludes their midpoint, so the product-convex small-field covariance proof cannot simply be applied to that sector.

In a common fixed real representation let $d\lambda_N$ denote the numerator variables and cutoffs of (1.1), $d\lambda_D$ its denominator variables and cutoffs. If only exponents are varied and the integrals are positive and differentiable, then
$$
 \partial_t\log(N_t/D_t)
 =\mathbb E_{N,t}[\partial_t A''_{k,t}]
 +\mathbb E_{D,t}[\partial_t(x A_{\zeta_0,t}(U_t))].
$$
Here the plus sign in the second term follows from the denominator's negative Wilson exponent. Changing the charts introduces the corresponding log-Jacobian derivatives; changing hard cutoffs also introduces moving-boundary contributions. Formula (1.102) of LFR I is the integral identity for a normalized redistribution kernel. At fixed graph/exterior it continues to hold with the original positive normalized kernel while the incoming density changes: the outgoing kernel integrates to one. This keeps the denominator of this chosen redistribution fixed, but its numerator still contains the true incoming $A''_{k,t}$; the fixed kernel alone gives no exponentiation or inductive bound. The existing history profile itself also requires attention: CMP 119 (2.24) places different earlier homogeneous coefficients on different layers by means of the interior cutoffs. Their difference from $x_k$ is not controlled by the size of the *external* $j$; for example the trajectory comparison permits $x_j-x_k$ to grow with $k-j$. Their derivative scales are those of the history domains. LFR II (1.14) therefore separates $x_k$ from its localized history remainder. The fixed small-$C^2$ modulation used in (4.14) cannot be applied to the whole history coefficient merely by identifying that remainder with the external scalar. A constant-source comparison of *unnormalized* fluctuation integrals cannot supply the missing bound: even in the actual flat constrained Gaussian, multiplying all quadratic weights by $1+t$ changes its log normalizer by $-n\log(1+t)/2$, where $n$ is the number of independent transverse coordinates. In a coarse cell with a growing number of fine degrees of freedom, $n$ grows with the fine volume. Its vacuum contribution must be identified and subtracted before estimating a history return. A Laplace bound on raw positive integrals includes this term. A native estimate for the centered derivative of the integrated $A''$, the retained large-field reserve, and its lifted localization is still open. The full sourced induction cannot be iterated from (4.8) alone.

### 4.9 Traceless tensor normalization test (P for the classical variation; O for the RG consequence)

Take smooth links with anti-Hermitian geometric curvature $F$. Then $s_p=-a^4\mathrm{Tr}(F_{\mu\nu}^2)/2+O(a^5)$ and the symmetric clover gives $\widehat F_{\mu\nu}=a^2 F_{\mu\nu}+O(a^4)$ when centered at its site. With the P2 unnormalized fundamental trace the leading action in a constant metric is
$$
 -\frac{\beta}{4}\int \sqrt{\det g}\,g^{\mu\rho}g^{\nu\sigma}\mathrm{Tr}(F_{\mu\nu}F_{\rho\sigma}).
$$
A traceless perturbation of the inverse metric $h^{\mu\rho}$ varies this action by $-(\beta/2)\int h^{\mu\rho}\mathrm{Tr}(F_{\mu\nu}F_{\rho\nu})$. Thus a coefficient $j$ of this clover contraction, with the displayed pair counting, corresponds at tree level to metric shear $2j/\beta$. The stress tensor normalized as a metric derivative carries a factor proportional to $\beta$; the bare clover lacks that factor.

If the blocked anisotropic action at fixed geometric shear were known to have its sole leading tensor coefficient proportional to $\beta_k$, uniformly matched between these precise lattice clovers and an exact metric-variation operator, the ratio of terminal to initial clover coefficients would be proportional to $\beta_K/\beta_0=x_*/x_0$, which tends to zero by N2-F(iv). This would conflict with the asserted lower singular-value bound on a traceless shear subspace. However a blocked hypercubic lattice does not furnish this metric-variation identity: the blocking map, coarse lattice and sources all need matching, and cutoff artifacts of the bare clover can mix back into the marginal sector. Anisotropic diagonal and off-diagonal tensor representations also need their own projection. The calculation is a normalization test, not a disproof of the proposed bound. Establishing the needed matching or redefining the observable by its stress normalization is an additional tensor problem.

### 4.10 First integrated contribution to the scalar quadratic response (P, Gaussian part on the all-small torus)

At arbitrary underlying depth, consider the *single next integration* in the homogeneous all-regular region. Use only the minimized Wilson functional $m_w(V)$ on the incoming step-$k$ links, with the normalized scalar profile $1+w$ in the underlying fine Wilson action. This calculation gives the untruncated Gaussian Wilson part of the effective action; the previously generated $E$-terms and the nonquadratic compact integral in CMP 119 (3.28)–(3.30) are additional terms. Assume the small-log chart and margins of §4.8. Write $W$ for the output coarse data, and $V(q;W)$ for the true one-step coarse-constraint chart solving one crossing bond per coarse constraint, in an internal tree section as in §4.6. These coordinates now live on the *current* lattice of mesh $h_k$, not on the original fine lattice. Put $f(q,W,w)=m_w(V(q;W))$ and denote its local critical point by $q_*(W,w)$. The independent-coordinate matrix is
$$
 D(W,w)=\partial_q^2 f(q_*(W,w),W,w).
$$
It includes the derivative of the constraint chart against the Wilson current. Group Haar and the solved-constraint Jacobian at this point supply the smooth local measure factor $J_*(W,w)$. In these coordinates the Gaussian contribution to the normalized action, up to source-independent constants, is
$$
 \mathcal L(W,w)=-\tfrac12\operatorname{Tr}\log D(W,w)+\log J_*(W,w) .
$$
This is the independent-coordinate form underlying CMP 119 (3.33)–(3.35), p. 273, isolated from its cutoff and interaction pieces. The internal gauge-orbit Gaussian can be integrated in a tree section: expanding instead the symmetric axial gauge introduces an orbit potential whose linear gauge variable is the center-to-site gauge parameter, with the same shift for each integrand. Haar invariance and orbit translation remove that potential on the real quotient. A smooth change of independent coordinates multiplies $J_*$ by the absolute Jacobian and conjugates $D$ by the tangent matrix; the two factors cancel in $\mathcal L$. If a soft gauge or boundary convention has further factors, only this displayed quotient Gaussian is being asserted. In particular the actual separate cutoff integral of (3.33) is not replaced by an untruncated Gaussian.

Here is a direct estimate for this contribution. Bond distances in the next statements are in current-mesh units. All constants are independent of the fine depth and torus volume.
$$
 \left|\partial_{W_b}\partial_{W_c}
 \left[\mathcal L(W,w)-\mathcal L(W,w(b))\right]_{W=1}\right|
 \le C e^{-c\,d(b,c)}
     \big(h_k\|\nabla_{\mathrm{phys}}w\|_\infty+
          h_k^2\|\nabla^2_{\mathrm{phys}}w\|_\infty\big).
 \tag{4.20}
$$
The coarse derivatives are Lie algebra chart derivatives on a fixed smaller ball. Constants depend only on fixed derivative order (here at most the two displayed derivatives and those forming the Hessian), $L,N$, and chart margins. In particular the coefficient at constant $w$ can be removed exactly from the source response: $m_w=(1+w)m_0$, $q_*(W,w)=q_*(W,0)$, and $D(W,w)=(1+w)D(W,0)$. The resulting extra $ -n\log(1+w)/2$, $n$ the number of current free coordinates, is independent of $W$. This calculation concerns the variation of the one Gaussian contribution, not an evaluation of the full one-loop beta function.

We give details of the local differentiation and summation behind (4.20).
* Split scalar perturbations into a smooth partition on current unit cubes, with derivatives through order two bounded after rescaling. Let $v_z$ be supported in a fixed enlargement of cube $z$. At fixed incoming $V$, differentiation of the fine stationary value gives exactly $\partial_{v_z}m_w=A_{v_z}(U_w(V))$. In this all-small geometry the determining cells have top mesh throughout the torus. Each such cell costs at most $C\|v_z\|_\infty$ by the fine plaquette count in (4.7). Its first derivative in $V_b$ has the extra factor $e^{-\lambda d(z,b)}$ by (4.16). Bounds for finitely many further coarse derivatives follow by Cauchy in the remaining coarse variables on the smaller analytic field chart. Apply the one-localized-derivative argument with each of the $V_b$ derivatives in turn; the minimum of these exponential bounds is at most $\exp[-(\lambda/m)\sum_i d(z,b_i)]$ for $m$ derivatives. For the unmarked $m_w$ itself, sum the individual energy densities at anchors and use the same argument to obtain decay in the diameter of their input bonds for any fixed derivative order $m\ge2$. Differentiations of the finite-patch coordinate map $V(q;W)$ preserve these bounds, by the chain rule. All these arguments use the fine profile $v_z$ only linearly as a test in the envelope formula. For the actual interpolation its $C^2$ norm must fit the chart; the partition chosen above has exactly that property.
* The critical point $q_*$ and the required gapped inverse exist on a volume-independent ball. At $W=1,q=0,w=0$ the Hessian is the reduced flat kinetic form on the current zero-next-data tree fields. CMP 95 (1.65)–(1.67) compares this minimized form to the ordinary current-mesh curl energy with constants independent of the number of fine steps. On the uniform all-small grid its straight and geometric flat readouts give the same minimum for coarse data modulo coarse gradients: their difference is an endpoint gradient as in (4.12), and an arbitrary coarse potential can be supplied by a fine gauge transformation (choose fine endpoint values for the geometric average, or a block-constant scalar for the straight average). Such a transformation preserves the fine curl. This transfer of (1.67) is only needed here on the uniform grid, not at an unmatched mixed interface. The finite-patch curl inequality of §4.6 gives a lower bound $c\|q\|_2^2$ there and an upper bound $C\|q\|_2^2$. For small $w$ use the energy comparison $(1-\|w\|_\infty)K_0\le K_w\le(1+\|w\|_\infty)K_0$ by minimization in the same fixed flat fibers. The preceding analytic derivative estimates show that the exponential row norms of the Hessian perturbation from small $q,W$ are $O(\|q\|_\infty+\|\log W\|_\infty)$: differentiate in the direction of their full displacement and sum the decaying kernel. They also bound all entries by a fixed exponential. For a positive real matrix satisfying these two-sided spectral bounds and the exponential row and column estimates, conjugation by multiplication by $e^{\theta d(i,i_0)}$ changes it by $O(\theta)$ in $\ell^2$-operator norm (use row and column sums of $(e^{\theta d(i,j)}-1)|D_{ij}|$). A sufficiently small $\theta>0$ therefore gives $|D^{-1}_{ij}|\le C e^{-\theta d(i,j)}$, uniformly. Small complex chart perturbations needed for field derivatives inherit invertibility by a Neumann expansion. The fixed-point map using the identity-data inverse applied to $\partial_q f$ consequently contracts in the sup norm on a small ball for small $\log W$, exactly as in §4.6. It constructs the actual regular critical branch with $\|q_*\|_\infty\le C\|\log W\|_\infty$.
* To differentiate at the moving saddle use $D q_{*,u}=-\partial_q\partial_u f$ for $u=W_b$ or $v_z$. The right hand side has exponential decay from $b$ or $z$ by the preceding local coefficient estimates. The exponential inverse gives the same localization for $q_{*,u}$. Further derivatives of this equation are finite sums of the same kernels with additional localized inputs. Summing over the intermediate sites preserves a smaller exponential in the diameter of all external indices: for each finite product, split each exponential into two parts, use one along the connecting tree between the external indices and sum the other over the internal sites. The current-scale torus has polynomial growth uniformly in volume. These bounds also apply to the $D$ derivatives and to the sum of per-patch log Jacobians in $\log J_*$.
* Differentiate $\operatorname{Tr}\log D$ using $\delta\operatorname{Tr}\log D=\operatorname{Tr}(D^{-1}\delta D)$ and $\delta D^{-1}=-D^{-1}(\delta D)D^{-1}$. Each term after one $v_z$ and two $W$ derivatives has the preceding kernel bounds on one connected closed chain. At least one external index fixes its location, so the trace sum does not introduce a volume factor. Its absolute value is at most $C\|v_z\|_{C^2(\mathrm{step})} e^{-c'\operatorname{diam}(b,c,z)}$. The same holds for $\log J_*$ by its localization. Integrate this estimate along $w(b)+t(w-w(b))$, $0\le t\le1$. Each localized perturbation has $C^2$ norm bounded by $C(1+d(z,b))(h_k\|\nabla_{\mathrm{phys}}w\|_\infty+h_k^2\|\nabla_{\mathrm{phys}}^2w\|_\infty)$. Summation over the current cubes yields (4.20).

For $w=-j^{(0)}/(2Nx_k)$ satisfying the original physical $C^5$ bound, this Gaussian part differs from its homogeneous-source value by at most $C r_0 g_k^2(h_k/\ell_0)e^{-c d(b,c)}$ at the indicated order. In the same quadratic projection the tree contribution has the larger bound in (4.19). Neither estimate bounds the remaining log integral of the compact fluctuation interactions or its large-field histories.

### 4.11 Compact small-fluctuation integral on the native independent-coordinate cutoff

**An estimate for a positive product-cutoff law (P).** Let $y_i$ be a fixed-dimensional real coordinate at each current bond, restricted to a product of bounded convex balls $K_i$. Suppose $S$ is twice differentiable on a slightly larger product neighborhood with $\nabla^2 S\ge m I$, $m>0$, and its Hessian has uniformly bounded exponential off-diagonal row and column sums in the current-bond distance. For $\mu\propto\mathbf1_{\prod K_i}e^{-S}\,dy$, there is $\lambda>0$ depending only on these bounds such that the covariance of two bounded differentiable functions satisfies
$$
 |\operatorname{Cov}_\mu(f,h)|
 \le C\,\|e^{+\lambda d(i,b)}\partial_i f\|_{L^2(\mu;\ell^2_i)}
           \|e^{-\lambda d(i,b)}\partial_i h\|_{L^2(\mu;\ell^2_i)}
 \tag{4.22}
$$
for any anchor $b$. One can interchange $f,h$.

Here are details for hard cutoffs. At finite volume the potential can be extended beyond the slightly enlarged product keeping a positive lower Hessian bound and bounded exponential off-diagonal rows. In the application $K_i$ lie inside Euclidean balls of radius $P$ and the smooth chart and Hessian bound also hold on the product of balls of radius $4P$, since $gP$ is small. Clip each coordinate smoothly to a ball of radius $3P$, using a radial map equal to the identity up to $2P$, with bounded first derivative and second derivative $O(1/P)$. The Hessian of the composite potential has a positive term $T'^*S''T'$ and an extra on-site term from $\nabla_iS\,T_i''$. Add a smooth convex radial on-site function vanishing on $|y_i|\le P$, with its Hessian already large enough by radius $2P$ to dominate the latter term and restore the lower bound on that site's directions. Inside radius $2P$ the original tangent map is the identity, so the lower bound there comes from $T'^*S''T'$; this argument applies also to configurations with a mixture of clipped and unclipped sites. The finite-volume size of the added on-site Hessian does not enter the off-diagonal estimate below. For general product shapes this proof uses precisely the comparable-ball enlargement just stated. Approximate the hard constraints by large smooth convex on-site penalties for squared distance to each $K_i$ (mollification of squared distance followed by a limit suffices). They commute with every scalar weight on the bond index. The positive densities converge to the restricted law by dominated convergence.

For a smooth approximant on the full space, let $L=-\Delta+\nabla S\cdot\nabla$ on $L^2(\mu)$, with the added on-site potentials included in $S$. The commutation relation is $\nabla L=(L+\nabla^2S)\nabla$. Integration by parts gives the covariance as $\langle(L+\nabla^2S)^{-1}\nabla f,\nabla h\rangle_\mu$. For example solve $(L+\delta)u=f-\mu f$, differentiate and let $\delta\downarrow0$; the scalar null space consists of constants, the vector inverse has norm at most $1/m$, and the integration-by-parts identity gives the limit on the orthogonal complement of constants. Smooth compactly supported approximations justify the identities, with the confining quadratic extension providing integrability. Conjugate the vector operator by $e^{\lambda d(i,b)}$. The diagonal scalar $L$ and every on-site Hessian commute with this weight. The change of the cross-site part of $\nabla^2S$ has operator norm $O(\lambda)$ by the exponential row/column estimates, uniformly in the approximants. A Neumann estimate gives norm at most $2/m$ for the conjugated inverse. This proves (4.22), and the limit retains it for the actual product balls. Functions in this argument can be extended boundedly with the given values on the product; alternatively first prove the estimate for smooth globally bounded functions and pass to restrictions by approximation.

**Application on an all-small Wilson step (P on the stated kernel, C for a general incoming history).** Retain the complete nonlinear minimized Wilson term, the coarse constraints, gauge-fixing, Haar density, and the fixed product cutoff on the independent variables, without adding inherited polymer terms. This is the Wilson-only small fluctuation integral, including the initial bare Wilson small step when its independent-coordinate guard is used. At later depths it is the explicitly isolated part of CMP 119 (3.15),(3.16); an incoming full $E_k,R_k,B_k$ action requires the additional checks below. In the all-small core of CMP 119 the remaining cutoff (3.16), pp. 268–269, is a product of norm balls on the *starred independent bonds*. The convex estimate is applied to this very product in its defining coordinates. No gauge reparametrization that changes the cutoff is used in this step. The preceding reductions of the other characteristic functions in (3.17)–(3.21) are a separate issue when rerunning the **full sourced density**. This result estimates the indicated integral and does not presume that all those reductions already hold for every sourced history.

For clarity, choose the nonlinear solved-bond chart and the symmetric on-site gauge factor of CMP 119 (1.14)–(1.16),(3.15), with real independent fluctuation coordinates $y$ about the stationary input and common rescaling $g=g_k^{\mathrm B}$ in the notation $x=1/g^2$. The nonlinear average constraints can be solved locally in one crossing bond per coarse bond as in §4.6. Their charts have bounded derivatives on a fixed ball; the solution and its Jacobian involve the current blocks at the endpoints, and the inputs from the background have the localized derivatives established in §§4.8–4.10. The Haar log density and log solved-constraint Jacobians have bounded derivatives per patch there. Unlike the quotient-tree computation of the determinant, here the symmetric gauge rows are retained in the potential. At identity their quadratic form controls the linear center-to-site transports. The flat Hessian gap on independent coordinates follows on the same two-block patch: zero curl implies a gradient, zero center-to-site gauges make its potential constant on each block, and zero coarse average equates those constants. Finite-patch norm equivalence gives a lower bound by the sum of curl and gauge squares on this chart, including gauge directions. CMP 95 (1.67) compares the flat minimized incoming Wilson form to the current curl. Positive weights near one preserve the gap by the energy comparison; the scalar-independent gauge form supplies the other square. At nonzero small-log backgrounds and with $g\|y\|_\infty$ sufficiently small, differentiating the localized current Hessian bounds of §4.10 gives an $O(b+g\|y\|_\infty)$ change in the exponential row norm, including the nonlinear solved-bond curvature term. The Haar/constraint log Hessian in these rescaled variables has size $O(g^2)$. This proves the uniform lower Hessian bound and exponential off-diagonal rows for the rescaled **real** action throughout the product of slightly enlarged balls.

Write $P(g)$ for a polynomial in $1+\log g^{-2}$ bounding the radii of the rescaled independent coordinates, with $gP(g)$ inside the chart. Using larger polynomial bounds only in estimates, not widening the actual integration domain, suffices. The kernel and its cutoff are held fixed during the real profile interpolation. In formulas, if $F(q,W,w)$ includes the minimized Wilson and axial positive action before rescaling, use the fluctuation action
$$
 S(y;W,w)=g^{-2}\big[F(q_*(W,w)+g y,W,w)-F(q_*,W,w)\big]
             -\log J(y;W,w)
$$
in the actual local chart. The coordinate symbol $q_*+gy$ means the defining fluctuation chart at the stationary point (with the nonlinear solved bonds included in $F$), not link addition on the group. The Jacobian term includes Haar and the solved-constraint factor. The extracted coarse saddle term is handled separately by (4.19). Since the first derivative in $y$ of $F$ at the center vanishes, its Taylor remainder starts quadratically in $gy$. Uniform bounds on localized derivatives of $S$ in finitely many external $W_b,W_c$ and normalized scalar $v_z$ directions thus cost at most $C(1+P(g))^p$, for a fixed finite $p$, with no inverse power of $g$. For example Taylor's integral writes the first term as a sum of $y_i y_j$ times the local Hessian at the interpolated field; derivatives of that Hessian with localized external marks are estimated as in §4.10. Derivatives with respect to $y_i$ of these marked functions decay exponentially from their external locations. Jacobian derivatives obey the same bounds since their individual patches are finite. These estimates hold uniformly on the enlarged chart used for the barrier argument.

Let $\ell(W,w)=\log\int_{\prod K_i}e^{-S(y;W,w)}\,dy$. For one scalar direction supported at $z$ and two output directions $b,c$, differentiating gives one expectation of $S_{vbc}$ with minus sign, three covariances of the two-marked and one-marked derivatives with plus signs, and minus their third joint cumulant. The first terms have the diameter decay just discussed; the covariances obey (4.22). For three one-marked functions use
$\kappa(f,h,l)=\operatorname{Cov}((f-\mu f)(h-\mu h),l)$ and choose the singleton separated from the other two (take the closest pair first). The minimal separation of that singleton from the pair is at least half the diameter. The product has derivative controlled by polynomial bounds on the two factors and by their two localized gradients. Equation (4.22), or equivalently the single-site block inverse followed by summation, therefore bounds this cumulant by the same polynomial times an exponential of the negative diameter. This reasoning applies also when the marked functions have exponential tails rather than finite support: sum the two-site derivative bounds with the inverse's exponential block kernel. Hence
$$
 |\partial_{W_b}\partial_{W_c}\partial_{v_z}\ell|
 \le C(1+P(g))^p\|v_z\|_{C^2({\mathrm{step}})}
                 e^{-c\,\operatorname{diam}(b,c,z)} .
$$
Partition and interpolate as in (4.20). The difference from the *same-cutoff* homogeneous scalar profile at $w(b)$ is bounded by
$$
 C(1+P(g))^p e^{-c' d(b,c)}
   \big(h_k\|\nabla_{\mathrm{phys}}w\|_\infty+
              h_k^2\|\nabla_{\mathrm{phys}}^2w\|_\infty\big).
 \tag{4.23}
$$
In particular for $w=-j^{(0)}/(2Nx_k)$ the polynomial times $g_k^2$ has a finite supremum on the sufficiently weak interval, so the spatial error in this compact projected integral is $O(r_0 h_k/\ell_0)$, summable over steps. For a *constant* profile the integral is the compact homogeneous integral in these chosen coordinates and guards. Unlike the pure Gaussian term it need not reduce to a $W$-independent factor. Matching it to an independently shot flow constructed using its own coupling-dependent cutoffs remains necessary.

For this Wilson-only positive kernel one can sharpen (4.23) to the same power of $g_k$ as in (4.20). First obtain uniform moments of the independent rescaled fields under the actual hard-cutoff law. Interpolate between $S_{\mathrm{quad}}(y)=y^TDy/2$, the stationary quadratic form, and $S(y)$, with the same symmetric cutoff. The Hessian bounds and (4.22) hold along the interpolation. Taylor's formula and summability of the localized third derivatives of the current action give
\[
 \sup_i|\partial_i(S-S_{\mathrm{quad}})|\le C g(1+P(g))^2 .
\]
The scaled log Haar/constraint Jacobian adds $O(g)$ to this gradient. Thus the difference of the coordinate mean from its zero quadratic mean, by differentiating the expectation along the interpolation and applying (4.22) at bond $i$, is at most $C g(1+P(g))^2$. The latter is uniformly bounded for $g$ in the admitted interval. Tilting the resulting law by a real linear functional $\lambda u\cdot y_i$ leaves its Hessian unchanged. The second derivative of the log Laplace transform is the variance under the tilt, bounded by $C|u|^2$ by (4.22) (or the same vector inverse without weights). Integrating twice gives a centered sub-Gaussian bound, hence $\|y_i\|_{L^p}\le C_p$ for each fixed $p$, uniformly in the number of current bonds, the fine depth and the cutoff radius.

In the marked score estimates above replace the sup bounds on $y_i y_j$ or $y_i$ by these $L^p$ norms before summing the exponentially localized Taylor coefficients. The coefficients themselves are bounded on the slightly enlarged small-field chart, independently of $P(g)$; differentiating in $y_i$ costs $g$ for each new derivative of an action coefficient. For the derivative of a product of two centered scores use Hölder's inequality with $L^4$ gradients and scores rather than their suprema. Consequently all expectation, covariance and third cumulant terms have constants independent of $P(g)$. The right side of (4.23) holds **without** the factor $(1+P(g))^p$. For the physically normalized scalar $w$, the compact one-step spatial error is therefore $C r_0 g_k^2(h_k/\ell_0)e^{-c'd(b,c)}$ on this kernel. The bound does not require a change of source-dependent cutoff along the interpolation.

**Continuous cutoff comparison for a homogeneous shift.** There is a discrepancy to keep in view: CMP 119 p. 265 after (3.4) sets $\delta_k$ proportional to $g_k p_0(g_k)$, p. 267 explicitly rescales $A'_k=g_k A_k$, but (3.16), p. 268, prints $|A_k|<\delta_k$. The initial-step (1.18), p. 250, and LFR I (1.9), p. 178, use $g^{-1}\delta$ in rescaled coordinates. The product-ball estimates above require only a polylogarithmic *upper* bound on the radius, and cover either reading. An argument that a shell has exponentially small mass must specify the reading.

Here is a shell estimate on the genuine positive kernel. Vary the common independent-coordinate radius $R$ in its rescaled variables, holding $S$ fixed. For each independent site $i$ take a smooth radial vector field $h_i(y_i)$ equal to $y_i$ on the boundary of that site's ball, zero for $|y_i|\le cR$, with uniformly bounded first derivative; norm comparability permits this choice also for the matrix norm unit ball. Differentiation of an integral over a scaled ball and the divergence theorem give exactly
$$
 \partial_{\log R}\ell_R
   =\sum_i \mathbb E_{\mu_R}\big[\nabla_i\cdot h_i-h_i\cdot\nabla_i S\big].
$$
The formula is valid for convex norm balls by approximation, or by their radial parametrization. For radii $R$ comparable to the growing $g^{-1}\delta$, each summand and its data derivatives are supported where a coordinate has size at least $cR$. The sub-Gaussian estimate above gives $C(1+P)^p e^{-c'R^2}$ for their fixed $L^p$ norms including localized gradients. Differentiating twice in output data, use (4.22) and the three-mark cumulant argument; retaining a smaller shell exponent and distance exponent gives $C(1+P)^p e^{-c'R^2-c'\operatorname{diam}(b,c,i)}$. Summing $i$ is uniform. Re-expressing a nearby homogeneous theory at inverse coupling $x+\Delta$ in the old common $g$ coordinate changes the physical fluctuation radius by a relative amount $O(|\Delta|/x)$ for the specified powers of $\log x$. For $R\asymp(\log x)^p$ with $p\ge1$, this radius contribution to the projected action is $O(|\Delta|x^{-2})e^{-c'd(b,c)}$ after shrinking the sufficiently weak interval if needed.

For the literal short radius one need not invoke a rare shell. Take $h_i=y_i$ throughout the ball. Then $\partial_{\log R}\ell_R=n-\mathbb E\sum_i y_i\cdot\nabla_i S$, $n$ the real independent dimension. The constant drops out of the two external field derivatives. On a product of balls of radius $R$, the remaining local score and its external derivatives about the moving stationary chart have size $C R(R+g)$ with diameter tails, and their gradients cost $C(R+g)$. The analogous gradients of localized external scores cost $C(R+g)$; their centered values are bounded by $C R(R+g)$ by summing those gradients over the product diameter coordinatewise. Thus the covariance and third cumulant estimates give $C(R+g)^2 e^{-c'd(b,c)}$ after summing $i$. For $R\asymp g(\log x)^p$ and relative radius change $O(|\Delta|/x)$, this is $O(|\Delta|x^{-2}(1+\log x)^{2p})e^{-c'd(b,c)}$, summable on the weak trajectory of §4.2. These are estimates for the continuous radius change of the projected small kernel. The vacuum $n\log R$ need not be small, and a changing integer partition or a change of an internal gauge convention still requires separate comparison.

For a previously generated interaction $P_{\mathrm{in}}$ in the exponent, the same proof applies if its rescaled local Hessian leaves a fixed positive gap when added to the Wilson and gauge Hessian, its off-diagonal rows remain exponentially summable, and its external data and scalar derivatives have the anchored estimates used in the Taylor and cumulant calculation. These are independent induction hypotheses. For a general returning component $A_k''$ depends on earlier large-field integrations, the old gauge graphs, and both sides of the preliminary cutoffs. Its integration is not the positive product-ball calculation just performed. Positivity alone establishes neither a Hessian gap nor the required source derivatives there. No complete density is bounded from below by keeping only the small-fluctuation product.

### 4.12 Cell subtraction for the compact Wilson-only small-step vacuum (P on that kernel)

A pointwise derivative estimate for single arbitrary fine-site perturbations is stronger than needed to compare the two *smooth* profiles in §4.4. Here we construct the anchored subtraction for the compact kernel of §4.11 at $W=1$, including its nonlinear fluctuation terms. This applies to that specified kernel, not to the full sum over histories. At $W=1$ the mixed center is identically flat at every positive scalar profile; the local charts, product domain and Haar coordinate factors at the center can be fixed for the entire interpolation. Use a smooth, translation-covariant partition of unity $\psi_y$ on current unit cubes, including a bounded number of coordinates per cube. Let $\ell_T(w)$ be its full torus log fluctuation integral. Define the torus anchor
$$
 a_{y,T}(w)=\int_0^1 D_{\psi_y w}\ell_T(tw)\,dt .
 \qquad \sum_y a_{y,T}(w)=\ell_T(w)-\ell_T(0).
 \tag{4.24}
$$

**Local derivative.** For profile directions $u_z,v_y$ supported in the respective enlarged current cubes and bounded in step $C^2$, the same positive-law calculation gives
$$
 |D_{u_z}D_{v_y}\ell_T|
 \le C(1+P(g))^p\|u_z\|_{C^2}\|v_y\|_{C^2}e^{-c d(z,y)} .
 \tag{4.25}
$$
Here one needs a localized derivative of the fine saddle with respect to the *other* source, not just the first derivative of a homogeneous scalar. To see this, differentiate the weighted contraction (4.14) with a perturbation supported in cube $z$. The extra principal term is $d^*(u_z\,dA')$. Write $A'=H B+a$, where $a$ has zero data and obeys the Landau condition. $dH B$ has the extra Hölder exponent by (190), so $P(u_z dH B)$ has the required $X_\beta$ bound with an exponential weight about $z$. For $P(u_z da)$ use (4.12): the multiplication and discrete-derivative terms are now supported near $z$, the coarse commutator meets only neighboring blocks, and their lifts decay away from $z$. The remaining differentiated terms in (4.15) are local Wilson factors with a single $u_z$ or an application of the localized $H,D'$ operators. The weighted linearized contraction therefore bounds $D_{u_z}U_w(V)$ and its gradient by $C\|u_z\|_{C^2}\exp[-c d(y,z)]$ in the scaled native norms. This statement is on the *all-small torus*, so current unit cubes are native top-mesh blocks everywhere; no lower-layer cardinality assumption is concealed in their supports.

At fixed incoming $V$, $D_{v_y}m_w=A_{v_y}(U_w(V))$. A further $u_z$ derivative differentiates the fine minimizer in this localized plaquette sum, with the just-proved decay. At zero coarse data all these derivatives vanish with the curvature. One can equivalently differentiate their Taylor formula twice in coarse links as in §4.10 to obtain bounds on the rescaled compact scores with polynomial factors in $P(g)$. The direct mixed score in the expectation $ -\mathbb E S_{u_zv_y}$ thus has exponential $y,z$ decay. The remaining term is the covariance of $S_{u_z}$ and $S_{v_y}$, controlled by (4.22). This proves (4.25). Applying the product rule to (4.24) now bounds $D_u a_{y,T}$ by the local $C^2$ size of $\psi_yu$ plus a convolution of the $C^2$ sizes of $\psi_z u$ with the decay in (4.25). Uniform-moment refinements are possible, but the indicated polynomial factor suffices here because the physical profile carries the additional $g_k^2$.

**A torus-independent finite patch rule.** If $\ell_0/h_k$ is large, put a cube of radius $R=\lfloor\ell_0/(16h_k)\rfloor$ in current units about $y$ on the universal grid. Keep its fluctuation variables, and fix all outside fluctuation coordinates to zero. For smaller ratios use zero for this scale's rule; its bounded number of anchors per cell is covered directly by (4.24). Define the finite-patch anchor by the same scalar score and interpolation, using the weights on the lift and this conditional distribution. The local action for interior variables can be defined by differences from zero exterior and zero interior: integrate the classical current-variable gradient along the straight line from zero to the given interior configuration, and add the local log Jacobians. These changes are finite absolute sums. At $W=1$ the classical fine saddle for zero current field has zero plaquette action for every profile, so it adds no exterior-dependent vacuum constant to this rule.

We verify the comparison needed for this definition. In the global small-log chart on the all-small torus the unique periodic fine saddle is the lift of the unique solution of the same constrained equations with periodic data on the grid. This follows directly from the local variational equation: a compactly supported zero-data variation on the grid may be summed over periods to give a periodic zero-data variation. Conversely the unique bounded small solution on the grid is periodic by uniqueness of the contraction when its coefficients are periodic. The construction of this contraction and its local estimates on the grid uses the same absolutely summable kernel bounds as above; at flat reference the unweighted constrained inverse obeys the same local equations and exponential estimates, obtained as a limit on increasing compatible periodic boxes at fixed fine mesh, with uniqueness from the positive constrained inverse or the localized contraction. For compact test directions the positive curl-space variational equation and the decay uniquely determine the limiting inverse on its fixed section. Therefore coefficients and derivatives evaluated at identical periodic configurations agree with their lifted values. A derivative in one periodic current coordinate is the sum over translated inputs per period; by translation invariance this is precisely the full-grid force at one copy, obtained by summing the localized fine energies. Summability permits the rearrangement. No equality between an isolated interacting polymer coefficient on two different tori is needed.

Conditional on outside current variables the torus law in the patch has the original product cutoff and a principal Hessian block with the same gap. Changing outside variables along a line changes the expectation of the anchor score by a direct derivative and a covariance with the derivative of the conditional potential. A derivative in an individual exterior coordinate at $i$ costs $C(1+P(g))^p e^{-c d(i,y)}$: the direct term has the localized score derivative of §4.11, and in the covariance the gradient in an interior coordinate $j$ of the boundary score is the off-diagonal Hessian $S_{ji}$, which decays from $i$. Use (4.22) and sum over $j$. These statements hold for every permitted exterior interpolation; the polynomial sup bounds suffice and do not require a bound on the mean at a nonzero boundary. Replacing all exterior coordinates by zero thus costs $C(1+P(g))^p e^{-c'R}$ per anchor. In comparing a torus patch with its lifted zero-exterior patch, the unwanted periodic copies of the patch are also at distance $\ge R$. Turn off those copies in the lifted conditional action, or first interpolate them inside larger finite boxes and then pass to the absolutely summable limit. Their contribution obeys the same exponential estimate. The score and conditional gradients used in this comparison are local derivatives of the action; no infinite total energy is being subtracted. Since $x_k\le x_*+(K-k)(b_0\log L+C_r\gamma^2)$ by N2-F(i),(ii), a fixed power of $P(g_k)$ in the copying error is absorbed by a smaller exponential in $\ell_0/h_k=L^{K-k}$. Thus this finite-patch rule is independent of the torus and has the required lift error for the stated small-sector kernel.

**Subtraction and error.** In the patch rule evaluate the source using the sampled $C^5$ extension of §4.4 from the anchor's physical cell; sum the resulting anchors inside that cell and subtract the value at zero. This specifies $L_a$ for the present contribution, including the interpolation, the chosen partition and the external fluctuation condition. To compare its profile with the actual smooth source, Taylor's formula at the projected point on the shrunken box now applies also to derivatives of order $m\le2$. The physical derivative error there is at most $C r_0\ell_0^{-m}((\mathrm{dist}(z,c)+a)/\ell_0)^{5-m}$. Multiply by $h_k^m$ to convert to the step $C^2$ norm, including the partition derivatives on each current cube. The error of $\psi_z(j-\mathrm{Ext}_c j)$ is at most $C r_0(h_k/\ell_0)^5(1+d(z,y))^5$. The finite-patch estimate (4.25) holds also with fixed zero exterior. In $w=-j/(2Nx_k)$ the factor $g_k^2/(2N)$ bounds any fixed power of $P(g_k)$ uniformly when multiplied by it. Hence the error per anchor is $C r_0(h_k/\ell_0)^5 + C e^{-c\ell_0/h_k}$. Multiplying by $(\ell_0/h_k)^4$ anchors per cell and summing the scales gives $O(N_T)$ as in §4.4.

At each fixed cutoff the compact parameter integrals, the small classical branch, and the fixed-domain anchored rule are real-analytic on a sufficiently small finite-dimensional source ball; the finite-patch rule only integrates finitely many compact variables. On the infinite grid the small classical branch is obtained by analytic contraction in the sup norm with fixed margins, so no infinite-dimensional integration is required for this assertion. Real-analyticity here uses smooth *fixed* auxiliary regulators. Cell-grid translation covariance follows from the construction. Face-reflection covariance requires the reflection-covariant source placement, a cell-symmetric partition and compatible chart/graph choices; equivalent reflected representations can be averaged when the corresponding full kernel is reflection invariant. This is a nonlinear small-sector vacuum construction. At later steps a genuine interacting incoming $E,R,B$ law and the contributions from earlier large-field regions must also meet the derivative and lift estimates. They are not included in (4.24).

### 4.13 First returning numerator with one retained large-field ancestor

**Native object and regulators.** Fix one eligible component $X$ of the actual return, with exactly one old ancestor and no new large-field birth during the required last $N_{\mathrm{hist}}$ steps. This is a history allowed by LFR I p.177, conditions (i),(ii); further primitive events can be fixed to make an individual positive summand. Write $h=k-N_{\mathrm{hist}}$, retain $T_h$ on $Z_h\cap X$, and fix the real exterior, internal graphs and full sequence of tests. The real integral (1.71) is a positive finite measure $\tau$ on $B,V_h$ and the variables still inside $T_h$. Earlier positive integrations can be disintegrated instead of averaged when a quantity on an old variable is tested. In particular no Gaussian or convex support is substituted for this law. With $U''=U''_{h+2}(\exp(i g_kB),V'')$, its exponent on pp.378–379 includes, in the printed convention,
$$
 -\tfrac12\langle DH''_{1,k,X}B,\zeta DH''_{1,k,X}B\rangle
 - A(x_k''(\cdot)\zeta_1\mathbf1_X,U'')
 + V(X^\sim,U_k)+E_k(X)-E_k(\Lambda\cap X)
 + \left(-\tfrac12d_G\log g_k^{-2}+\log\sigma_0\right)
        |(\mathbb B_0\setminus T_0)\cap X|.
$$
The first line of the integral retains $dV_h\,\chi_{h,1,2} T_h(Z_h\cap X)$. The profile $x_k''(\cdot)$ is the appropriate preexisting history coefficient. Split (1.14) as written: the residual $x_k''(\cdot)-x_k$ may be nonzero, even at zero external source. In applying positivity use the **combined nonnegative Wilson coefficient with its actual cutoff**, not its signed residual in isolation. In particular a nonempty earlier $j$-layer in the active set keeps its earlier coefficient; we do not replace that term by a copy of the $k$-layer, nor differentiate the unweighted remainder as if it were the small smooth source. The separate denominator (1.1) integrates coarse $V'$ in the other fiber on $\Lambda$, and its vacuum normalization is not this law's mass.

For the direct tests below all integration maps, partitions, graphs and sharp selectors are exactly those of the given unweighted history. Inserting a bounded score *at fixed test values* needs no moving-boundary derivative. For example one may tag a birth above $(1+\delta)\epsilon_j$ with fixed $0<\delta<1$ and a regular core below $(1-\delta)\epsilon_j$; in this tagged subevent every relevant original cutoff has its specified slack. Positivity extends an upper bound for the unmarked operator to any such subevent. This is an explicit subevent wherever nonempty, not a claim that the full native distribution satisfies a pointwise margin. The paper's fixed-guard estimates that apply to the full real operator require no such pointwise margin because no selector is moved. Extending them to a source-dependent change of a selector requires a shell estimate, as in §4.11 for the small sector.

One can verify that the strict bare birth and flat exterior used as this local test are compatible. Deep inside a fine $L$-block of an initial native defect cube, take several disjoint small plaquette circulation fields of one Cartan color, away from the block's faces. Only the coarse bonds touching that block can see their fine averages (at most eight scalar constraints in four dimensions). For sufficiently large published $L$ one can put more disjoint circulations there than affected coarse constraints. Choose a nonzero linear combination with zero first block average, and scale it until its largest plaquette is strictly above $(1+\delta)\epsilon_0$ and still in the small logarithm chart. At least one curvature is nonzero: the curls on disjoint neighborhoods cannot all vanish for nonzero coefficients. In this commuting chart the allowed path logarithms add exactly, and the implicit group mean (0.10) is the exponential of the arithmetic mean. Thus the nonlinear first coarse field is exactly identity. All fine links outside that deep support are identity; all plaquettes meeting it lie entirely inside the old fixed region. For subsequent identity determining data outside the old region, extending by identity minimizes the unfixed nonnegative Wilson part, since it is zero there. The violating test and the exterior zero-curvature tests have strict slack, persisting on finite-dimensional neighborhoods for each fixed depth. This constructs a nonempty birth with one spatial component, even if several violated plaquettes fall within that component. It does not establish every auxiliary chart test for arbitrary exteriors; the direct-mark bounds below are conditional only on an actually admitted lineage, and never differentiate the history set.

**An absolute marked bound on the genuine positive component.** Denote $M=\tau(1)$ and $\mu=\tau/M$ when $M>0$. Real (1.89), LFR II p.387, gives for this individual positive contribution
$$
 M\le T'_k(X)1\le\exp[-c_0 p_0(g_k)],\qquad c_0=2/(1+\beta_0)>1
 \tag{4.27}
$$
with the published small comparison parameter. If $S$ is any real bounded score on the *actual* old integrations, set $M_t(F)=\tau(e^{tS}F)$ and $\mu_t=M_t/M_t(1)$. Direct differentiation, without any variance or mixing assumption on $\mu$, gives

$$

A field-independent log vacuum increment $b(t)$ multiplies the mass by $e^{-b(t)}$; it cancels in $\mu_t(F)$ but contributes $-b'(t)M_t(F)$ to the mass derivative after normalization. Retaining this distinction is essential because (1.89) is an absolute bound, not a lower bound on $M$.

For instance, choose any nonempty part $D$ of an earlier $j<k$ layer among the $\mathbb B_0$ blocks actually covered by (1.96),(1.98), and insert the direct source

 S_D=-\sum_{p\in D} v_p\,\zeta_1(p)s_p(U'').
$$
This retains the nonempty $x_j-x_k$ profile inside the old law. The layer bound in §4.8 gives $\|S_D\|_\infty\le C\|v\|_\infty \epsilon_k^2(1+R_k)^P$, uniformly over the retained primitive older variables. Indeed each native block contributes $C\epsilon_j^2$, there are at most the counted $|\mathbb B_0|$ blocks, and the original fixed-step interscale comparisons apply for $k-N_{\mathrm{hist}}\le j\le k$. This yields a mass-weighted connected response and a finite real direct-tilt bound for the genuine old operator at arbitrary **underlying** depth, with at least $e^{-p_0(g_k)}$ left for $\gamma$ small. **Scheduled older integrations and a localized bare tilt (P for the fixed positive history).** There is a second test that retains *all* the scheduled intermediate positive integrations even when a primitive old variable is outside its regular chart. With all maps and cutoffs frozen, expand that positive history before its scalar and field-dependent factors are reallocated to separate polymers. For any bounded bare mark $S_{\mathrm{bare}}=\sum_{p\in D_0}v_p s_p(U)$ on the old variables, a partial integral conditioned on its remaining data inserts the exact conditional expectation of $e^{tS_{\mathrm{bare}}}$. More explicitly, if a scheduled integration has positive kernel $K_i$ and incoming unperturbed weight $w_i$, its log response obeys
$$
 b_{i+1}(t)=
 \log\frac{K_i(w_i e^{b_i(t)})}{K_i(w_i)},\qquad b_0(t)=tS_{\mathrm{bare}}.
 \tag{4.29}
$$
Only evaluate this ratio where the denominator is positive. Iteration, or the simple order inequality for each positive kernel, gives $|b_i(t)|\le |t|\|S_{\mathrm{bare}}\|_\infty$ at every stage, not that bound multiplied by the number of steps. Its first derivative is the corresponding conditional expectation of $S_{\mathrm{bare}}$ under the tilted law and its second derivative is the conditional variance. The statement remains true for a finite positive sum of histories, and for the old large-field integration itself. If at a stage a fixed-on-the-fiber log scalar $c_i(t)-c_i(0)$ is removed, (4.29) acquires that subtraction. It telescopes only for identical scalar normalizations along the specified path; a term depending on the remaining exterior data must be kept inside the next kernel. This describes how earlier small-step and old nonconvex integrations jointly enter an actual raw marked numerator. It does not imply that the localized D1 subtractions are that same scalar on both fibers.

There is an especially simple exact case. If every link of a marked bare plaquette is in the retained old variables, then its factor $e^{t v_p s_p}$ is measurable before the old integration and passes through every scheduled integration in the regular exterior unchanged. The integrated *raw* $A_k''$ gains precisely the bare log factor for that plaquette, in a frozen representation, however nontrivial the unmarked $x_j-x_k$ layer is. When some marked links have already been integrated, (4.29) is the necessary conditional replacement. This argument neither equates the two return fibers nor assigns an arbitrary source-dependent scalar to the D1 vacuum recipe.

In particular a source supported on the underlying fine plaquettes of a *bare birth* of bounded native size has $\|S_{\mathrm{bare}}\|_\infty\le2N|D_0|\|v\|_\infty$, where $|D_0|\le C_{N,L,M}R_0^4$ for one initial native birth cube. Old fine variables need not be convex or Gaussian. For a smooth admissible source supported in a cube of physical diameter $r$, zero extension and Taylor's integral remainder give $\|v\|_\infty\le C r_0 (r/\ell_0)^5$. For a single bare birth $r\le C a R_0$, so $\|S_{\mathrm{bare}}\|_\infty\le C r_0 R_0^9 L^{-5K}$, uniformly bounded (and tending to zero at fixed terminal coupling). The latter follows from $x_0\le C_*(K+1)$ on either admitted comparison trajectory. More generally a source confined to one earlier native cube of diameter $C h_j R_j$ gives the bound $C r_0 R_j^9 L^{9j-5K}$; this proves uniformity for $j\le\alpha K$, any fixed $\alpha<5/9$, using the logarithmic growth of $R_j$. Thus for such a localized genuine bare source, (4.28)–(4.29) give a finite-source bound with the old mass, independent of all the intervening fine integrations, in the **fixed positive lineage**, including its $j<k$ Wilson profile. The count does not give this bound for a source with general physical support or for $j$ near $K$. The absolute bound here is on the raw numerator divided by its original fixed reference factors (or the bare mark lifted to the unweighted $\tau$); identifying it with the new numerator after recomputing the sourced regional action and $E_k(X)-E_k(\Lambda)$ is an additional requirement.

**An actual compact counterexample to a normalized-score shortcut (P).** Let a bare $P_0$ cube $B$ contain a fine plaquette outside the first threshold, and $E_B$ denote the event that the cube has at least one such plaquette in the exact homogeneous Wilson ensemble. Let $f_B$ be a nonnegative smooth lattice restriction equal to one on its tested plaquettes and zero outside a doubled cube, with values at most one. The identity $s_p=\|I-U_p\|_F^2/2$, and equivalence of fixed matrix norms, give $\mathbb E[S_f\mid E_B]\ge c g_0^2p_0(g_0)^2$, $S_f=\sum f_B(p)s_p$, with the fixed normalized-trace conversion absorbed into $c$. The event has positive probability: a fine link in a neighborhood of a nontrivial group element with appropriate other links near identity realizes a strict violation on a nondegenerate torus; Haar measure of this open set is positive.

For the unrestricted real Wilson measure, put $n$ equal to the number of fine sites. The event that all links lie in an identity ball of radius $c/\sqrt\beta$ has Haar mass at least $\exp[-C n(1+\log\beta)]$ and Wilson exponent at most $Cn$. Hence $\log Z(\beta)\ge-Cn(1+\log\beta)$. Monotonicity of $\mathbb E_\beta\sum s_p$ follows from its derivative being minus its variance. Therefore
$$
 \mathbb E_\beta\sum s_p\le\frac{2}{\beta}
             [\log Z(\beta/2)-\log Z(\beta)]
 \le Cn(1+\log\beta)/\beta .
$$
Translation and hypercubic symmetry imply the same bound per plaquette after division by their number. The doubled native cube has $O(R_0^4)$ fine plaquettes, independent of the total torus volume. Consequently
$$
 \left.\partial_t\log\mu_{\beta,t f_B}(E_B)\right|_{0}
 \ge g_0^2\left[c\,p_0(g_0)^2-C R_0^4(1+\log g_0^{-2})\right].
 \tag{4.30}
$$
Here $\mu_{\beta,t f_B}$ is the actual compact lattice probability under the scalar tilt, as opposed to a Gaussian model. For thresholds with $2p_0>4r+1$, as is available in the large-exponent hierarchy used in LFR II p.383, the right side divided by $g_0^2$ grows without bound. Thus bounding the **normalized** logarithmic birth score by $C g_0^2\|f_B\|_\infty$, even with only this one direct large-field event, is false. One can reduce the source parameter interval to fit the physical $C^5$ ball for each cutoff. The bound does not disprove a statement with a fixed physical test-function norm: the derivative norm of this localized $f_B$ grows as the native cube shrinks. Nor does it disprove a bound on the rare mass times that score. It shows concretely why dropping $M$ in (4.28), or importing the independent-coordinate small-sector covariance for the old conditional law, would not supply the required induction.

**First unverified normalized contribution.** In (1.71) the old $T_h$, its gauge and history cutoffs, the truncated weighted Wilson action and fluctuation term have different derivatives. After normalizing the reinjection denominator as in (4.21), an exterior-dependent score still includes

and the derivatives of the lower-scale input actions inside $T_h$, of the joined determining-set background in (1.14)–(1.20), and of its chart/Jacobian. Multiplying an operator by a source-dependent scalar leaves its normalized old conditional law unchanged while altering its absolute derivative, which is why a proof must identify the vacuum subtractions explicitly. The real results (4.27)–(4.30) verify direct old Wilson marks, localized raw tilts, and certain mass-weighted connected responses, but do not close the missing normalized estimate or its iteration.

## 8. Summary

A scalar source changes each Wilson plaquette coefficient by its value at that plaquette. On a sufficiently weak terminal interval the assumed homogeneous flow places small constant shifts on shot trajectories. A clover tensor insertion has a bound by nearby Wilson plaquette actions on the compact configuration space.

For a regular first step the true constrained Wilson minimum changes by curvature times local source oscillation, independently of cutoff and volume. On a returning component the derivative at zero source obeys the native mixed-scale bounds. Data near a flat reference, with nontrivial torus holonomy, allow stronger conclusions. A commutator estimate preserves the Hölder exponent, yielding a finite-source nonlinear classical branch with an exponentially localized lift. The actual return denominator then has a uniform normalization and a bounded derivative after subtracting its moving saddle.

On the all-small chart the spatial quadratic response separates into the classical Wilson term and a compact fluctuation integral. The latter has exponential covariance on its cutoff and uniformly bounded rescaled-field moments. Its spatial error is bounded by the source gradient times the squared running coupling and scale length. Direct old Wilson marks retain the rare factor, but the full inherited numerator still lacks this estimate.

For the vacuum subtraction, a smooth extension from each cell reproduces neighboring source values to fifth order. Localized source scores define a torus-independent finite-patch subtraction for the compact Wilson-only small-step kernel. Conditioning on exterior fields bounds its copying error exponentially, and the remaining scale errors are summable per cell. The complete scalar and tensor interfaces remain open. Prior interacting increments, history-dependent Wilson coefficients, the exact large-field normalization and tensor mixing still require control. Downstream reconstruction also needs a specified subtraction with derivative bounds at separated supports. An extensive bound on fixed-cell remainders alone does not determine the fields’ contact terms at coincidence.

# Raw core sources in the large-field operation

## 1. Result, its physical source, and its exact scope

This note proves one previously missing **actual mixed-operation module**. A literal source on still-explicit original plaquettes inside old large-field cores can be carried through Balaban's complete erased-R integration, localization and connected exponentiation. Its old group variables are retained until they are really integrated. Its compulsory mass is never divided out. The constants of the resulting source-dependent R activities are uniform in the age of those old variables and the number of historical integrations.

This is more than the conditional-expectation contraction of Integrated Raw Source Transport. The present result supplies a complex-exterior analytic output and its actual connected flat-field vacuum for this class of raw inputs. The proof uses the detailed large-field capacity accounting of **LFR II**, followed by the actual polymer expansion (1.90)–(1.100).

**Full R1 is still open.** The source in this result is the strict raw-core part of the literal source. It is a history-dependent part of an exact splitting. The other part contains source plaquettes crossing different component operations, and the source readouts that earlier regular integrations have already turned into E/B activities, including crossings of an integration boundary. It is not legitimate to replace those readouts by fresh source plaquettes or to reopen their integrated variables and call them raw. Section 6 identifies the remaining transition precisely. This note also does not prove the source-local, torus-independent cell counterterm required by R2.

The primary sources used are:

- **B119:** T. Bałaban, CMP 119 (1988), 243–285.
- **LFR I:** T. Bałaban, Large field renormalization I, CMP 122 (1989), 175–202.
- **LFR II:** T. Bałaban, Large field renormalization II, CMP 122 (1989), 355–392.

Journal page numbers are used. The only interface assumed remains N2-F. A separate statement in §3 explains the weak-coupling choice and the remaining running-coordinate dictionary R0.

Write \(s_p=N-\operatorname{Re}\operatorname{Tr}U_p\), so \(0\le s_p\le2N\). At a fixed history \(\mathfrak h\), a **strict raw-core plaquette** has all four original link variables still explicit and its entire link support contained in one actual component operation \(T'_k(X)\) at the transition being studied. The complete plaquette must stay in that one operation until its first actual integration. A plaquette crossing two component operations, or whose links have already been integrated at different times, is assigned to the complementary readout even when its remaining links are raw. Let \(\mathcal P_{\mathrm{raw}}(\mathfrak h)\) be the union of these unambiguously component-owned plaquettes. Set

\[
 V_{\mathrm{raw}}(z;\mathfrak h,U_0)
   =\sum_{p\in\mathcal P_{\mathrm{raw}}(\mathfrak h)}z_p s_p(U_0),
 \qquad
 \|z\|_\infty=\max_p|z_p|.
 \tag{MR.1; P: definition of the actual raw readout}
\]

The physical scalar specialization is \(z_p=j^{(0)}(x_p)\), with precisely the source anchor in the preamble. No power of the lattice spacing or bare coupling has been inserted. For every history, the full physical insertion splits exactly into this expression and the complementary old readout:

\[
 V_z(U_0)=V_{\mathrm{raw}}(z;\mathfrak h,U_0)
                   +V_{\mathrm{rest}}(z;\mathfrak h,U_0).
 \tag{MR.2; P}
\]

The equality holds before integration, and remains valid when each side is carried through the same frozen history. The theorem below concerns the transition on the first part, including its interactions with the source-free background activities. Mixed derivatives involving \(V_{\mathrm{rest}}\) require its own admission.

For the source-free R kernel \(T'_k(X)\) of LFR II (1.71), define \(T'_k(X;z)\) by multiplying its actual historical integrand by \(\exp V_{\mathrm{raw}}\) on the raw plaquettes owned by that component. This operation leaves the source-zero maps, selectors, cutoffs, gauges, old masses and all non-source factors fixed. It does not replace the old integration by the normalized fresh reference.

The main conclusions are: a uniform small bound for \(T'_k(X;z)\) on a fixed source polydisc and the printed complex exterior domain; the corresponding connected R expansion; a uniform all-order source-jet bound for its nonboundary R activities; and the explicit local-in-the-polymer identity subtraction generated by that expansion. These conclusions are proved below under Balaban's admitted geometry and an explicit strengthened finite cost budget that the same small-coupling construction permits.

## 2. Original-variable ownership and the actual capacity count

### 2.1 Where the original variables are still present

B119 (1.29), p.254, explicitly contains the integration in the original group variables \(V_0\) on \(\Omega_1^c\). B119 (2.2)–(2.3), p.255, identify the lowest determining layer and the large-field region as

\[
 \Gamma_0=\Omega_1^c,\qquad Z_1=\Lambda_1^c,\qquad
 \Lambda_1\subset\Omega_1,\qquad \Gamma_0\subset Z_1.
 \tag{MR.3; L: B119 (1.29), p.254; (2.1)–(2.3), pp.254–255}
\]

A plaquette all of whose original links remain in this group integration is therefore an actual bounded function of those integration variables. For the factorized R theorem it is included in (MR.1) only when its entire support is integrated by one component operation, as stipulated in §1. Gauge changes in the later operations do not alter its trace. If a previous operation has integrated one of its links into a conditional effective activity, or if its still-explicit links cross distinct current component operations, the plaquette is outside this strict class. Its old physical readout belongs to \(V_{\mathrm{rest}}\).

For each candidate original plaquette, record its membership in the initial raw core and retain that label until its first actual integration. This record is only a capacity owner; it does not override the requirement that its four links belong to one actual operation. A candidate which ceases to satisfy that requirement is transferred, with its actual source function, to \(V_{\mathrm{rest}}\). All source occurrences on a strict raw plaquette use the same record. In particular, a second source derivative does not create a second geometric owner. Distinct raw plaquettes are counted distinctly. A new regular or R activity produced by integrating one of them is thereafter an analytic activity, and is never counted again as an original raw plaquette.

This accounting works for arbitrary old age. It refers to the original layer, not the volume of the current image of the old core. Current coarsening, mergers and addition of collars do not manufacture new original source variables. If a large-field component is partitioned into later integration pieces, only whole plaquettes contained in one piece are assigned to that piece. Crossing plaquettes remain complementary readouts. The budget is partitioned according to the distinct complete plaquettes actually assigned; the entire original budget is not recharged for every piece.

### 2.2 Counting at the layer where the variable exists

Use \(d=4\). For a history restricted to one current component, let \(|Z_j|_j\) count its volume at layer \(j\), where one layer-\(j\) lattice cell has side \(L^ja\). Let \(d'_j(Z_j)\) be LFR II's linear-size functional based on \(MR_j\)-cubes. Its normalization is fixed by the actual volume estimate used on p.380, immediately below (1.75). Introduce

\[
 H(\mathfrak h)=\sum_{j\le k}M^4R_j^5d'_j(Z_j).
 \tag{MR.4; P: the printed large-field capacity, with its coefficient separated}
\]

The source-free proof bounds the normalization and vacuum costs by a constant times this expression. For the strict original core, simple counting gives

\[
 \begin{aligned}
 n_{\mathrm{raw}}(\mathfrak h)
 &=|\mathcal P_{\mathrm{raw}}(\mathfrak h)|\\
 &\le C_L|Z_1|_1
 \le C_L M^4R_1^4d'_1(Z_1)
 \le C_L H(\mathfrak h).
 \end{aligned}
 \tag{MR.5; P}
\]

Here \(C_L\) includes the six plaquette orientations and the fixed factor \(L^4\) converting the layer-one volume to original fine volume. The middle inequality is exactly the cube-volume versus \(d'_1\) comparison used in the paragraph on p.380. Since the allowed large-field components contain several cube layers, no isolated zero-size component is lost in that comparison. Equivalently one may insert \(d'_1+1\) throughout and absorb the extra constant using the minimum component size.

The same count holds for a fixed-width original-lattice collar that remains entirely inside the actual original-variable integration region. A collar of width at most a fixed number of fine links intersects only a bounded number of neighboring \(MR_1\)-cubes per original cube; the constant \(C_L\) changes by a fixed factor. A collar is not permission to introduce a variable which has already been integrated.

For every complex plaquette array \(z\),

\[
 |V_{\mathrm{raw}}(z;\mathfrak h,U_0)|
 \le 2N\|z\|_\infty n_{\mathrm{raw}}(\mathfrak h)
 \le C_{N,L}\|z\|_\infty H(\mathfrak h),
 \qquad
 |e^{V_{\mathrm{raw}}}|
 \le e^{C_{N,L}\|z\|_\infty H(\mathfrak h)}.
 \tag{MR.6; P}
\]

This estimate is pointwise on the actual old group variables. It does not require an inverse old mass, a conditional energy expectation, or a small old plaquette. It is also not a physical-volume bound. Its purpose is to charge the genuinely raw mark to a large-field capacity which is already present in the complete R estimate.

The proof extends to a strict-core clover whose entire original link support remains in the same integration. The clover and its curvature-pair trace are bounded local functions of a fixed number of group matrices, so replacing \(2N\) in (MR.6) by a constant depending on \(N\) and the fixed number of tensor components gives the same result. This observation is only raw tensor admission; it is not tensor marginal renormalization.

### 2.3 What this count does not cover

An already integrated regular source may depend on a large number of fine source anchors, even when its current carrier is small. There is no implication that its absolute norm is bounded by (MR.5). The actual E/B readout and its vacuum subtractions must be used.

In particular, the following two assertions are different:

\[
 \#\{\text{still-explicit original source plaquettes in an old core}\}
       \le C H(\mathfrak h),
 \qquad
 \#\{\text{all original source plaquettes influencing its effective action}\}
       \le C H(\mathfrak h).
 \tag{MR.7; P for the first assertion; O and not used for the second}
\]

The first is (MR.5). The second has not been proved and is generally not the appropriate quantity after regular integration. The theorem in this note never substitutes the second count for the first.

## 3. Stability of the printed capacity estimate under a fixed extra cost

The point of (MR.6) is that it changes a coefficient in the actual large-field ledger. It does not change the Wilson action or any Gaussian normalization. This is different from spending a fixed fraction of every original Wilson plaquette action; §6 explains why that latter argument incurs a divergent Gaussian vacuum.

### 3.1 The ledger on the page

Let \(P_j=p_0(g_j)\). Separating the harmless fixed coefficient in the first exponential, LFR II (1.79), p.383, has the form

\[
 T'_k(X)1\le
 \sup_{\mathfrak h}
 e^{A_0H(\mathfrak h)}
 \prod_{\text{ordinary births }(j,i)}
   e^{-c_bP_j^2(d'_j(Z_j^{(i)})+1)-2P_j}
 \prod_{\text{preparatory births }(j,C)}e^{-P_j}.
 \tag{MR.8; L: LFR II (1.79), p.383, with fixed constants renamed}
\]

The supremum is over the actual admissible histories. Its compatibility conditions, including (1.76), are retained. The products are not replaced by independent arbitrary defects. The positive cost includes all intermediate normalization, vacuum, Gaussian-integral and admissible-history entropy costs specified on pp.380–383.

**Lemma 3.1 (P: coefficient-stable large-field ledger).** For every fixed finite \(A\ge A_0\), the same admissible construction admits a sufficiently small coupling ceiling \(g_A>0\) for which the right side of (MR.8), with \(A_0\) replaced by \(A\), is bounded by

\[
 \exp\{-2(1+\beta_0)^{-1}P_k\}
 \tag{MR.9; P}
\]

on an erased component \(X\). The constants are independent of \(k\), the age and size of its earlier components, and the number of histories in the sum. Here the geometric and coupling-comparison conditions are exactly those used in LFR II (1.80)–(1.89).

**Proof.** We spell out why the coefficient may be changed. In the proof of (1.80), all positive costs are linear in that coefficient. The sum of the future costs until a component satisfies the erasure criteria is bounded in (1.81), p.385, by
\(C_{\mathrm{geo}}A M^4L^5R_j^6(d'_j(Z)+1)\).
The first birth supplies \(cP_j^2(d'_j(Z)+1)\), as stated in (1.82). If no new birth occurs, (1.83) subtracts exactly the next positive cost from the reserved future budget, so the inductive bound is preserved. If a preparatory birth occurs in a small component, the extra cost up to its next eligibility is at most \(C_{\mathrm{geo}}A M^4R_j^6\); its reserve is \(P_j\), as explained at the top of p.386.

For a merger, (1.84)–(1.87), p.386, use a spanning tree of the components being joined. Removing one endpoint reduces the argument to a merger of two previously treated components. The two remaining first-birth reserves supply at least \(2(1+\beta_0)^{-1}P_{j+1}\). The discrepancy between the two future-cost sums and the cost for their union is at most \(C_{\mathrm{geo}}A M^4R_{j+1}^6\), precisely the last error in (1.88), p.387. Thus the following finite set of sufficient inequalities closes the same induction:

\[
 cP_j^2\ge C_{\mathrm{geo}}A M^4L^5R_j^6,
 \qquad
 (1+\beta_0)^{-1}P_j\ge
              C'_{\mathrm{geo}}A M^4L^5R_j^6.
 \tag{MR.10; P: sufficient reserve hierarchy for the coefficient \(A\)}
\]

The constants may be enlarged once to cover the finitely many initial and preparatory cases. No step in this induction requires that \(A\) have the original numerical value.

B119 (2.4)–(2.5), p.255, give \(P_j=A_{\!p}(\log g_j^{-2})^{p_0}\) and
\((\log g_j^{-2})^r\le R_j<L(\log g_j^{-2})^r\).
Choosing the fixed construction exponents with \(p_0>6r\), as is permitted when the proof asks for \(p_0\) sufficiently large, makes both inequalities (MR.10) valid for all \(g_j\le g_A\). Alternatively, a preselected construction need only be checked directly against (MR.10); no exponent change is necessary if those inequalities already hold. All construction constants are fixed before the source is introduced.

The induction gives the nonnegative reserve \(\kappa_k(X)\) at erasure, as on p.387. Keeping its first-birth remainder gives exactly (MR.9), which is the conclusion (1.89) with the enlarged coefficient. This proves the lemma. ∎

For a source radius \(r_c>0\) fixed in advance, use the lemma with

\[
 A=A_0+C_{N,L}r_c.
 \tag{MR.11; P}
\]

The old-source factor (MR.6) is then paid within the same history estimate. There is no growth of \(A\) with the age of a source: its original plaquette has one owner and is inserted once in the historical integrand, rather than once per scale.

### 3.2 Relation to the allowed N2 trajectory

The small-coupling ceiling in Lemma 3.1 is a condition on the running coordinate used in the actual source-free construction. It is not automatically the numerical \(\gamma\) of N2-F.

For completeness, N2-F alone allows entry into any prescribed **numerical** weak interval. Put \(D=b_0\log L>0\),
\(M_*=\max(0,C_r\gamma^2-D)\), and choose
\(A_*\ge\max(\gamma^{-2},2C_r/D)\).
If \(x_K>A_*+M_*\), then

\[
 x_k\ge x_K+\tfrac12D(K-k),\qquad k\le K.
 \tag{MR.12; P from N2-F}
\]

To prove this, whenever \(x_k\ge A_*\) the recurrence gives
\(D/2\le x_k-x_{k+1}\).
If a last index had \(x_j<A_*\), its next value would be below \(A_*+M_*\), since every upward jump is at most \(M_*\). The remaining values decrease and could not reach \(x_K>A_*+M_*\). Thus there is no such index, and summing the decrement proves (MR.12).

A sufficiently small permitted terminal \(g_*\) enforces any chosen \(A_*\), uniformly in cutoff. This is the same last-crossing argument as main §4.28. To apply it to \(g_A\), the physical running coefficient must first be identified with N2-F's coordinate. That is R0. The bare conversion \(X_{B,0}=2N^2x_0\) alone does not settle the running conversion.

Accordingly, Lemma 3.1 is a proved statement about the specified admitted R operator. Its application to every step of the proposed Wilson trajectory remains **C on R0 and its admitted geometric dictionary**. Selecting one sufficiently weak terminal coupling is permitted by the existence argument (see [lattice_conventions.md](lattice_conventions.md) §5); no claim for all terminal couplings in the original N1-S interval is made here.

## 4. The actual complex marked kernel and its two jets

### 4.1 Domination before any quotient

The paragraph preceding (1.73), LFR II pp.379–380, is particularly useful. For its analytic exterior extension, only the displayed quadratic form and \(V(X^-)\) change. After the real exterior \(v\) is fixed, the remaining effect is a small complex exponential. In operator notation this says

\[
 T'_{k,c}(X)F=T'_{k,r}(X)(e^\sigma F),
 \qquad \sup|\sigma|\le\varepsilon_c,
 \qquad
 |T'_{k,c}(X)F|\le T'_{k,r}(X)(e^{|\sigma|}|F|).
 \tag{MR.13; L: LFR II (1.73), pp.379–380}
\]

Here \(T'_{k,r}\) is the actual positive real kernel, including all its old masses and historical integrations. The construction makes \(\varepsilon_c\) a fixed small constant on the stated complex domain. It is not an arbitrary bound assumed for a new sourced minimum. The geometry and field domain are those of the frozen source-zero operation.

For the strict core, \(V_{\mathrm{raw}}\) is a function of precisely the old real group variables in this representation. It is independent of the exterior complex continuation. Multiplying the historical integrand by this function and using (MR.6) gives, for \(|F|\le1\),

\[
 \begin{aligned}
 |T'_{k,c}(X;z)F|
 &\le e^{\varepsilon_c}
       T'_{k,r}(X)(e^{|V_{\mathrm{raw}}|})\\
 &\le e^{\varepsilon_c}
       \sup_{\mathfrak h}
       e^{(A_0+C_{N,L}r_c)H(\mathfrak h)}
                      \{\text{the birth factors in (MR.8)}\}\\
 &\le e^{\varepsilon_c}
               e^{-2(1+\beta_0)^{-1}P_k},
 \qquad \|z\|_\infty\le r_c .
 \end{aligned}
 \tag{MR.14; P}
\]

The second line applies the same integrations and history sums as in the proof of (1.79), after the extra history cost has been inserted. It is not the invalid assertion that a supremum bound for an unweighted total mass bounds an arbitrary reweighted mass. The history-dependent majorant is inserted *before* that proof is applied, and Lemma 3.1 supplies the enlarged cost estimate.

**Theorem 4.1 (P: actual raw-core admission through erasure).** Under the explicit R-operator and reserve hypotheses of §§2–3, \(T'_k(X;z)\) is jointly holomorphic in the printed complex exterior variables and the finitely many source values on its actual original raw core. Within each fixed admissible coordinate chart, this means bounded-operator holomorphy on the space of bounded old-variable test functions \(F\) which are independent of the exterior parameter. If \(F\) also depends on that parameter, its joint holomorphy is required to conclude holomorphy of the output; the numerical domination (MR.14) itself only requires the displayed absolute bound. The operator obeys (MR.14), uniformly in cutoff, volume and history depth. No logarithm of \(T'_k(X;z)1\) and no inverse of its old mass are needed.

At finite cutoff all source functions in (MR.1) are bounded on the old compact group factors. Their exponential is entire in the finite source variables. Equation (MR.14) supplies a common majorant on smaller source domains; the source-zero analytic integrand in (MR.13) supplies exterior holomorphy on its fixed domain. Differentiation under the actual integrals and finite history sums proves the theorem. The same uniform estimate permits the normally convergent infinite connected series in §5. ∎

### 4.2 Exact first and second old-source derivatives

For literal raw directions \(f,g\), put \(V_f=\sum_{p\in\mathcal P_{\mathrm{raw}}}f_ps_p(U_0)\). The source-zero first and mixed second density jets are

\[
 T'_{k,f}(X)F=T'_k(X)(V_fF),
 \qquad
 T'_{k,fg}(X)F=T'_k(X)(V_fV_gF).
 \tag{MR.15; P}
\]

The second expression is an actual joint old-field moment. Replacing it by a product of two conditional means would discard its contact and covariance contributions. If a normalized fresh reference \(q_v(du)\) is appended by the R reset, the three kernels are exactly \(q_vT'_k1\), \(q_vT'_kV_f\), and \(q_vT'_k(V_fV_g)\); normalization of \(q_v\) cancels none of these old factors.

Cauchy's formula applied to (MR.14) yields, for \(m\ge1\) and finite source arrays \(f^1,\ldots,f^m\),

\[
 \left|D_z^mT'_{k,c}(X;0)
                [f^1,\ldots,f^m]F\right|
 \le m!\left(\frac e{r_c}\right)^m
        e^{\varepsilon_c-2(1+\beta_0)^{-1}P_k}
                        \prod_{i=1}^m\|f^i\|_\infty,
 \quad |F|\le1.
 \tag{MR.16; P}
\]

Indeed choose source-parameter radii \(r_c/(m\|f^i\|_\infty)\) and use \(m^m\le e^m m!\); one may first shrink the radii and then pass to the limit. Directions of zero norm give the zero derivative. The number of original source anchors does not occur in (MR.16), because their once-owned capacity cost was paid before Cauchy differentiation.

On an inner complex field chart, a further Cauchy estimate gives the corresponding exterior derivatives with the usual inverse chart margin. Each determining direction is normalized by its own layer radius. This proves a fixed-domain analytic operator bound. It does not silently replace a later reconstruction map by a current unit-lattice coordinate, or establish the complete endpoint-kernel norm of R1 for source readouts not in (MR.1).

### 4.3 A useful limited collar extension

The same proof covers a local raw readout which is a fixed-degree holomorphic polynomial in one component's old group matrices and **directly admitted exterior link matrices**, provided its actual formula obeys a uniform bound \(C\) per source anchor on the same complex field domain and its anchors satisfy (MR.5). Its raw variables must all belong to that one actual component operation; another component's variables cannot be renamed exterior links in this criterion. For example, a plaquette made from old group factors and directly supplied exterior links with operator norms and inverse norms at most \(B\) has holomorphic Wilson value bounded by \(N+NB^4\). Its source factor therefore satisfies (MR.6) with a changed fixed constant.

This is a proved algebraic extension with a checkable readout condition. A reconstructed old link whose dependence on the exterior has not been shown to stay in that domain does not satisfy the condition merely because its real value is unitary. A link which has already been integrated is not such a factor at all. Those cases are addressed in §6.

## 5. Actual connected R activities and the vacuum they generate

### 5.1 The marked expansion is the printed expansion

Keep the source-free interaction terms \(V(Y)\) from LFR II (1.68)–(1.72), with their original locality, analytic domains and small bounds. In the curly bracket of (1.72), replace each erased-component operation \(T'_k(X_h)\) by the actual \(T'_k(X_h;z)\) of §4. The source on a compulsory surviving component \(Y_i\) remains in that component's actual kernel outside this bracket.

The Mayer interpolation and component grouping are exactly those in (1.90)–(1.91), p.388. They give

\[
 \mathcal C_k(z)=
  1+\sum_{r\ge1}\ \sum_{\{X'_1,\ldots,X'_r\}\ {\rm compatible}}
             \prod_{\ell=1}^r F_k(X'_\ell;z).
 \tag{MR.17; P: the actual marked version of LFR II (1.90)}
\]

Each \(F_k\) is defined by the full sum and integral of (1.91), with the replacement just specified. No new conditional expectation is introduced. The factorization used there remains valid because raw source plaquettes have been assigned to their actual disjoint old-core integrations. To retain the boundary distinction, put

\[
 |F_k(X';z)|
 \le c_1(X')\,e^{-(1+\beta_{\rm geom})\kappa
                          d_{k,\mathcal Y}(X')},
 \quad
 c_1(X')=
 \begin{cases}
 e^{-P_k},&X'\cap\mathcal Y=\varnothing,\\
 \alpha^{1/3},&X'\cap\mathcal Y\ne\varnothing .
 \end{cases}
 \tag{MR.18; P}
\]

Here \(\beta_{\rm geom}\) is LFR II's positive decay-margin parameter; it is not the Wilson coupling. The constants and geometry are those in (1.97), pp.389–390. A boundary carrier is not assigned full tree decay through a compulsory surviving component.

### Theorem 5.1. Raw-core R output and source jets — P

The actual series (MR.17) has the normally convergent connected representation

\[
 \mathcal C_k(z)=
       \exp\left\{\sum_XR'_k(X;z)\right\}.
 \tag{MR.19; P: marked version of LFR II (1.98)}
\]

Each connected activity is analytic on the original complex field domain, jointly holomorphic in the source values which it actually sees, and localized in its printed carrier with the old source positions retained. It obeys

\[
 |R'_k(X;z)|\le
  Cc_1(X)e^{-(1+\beta_{\rm geom}/2)\kappa
                           d_{k,\mathcal Y}(X)}.
 \tag{MR.20; P}
\]

After the same division into boundary and nonboundary terms as on p.390, the nonboundary R activities satisfy

\[
 |R_k^{\rm raw}(X;z)|\le
       C e^{-P_k}e^{-\kappa d_k(X)}.
 \tag{MR.21; P}
\]

The boundary activities keep (MR.20) with the boundary alternative for \(c_1\). They are not claimed to satisfy (MR.21).

**Proof.** The absolute majorant (MR.18) is exactly the convergent polymer majorant used for (1.98)–(1.99). The connected series is a sum of finite products of the marked polymer activities with the same combinatorial coefficients and exclusion relations as the source-free series. The printed convergence proof uses their absolute bounds, the fixed compatibility geometry and the spare decay margin. These are unchanged; in particular, it uses no positivity of an individual sourced activity. The same absolute majorant therefore controls the series uniformly on every smaller source and field domain. Uniform convergence of holomorphic finite-cutoff activities proves joint holomorphy and the connected identity. Localization is inherited from the union of their actual carriers. The division and the replacement of the exterior-aware size by \(d_k(X)\) for the nonboundary class are precisely the geometric argument leading to (1.100). This proves (MR.19)–(MR.21). ∎

There is no assertion that an individual conditional mass \(T'_k(X;z)1\) has a uniform zero-free source disk. The logarithm in (MR.19) is the connected logarithm **after the sum over erased components**. Its existence follows from the small-activity expansion. This distinction is essential and avoids the rare-sector obstruction in Integrated Raw Source Transport.

Cauchy's formula gives, for the nonboundary activities,

\[
 \bigl|D_z^mR_k^{\rm raw}(X;0)
                  [f^1,\ldots,f^m]\bigr|
 \le C\,m!\left(\frac e{r_c}\right)^m
       e^{-P_k}e^{-\kappa d_k(X)}
                      \prod_i\|f^i\|_\infty .
 \tag{MR.22; P}
\]

The corresponding boundary estimate replaces the last two factors by the right side of (MR.20). On \(\|z\|_\infty\le r_c/2\), a one-variable Cauchy estimate also supplies a factor \(C\|z\|_\infty/r_c\) for the difference \(R_k^{\rm raw}(X;z)-R_k^{\rm raw}(X;0)\). Thus a sufficiently small physical source changes the admitted activity bounds by a proportionate amount.

### 5.2 The connected flat subtraction, including the second contact

For a nonboundary carrier, define its newly generated source vacuum by

\[
 \begin{aligned}
 v_k(X;z)&=R_k^{\rm raw}(X;1,z)-R_k^{\rm raw}(X;1,0),\\
 \widehat R_k(X;U,z)&=
 R_k^{\rm raw}(X;U,z)-R_k^{\rm raw}(X;U,0)-v_k(X;z).
 \end{aligned}
 \tag{MR.23; P: actual connected identity extraction}
\]

Here \(1\) means the flat regular background, with its auxiliary determining-field variables set to their flat values. This is the source-dependent version of the actual R subtraction in B119 (2.30), p.260. The value \(v_k\) is a deterministic function of the source values occurring in the localized connected activity. It is not an exterior-dependent conditional mean.

This subtraction includes the complete connected second source coefficient generated at this operation. To see its content, write the finite-polymer bracket as \(\mathcal C\), and differentiate its connected logarithm:

\[
 (\log\mathcal C)_f=\frac{\mathcal C_f}{\mathcal C_0},
 \qquad
 (\log\mathcal C)_{fg}
   =\frac{\mathcal C_{fg}}{\mathcal C_0}
                  -\frac{\mathcal C_f\mathcal C_g}{\mathcal C_0^2}.
 \tag{MR.24; P, evaluated at zero source}
\]

The numerators contain (MR.15) when the two marks meet in one old operation, and the genuine product terms when they occur in different operations. The connected expansion distributes (MR.24) over its connected carriers. Its flat value, not a product of separately normalized old means, is what enters the second derivative of \(v_k\).

The subtraction removes the constant field coefficient only. Gauge invariance under constant SU(N) conjugation eliminates the linear field coefficient: its color-linear functional would have to be invariant on \(\mathfrak{su}(N)\), hence is zero. In any normalized regular local chart \(\xi\) whose unit ball lies in the admitted outer domain, ordinary Cauchy estimates therefore give

\[
 |\widehat R_k(X;\xi,z)|
 \le C e^{-P_k}e^{-\kappa d_k(X)}
              \|\xi\|^2\,\frac{\|z\|_\infty}{r_c},
 \qquad \|\xi\|\le\tfrac12,\quad\|z\|_\infty\le\tfrac12r_c.
 \tag{MR.25; P}
\]

The same estimate holds for source jets with the factorial factor in (MR.22). The entire quadratic field output is retained; no scalar-only projection is silently imposed. Identifying its full dimension-four coefficients after an old/current reconstruction, with all endpoint-layer norms, is a separate marked-image task.

Boundary terms remain boundary terms, as in B119 (2.40)–(2.42); this note does not subtract their flat values into a global vacuum. ## 6. The reconstructed readout which still prevents full R1

### 6.1 The remaining first and second kernels are explicit

At an actual mixed transition, split the incoming source density after the already performed regular integrations and their prescribed vacuum extractions into the strict raw factor and the complementary density. Write its complementary two-jet as

\[
 D_{\mathrm{rest}}(\lambda,\mu;v,\zeta)
 =1+\lambda B_f(v,\zeta)+\mu B_g(v,\zeta)
                   +\lambda\mu B_{fg}(v,\zeta)+\cdots .
 \tag{MR.26; P: definition of the actual complementary density jets}
\]

No pointwise logarithm is assumed here. The variables \(\zeta\) are the actual old variables still present; \(v\) is the actual exterior. The two mixed density outputs are

\[
 \begin{aligned}
 T'_fF&=T'_k\bigl[(V_f+B_f)F\bigr],\\
 T'_{fg}F&=T'_k\bigl[
   (V_fV_g+V_fB_g+V_gB_f+B_{fg})F\bigr].
 \end{aligned}
 \tag{MR.27; P}
\]

The pure raw terms \(T'_kV_f\) and \(T'_k(V_fV_g)\) are now bounded by Theorem 4.1. The exact remaining kernels are \(T'_kB_f\) and
\(T'_k(V_fB_g+V_gB_f+B_{fg})\), together with the associated intercomponent localization terms. If a complementary source logarithm has jets \(A_f,A_{fg}\), then \(B_f=A_f\) and \(B_{fg}=A_{fg}+A_fA_g\); (MR.27) agrees with the old-readout formula of [Tensor quadratic admission and the marked transition](local_sources/tensor_quadratic_admission.md). Nothing is replaced by a source on the fresh reference variable.

The quantities \(B_f,B_{fg}\) are not arbitrary convenient centers. They are the density jets produced by the actual prior constrained regular integration, including its full contact coefficients and the specified identity subtraction. An estimate for another conditional innovation does not estimate them.

### 6.2 Why an integration-boundary collar is not enough

There are four distinct situations.

1. A fixed-width collar still contains only actual original group variables belonging to one component operation. Its source anchors are covered by the count (MR.5), with a changed constant. The theorem applies.
2. A source plaquette has raw variables in two different component operations. Its bounded real value does not make it a factor of either operation alone. It belongs to the complementary intercomponent readout and requires its own localized marked interaction. The present factorization theorem does not cover it.
3. A boundary plaquette is an explicit finite product of one component's raw group factors and directly admitted analytic exterior links. Its literal formula can be checked against §4.3. The theorem applies once that direct formula and its uniform complex bound are verified.
4. A plaquette contains a link already integrated over the regular domain \(\Lambda_1\), or a link reconstructed through a nontrivial old/current minimizer or chart. Its readout is a conditional effective function or a composition whose complex image must be checked. Increasing the drawn collar does not recover that old integration variable. This is the main outstanding case in (MR.27).

B119 (1.22), pp.251–252, already exhibits the issue at the first step: a boundary coordinate change includes the background correction \(H_{1,\Lambda_1}\), and the subsequent conditional integration (1.26), p.253, produces E and B activities. The exact source must be carried through both operations. Analyticity and a bound for the source-free effective action do not, by themselves, identify the marked conditional readout.

The obstruction is therefore not an unexplained inverse mass in the strict-core operation. That inverse has been avoided. It is the admission of these actual reconstructed and already integrated source jets on the correct mixed field domains.

### 6.3 A sufficient mixed-input condition, stated without assuming it

The proof above gives a concise sufficient condition for extending this R-stage module. Suppose the **actual** \(D_{\mathrm{rest}}\), after the designated deterministic vacuum and marginal subtractions, is jointly holomorphic on the same source and exterior domains, factors or localizes with the printed component geometry, and obeys, for each history,

\[
 |D_{\mathrm{rest}}(z;v,\zeta)|
       \le e^{C_{\mathrm{rest}}H(\mathfrak h)}
       \quad (\|z\|_\infty\le r_c).
 \tag{MR.28; O for the actual remaining readout}
\]

Also require that its intercomponent source-dependent \(V(Y;z)\) terms obey the localized analytic bounds used in (1.68), with fixed spare constants. Under these explicitly additional hypotheses, replacing \(A\) in Lemma 3.1 by
\(A_0+C_{N,L}r_c+C_{\mathrm{rest}}\) proves the same kernel and connected R estimates for the full incoming source:

\[
 \text{(MR.28) and the admitted intercomponent bounds}
 \ \Longrightarrow\
 \text{(MR.14), (MR.19)–(MR.23) for the full mixed input.}
 \tag{MR.29; C}
\]

This is an implication, not a claim that (MR.28) holds. It is enough to prove an integrated version of (MR.28) against the actual positive kernel, with the same history cost and complex-field locality; pointwise control is one sufficient route. Conversely, the integrated \(L^p\) transport estimate from the previous note does not itself provide this local complex-field estimate.

A proof of (MR.28) must keep the actual source locations, old endpoint layers, all source-dependent identity and dimension-four outputs, and the specified vacuum recipe. It may not satisfy the bound by dividing out the full exterior-dependent conditional expectation. The complementary source is not allowed to be redefined during the argument.

### 6.4 Why a uniform action reserve does not bypass the missing subtraction

For an unrenormalized old physical mark with \(|V_f|\le\|f\|_\infty S_{\mathrm{old}}\), positivity gives the elementary estimate

\[
 |T'_k(V_{f^1}\cdots V_{f^m})|
 \le \frac{m!}{(\delta\beta)^m}
       \prod_i\|f^i\|_\infty\,
               T'_k(e^{\delta\beta S_{\mathrm{old}}}).
 \tag{MR.30; P}
\]

It follows from \(u^m\le m!t^{-m}e^{tu}\) for \(u,t\ge0\). The last kernel is differently weighted. Its bound does not follow just by quoting (1.89) for \(T'_k1\). If the insertion covers all fine degrees of freedom, the resulting normalization cost is precisely one of the vacuum terms which has to be extracted.

For example, with \(D\) centered real Gaussian coordinates of inverse covariance \(\beta I\) and \(S_D=|A|^2/2\), direct integration gives

\[
 \mathbb E_\beta e^{rS_D}
 =\left(\frac{\beta}{\beta-r}\right)^{D/2},
 \qquad
 \log\mathbb E_\beta e^{rS_D}
   =\frac D2\log\frac{\beta}{\beta-r}
   \ge\frac{Dr}{2\beta},
 \quad 0<r<\beta .
 \tag{MR.31; P}
\]

For \(D\) of order fine volume and \(\beta\) of order ultraviolet depth, this is not a physical-volume bound. The example does not disprove sourced Wilson stability. It proves that an unrestricted action-reserve argument must include its changed vacuum normalization. The exact Wilson analogue of the divergent bare response is proved in Theorem 2.2 and Corollary 2.3 of [Wilson action lower bounds and the first SU(2) integration](local_sources/wilson_action_and_first_integration.md).

The present core theorem avoids this error: its raw source count is at most a once-owned large-field capacity before any bound is taken, and its newly generated **connected** R vacuum is then extracted by (MR.23). An arbitrary already integrated regular source is not automatically in that class.

## 8. Consequences

The remaining source problem contains several different operations. This note completes one of them: literal sources on original plaquettes whose links remain explicit inside one actual large-field component can be carried through its full erased-R operation.

The proof retains the original source variables and every compulsory mass. It uses the original-scale volume of their core, rather than the volume of a later coarse image. Each plaquette receives one capacity charge and is never reopened as raw after integration. The resulting source exponential changes only a fixed coefficient in Balaban’s historical capacity ledger.

The published large-field argument is stable under that finite coefficient change. Its future-reserve and merger induction still yields a uniformly small erased-component mass, after the stated weak-coupling choices. Combining this estimate with the printed complex-exterior domination gives holomorphic marked kernels and uniform source derivatives.

The actual Mayer expansion then produces connected R activities on the original field domains. Boundary and nonboundary activities retain their different decay estimates. The source-dependent vacuum is the flat value of the connected activity, including its genuine second contact. No uniform logarithm of an individual rare conditional mass is assumed.

The completed statement has a sharp restriction. A source plaquette crossing different component operations, involving an already integrated link, or requiring an unverified old/current reconstruction belongs to the complementary readout. Its first and second density jets are written explicitly, including their mixed products with the controlled raw marks.

Full R1 therefore still requires admission of those complementary jets and an invariant marked state through every transition. R0 must identify the actual running coordinate with N2-F. R2 must additionally prove source-local physical-cell vacuum comparison and complete normalized pressure bounds. The new result removes the old-core erasure from that list; it does not replace the unresolved regular, boundary, reconstruction, or normalization arguments for this same theory.

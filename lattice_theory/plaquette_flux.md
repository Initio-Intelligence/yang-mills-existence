# Plaquette witnesses and boundary flux

References H8, H9, RB9, and V9 refer to the companion observable notes in this directory. The kernel estimates below use its H8-D and H9.9 as inputs. All distances of a unitary matrix from the identity in Sections 1–2 are operator norms.

## 1. A current-scale witness for the actual local P test

Let the P test in a source cube at depth \(j\) have threshold \(\varepsilon_j L^{-2j}\). Enlarge it to include the complete coarse-link support of the copied determining data of CMP119 (2.16), with the one-plaquette boundary margin used in defining those data. There is a fixed \(c_P>0\), independent of \(j\), such that
\[
 P\text{ bad in the cube}\quad\Longrightarrow\quad
 \max_{p\text{ in the enlarged coarse support}}\|V_j(\partial p)-1\|
 \ge c_P\varepsilon_j.                                               \tag{1}
\]
This implication also applies when the localized minimum is assigned a bad label because the small regular chart could not be constructed.

Indeed, in the extension \(Q_j^{s*} V_j\) an interior bond of a coarse block is assigned the identity, and a crossing bond is assigned the directed coarse link. A one-step compatible block contour for this particular field has identical parallel transports for every member of its mean, internally the identity and across the designated face the coarse link. Hence the mean field is the same copied configuration at the next depth. Iterate, as in H9 immediately after (H9.10). Each native plaquette of this copied field maps either to a coarse plaquette or to a word reducing to the identity. At a mixed-level boundary, CMP102 (7) completes a missing edge with the corresponding averaged finer field. The copy identity supplies exactly the missing copied edge, so the same two possibilities exhaust the interface plaquettes. This uses the special copied data for the local P test.

If all those coarse plaquettes have defect below \(c_P\varepsilon_j\), the input parameter \(\varepsilon_1\) of CMP102 (7) can be taken to be \(c_P\varepsilon_j\). For the fixed weak endpoint this is below \(a_1\). CMP102 Theorem 1, (2), (8) bounds the local minimum's curvature by \(B_3 c_P\varepsilon_j L^{-2j}\) on the depth-\(j\) region. The P test lies in that region by the two-layer localization margin of CMP119 pp.256–257, as checked in H9. Choose \(B_3 c_P<1\). This proves (1) in all eligible test cubes. LFR I after (1.75) gives (1) separately for strict extension failure with its own constant \(c_\chi\) and the designated retained support, as recorded in (H9.35). No converse is used.

## 2. A restricted normalized estimate following actual reset draws

Fix an observation level \(j\), a reset level \(s\) and lag \(m=j-s\). Start the cone after the specified sweep of accepted LFR resets at level \(s\), before the \(m\) deterministic block averages feeding the terminal witness. Only use observations occurring in that order in the physical history. In particular, a reset made after a test cannot witness that test. Take the union \(D_s\) of the backward plaquette determining neighborhoods of all candidates in (1) or (H9.35). It suffices to impose the following two pointwise conditions on the selected sector.

1. The physical links determining the cone are carried through the intervening ordinary block averages up to gauge. Every subsequent current-field replacement in that cone, including an actual (1.76) replacement on a branch later declared failed, is excluded.
2. In the actual reset frames, form the reference snapshot on \(D_s\) by setting the final accepted reset fluctuation to zero on every updated link in this sweep and retaining the other links. Its plaquettes throughout \(D_s\) satisfy
\[
 \max_{p\subset D_s}\|V_s^{\rm ref}(\partial p)-1\|<\tfrac12 t,
 \qquad t=c_{\rm obs}\varepsilon_j K_L^{-m}.
                                                                    \tag{2}
\]
Here \(c_{\rm obs}\) is the appropriate constant from Section 1, decreased if necessary. This is a selection of actual snapshots. The argument does not assert that the source guarantees this stronger margin on every accepted reset. Disjoint reset domains from one sweep are used. At a shared edge of coordinate representations one must use a common gauge or the same actual endpoint frames on the two fields.

On this sector each terminal P birth or strict-extension failure forces at least one successful reset draw touching \(D_s\) to satisfy
\[
 \|B'_a\|_\infty\ge c\,\frac{\varepsilon_j}{g_s K_L^m}.                \tag{3}
\]
To prove this, use the reset coordinate of LFR I (1.81), (1.99)–(1.101) and LFR II (1.1)–(1.2), relative to the retained minimum in the final sweep. On an updated link the group increment has norm at most \(C g_s\|B'_a\|_\infty\) by the exponential formula. On retained links the difference vanishes. For unitary links the product telescoping inequality bounds the plaquette difference by the sum of its four link differences, including adjoints for reverse orientations. If every relevant draw is below \(t/(8 Cg_s)\), every actual plaquette in \(D_s\) has defect below \(t\). The one-step estimate iterated \(m\) times in (H9.9) applies by condition 1 and gives a terminal maximum below \(c_{\rm obs}\varepsilon_j\). This contradicts Section 1. This argument uses the whole preimage of the terminal candidate set. No bound is obtained by checking the central plaquette alone.

For deterministic anchors choose deterministic supersets of eligible reset addresses for each observation. An address can be a level and the lexicographically first site of the connected eligible component; each such address at a specified sweep is drawn at most once. Take \(A_i\) possible addresses and thresholds \(u_i=c\varepsilon_{j_i}/(g_{s_i}K_L^{m_i})\). For a family of observations on the sectors above, requiring distinct witnessing addresses (in particular, with disjoint enlarged address neighborhoods), and an original nonnegative mark \(H\) known before the draws, one obtains
\[
 \mathbb E[H\,1_{\rm selected\ family}]
 \le \mathbb E[H]\prod_i A_i\min(1,C_Me^{-t_Mu_i^2}).                  \tag{4}
\]
For each distinct address assignment, H8-D bounds the probability of a large innovation at the draw conditional on its physical past. Even if the assigned addresses occur in a random order, multiply at each assigned draw by its large-event indicator divided by its deterministic probability bound, using multiplier one for all other draws. The nonnegative product is a supermartingale initially one. On joint occurrence the product equals the reciprocal product of the probability bounds. Its terminal expectation against \(H\) is at most \(\mathbb E H\). Union bound over the assignments. Selection of (2), of no intervening replacement, and of the bad test occurs by a pointwise event inclusion before using this inequality. Conditioning the reset law on these terminal selections would be unjustified.

For one fixed lag and a fixed observation scale, cube diameter \(O(R_j)\) and the \(O(R_s)\) eligibility anchor radius in scale-\(s\) units give \(A_i\le C(1+L^m(1+R_j)+R_s)^4\), up to fixed source geometry constants. Smooth UV trajectory with fixed lag gives \(g_s\asymp g_j\) and \(\varepsilon_j/g_s\asymp p_0(g_j)\). The logarithm of the address multiplicity is then absorbed by a fixed fraction of \(u_i^2\) at the weak endpoint. This produces exponential rarity of the indicated restricted P/failure families at arbitrary absolute depth. For a fixed lag and equal-scale canonical observation cubes, enlarge then color to select a fixed fraction whose witness-address neighborhoods are disjoint. Extensions to unbounded lag require keeping the displayed \(K_L^{-m}\), geometry, and coupling ratios.

The remaining alternative when the reference margin fails is a raw background defect on the same ancestral cone. Some such defects involve only retained links; others involve the reset minimum. Their annealed cost is open. Equation (4) gives no old conditional mean bound by itself.

## 3. A box with a compulsory flux even after freeing the offending links

There is a concrete obstruction to a uniform same-exterior gap obtained just by freeing a bounded neighborhood of a bad plaquette. Consider SU(2) fundamental Wilson action on \(\{0,\ldots,d\}^4\), \(d\ge4\), with tangential boundary links fixed and all other links integrated. Write \(a(U)\in[0,\pi]\) for the bi-invariant geodesic distance from the identity, normalized so that \(w(U)=1-\tfrac12\operatorname{ReTr}U=1-\cos a(U)\). Fix the boundary links from
\[
 U_2^*(x)=\exp(i\theta x_1\sigma_3),\qquad U_1^*=U_3^*=U_4^*=I .
\]
Every oriented 12 plaquette of this field has angle \(\theta\); all other plaquettes are flat. Suppose \(0<\theta<\theta_*\) and \(d^2\theta<\pi\).

For a 12 slice at fixed \((x_3,x_4)\), its perimeter holonomy has angle \(d^2\theta\). Multiplying the transported elementary plaquettes in a tree-ordered scan yields the perimeter word, by cancellation of interior edges. Bi-invariant distance therefore gives \(\sum_{p\in {\rm slice}}a_p\ge d^2\theta\) for every extension, Abelian or otherwise. Uniformly for \(a\in[0,\pi]\) and sufficiently small \(\theta\),
\[
 1-\cos a\ge 1-\cos\theta+\sin\theta(a-\theta)+c(a-\theta)^2.           \tag{5}
\]
For \(a\le1\) this follows by Taylor's integral remainder and \(\cos t\ge\cos 1\) on the interval joining \(a,\theta\). On \([1,\pi]\) the quotient of the tangent remainder by \((a-\theta)^2\) converges uniformly to \((1-\cos a)/a^2>0\). Summing over disjoint slices, retaining nonnegative costs of other orientations, proves
\[
 S(U)-S(U^*)\ge c\sum_{p\parallel12}(a_p-\theta)^2.
                                                                    \tag{6}
\]
In particular \(U^*\) is a global minimizer and all minimizers have angle \(\theta\) at every 12 plaquette. Dropping constant terms from plaquettes lying entirely on the fixed boundary leaves the same action differences.

For an interior 12 plaquette choose \(B=\{a_p\ge\theta/2\}\). All four of its links may be integrated. Nevertheless \(\inf_B S-\min S=0\), while \(\inf_{B^c} S-\min S\ge c\theta^2/4\). In the exact normalized Wilson conditional, H8-E gives
\[
 \mathbb P_e(B)\ge 1-\exp[-c'\beta\theta^2+C n(1+\log\beta)] .         \tag{7}
\]
Here \(n\) counts free links. For fixed \(d\) and \(\theta=2p_0(\beta^{-1/2})/\sqrt\beta\), the right hand side tends to one. Every concentric subbox whose boundary is inherited from this constant-flux field obeys the same argument for its own side length. The example covers side lengths growing with \(\beta\) as long as the stated small angle and entropy conditions hold. This addresses the raw superthreshold plaquette witness in (1) or (H9.35). Neither implication there reverses. The example does not assert that a particular source P label or (1.75) label is compulsory, nor refute an annealed Yang–Mills estimate.

## 4. A sufficient boundary criterion for the unconstrained Wilson reference

For the normalized fundamental Wilson conditional (H8.34), with any compact matrix group in a faithful unitary representation, let \(S_e^0\) be the action of an explicit completion of the same boundary using precisely the plaquettes incident to free links (so constants common to all completions drop). If the bad witness family requires \(N\) distinct incident plaquettes of defect at least \(t\), then the nonnegative action obeys
\[
 \Delta_B(e)\ge c_G Nt^2 - S_e^0 .                                      \tag{8}
\]
Indeed \(1-\mathrm{Re}\,\mathrm{tr}(U)=\|I-U\|_{\rm HS}^2/(2\dim)\) for normalized trace, and the conditional minimum is at most the competitor energy. If a gauge on the tangential boundary links makes all of them \(\delta\)-close to the identity, choose identity on every free link in that gauge. Extend the boundary gauge arbitrarily into the interior. Incident plaquettes in the box interior are then flat and only the one-link boundary collar contributes. In a box of side \(d\) its number is at most \(C d^3\). Each has action \(\le C_G\delta^2\), by telescoping the products, so \(S_e^0\le C_G d^3\delta^2\). Thus the boundary criterion \(C_G d^3\delta^2\le c_GNt^2/2\) supplies a positive excess for H8-E. It applies to an actual Wilson conditional of the specified form. In a constrained or mixed blocked law one must supply admissibility and conditional mass of a neighborhood of the competitor, control of the non-Wilson likelihood, and probability of excessive boundary energy. These are precisely the steps not resolved by (8).

Primary references are Bałaban, CMP102 pp.278–279, CMP119 pp.245–246, 256–257, and [Large field renormalization I](https://doi.org/10.1007/BF01257412) and [II](https://doi.org/10.1007/BF01238433). The inputs from the companion notes are H8.20–H8.27, H9.9–H9.13, and H9.35. The full V9.3 history sum, terminal estimate and existence theorem remain open here.

holds, for \(v\) sufficiently small. Indeed, negation of the second condition permits Proposition 1 on printed p.194 with parameter \(v/(2B_M)\). ## 6. Prefix occupancy without unchanged-stencil restrictions on the observed births

There is a different use of the fact that a current-field replacement is attempted only on an already existing large-field component. It pays for the *local presence* of later births and failures in a bounded prefix, including those whose physical inputs have already been replaced. Persistence and individual reset outcomes are not charged a new independent rate.

Let \(s_i=Mh_iR_i\) as in H8.2. Use the original source lift and eligible components from H9, at their actual cuts. Distinguish the ordinary transition creating depth-\(i\) labels from the later sweep that processes the resulting depth-\(i\) density. CMP119 (2.18), (3.3)–(3.21) forms the new density and its large-field sets; LFR I p.177 applies its operation to a term **already present** in the expansion of this density, and (1.73), (1.76) operate in an eligible component thereof. Consequently a replacement interfering with the backward stencil of an ordinary birth at depth \(i\) occurred at depth \(h<i\). Changes of frame at fixed current fields in earlier preparatory operations have the pass-through meaning of H9 §1. Any actual (1.76) integration counts as a replacement even if a later selector failed.

**Geometric predecessor.** In the geometry creating active cells through level \(h\), any pre-sweep active cell has an ordinary birth of some depth \(b\le h\) at distance at most \(C\sum_{n=b}^h s_n\). The same holds for a point in the support of an attempted replacement on an eligible component with one additional \(Cs_h\). To see this, trace a cell backwards one cover at a time. In the union of an enlarged old set and the enlarged new flags, a point is either within a fixed number of current cells of a current flag or within that distance of an old set. In a bounded rectangular fill choose a pre-fill point of the component, at distance at most 100 current cells. Apply the preceding alternative there. A merger used only as a union needs no jump across the merged component. Accepted processing removes the processed component from the unresolved set; a failed attempt keeps an already selected component. Neither introduces a disjoint new occupied cell. Iterating in the old-set case gives the asserted sum. At the first step the initial flags provide the base. This is the local source-cover and fill argument of H8 §2 and H9-E. A failed preparatory selector carried by an eligible component is treated via that pre-sweep component. Backtracking does not follow a nonlocal term of a polymer expansion. It follows the actual geometrical labels.

**Witness redirection.** For a birth of depth \(i\le J\), first check the original determining stencil required by H9-C. If it is unchanged, H9-C gives a fine-plaquette or ordinary gauge witness. Otherwise select a prior current replacement that meets that stencil. H9 bounds the physical diameter of the backward stencil by \(Cs_i\). The replacement at \(h<i\) acts on an eligible active component within \(Cs_h\) of the intersection. Apply the preceding geometric predecessor argument to get an ordinary birth at \(b\le h<i\). Repeat from that birth. Because depth strictly decreases this stops at an unchanged-stencil birth, possibly at depth zero. The starting cell can itself be active or an attempted preparation before the initial redirection, by the same predecessor argument.

The radius of the resulting witness neighborhood is at most \(C' s_i\), uniformly in the number of redirections. Indeed, the stencil-jump costs are \(C s_{i_r}\) at strictly decreasing birth depths. The predecessor intervals \([b_r,h_r]\) following successive jumps have disjoint interiors since \(i_{r+1}=b_r\) and \(h_{r+1}<i_{r+1}\). Endpoint costs have bounded multiplicity. Finally H8.3 gives \(\sum_{n\le i}s_n\le C_s s_i\). Thus we can choose a fixed enlargement of the H9-E cell neighborhoods independently of \(J\). Near periodic boundaries use torus distance and increase the fixed geometric size margin as in H9.

Let \(\mathscr A_J^{\rm all}\) denote the actual occupied active cells at the H9 restart cut, allowing arbitrary earlier ordinary creations, completed resets and attempted preparations, with the terminal cells retained in the unresolved geometry. Let \(\Gamma_*\) have the same definition as (H9.22) up to a fixed factor. Then
\[
 \Pi(S\subset\mathscr A_J^{\rm all})\le e^{-c\Gamma_* |S|}.           \tag{10}
\]
For each occupied cell, the preceding redirection yields an original fine plaquette witness at H9's worst prefix threshold, or an actual large gauge innovation at a depth \(\le J\). The final birth reached by redirection has an unchanged stencil, though the observed birth may have arisen from replacement variables. At depth zero a bad initial source cube supplies its defining original plaquette. Color the fixed enlargement to select \(c|S|\) disjoint physical neighborhoods. Count only final witness coordinates in them, exactly as H9-E: at most \(\exp[C(J+\log(1+R_0))]\) choices per neighborhood. H9.24 pays the selected distinct witnesses under the original law. Its exponent absorbs this entropy via H9.22. The count does not enumerate redirection paths, nor multiply the conditional probabilities of nested failures. In particular the same fine defect may pay for many successive births in one neighborhood; (10) only claims independent costs for geometrically separated endpoints.

This proves that H9-F's clean evolution and ordinary-size estimate can start from the enlarged occupied base at \(J\): the deterministic H8-A tree needs only base-cell occupancy and clean evolution after that cut. Old marked estimates inherited solely from deterministic envelopes, this base occupancy, and the H8/H9 geometry apply to the corresponding enlarged selections; no estimate of an old boundary-dependent source function at its reset is supplied. The enlarged result does not assert that an ordinary flag itself is an unchanged-field fine-plaquette test. If the literal source clean-window length exceeds the available number of preceding steps on part of the prefix, it permits no eligible replacements there; (10) continues to hold without requiring that all allowed branches actually occur.

## 7. Arbitrary intervening processing inside the influence of a rare base

Let \(t\) be a deterministic cut with a set \(A_t\) of active base cells satisfying \(\Pi(S\subset A_t)\le e^{-q|S|}\) for every deterministic family of distinct base cells. Examples are the H9 restart cut with \(q=c\Gamma_*\) and  Two's pure Q/R strict-carrier cut with \(q=c p_0(g_{t-1})^2\). For fixed \(H<\infty\) let \(I_{t,j}\) be the set of current-grid cells whose center is within \(H s_j\) of the center of some occupied \(A_t\)-cell, for \(j\ge t\). This is a geometric event in the same original law, with arbitrary subsequent processing. For \(n\) specified distinct current cells,
\[
 \Pi(S\subset I_{t,j})\le
 \min\{1,\exp[-c_H n\{q-C_H(1+j-t)\}]\}.                             \tag{11}
\]
Color the current centers to select \(c_H n\) mutually disjoint candidate neighborhoods, enlarging for half a base-cell diameter if needed. H8.3 gives at most \(C_H(1+s_j/s_t)^4\le\exp[C_H(1+j-t)]\) possible base anchors per retained center. For each assignment the selected base cells are distinct. The assumed normalized joint bound pays each such cell, and the union bound proves (11). The coloring can be performed on the torus. This reasoning imposes no condition on a transition kernel between the two cuts.

In particular, any class of real occupied cells admitting the following *existence* statement lies in \(I_{t,j}\) after fixing \(H\). Starting at the observed cell, a path of ordinary covers, fills, or the redirections of Section 6 reaches a base cell in \(A_t\), and its charged levels each occur with bounded multiplicity. The preceding radius proof gives the required uniform \(H\). Subsequent ordinary births can occur arbitrarily in the same component if this existence statement continues to hold for the specified cells. The statement can instead be imposed directly as membership in \(I_{t,j}\); no causal screening is then asserted. There is no claim that every post-\(t\) birth can be assigned to such a base.

If \(j-t\le c_H' q\) for a sufficiently small fixed constant, (11) gives \(e^{-c_H'' q n}\) at all these cuts. A connected actual component \(Y\) with **every** cell in \(I_{t,j}\) then satisfies, summing over such components,
\[
 \mathbb E\sum_{Y\ni x,y}\exp(\lambda\ell_j(Y))
 \le C\exp[-c q(1+r/s_j)]
 \quad\text{if }q\ge C_H(1+\lambda R_j^4).
                                                                    \tag{12}
\]
Use \(\ell_j(Y)\le C R_j^4 |Y|\), at most \(C^{|Y|}\) anchored connected animals, and \(|Y|\ge c(1+r/s_j)\), as in H8. If merely a single root is in \(I_{t,j}\), (11) gives its one-point probability, with no assertion about the diameter of its actual component. For example, at \(t=J\), the interval \(j\le c\Gamma_*\) pays \(e^{D_sj}\) for the bare microscopic envelopes as in H9, for small fixed \(c\). Since \(\Gamma_*\gg R_0^4+J\), (12) gives the selected same-component sharp scale sum: split the exponential to absorb age, leave \(e^{-c\Gamma_*}\), and use the remaining \(\Gamma_*/R_j\) dilation to bound the scale sum by \(C r^{-8}T(r)^{2s}\), in terminal units. This is a sector in which intervening processing can actually occur. Full posterior covariance with an arbitrary complement at those cuts requires Annex N's larger age budget or a stronger complementary estimate.

One can restart H8's clean tree at any deterministic last cut \(j_0\) of this interval by taking as its new occupied base the genuine active cells lying in wholly selected components of (12) at \(j_0\). They inherit (11) on every family. Require clean evolution after \(j_0\) in exactly the H9-F sense, allowing mergers within the selected class. The H8 binary proof now gives an exponent \(c q 2^{\lfloor(j-j_0)/w_{j_0}\rfloor}\). This construction combines a short interval of arbitrary processing with subsequent clean branching. It never multiplies two conditioned physical birth probabilities. If the new base is at a large absolute time, the old microscopic age \(j_0\) must still be charged when marks are introduced.

## 8. The fixed-flux obstruction for a general matrix gauge group

The preceding obstruction applies to any nontrivial compact connected matrix group \(G\) with a faithful unitary representation \(\rho\), using its normalized fundamental Wilson cost \(w(g)=1-\operatorname{Re}\operatorname{tr}\rho(g)\). Use the invariant Lie-algebra norm \(\|X\|^2=-\operatorname{Re}\operatorname{tr}(d\rho(X)^2)\) and its bi-invariant group distance. All constants in this section may depend on this fixed representation.

**Small perimeter bridge.** For every \(N\ge1\), and \(Y\) in a sufficiently small fixed ball, the minimum of \(\sum_{i=1}^N w(g_i)\) subject to \(g_1\cdots g_N=\exp Y\) is \(Nw(\exp(Y/N))\). Every minimizing increment equals \(\exp(Y/N)\).

Existence follows by compactness. The equal-increment competitor costs at most \(C\|Y\|^2/N\). The faithful cost is strictly positive off any identity neighborhood, so all minimizing increments belong to one fixed small exponential chart if \(\|Y\|\) is small. In this chart \(w(\exp X)=\frac12\|X\|^2+O(\|X\|^4)\). Let \(J(g)\) denote the Lie-algebra gradient of \(X\mapsto w(g\exp X)\) at zero. The derivative of \(X\mapsto J(\exp X)\) at zero is the identity for this metric, so that map, and hence \(J\), is injective on a fixed smaller chart. The class-function identity \(w(g\exp X)=w(\exp X g)\) equates the two trivializations of the differential. At a minimum, varying \((g_i,g_{i+1})\) to \((g_i\exp X,\exp(-X)g_{i+1})\) preserves the product and implies \(J(g_i)=J(g_{i+1})\). Thus all minimizing increments equal \(\exp Z\). Comparison with the competitor and the quadratic lower bound give \(\|Z\|\le C\|Y\|/N\). Both \(NZ\) and \(Y\) lie inside the same injectivity ball, and \(\exp NZ=\exp Y\), proving the claim, including the trivial case \(N=1\).

A corresponding gap is uniform in \(N\). Put \(\theta=\|Y\|/N>0\). If \(d(g_i,1)\le\theta/2\) for one increment of any feasible path, then
\[
 \sum_i w(g_i) -Nw(\exp(Y/N))\ge c\theta^2                          \tag{13}
\]
whenever \(\|Y\|\) is smaller than a fixed constant. For energy larger than twice the minimum the bound holds from the quadratic lower bound. At energy no larger than twice the minimum, all increments are in the small chart and \(\sum_i y_i^2\le C N\theta^2\), where \(y_i=d(g_i,1)\). The group triangle inequality gives \(\sum_i y_i\ge N\theta\). Therefore \(\sum_i y_i^2-N\theta^2\ge\sum_i(y_i-\theta)^2\ge\theta^2/4\). The total absolute quartic remainder in the action and its minimizing value is at most \(C[(\sum_i y_i^2)^2+N\theta^4]\le C\|Y\|^2\theta^2\). Decreasing the fixed radius for \(Y\) yields (13).

Choose a unit Lie-algebra generator \(T\), prescribe the box boundary by \(U_2^*(x)=\exp(x_1\theta T)\) and take \(N=d^2,\ Y=d^2\theta T\). On each 12 slice the product of the tree-transported plaquette holonomies is exactly the prescribed perimeter \(\exp Y\), with a consistent orientation and boundary basepoint. Wilson costs and distances are conjugation invariant. The bridge theorem thus proves that each slice costs at least its value at \(U^*\). Every other orientation has nonnegative action and is flat at \(U^*\). Summing slices and applying (13) on the particular slice containing the interior marked plaquette gives the same conditional probability conclusion as (7). For this general-group result the total unwrapped slice flux \(d^2\theta\) must be sufficiently small for the representation. In the SU(2) proof of Section 3 the wider interval \(d^2\theta<\pi\) suffices.

### 8.1 Positive excess above a nonflat homogeneous boundary

In the same general-group box, let the boundary come from a homogeneous flux \(0\le\theta\le v/2\), with \(d^2\theta\) inside the small ball of the bridge theorem. Let \(B\) require at least \(m\) distinct plaquettes with group distance \(\ge v\), allowing all six orientations. Then for small \(v\),
\[
  \inf_B S-\min S\ge c_G m v^2.                                      \tag{14}
\]
The estimate retains the exact generally nonzero minimum. To prove it, take any 12-slice with \(l\ge1\) violating plaquettes. If its energy is at least twice the slice minimum, its excess is at least half its energy, hence \(\ge c l v^2\) since compactness and faithfulness give \(w(g)\ge c d(g,1)^2\) on the group. Otherwise apply the small-energy computation proving (13) with \(\sum(y_i-\theta)^2\ge l(v-\theta)^2\ge l v^2/4\). Its total Taylor error \(C\|Y\|^2\theta^2\) is absorbed by a fixed fraction of \(l v^2\). Slices without violations have nonnegative excess by the exact bridge minimum. Each violating plaquette in a different orientation costs \(c v^2\) above its zero baseline. Sum all contributions. The special case \(\theta=0\) follows immediately by nonnegativity and the pointwise cost bound.

Applied to the actual unconstrained Wilson box law (H8.34), (14) gives \(\mathbb P_e(B)\le\exp[-c_G\beta m v^2+C_G n(1+\log\beta)+\operatorname{osc}W_e]\). The bound allows a nonzero same-exterior baseline even if its total value exceeds the energy of one violation. This is a positive restricted reference theorem; carrying it to a mixed coarse Wilson-minimizer action requires the conditional-density and support identities in V9.

## 9. An annealed boundary estimate in the original periodic Wilson law

This result uses §2.1, (2.2) of the observable construction as a standing microscopic Wilson input. It averages over the actual retained boundary, allows arbitrary boundary fields, and proves a joint conditional-goodness statement for the original Wilson boxes. It does not use reflection positivity of a blocked law.

**Surface gauge.** Let \(\partial D\) be the tangential cubical surface of \(D=[0,d]^4\), in lattice units. Set \(E_\partial=\sum_{p\subset\partial D}w(U_p)\), with faithful normalized unitary Wilson cost. There is a gauge on boundary vertices such that
\[
 \sum_{e\subset\partial D}\|I-U^g_e\|^2\le C_G (1+d)^4 E_\partial .
                                                                    \tag{15}
\]
Here and below fixed dimension and representation are understood. For each boundary vertex \(x\) choose the first incident facet in a fixed order. Take the path from the origin to the corner of that facet with all other coordinates zero, then traverse the other three coordinate directions in a fixed order to reach \(x\). Both stages are tangential to the boundary. Define the gauge by parallel transport along these paths. It need not be a tree gauge. For a boundary edge, the transformed edge is the word obtained by following one root path out and the other backwards, with the edge between them.

For an edge whose endpoints use the same facet, the common path to its corner cancels up to conjugacy. The remaining loop is in one cubic facet. Sliding the single coordinate step of the edge past the subsequent axis segments of the endpoint path produces at most two unit-width rectangles, each of length at most \(d\). Their boundaries multiply to the original word up to conjugation. Each face plaquette appears at most twice. Cauchy–Schwarz, telescoping and \(\|I-U_p\|^2\le C_G w(U_p)\) bound this edge's squared defect by \(C d E_\partial\). There are \(O((1+d)^3)\) such edges.

For an edge with differing chosen facets, its loop consists of at most nine coordinate segments. Subdivide each of the four coordinates at zero, \(d\), and all loop-vertex coordinates. This induces a cubical boundary cellulation with a bounded number of coarse cells, independent of \(d\). The loop is a word of bounded length in the coarse 1-skeleton. Only finitely many combinatorial types of these cellulations and bounded words exist. The surface is a 3-sphere, whose 2-skeleton presents the trivial fundamental group. Thus for each type and word there is an expression as a product of conjugates of oriented coarse 2-face boundaries, by cellular edge-path homotopy; the maximum number of factors is a universal finite constant. Every contributing coarse face is a coordinate rectangle with at most \(d^2\) unit plaquettes. Expanding its boundary word into conjugated plaquettes by a row scan uses each of its unit plaquettes once. The full filling therefore has at most \(C d^2\) factors and uses every individual plaquette at most \(C\) times. Cauchy–Schwarz now gives \(C d^2 E_\partial\) for this edge. A boundary edge lies in at least one common facet. If the endpoints choose different facets then at least one endpoint is in their common facet and an additional facet, hence lies in the cubical 2-skeleton of the original box (the union of its ridges). There are only \(O((1+d)^2)\) such vertices and each touches boundedly many edges. Summing both contributions proves (15). The simpler uniform per-edge estimate without this ridge count would give the weaker exponent five.

Set each free link to the identity in the resulting frame (extend the vertex gauge arbitrarily to the interior). An incident plaquette has action at most \(C_G\) times the sum of squared fixed edge defects on it; all-free plaquettes cost zero. Each tangential edge occurs in boundedly many plaquettes. Hence this explicit completion satisfies
\[
 S_e^0\le C_G (1+d)^4 E_\partial.                                  \tag{16}
\]

**Good and exceptional boxes.** Consider the normalized original periodic Wilson law on the cofinal tori for which the §2.1 exponential reserve is uniform, so \(\mathbb E\exp(\eta\beta\sum_{p\in X}w(U_p))\le K^{b(X)}\) at a fixed \(\eta>0\). Embed a deterministic family of boxes of side \(d\) with disjoint boundary plaquette sets. Require also that no action plaquette contains free links from two boxes, and that their boundary links are exterior to all the free link sets. Condition on all links outside the union of these free interiors. The conditional law is a product of the exact Wilson box conditionals because the action is a sum of plaquettes and reference Haar measure is a product. For box \(i\) let \(B_i\) require at least \(m_i\ge1\) distinct incident plaquettes of defect \(\ge v\), and write \(T_i=\beta m_i v^2\). If \(C_G(1+d)^4 E_{\partial,i}\le c_Gm_i v^2/2\), (8), (16), and H8-E prove the fiberwise bound
\[
 P(B_i\mid e)\le\min(1,\exp[-cT_i+C_G(1+d)^4(1+\log\beta)]) .
                                                                    \tag{17}
\]
Conversely each exceptional boundary has \(\beta E_{\partial,i}>cT_i/(1+d)^4\). For any prescribed subset \(A\) of such boxes, exponential Markov on their union of boundary plaquettes gives
\[
 P(\text{all boundaries in }A\text{ exceptional})\le
 \exp\left[-c\sum_{i\in A}T_i/(1+d)^4+C(1+d)^3|A|\right].          \tag{18}
\]
Indeed the unit-cube cover size of their combined boundaries is at most \(C(1+d)^3|A|\), and the energies add without multiplicity. Patterning exceptional and good boxes, taking conditional probabilities on the good factors, and integrating over the same exterior yields
\[
 P(\cap_i B_i)\le
 \prod_i\left\{
 e^{-cT_i+C(1+d)^4(1+\log\beta)}
 +e^{-cT_i/(1+d)^4+C(1+d)^3}
 \right\}.                                                        \tag{19}
\]
Each term represents an upper bound that may separately be capped at one. For example, the product with the uncapped terms shown follows by summing the \(2^{\#\rm boxes}\) subsets and factoring the sum; no extra multiplicative count is then required. For comparable boxes of side \(d\asymp (1+\log\beta)^r\) and \(v\asymp p_0(\beta^{-1/2})/\sqrt\beta\), with \(P\ge5r,r\ge2\), (17) has positive rate comparable to \(T_i\), and (18) has rate comparable to \(T_i/(1+d)^4\). The latter grows at least as \(m_i(\log\beta)^{6r}\), paying both \(d^3\) boundary entropy and a multiple of \(d^4\) ordinary box size at one weak threshold. Constants may depend on the chosen cofinal uniform \(K\). A different torus sequence must keep the explicit prefactor in the microscopic reserve.

The output is stronger than a bare bad-plaquette probability in one respect: it explicitly identifies a high-probability set of exteriors on which the same-exterior excess (8) holds. Already-established Wilson chessboard estimates can yield better unconditioned exponents for the raw event. Equations (15)–(19) supply neither the retained exterior law nor the constrained-volume lower bound of a general blocked P/failure history. An actual application at a later cut requires those additional identifications.

### 9.1 Surface energy of the actual coarse output in the unchanged prefix

For a coarse-box boundary at level \(j\), take the union \(\mathcal T_j\) of the fine determining stencils of its coarse plaquettes under the deterministic block maps. It has a cover by \(C(1+d)^3 L^{4j}\) unit fine cubes. A fixed fine plaquette is in at most \(C\) of these per-plaquette stencils, since their physical diameters are at most \(C h_j\), and their coarse centers belong to a fixed lattice of spacing \(h_j\). On the event that the relevant complete stencils had no previous current-field replacement, (H9.9) gives
\[
 \sum_{p\subset\partial D} w(V_j(\partial p))
 \le C K_L^{2j}\sum_{p'\in\mathcal T_j}w(U_0(\partial p')).           \tag{20}
\]
Indeed, for a single plaquette its defect is bounded by \(C K_L^j\) times the maximal fine defect on its stencil: (H9.9) applies if the latter product is below the chart radius, and otherwise the compact maximum of the coarse defect gives the bound after a fixed constant increase. Square, use equivalence of the squared group defect and the faithful Wilson cost, then sum with the bounded overlap just described.

For a deterministic family of boxes, suppose their fine stencil tubes are disjoint (a bounded-overlap family also works with a fixed exponent loss). Let \(F_i\) mean failure of the sufficient boundary-energy test in (17), now at the *actual coarse exterior* with threshold \(v\). Using the original Wilson marginal, Markov's inequality and its §2.1 reserve at the fine scale, (20) gives
\[
 P(\text{all specified tubes unchanged and all }F_i)
 \le \exp\left[-c\frac{\beta v^2\sum_i m_i}
 {(1+d)^4 K_L^{2j}}+C\,\#\{i\}(1+d)^3 L^{4j}\right].              \tag{21}
\]
There is no conditioning on the unchanged event. On the literal prefix of Section 6.1 it holds automatically. Choose \(d\asymp R_j\), \(v\asymp\varepsilon_j\), \(j\le\kappa\log\log\beta\). Uniform comparability of inverse couplings on that prefix makes the negative term per box of order at least \(m_i(\log\beta)^{2P-4r-2\kappa\log K_L}\); its entropy is at most \(C(\log\beta)^{3r+4\kappa\log L}\). Taking \(\kappa\) smaller so that the former exponent exceeds both \(4r\) and the latter exponent supplies an ordinary-size reserve on the actual coarse-boundary exceptional sector. This choice is available because \(P\ge5r\). Equation (21) controls the retained boundary *data* of a Wilson reference at those cuts. The source law on its free coarse links is a pushforward with inherited constraints and interactions. Equations (17), (19) cannot be transferred to that conditional law without the separate density and support comparison.

## 10. Real boundary-action oscillation in a recent mixed history

Here the setting is the *actual mixed* representation (1.103)–(1.104) of LFR II, pp.391–392. This section bounds a particular action contribution for the same real conditional support, without differentiating either a cutoff or a minimizing field.

### 10.1 The fixed-family bracket

Fix the family \(\mathcal Y=\{Y_1,\ldots,Y_m\}\) at this step; let \(Y=\bigcup_iY_i\). On the coarse \(M\)-grid let \(\partial_M^+Y\) denote exterior cells sharing a wall with a cell of \(Y\), with a fixed-width enlargement allowed by the support convention. There are constants, independent of the volume, such that the change of logarithmic density due to the square bracket of (1.103) is bounded on its real positive support by
\[
 |\log\mathcal L_{\mathcal Y}|\le C(m+|\partial_M^+Y|).
                                                                    \tag{22}
\]
Use the ordinary sufficiently large source tree exponent, chosen to absorb the fixed counting constant below. If a particular set of fixed source constants cannot be enlarged, the needed explicit proviso is that its tree exponent exceed the entropy constant in this count. Counts here and below are in native \(M\)-cells, not \(MR_k\)-cells.

For this claim use the bound *before* the last exponentiation. LFR II at (1.97)–(1.99) gives \(|B'^{(k)}(X)|\le C e^{-\lambda d_{k,Y}(X)}\) for the terms feeding (1.103). In (1.67) the relative size is the length in \(M\)-units of a **single tree contained in \(X\)** meeting every \(M\)-cube of \(X\setminus Y\). Traversing a long connection between two outside cubes contributes to this length, even when the tree runs through \(Y\). This size need not visit every cell inside \(Y\). The source's activity construction on pp.388–390 uses the two alternatives for its localization domains, containing a fixed component or disjoint from it. Unions in the connected exponential preserve this property. Only supports meeting the fixed family occur in (1.103). The underlying domains are wall-connected unions of native cells by CMP109, pp.256–257, definition preceding (0.24).

Consider \(P=X\setminus Y\). If \(P\) is empty, \(X\) is one of the fixed components; there are at most \(m\) possibilities, because the \(Y_i\) are disjoint components without a common wall. If \(P\ne\varnothing\), there is a root cube in \(P\cap\partial_M^+Y\). For a fixed root and relative size in \([n,n+1)\) there are at most \(\exp[C_d(1+n)]\) possible \(P\). Indeed a doubled traversal of the supporting tree starts at a point in the root cube, has length at most \(2(n+1)\) in cell units and visits all of \(P\). Recording the cells containing samples at unit arclength gives a bounded-step cell word of length \(O(1+n)\). All visited cells lie in a fixed enlargement of that word, with \(O(1+n)\) cells. Count words and subsets of this enlargement. Every fixed component included in \(X\) must touch \(P\) along a wall because \(X\) is connected. Thus all possibilities for which fixed components appear cost at most \(2^{2d|P|}\), also absorbed by increasing the same counting constant. This proves the summable rooted count. It allows many arms along a boundary; the weight covers the tree joining the exterior arms. It does not apply if that connecting length is discarded. The result is a uniform bound by the right side of (22) on the sum of absolute values *inside* the normalized expectation in (1.103).

On the real support each normalized old operation is a probability kernel. Thus the exponential inside the square bracket lies between \(\exp(-C(m+|\partial_M^+Y|))\) and its reciprocal, and its normalized expectation lies in the same interval for every retained field. This proves (22), including when the normalized kernel varies with that field. No lower bound on \(z_i=\mathcal T'_k(Y_i)1\) is used. The \(z_i\) factors in front of the bracket cancel only in a conditional law fixing \(V_k|_{Y_i}\). Neither (22) nor the fixed-family convergence on p.391 sums over the possible \(Y_i\)'s. The factor for a domain equal to a processed component in (1.69) can be as large as \(C\sum_j|\Gamma_j^*\cap Y|\) in native units; it is in the corresponding operation (1.71)–(1.72). It is not uniformly bounded by a constant per current component before normalizing.

### 10.2 What the historical terms add

The bracket (22) is only the contribution of the newly treated fixed family. In (1.104), \(A'_k\), the characteristic functions, the retained Wilson minimum, and the operations for the remaining \(Z_k\) are still present. As to real inherited **boundary action** terms, there is the following useful sufficient history budget. In the ordinary pre-resummation representation of CMP119 (2.40)–(2.42), each birth-scale term has size \(B_0 e^{-\kappa d_j(X)}\), and its native support intersects \(Z_j\). Summing first around an intersecting \(M\)-cell as in CMP119 pp.263–264 yields
\[
 \sum_{j,X} |B^{(j)}(X)|\le C\sum_{j:\, Z_j\ne\varnothing}|Z_j|_{M,j}. \tag{23}
\]
This bound charges *all* nonempty affected birth-scale boundary sets, wherever they are on the torus. It applies to the stated ordinary-tree representation, in particular to ordinary-history terms prior to a further fixed-family resummation; an already resummed brace with a relative metric must use (22) or its pre-bracket representation. For relative terms of (1.99) disjoint from the fixed \(Y\) but meeting another boundary \(Z_k^\sim\), the tree meets every support cube and the same ordinary rooted count applies with roots in that enlarged boundary. Count each retained contribution only once. The fact that every coefficient depends on the fine minimizing field restricted to \(X\) does not imply a strict locality of the pulled-back function of the current links. The global absolute sums here allow such dependence.

In particular for carriers with at most a fixed number of recent grades, a fixed number of small components of side \(O(MR_j)\) at each of those grades, and no additional inherited boundary terms from older grades, (22)–(23) cost at most \(C\sum_{j\ {\rm affected}}(1+R_j)^4\). This is \(o(p_0(g_k)^2)\) at the weak endpoint by H9.2 and fixed age. More generally use the displayed native total itself. If one has a bound only on the *current physical size* of an old longitudinal region, recovering its size in the earliest native mesh may cost \(L^{4(k-j)}\). The source value bounds do not sum an arbitrarily old history merely because its current localization is one cube. For a globally extensive population (23) is correspondingly extensive. To turn it into a local variation bound one needs to control the responses of the distant minimizing-field arguments or another localized comparison.

For a recent fixed-family mixed carrier satisfying this total budget, call it \(C\mathcal V\), freeze the actual multipliers supported on retained \(Y\), and keep every other constraint. Let \(\nu^{(0)}_e\) denote the normalized conditional law obtained by dropping *only* the identified bounded real boundary action from the actual density in H8.31. It uses the same hard support, the same physical coordinates and Haar factors, the same mixed minimum, the same linear and quadratic terms, and all remaining potentials. In positive mass fibers,
\[
 e^{-2C\mathcal V}\nu^{(0)}_e(f)\le \mu_e(f)\le
 e^{2C\mathcal V}\nu^{(0)}_e(f),\qquad f\ge0.                     \tag{24}
\]
This is an actual likelihood comparison for those contributions in the mixed law, including disintegrations that further freeze exterior real coordinates. The identified real boundary action by itself cannot spend a full one-cube birth exponent in this class. To obtain the physical Q/R bound one would still need, for this *same* reference and event, either \(\nu^{(0)}_e(B_{Q/R})\le e^{-c p_k^2}\) or a same-support marked tilt estimate (and relevant comparison to the actual mixed Q/R fluctuation) such as
\[
 \frac{\int_{D(e)} e^{u\sum_{b\in P}|A_b|^2}\,d\widetilde\nu^{(0)}_e}
      {\int_{D(e)} d\widetilde\nu^{(0)}_e}
 \le e^{C|P|+o(p_k^2)}.                                             \tag{25}
\]
No normalization of a new cutoff is understood. The small recent boundary budget does not force the denominator in (25) to contain a typical neighborhood of the mixed minimum, or annul a large linear displacement imposed by fixing an exterior. LFR II pp.388–392 estimates the boundary coefficients and exponentiates the bracket; it does not provide (25) for arbitrary retained exteriors. The real value estimate (24), in its stated history scope, therefore removes one of the potential costs while leaving this specific conditional input open. Old source responses arising on differentiation of normalized old operations require their own marked bounds.

### 10.3 Scope check and two separated live integrations

The rooted count used in (22) is already in the source T1 paper, Theorem 7.132, proof of (7.201). Its Lemma 7.194 verifies the same fixed-family real conditional disintegration. The new use in (22)–(24) is the recent-history oscillation budget. The underlying source is LFR II pp.373–374 (1.61)–(1.64), which performs the last localization of old-variable dependence, pp.378–379 (1.71)–(1.72), which factorizes the original component integrations, and pp.390–391 (1.98)–(1.103), where every incident normalized old operation acts only on the activities containing its component. The distinction between the first-stage log and its re-exponentiation matters.

For clarity, let \(e\) fix all its new fields, exterior coordinates, geometry, and all physical variables belonging to remaining operations on \(Z_k\) that are to be treated as exterior. Hold the family of live \(Y_i\)'s fixed; do not fix the component's old variables \(\xi_i\) being integrated in its \(\mathcal T'_k(Y_i)\). If extra labels have already been resummed in the first-stage logarithm, those summands are not additional observed random labels. In particular, one cannot condition on a particular first-stage Mayer diagram and then reuse the first-stage bound. The support statement is for the complete real pre-(1.103) logarithm in (1.98), represented by convergent coefficients \(W_X(\xi_{I(X)};e)\); \(I(X)\) lists the whole live components incident to \(X\). On the physical real support the sum is real and the real parts may be used. Each labelled old measure \(\mathcal T'_k(Y_i)/z_i\) is a probability kernel depending on fixed exterior data; these form a product over the old integration variables before multiplication by \(\exp\sum W_X\). This is exactly the independence statement needed. It does not imply independence of the resulting full law.

Take two fixed live components and, if others exist, condition on *all their variables as well*. For \(W_X\) to depend on both \(\xi_1\) and \(\xi_2\) it must contain \(Y_1\) and \(Y_2\). Then its outside cells include one adjacent to each, and the tree in (1.67) has length at least \(d_M(Y_1,Y_2)-C\). Root the count at the smaller outer boundary and keep a fraction of the exponent for this distance. With enough fixed tree-exponent reserve,
\[
 a:=\sum_{X:\,1,2\in I(X)}\|W_X\|_\infty
 \le C\min_{i=1,2}(1+|\partial_M^+Y_i|)e^{-\gamma d_M(Y_1,Y_2)}.    \tag{26}
\]
After the other variables are fixed, absorb *all* terms involving only \(\xi_1\) or only \(\xi_2\) into their respective one-core measures. These may be large. Constants cancel at this conditional level. The actual density relative to the new normalized product is \(e^{W_{12}}/\nu_1\otimes\nu_2(e^{W_{12}})\), \(|W_{12}|\le a\). Its distance from the product in total variation is \(\le C\min(1,a)\). Subtracting the product expectation and the two marginal means gives, for arbitrary bounded real \(f_i(\xi_i;e)\),
\[
 |\operatorname{Cov}(f_1,f_2\mid e,\xi_{\ne1,2})|
 \le C\|f_1\|_\infty\|f_2\|_\infty\min(1,a).                           \tag{27}
\]
These really can be old marks *measurable on the retained old coordinates of their own operations*. An old microscopic mark already integrated into a changed exterior-dependent conditional source must first be represented on this same lift. Integrating the conditional covariance against its posterior preserves (27); recombining the conditional means introduces a further covariance not covered here. Unfrozen spectator components can mediate correlations through several potentials. For actual small, mutually separated components T1 Theorem 7.132 already supplies the relevant many-body comparison. There is no assertion that (27) finishes that problem for general shapes or for an arbitrary exterior.

Another scope restriction of (23) is that history budgets can be used as *measurable geometry selectors* in the outer law. One may disintegrate on the fixed admissible geometry, then apply the uniform bound on every support point of its original conditional fiber. For example, fixed curves of the counts \(|Z_j|_{M,j}\) are such geometric data. Selecting a subdomain in the *varied current field* because its potentials happen to be small cannot justify dropping the potential in its parent normalization. In that case both measures would have to be restricted and normalized on that subdomain, and the original posterior weight restored separately. The comparison in (24) keeps the exact same allowed set in the numerator and denominator and requires that the reference normalization be nonzero; the bounded density guarantees that on positive-mass fibers.

### 10.4 What pinning a shell does at the current and microscopic levels

At a microscopic Wilson cut, fix all tangential links in a shell separating an interior from an exterior, as in Section 9. The action and Haar measure factor into an interior and an exterior integral. If more data are revealed, the factorization persists for selectors measurable on the exterior and for base-independent gauge draws there. It also persists for products of positive weights each of which is provably supported on just one side and on the shell. For example one may fix an actual initial \(P_0\)-bad cube on the outside, with its enlarged test disjoint from the free box, keeping arbitrary Wilson interior variables. The proof of (17) then holds on the same interior and actual retained exterior, including that genuinely mixed initial label. At later early cuts a P/strict-failure test with unchanged full stencil forces a fine plaquette by (1) and H9.9. Conditioning on the microscopic exterior alone, its intersection with later selection indicators remains bounded by this Wilson witness cost on the same good boundary. Conditioning *again* on an arbitrary later selection or on its exact coarse output changes the law. These statements do not yield (25).

There is a direct check that a shell of current block averages alone cannot replace the actual minimizing field by an exactly Dirichlet local one. Consider the tangent theory at the identity and nonzero slab momentum \(q=(q_1,0,0,0)\) in a Cartan color direction with polarization 2. At linear order the two block-contour definitions CMP109 (0.4),(0.12) give the coarse link as the mean of length-\(L\) directed fine line sums over block starts, plus the difference of endpoint block contour coordinates. For (0.4) the returning paths are translates of the outgoing paths; for (0.12) the two symmetrized endpoint means give the same difference. This endpoint contribution is a coarse gradient, whose transverse projection at this momentum vanishes. The uniform block averaging in coordinates 2,3,4 annihilates the nonzero transverse Fourier aliases for this transverse coarse link; those fine modes do not enter its constraint. At zero transverse fine momentum the Maxwell quadratic form is diagonal in transverse polarizations, and a longitudinal coarse constraint at nonzero \(q_1\) is pure gauge. Thus, up to a fixed normalization and ignoring the unconstrained constant mode, the flat constrained Wilson second variation on the physical transverse sector reduces to
\[
 \min_f \sum_n(f_{n+1}-f_n)^2,qquad
 a_n=L^{-1}\sum_{r=0}^{L-1} f_{Ln+r}
\]
with prescribed coarse \(a_n\); the native coarse link has one additional constant factor \(L\). Equivalently the fine increments are unit white Gaussian increments with their constant Fourier mode removed on a periodic torus. The coarse increment \(a_{n+1}-a_n\) is a moving average of these increments with triangular weights \((1,2,\ldots,L,\ldots,2,1)/L\). Its lag-zero covariance is \(c_0=(2L^2+1)/(3L)\) and its lag-one covariance is \(c_1=(L^2-1)/(6L)>0\); greater lags vanish before the constant-mode removal. At nonzero momentum the exact transverse coarse precision is proportional to
\[
 \frac{2-2\cos q_1}{c_0+2c_1\cos q_1}.                               \tag{28}
\]
The numerator does not cancel the denominator's roots. The rational symbol is not a trigonometric polynomial for \(L>1\), so there is no uniform finite-range precision on growing tori. Pinning a finite number of coarse slabs therefore does not exactly screen the quadratic conditional mean from a farther slab. The source's exponentially local minimizing-field comparisons are compatible with this calculation. It rules out an exact separator argument; it does not by itself estimate the size of a true mixed conditional tail.

Pinning the literal microscopic shell in addition would split the *original* Wilson energy. The coarse linear constraints split too only with a sufficiently thick microscopic shell covering the determining stencils of every crossing average and with all its endpoint constraints specified. For a later mixed law this factorization must also be shown for every retained characteristic function, any old integrated/reset kernel and its normalizer, and the complete effective-action weights after conditioning. LFR II (1.61)–(1.65), pp.373–375, achieves old-coordinate locality through convergent localization terms; it does not assert pointwise finite range of the new global \(U_k\) as a function of current fields. In (1.103), fixing \(U_k\) via all its exterior data is legitimate for (27); varying the current interior to test (25) changes that argument. Treating all values of \(U_k\) on the shell as separate independent fixed Dirichlet data can also restrict allowed interior configurations through the minimizing equation. A normalized joint law of that additional conditioning, with its original coarea and denominator, would have to be constructed. These are different levels of disintegration. The evidence establishes (24) and (27) on their actual mixed fibers, and a literal Wilson shell result with an exterior initial label. It has not established (25) for the exact subsequent mixed Q/R physical coordinates.

A close existing comparison is T1 §7.134, Proposition 7.206, (7.269). It compares actual mixed *ordinary* integrations for bounded tests of independent canonical coordinates, provided the complete local kernels agree on a protected collar or their discrepancy in the coupled majorants is bounded by the stated \(\Delta_b\). ## 17. Testing the renormalized tensor phase variance on actual single digits

Fix a terminal scale \(K\) and a unit \(v\) in a stress or Weyl sector. Use the actual covariant seed and all matching bulk projections, retaining finite-torus errors if applicable.  \|P_n\ell_{i+1}(L_{i,r}-\bar L_i)I_iv\|^2\le C. \tag{36}
\]
For grouped digits use their uniform word measure. This only tests the single-digit terms. Revealing the digits successively gives the full Doob variance identity with the actual random incoming field in place of its deterministic incoming mean.

Write \(\delta_r D_r=D_r-E_sD_s\) and \(I_i^0=\iota_i+S_i^0\). N8.14–19 and reproduction of the critical classical coefficient at every digit give

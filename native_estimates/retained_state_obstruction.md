# Why conditioning on a retained rare event does not bound a supremum

The pointwise supremum over retained marked states cannot, in general, be small. The joint event and flat-test estimates below concern integrated insertions. The local R-test requires the [structured cluster estimate and parameter conditions](local_test_estimates.md); transfer of derivative, normalized mark, reset and shell estimates to that same representation is a separate hypothesis. The literal test and the local test cannot be interchanged in a norm estimate solely because the Wilson marginals agree.

## 2. Lemma 2.1, Propositions A and B

**Lemma 2.1.** The formula gives every step: the one-link conditional density \(\propto e^{\beta\operatorname{Re}\operatorname{Tr}(US^*)}\) with \(\|S\|_{\rm tr}\le n_sN\); \(s(U_p)=\tfrac12\|U-W\|_{\rm HS}^2\); the second-order bound (2.2) at a maximizer (uses \(\rho\le\pi r/2\le1\) and \(e^\rho-1-\rho\le\rho^2\)); the disjoint ball \(B(Y,\tau)\subset B(U_*,5\tau)\); and \(\beta(\pi^2/4)n_sN(5\tau)^2=C_1t\). The bound is conditional on all other links, hence valid on any space carrying a Wilson marginal. Cor. 2.2, Thms 3.1–3.2 and Cor. 3.3–3.4 follow as written.

**Proposition A under lift β, deduction given §1.** \(\{e_p\ge NP_0\}\) forces the first-step flag of its cube ((1.1), p.246), the cube lies in \(\Omega_1^c=\Gamma_0\) (p.248), the component is ineligible for \(j<N_B\) (§1.2), and on that event \(O_x\) is a function of conditioning data. The arithmetic to \((N/16)e^{-C_1N}L^{4j}\) is right. Under lift α step 3 of its proof fails; R1's Cor. 3.3 then gives the weaker \(e^{-C_1N}L^{4j}/(16|\Pi|)\), which still diverges.

**Proposition B under lift β, deduction given §1.** The mechanism is simpler than the note suggests: in a retained footprint the mark is the microscopic field, of size \(a^{-4}\) at typical configurations (already its centering constant \(a^{-4}c_E\)), while the regulator there is polylogarithmic.

*The positivity assumption.* It is not needed for the literal norm: Annex Y after (M.11) says "The supremum is over the specified ambient versions", and (M.36) is a pointwise identity, so the bound at the single point \(\omega_0\) suffices. For an essential-supremum version, replace \(|U_{p'}-1|=\varepsilon_0\) by \(|U_{p'}-1|\in(\varepsilon_0,2\varepsilon_0)\): the fine configurations near that one form a nonempty open set, which has positive Wilson probability on a finite lattice; the first-step flag is then deterministic and \(x\) is retained for \(j<N_B\) whatever the later labels are. The regulator of every carrier is then bounded by a polylogarithm on a positive-probability subset (energies of the other retained coordinates are tight), and \(e^{\kappa|X|}\) dominates for large carriers. So the doubt R2 records is settled: deduction for the literal norm; the essential-supremum version uses only open-set positivity and tightness, which are required as further assumptions.

*Under lift α* the proof fails as written (\(Q_jO_x(\omega_0)\ne O_x(\omega_0)\)), but Thm 3.1 with \(r=\infty\) gives \(\operatorname{ess\,sup}|Q_jO_{x_*}|\ge a^{-4}P_0/(8|\Pi|)\), and the regulator of a state with compact coordinates only is at most \(C\beta_j|X|\). Hence the regulator part is \(\ge c\,g_j^2P_0L^{4j}/|\Pi|\). deduction, for a regulator with no unbounded coordinates in the carrier.

**What this establishes.** In every lift in which a label forcing microscopic energy is conditioning data, the step-\(j\) M-03 value norm of the exact one-field coefficient diverges in both its averaged and its regulator part. R1's moment window therefore cannot repair the norm. The finite-cutoff construction records the matching sufficiency statement (`Observable normalization`, condition (1): "a single unrefined probability reserve … cannot alone convert the microscopic budget to the current budget at all orders when \(B=o(G^{1/d})\)"); the comparison argument proves it is necessary.

**What it does not establish.** That no lift at all has a bounded norm. If labels and old variables are averaged at every step (Annex I), the lower bounds above do not apply. That route needs rarity of a history conditional on retained **fields**, pointwise, and PE's Proposition F shows this is false (§4.5). So the escape is closed for a different reason. deduction for the logic, given Prop. F.

## 3. The S-05 side finding

**The display.** Annex U (S.30): \(|t_{m,n,j}(X)|\le A_0^{m+1}(m!)^{p_0}h_j^{-4m}w_j\exp[-c\,\tau(X)/((m+d_0)h_j)]\), with \(\tau\) the minimum spanning-tree length, \(h_j=L^{-j}\), \(\sum_jh_jw_j<\infty\). "H" in R2 is only a number. R2's deduction is right: summing over \(j\), \(|C_{m,n}-C^{\rm ter}_{m,n}|\) is bounded on \(\{\tau\ge H\}\) by a finite function of \(H\) alone, uniformly in \(n\)..

**The example is wrong for the field named.** With \(G(x)=|x|^{-4}\Lambda^2I(x)\), \(I(x)=1-2xx^T/|x|^2\), the three-point function of the scalar is \(8\operatorname{tr}[G(x-y)G(y-z)G(z-x)]\), the trace of \(\Lambda^2\) of a product of three reflections. Such a product has determinant \(-1\), and \(\operatorname{tr}\Lambda^2R=0\) for every \(R\in O(4)\) with \(\det R=-1\). So the scalar Gaussian three-point function vanishes at separated points. 

**The finding stands for the family S-05 covers.** S-05 is uniform over the 21 labels. For the member \((F_{01})^2\), \(C_3=8\,G_{01,01}(x-y)G_{01,01}(y-z)G_{01,01}(z-x)\) with \(G_{01,01}(x)=|x|^{-4}[1-2(x_0^2+x_1^2)/|x|^2]\). At \(x=0\), \(y=\delta e_0\), \(z=He_2\) this is \(-8\,\delta^{-4}H^{-8}\) (finite comparison: \(C_3\delta^4\to-8.0000\)). The scalar four-point function with one colliding pair also grows like \(\delta^{-4}\). So (S.30), uniformly in \(n\), is false for the Gaussian hierarchy unless the cluster singularity is assigned to \(C^{\rm ter}\). deduction, with that caveat, which R2 states.

**(S.30′) has the same defect.** R2 writes "the leaf integral of \(\delta^{-4}\) against \(\delta^5\) converges". The weight in (S.30′) is \((\sum_i|X_i-X_1|)^5\), which is \(\approx H^5\), not \(\delta^5\), when only one pair collides. The integral \(\int d^4y\,|C_3|\) over \(a_n<|y-x|<H/4\) is \(\ge c\,H^{-8}\langle|1-2(e_0^2+e_1^2)|\rangle\log(H/a_n)\), and the angular average is \(0.50>0\). So the absolute, anchor-integrated quantity in (S.30′) diverges like \(\log(1/a_n)\) for the Gaussian hierarchy, again unless \(C^{\rm ter}\) carries it. The full-diagonal weight does not see partial diagonals..

**What S-02 really consumes.** The proof of Theorem 4.2 reads: "Summing (S.32) in (S.27), adding (S.29), and using (S.28) for order one …". The kernel bound enters only through the dual-norm inequality (S.32), \(\|T_{m,n,j}\|_{6m,*}\le A_2^{m+1}(m!)^{p_0+5}h_jw_j\) on \(\mathcal S_0(X_m)\). Tests in \(\mathcal S_0\) vanish to all orders on **every** pairwise diagonal (Annex U (S.1)), and \(\nu_{6m}\) has ample derivatives to use that. For the Gaussian \(C_3\) above, shell \(j\) (finest line at \(h_j\)) gains \(h_j\) from one order of partial flatness and converges at the full diagonal with order five. So (S.32) is attainable where (S.30) and (S.30′) are not. This is power counting for the Gaussian case, not a written-out proof. The right substitute to name in S-05 is (S.32) itself.

## 4. Downstream sufficiency

### 4.1 Integrated flat tests

A pointwise-in-anchor step-\(j\) budget is impossible for clustered anchors **in the regular sector too**. For \(m=2\) and \(|x-y|=\delta\ll h_j\), the exact coefficient contains \(\operatorname{Cov}(O_x,O_y\mid\mathcal F_j)\sim\delta^{-8}\); for \(m\) labels at one point, the transported product coefficient has \(L^1\) norm at least \(|\mathbb E\,O_x^m|\), and \(\mathbb E|O_x|^m\ge\tfrac12e^{-C_1NP_0}(a^{-4}NP_0/8)^m\), in every lift. The criterion already allow for this: M-06 asks for "a carrier-bound sum with fixed q,p" and "common fixed factorial and spatial-order parameters"; F-06 for "a linear-in-m spatial distribution order"; F-07 for "the joint observable norm required by S-06"; the M-07 note works with smeared sources ("Fix finitely many smeared sources \(f_1,\dots,f_n\)", §2.1). So for \(m\ge2\) the M/F/S chain is distributional in the anchors with or without large fields.

Anchors in the deep core of one retained event lie within a physical distance \(\ll h_j\) of each other: they are a collision cluster. What the obstruction adds is the **one-field** coefficient at a core anchor, and one core anchor joined to distant ones. For that coefficient the per-event inequality is exactly the step-\(j\) budget in \(L^1(\mu)\), pointwise in the anchor: \(\mathbb E[\mathbf 1_Eh_j^4|Q_jO_x|]\lesssim L^{4(j-b)}\mathbb P(E)\). This is R1's window at \(r\,m=1\). The clarification therefore keeps the original test at the one point of the \((r,m)\) plane where it can hold, and treats everything else as collisions are already treated.

### 4.3 Does "\(L^{4(j-b)}\) once" survive several marks in one footprint? — deduction for the bookkeeping of one event, additional assumption for the general shell attribution

Take one retained event with \(k\) anchors in its onion and the other \(m-k\) at distance of order \(h_j\). An anchor in the belt \(\Gamma_n\) carries a mark of size \(h_n^{-4}\) and is summed over cells of volume \(h_n^4\). So each anchor contributes the number of native cells of the event, times a moment of native energy:

\[
\text{contribution}\ \approx\ \mathbb P(E)\ \times\ L^{4(j-b)}\ (\text{positions of the core relative to the root})\ \times\ \bigl(C\,|Y|_\natural\cdot\text{poly}\bigr)^k.
\]

The factor \(L^{4(j-b)}\) does occur once, independently of \(k\) and of the moment order. If all anchors are inside the footprint it does not occur at all: that contribution belongs to the birth shell.

**What R2's inequality omits.** \(|Y|_\natural\), the number of cells of the event counted at their native scales, is at least \((MR)^4\) per cube per step alive. It is polylogarithmic in the coupling and unbounded as the cutoff is removed. \((C|Y|_\natural)^k\) is not \(C^k\). R2 notes a factor "\(\le e^{4R_b/e}\) uniformly in \(m\)" but does not charge it to anyone. It must be charged to the event: \((An)^k\le(k!)^{q_0}\exp(q_0A^{1/q_0}n)\) for \(n\ge1\), so an exponent \(\sigma|Y|_\natural\) with \(\sigma\ge q_0A^{1/q_0}\) pays it at the price of \(q_0\) in the factorial exponent. Without this term H-02 could be passed with \(\sigma\) just above the carrier threshold while S-05 could not absorb the occupancy uniformly. The repair is cheap: under PE's hypothesis (J) the proof of Theorem R already bounds the weight \(\prod_{(n,Q)}e^{\sigma(MR_n)^d}\) over the whole space-time ancestry, which dominates \(e^{\sigma'|Y|_\natural}\). (b: (J) and PE's geometric readings.)

**Fixed factorial budget.** With integrated anchors the \(m\)-dependence per event is \((C|Y|_\natural\,\text{poly})^k\), polylogarithmic in the birth-scale coupling. Against \(e^{-cp_0(g_b)^2}\), \(\sup_u u^{4rk}e^{-Au^{2p_0}}\le C^k(k!)^{2r/p_0}\) with \(u=\log g_b^{-2}\) (so \(R_b^4\sim u^{4r}\), \(p_0(g_b)^2\sim u^{2p_0}\)): a fixed factorial power, also after summing over birth scales. This is the opposite of Annex Z Prop. EC.7, where growth is a **power of the age** to the \(m\)-th power; that is what pointwise accounting produces (\(L^{4(j-b)k}\)) and why R1's window cannot give a fixed \((C,p)\). R2's §4.3 computation is a heuristic illustration of this; R1's own Cor. 3.4 is the proved statement. The integrated count is an additional assumption beyond one event: it assumes that anchors closer than the current scale have been fused into one composite insertion, so that later events do not multiply by \(C^k\) again.

### 4.4 Is the per-event inequality plausible? For how long can an event stay retained?

**Against the source's exponents: yes, with a wide margin.** source input; additional assumption. A region born at step \(b\) lives at most \(\log_2d_b'+1+R_b\) steps without a new record (§1.2), and its unnormalized factor is \(\exp(-\tfrac12\gamma_0A_1^2p_0^2(g_b)(d_b'+1)-2p_0(g_b))\) (LFR II p.381, source). Each extension costs a further birth or a failure, the latter \(\exp(-p_0(g))\) for \(R\) more steps (p.386). So the amortized suppression per step is at least of order \(p_0(g)/R(g)\sim(\log g^{-2})^{p_0-r}\), against a growth of \(4\log L\) per step. This holds at every scale including the terminal ones, provided the terminal coupling is small enough for Bałaban's own induction (which needs the stronger \(p_0(g)\gtrsim M^dR^{d+2}\)). Oversized components are covered: their extra lifetime is \(\log_2d'\) and their exponent is proportional to \(d'\).

**PE's Theorem R states this cleanly.** conditional. Given a normalized joint record bound (J), \(\mathbb P(\text{records})\le\exp(-2\theta\sum c^{\rm Bal})\), the per-step reserve \(c_0M^dR_n^{d+1}\) per ancestor cube that Bałaban's induction (1.80)–(1.88) budgets pays \(L^4\), the carrier weight and the ancestry entropy at every step, for chains, merges, failures and arbitrary lifetimes. The interpretation of pp.384–387 in the source and the reserve rules are is PE quotes them. The formula gives the structure of Lemma 3.2 and of the summation; The argument assumes the geometric readings (G1)–(G5), the determinism (G3), or the \(\theta\)-scaled reserve line by line. Note a transcription slip in PE's table (\(A_0^2\) for \(A_1^2\) in (1.79)) and that (1.79) carries a factor \(\tfrac12\) that (1.85) as printed does not; both affect constants only.

**What is not known.** (J) is an **unproved** statement about the normalized law. The source's factors are unnormalized activities (R1 (S4), Annex AK §6). (J) is proved for first-step births (K-02, R1 Lemma 4.1; PE Cor. 3.7 adds a geometric reading). For births at depth, P-flags and preparatory failures it is open, and PE's Lemma 4.2 shows microscopic energy certificates cannot reach them beyond depth \(\approx\tfrac14\log_L(p_0^2/R^4)\). There is no identified evidence that (J) or (E) is false: the only lower bound available, \(\mathbb P\ge\tfrac12e^{-C_1NP_0}\) for first-step events, is consistent with (E) because \(P_0\gg R\log L\). **So the clarified H-02 is attainable on paper and consistent with every bound I know, but its analytic core is as open as before.** The clarification removes an impossibility; it does not make H-02 easy.

### 4.5 The new evidence in `Joint events indexed by retained shapes`

* **Proposition F.** The formula gives the construction: the centre-valued bonds give two vortex sheets; for a slice through the box the loop \(\partial Q\) and the ring plaquettes are exterior, \(\operatorname{hol}(\partial Q)=z^{-1}\), and non-abelian Stokes forces \(\sum_{p\in Q\cap P(B)}\|y_p-1\|_{\rm HS}\ge\sqrt N\sin(\pi/N)\) for every filling. Conditional on such an exterior a first-step flag inside has probability one. The same happens without any exterior large field: a flux tube that flares outside the box keeps every exterior plaquette small and still forces the interior. So R2's clause "conditional on the retained data outside a declared neighbourhood" is false if retained data include fields, which they do in lift β. The clause must read: joint, marked, or conditional on labels. 
* **Proposition N for the Wilson density, deduction for the inequality chain** (Stokes lower bound \(0.64\,b\,s^4\) against the allowed \(b(8C_{\rm ext}r_0+2)s^3+c_Fs^4\)). The proof assumes the constants of R1's §5.2 setting beyond (LD) and (5.2). Its consequence is that inherited low-margin events are not conditionally rare and must be charged jointly to the configuration that forces them. This supports the same change of wording. The transfer to the step-\(k\) density (N.2) is the derivation's additional assumption.
* **Theorem R** — see §4.4. It answers the plausibility question conditionally on (J).
* **§2.5 (\(p_0>6r\))**, see §1.2.
* PE's point that \(b\) is the **oldest** birth in the ancestry is right and is used in §5.

### 4.6 Multiple-anchor reset bounds

1. **S-04.** R2 writes that S-04 "attributes retained-sector contributions to the shell of their current carrier" and lists the proof of this as open. S-04's criterion ("including outer means, collision vertices, history resets and normalization") does not mention carried sector records. This must be written into the construction (§5, sixth entry).
2. **H-04, reset conversion.** The conditional estimate requires the step-\(j\) budget "of the outputs of completed resets (H-03)", while R2's own open item 3 says that for several anchors this fails and must be restated. Two or more anchors inside one replaced footprint are a collision vertex and need the anchor-summed form. For one anchor the requirement is the marked R quotient, the "blocking item" of `Analytic families of transported insertions` §10.5; it is not excluded by the obstruction but is open, and H-03's accepted theorem is law-level only. The wording must say that this is H-04's obligation.
3. **S-05** must absorb the occupancy factor unless H-02 pays it (§4.3). In §5 H-02 pays it.

### 4.7 What the integrated formulation does not serve

* **E-02, E-03, E-04.** These ask for arbitrary-vector operator bounds ("pressure derivatives and vacuum moments are insufficient"). A probability-weighted bound says nothing about a vector concentrated on a rare sector. If these proposition were to use the regulator part of M-07's output (domination of the transported field by retained energy, pointwise in the conditioning data), that route does not exist on retained sectors, by Prop. B. The clarification loses nothing here, because the lost bound was never attainable; but the E-proposition will need their own treatment. The native-scale record is dominated pointwise by native energy, which is the "\(a^{-4}\) loss … an input, not the conclusion" of the E-04 criterion. additional assumption. The hypotheses do not specify from the criterion how M-07 is meant to feed E-02–E-04.
* **R-02, R-03.** Angular defects of a native-scale record stop contracting at its birth scale. Their total contribution is \(\sum_b(\text{defect at }h_b)\times\delta_b\), which tends to zero with the cutoff if the weights \(\delta_b\sim e^{-cp_0(g_b)}\) are summable over scales; they are (\(e^{-A(\log b')^{p_0}}\)). additional assumption. The conditional theorems P0–P4 were not written with sector records in mind.
* **H-05.** `Analytic families of transported insertions` §10.3 already notes that weighted sector offsets give "fixed-order weighted bounds but **not** a fixed-factorial budget in \(m\)". That risk is independent of this assumption.
* **Sector-dependent identity terms.** After \(k\gtrsim P_0/\log L\) steps the regular-sector mean of the centred microscopic density differs from zero by about \(a^{-4}P_0e^{-cP_0}\gg h_k^{-4}\). It is a c-number per sector, to be carried in the identity coefficient and the outer responses. It is harmless on flat tests and another reason pointwise budgets near sectors are the wrong object. additional assumption.

### 4.8 A correction to R2's remark on (10.3)

R2 states that (10.3) of `Analytic families of transported insertions` "also fails at anchors inside retained finer-scale regions". This holds for pointwise anchors. That note fixes **smeared** sources (§2.1), and for a source smeared over a current cell the retained part contributes \(\|f\|_\infty|Y|_\natural(1+\text{native energy})\), a polylogarithm that the recorded-energy regulator (10.1) is designed to absorb. So the M-07 route was already working in the integrated formulation. R2's point about the name clash "H-02′" is right.

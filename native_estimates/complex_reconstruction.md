# Reconstruction of a complex field about a fixed real center

The coupling recurrence is conditional. For the projected sum $C_n$ of the actual constrained Gaussian increments, Gaussian calibration requires identification with the composite constrained Gaussian and $\sup_n|C_n-nd_B|<\infty$. The finite-dimensional projection and the logarithmic shell coefficient do not prove this uniform bound. In the transverse decomposition it requires $\sup_n|\mathcal B_n|<\infty$. Level-adapted restriction, real-centered complex reconstruction, the multiscale boundary restriction, flat-vertex constants and a common coupling ceiling are required in the same representation. See [Gaussian calibration](gaussian_calibration.md) and [complex reconstruction](complex_reconstruction.md).

## 2. The lemmas

### 2.1 Lemma 2.1: the axial gauge depends on the reference

[R] (1.19) source: "the axial gauge conditions relative to \(U_0\) … \((\bar R^n_{0,x_{n+1}}\tilde U'^n)(\Gamma_{x_{n+1},x_n})=\prod_{b\subset\Gamma_{x_{n+1},x_n}}R(\bar U_0^n(\Gamma_{x_{n+1},b}))\tilde U'^n_b=1\)", with (1.20) \(\tilde U'^n=(\overline{U'U_0})^n(\bar U_0^n)^{-1}\), and "The conditions (1.19) determine uniquely an element in each orbit given by the subgroup (1.14)" (\(u(y)=1\), \(y\in\mathfrak B_k\)).

*Computation deduction.* With \(g_b:=\bar U_0^n(\Gamma_{x_{n+1},b_-})\) and \(\bar U^n:=(\overline{U'U_0})^n\), the factor for the bond \(b\) is \(g_b\bar U^n_b(\bar U^n_{0,b})^{-1}g_b^{-1}\), and \(g_{b'}=g_b\bar U^n_{0,b}\) for the next bond \(b'\). The ordered product telescopes to \(\bar U^n(\Gamma_{x_{n+1},x_n})\,\bar U_0^n(\Gamma_{x_{n+1},x_n})^{-1}\). So (1.19) reads
\[
\bar U^n(\Gamma_{x_{n+1},x_n})=\bar U_0^n(\Gamma_{x_{n+1},x_n})\qquad(0\le n\le j-1,\ x_j\in\Lambda_j):
\]
the holonomies of the level-\(n\) averages of \(U\) along the tree contours must equal those of \(U_0\). For \(n<j\) inside a \(j\)-block these holonomies are **not** fixed by the data \(V\) (only \(\bar U^j=V\) on \(\Lambda_j\) is). Hence two references \(U_k(V_0)\ne U_k(V_1)\) give two different gauge conditions, and the axial representatives of the same orbit differ by a gauge transformation in (1.14).

*Consequence.* On a real point \(W\in T(V_0)\cap T(V_1)\), \(E_{V_0}(W)\) and \(E_{V_1}(W)\) are both minimisers of the data \(W\), in the gauges \(Ax(\mathfrak B_k,U_k(V_0))\) and \(Ax(\mathfrak B_k,U_k(V_1))\): equal up to \(u\in(1.14)\), \(u\) real, not equal. The identity theorem in the corresponding proof needs equality. So Lemma 2.1 is not proved, when interpreted as equality of two chosen axial representatives. What *is* true: \(E_{V_0}=E_{V_1}^{u(\cdot)}\) on the overlap with \(u(W)\) holomorphic (composition of the two gauge-fixing maps (106) of [A]), and presumably \(\lvert u-1\rvert\) small by (1.70)–(1.72)-type bounds; The argument requires the bound and the note does not need it (next paragraph). The source's own phrase "extended … to all orbits by (181), treating the equality as a definition for the remaining \(v\)" (p.307) shows that the source too has a well-defined *orbit* and a representative only after a choice.

*Why Theorem 3.1 survives.* In the proof, \(T=\min\{T_r,T_s\}\le T_s\), and Lemma 2.5 with \(r=0\) gives \(M'(\mathbf U_t)\in T(V_0(0))\) for all \(\lvert t\rvert<T_s\supset D(0,T)\). So the single extension \(E_{V_0(0)}\), \(V_0(0)=M'(U)\), \(U\) the real part of \(\mathbf U\), defines \(f(t)\) on the whole disc; it is holomorphic there (composition of P9♮'s holomorphic \(\mathscr H\), the analytic gauge map, and the analytic data map \(t\mapsto M'(\mathbf U_t)\), [A] Props 6–7); on \([-1,1]\) its values are the minimisers in a *real* gauge, for which the norms of \(\partial\cdot-1\) and \(D^*\partial\cdot\) are gauge invariant, so Theorem 1's bound \(m\) applies. Lemma 2.6 must then be read with \(r=0\): \(W_0=U_n(M'(U))\in\mathfrak U_n(B_3\cdot2\hat\kappa(0)\ell_n^2)\), which (Ce2) allows. Nothing else in the proof uses \(r\ne0\).

*What the theorem is about.* Define, for \(\mathbf U=e^{i\xi A'}U\) with \(U\) real (clause (i)),
\[
U_n(Z;M'(\mathbf U)):=E_{M'(U)}\big(M'(\mathbf U)\big)\quad\text{in the axial gauge }Ax_n(\mathfrak B_n,U_n(Z;M'(U))).
\]
This is the natural reading of [I] (1.15) ("the functions \(U_n(V)\) in the axial gauges"), it is a single-valued holomorphic function of \((A',U)\) on the tube, it is equivariant under real gauge transformations by (181), and it is the object for which Theorem 3.1 is proved. The same proof, with the tube radius halved, gives the same bound for \(E_{V_0}\) for *any* real reference \(V_0\) satisfying (7) with \(\varepsilon^\sharp\) and \(M'(U)\in T_{1/2}(V_0)\) (half tube): the bound of clause (iv) is robust under the choice of reference, with the same constant. The manuscript should fix the convention (§5, item A).

## 3. Theorem 3.1

### 3.1 The proof, step by step (with the single-tube reading)

1. \(T\ge T_0\): (Ce1) gives \(T_s\ge T_0\); (Ce2) and monotonicity of \(\hat\kappa\) give \(T_r\ge T_0\); the first condition in \(T_r\) is never binding (\(32\xi a_1(1+T_s)\le\frac14+\frac1{20}\)). 
2. \(f(t)=\partial E_{V_0(0)}(M'(\mathbf U_t))(p)-1\) holomorphic on \(D(0,T)\): Lemma 2.5 with \(r=0\), P9♮(1) (holomorphy of \(\mathscr H\)), analyticity of the gauge map and of \(t\mapsto M'(\mathbf U_t)\). 
3. \(\lVert f\rVert\le M_{\rm p}\) on the disc: Lemma 2.6 with \(r=0\). 
4. \(\lVert f\rVert\le m=2B_3\hat\kappa(1)\xi^2\) on \([-1,1]\): real data, (7) with \(\varepsilon_1=2\hat\kappa(1)\ell_n^2\le\varepsilon^\sharp\le a_1^{[V]}\) ([A] Prop. 2, iterated averaging [A] (43)), Theorem 1 on \(\Omega_n\) with \(L^{-2n}\ell_n^2=\xi^2\), gauge invariance of the norm for the real representative. 
5. Ratio. Case \(T=T_s\): \(\hat\kappa(1)\ell_n^2\ge1376a_1^2\ell_n^2=1376\,C_1^2\varepsilon^{\sharp2}/(4096T^2)\ge C_1^2\varepsilon^{\sharp2}/(3T^2)\), so \(M_{\rm p}/m\le3(2B_3+3B_5)T^2/(B_3C_1^2\varepsilon^\sharp)\le KT^2\). Case \(T=T_r\): \(\hat\kappa(T)\le\frac12(1+T)^2\hat\kappa(1)\) (check: \(\hat\kappa(1)/2=2a_c+688a_1^2\ge2a_c+344a_1^2\)), \(M_{\rm p}/m\le(2B_3+3B_5)(1+T)^2/B_3\le4(2B_3+3B_5)T^2/B_3\le KT^2\) using \(C_1^2\varepsilon^\sharp\le1\). 
6. Interpolation: \((KT^2)^{\log(1+\sqrt2)/\log T}=(1+\sqrt2)^{2+\log K/\log T}\le(1+\sqrt2)^3\) for \(T\ge\max\{K,e^2\}\); if \(K<1\) the factor is \(\le(1+\sqrt2)^2\). With \(\hat\kappa(1)=4a_c+1376a_1^2\): \(\lVert f(i)\rVert\le8(1+\sqrt2)^3B_3(a_c+344a_1^2)\xi^2\). 

**Depth independence.** The disc radius is \(T\asymp\varepsilon^\sharp/(a_1\ell_n)\) or \(\asymp\varepsilon^\sharp/(a_c\ell_n^2)\): polynomially large in \(\ell_n^{-1}\), and the crude/sharp ratio is \(\asymp\varepsilon^\sharp/(B_3\hat\kappa\ell_n^2)\), at most \(KT^2\). A harmonic-measure loss \((M/m)^{c/\log T}\) with \(M/m\) polynomial in \(T\) is bounded. This is the correct and complete reason; the analysis \(O(1)\)-disc computation (loss \(\ell_n^{-0.41}\)) used a disc too small by the factor \(T\).

### 3.3 Substituting C-05 Theorem P9♮ for "Proposition 9 at its word"

### 4.2 Lemma 4.2 in full, with constants (Lemma 4.2′)

**Setting.** \(U\) real on \(Z\) with \(\lvert\partial U-1\rvert\le a_0\xi^2\); \(\mathbf U=e^{i\xi A'}U\) with \(\lvert A'\rvert\le a_1\) and complex curvature \(a_c\); a complex bond field \(\mathbf A\) with \(\lvert\mathbf A\rvert\le b_0\), \(\lvert\nabla_U\mathbf A\rvert\le b_1\), where \((\nabla_{U,\mu}\mathbf A_\nu)(x):=\xi^{-1}[\operatorname{Ad}_{U(x,x+\mu)}\mathbf A_\nu(x+\mu)-\mathbf A_\nu(x)]\); \(\mathbf U(s):=e^{i\xi s\mathbf A}\mathbf U=e^{i\xi A''(s)}U\); \(a_1(s):=\sup\lvert A''(s)\rvert\), \(a_c(s):=\sup_p\lvert\partial\mathbf U(s)(p)-1\rvert/\xi^2\). Operator norms throughout; \(\lvert\operatorname{ad}_Y\rvert\le2\lvert Y\rvert\), \(\lvert\operatorname{Ad}_{e^Y}-1\rvert\le e^{2\lvert Y\rvert}-1\), \(\lvert\operatorname{Ad}_V X\rvert=\lvert X\rvert\) for unitary \(V\).

> **Lemma 4.2′.** Let \(\lambda\ge1\) and assume \(\xi(a_1+\lambda b_0)\le\frac1{10}\), \(40\,\xi\lambda b_0\le1\), \(a_c\xi^2\le\frac14\) and \(\lambda\xi^2\big(9b_1+27(a_1+\lambda b_0)b_0\big)\le\frac18\). Then for real \(0\le s\le\lambda\) (and, applied to \(s\mathbf A\) in place of \(\mathbf A\), for complex \(\lvert s\rvert\le\lambda\)):
> \[
> a_1(s)\le\tfrac54\,(a_1+sb_0),\qquad
> a_c(s)\le a_c+s\Big[9b_1+27\,(a_1+sb_0)\,b_0+15\,\xi b_0\,a_c\Big].
> \]
> In the notation of the note: \(C_P=27\), with the \(b_0\xi a_1^\nabla\) term absent and the \(\xi b_0\) term carrying the factor \(a_c\).

*Proof.* (i) \(A''(s)=\frac1{i\xi}\log(e^{i\xi s\mathbf A}e^{i\xi A'})\). With \(\sigma:=\xi(a_1+sb_0)\le\frac1{10}\): \(e^{P}e^{Q}=1+Y\), \(\lvert Y\rvert\le e^{\sigma}-1\le1.106\sigma\), \(\lvert\log(1+Y)\rvert\le\lvert Y\rvert/(1-\lvert Y\rvert)\le1.24\sigma\). Hence \(a_1(s)\le1.24(a_1+sb_0)\).

(ii) Fix \(p=(x,x+\mu,x+\mu+\nu,x+\nu)\) and write \(P(s)=g_1g_2g_3^{-1}g_4^{-1}\) with \(g_1=\mathbf U_s(x,x+\mu)\), \(g_2=\mathbf U_s(x+\mu,x+\mu+\nu)\), \(g_3=\mathbf U_s(x+\nu,x+\mu+\nu)\), \(g_4=\mathbf U_s(x,x+\nu)\), \(\mathbf A_1=\mathbf A_\mu(x)\), \(\mathbf A_2=\mathbf A_\nu(x+\mu)\), \(\mathbf A_3=\mathbf A_\mu(x+\nu)\), \(\mathbf A_4=\mathbf A_\nu(x)\). From \(\dot g=i\xi\mathbf A g\), \(\frac{d}{ds}g^{-1}=-g^{-1}i\xi\mathbf A\) and \(g_1g_2g_3^{-1}=Pg_4\):
\[
\frac{dP}{ds}=i\xi\,\mathcal C\,P,\qquad\mathcal C=\mathbf A_1+\operatorname{Ad}_{g_1}\mathbf A_2-\operatorname{Ad}_{Pg_4}\mathbf A_3-\operatorname{Ad}_P\mathbf A_4.
\]
With \(\operatorname{Ad}_{g_1}\mathbf A_\nu(x+\mu)=\mathbf A_\nu(x)+\xi(\nabla_{\mathbf U_s,\mu}\mathbf A_\nu)(x)\) and \(\operatorname{Ad}_{g_4}\mathbf A_\mu(x+\nu)=\mathbf A_\mu(x)+\xi(\nabla_{\mathbf U_s,\nu}\mathbf A_\mu)(x)\),
\[
\mathcal C=\xi\big(\nabla_{\mathbf U_s,\mu}\mathbf A_\nu-\nabla_{\mathbf U_s,\nu}\mathbf A_\mu\big)(x)-(\operatorname{Ad}_P-1)\big(\mathbf A_\mu(x)+\mathbf A_\nu(x)+\xi\nabla_{\mathbf U_s,\nu}\mathbf A_\mu(x)\big).
\]
(The identity was illustrative only to \(2\cdot10^{-5}\) relative error on 1061 random plaquettes.) Now (a) \(\operatorname{Ad}_{\mathbf U_s(b)}=\operatorname{Ad}_{e^{i\xi A''_s(b)}}\operatorname{Ad}_{U(b)}\), so \(\lvert\nabla_{\mathbf U_s}\mathbf A-\nabla_U\mathbf A\rvert\le\xi^{-1}(e^{2\xi a_1(s)}-1)b_0\le2.6\,a_1(s)b_0\) (\(\xi a_1(s)\le0.124\)); conditional with \(\delta:=\lvert P(s)-1\rvert\le\frac12\): \(\lvert\operatorname{Ad}_P-1\rvert\le2\delta/(1-\delta)\le4\delta\), \(\lvert P\rvert\le\frac32\). Hence, as long as \(\delta\le\frac12\),
\[
\Big\lvert\frac{dP}{ds}\Big\rvert\le\tfrac32\xi\lvert\mathcal C\rvert\le3\xi^2\big(b_1+2.6a_1(s)b_0\big)+6\xi\delta\big(2b_0+\xi b_1+2.6\xi a_1(s)b_0\big).
\]
In the second group use \(\delta\le\frac12\) on the two terms carrying an extra \(\xi\): \(6\xi\delta\cdot\xi b_1\le3\xi^2b_1\) and \(6\xi\delta\cdot2.6\xi a_1(s)b_0\le7.8\xi^2a_1(s)b_0\). So, with \(c(s):=\lvert P(s)-1\rvert/\xi^2\) and \(a_1(s)\le1.24(a_1+sb_0)\),
\[
\dot c(s)\le6b_1+15.6\,a_1(s)b_0+12\,\xi b_0\,c(s)\le6b_1+19.4\,(a_1+sb_0)b_0+12\,\xi b_0\,c(s).
\]
Grönwall on \([0,s]\), \(s\le\lambda\), \(x:=12\xi b_0s\le0.3\): \(c(s)\le e^{x}\big[a_c+s(6b_1+19.4(a_1+sb_0)b_0)\big]\). With \(e^{x}\le1+1.2x\le1.36\) for \(x\le0.3\): \(c(s)\le a_c+14.4\,\xi b_0s\,a_c+1.36\,s(6b_1+19.4(a_1+sb_0)b_0)\le a_c+s[9b_1+27(a_1+sb_0)b_0+15\xi b_0a_c]\). The bootstrap \(\delta\le\frac12\) holds throughout: under the hypotheses the right side times \(\xi^2\) is \(\le\frac14+\frac18+\frac{15}{40}\cdot\frac14<\frac12\). Take the sup over \(p\). \(\square\)

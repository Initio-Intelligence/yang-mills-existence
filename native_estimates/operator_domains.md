a **ceiling on the current coupling**, absolute once \(d,L,\kappa,M,C_0,C_1\) are fixed. ### 5.4 (Res-pat): the \(\beta\)-reserve bookkeeping and the \((1+\beta)\) of (3.43)

**Text.** (2.34)–(2.39), p.261: coefficient \((1-\beta(1-2^{-(j-n)}))\alpha_{0,n}\) (resp. \(\alpha_{1,n}\) in (2.39)) "on \(X\cap(\Omega_n\setminus\Omega_{n+1})\) for \(n=1,\dots,j-1\), or on \(X\cap\Omega_j\) for \(n=j\)"; "The number \(\beta\) is a small positive constant, but not too small, e.g., we can take \(\beta=1/4\)." Note that (2.34) constrains the **real** \(U\) and the complex \(\mathbf U\) alike, and that (ii) (2.38), the gauge-regularity of the real part, carries no \(\beta\). (3.43), p.276: "The remaining terms satisfy such conditions also, but with analyticity space (I.6.34) replaced by the space \(\tilde U^c_{k+1}(Y,(1+\beta)\tilde\alpha_0,(1+\beta)\tilde\alpha_1)\times\{A:\operatorname{supp}A\subset Y\cap\Lambda^{(k)*}_{k+1},|A|<C_1p_1(g_k)\}\)." P.277: "in the domain \(\Omega^c_{k+1}\) there is no regularity improvement of the configuration \(U_{k+1}\); on the other hand we have to use a part of the analyticity domains of terms in \(\mathbf B_k\) for the function \(\mathbf H_k\). This is the reason for putting the powers of 1/2 in the conditions (2.36)–(2.39). The analyticity domains become smaller after each step, but the difference is very small and exponentially decreasing in the number of steps. The localization of the fluctuation field, and the exponential decay of the minimizing function \(\mathbf H_k\) imply that this difference is still much greater than bounds on this function." The small-field original, [I] (3.16) (text p.273): the extension is defined on "\(U^c_j(X,\frac12\alpha_0,\frac12\alpha_1,\alpha_0)\cap\{(\mathbf U,\mathbf J):\ \mathbf U,\mathbf J\) satisfy the conditions (i)–(iii) for \(j=k+1\), and with the constants \((1+\beta)\alpha_0,(1+\beta)\alpha_1,\alpha_0\}\)" — i.e. **the \((1+\beta)\) applies to the new top scale \(k+1\) only**, intersected with the old term's own space for the rest.

**Reading additional assumption, with the argument.** (i) *Old layers \(n<j\).* The reserve between a birth-\(j\) term and a birth-\((k+1)\) term is \(\beta(2^{-(j-n)}-2^{-(k+1-n)})\ge\frac\beta2 2^{-(j-n)}\) — exactly (Res-pat)'s first clause; the p.277 sentence names this as the mechanism ("exponentially decreasing in the number of steps", "the exponential decay of \(\mathbf H_k\) … much greater"). (ii) *The \((1+\beta)\) cannot apply literally to all layers.* Outside \(\Omega_{k+1}\) the T step changes neither the variables nor the background; an old term's dependence on \(\mathbf U\) there is direct, so a function built from birth-\(j\) terms cannot be analytic on a larger domain than theirs at an old layer. But \((1+\beta)(1-\beta(1-2^{-(k+1-n)}))>1-\beta(1-2^{-(j-n)})\) for every \(n<j\le k\) (it reduces to \(2^{-(j-n)}-(1+\beta)2^{-(k+1-n)}\ge1-\beta\), false for \(j-n\ge1\), \(\beta\le\frac14\)). So the all-layer reading of (3.43) is inconsistent with (2.34)–(2.39), and the only consistent reading is the one [I] (3.16) states explicitly: \((1+\beta)\) at the top scale, the inherited profile below. (iii) *Why the top scale affords \((1+\beta)\).* A birth-\(j\) term's domain on \(X\cap\Omega_j\) is stated in scale-\(j\) units; re-expressed in scale-\(n\) units on a layer \(n\ge j\) it gains \(L^{n-j}\) in the field slot (2.39) and \(L^{2(n-j)}\) in the plaquette slot (2.34)–(2.35). At the top scale with \(j\le k\) the gain is \(\ge L\ge2\) against the claimed \((1+\beta)\alpha_{\cdot,k+1}\approx1.25\,\alpha_{\cdot,j}(1+O(g^2))\) per step — so the \((1+\beta)\) is affordable there and gives the *new* term a reserve \(\frac32\beta\) rather than \(\frac\beta2\) at its own layer when it is next composed. (iv) *Consequence for \(r_T\).* The profile actually available is **at least** (Res-pat): \(\varrho_x\ge\frac\beta22^{-(j-n)}\alpha_{\wedge,n}\) for \(n<j\), \(\varrho_x\ge\frac\beta2L^{n-j}\alpha_{\wedge,j}\) for \(n\ge j\) (the latter conservative by the factor \((1+\beta)\)-induced \(\frac32\beta\) at \(n=j\) and by \(L^{2(n-j)}\) in the plaquette slot). So the \((1+\beta)\) does **not** reduce \(r_T\); [DN]'s doubt 5 ("seems to enlarge the domain at layer \(k\) beyond that of a birth-\(k\) term") is resolved: it does enlarge it, legitimately, by the scale gain, and only at the top scale. The \(L^{n-j}\) gain for \(n\ge j\) is **load-bearing** for the uniformity of \(r_T\) in the age \(k-j\) at the current layer (without it the bound would carry \(1/\sqrt{1+k-j}\) from asymptotic freedom), and it rests on the scale-\(j\) normalization of the \(n=j\) clause of (2.34)–(2.39), which is printed.

Regard (Res-pat) as **settled at the level additional assumption**: it is the unique reading of the printed domains under which (3.43) is not self-contradictory, it matches [I] (3.16) literally, and it is what p.277 describes. What remains unprinted is the inductive shedding of the top-scale \((1+\beta)\) (by the R step or the next step's \(1-\beta/2\)); that is a source bookkeeping matter, not an input to \(r_T\).

## 6. Consistency of the constants

### 6.1 Order and size

The chain imposes, in this order: (1) \(d,L\) → \(\Delta_*=(L+2)^d-L^d\), \(b_0=\log(2^{d+1}\Delta_*^2)\). (2) \(\kappa\): T′ alone needs \(\kappa>b_0+1\); ST needs \(\kappa_2=\kappa-1-b_0-\theta\ge1\) with \(\theta\ge2\) (Prop. R); Prop. N needs \(b''=\kappa_2-1-\vartheta-2\log\Delta_*\ge(d-1)\log L+\log2\). For \(d=4,L=2\): \(b_0=14.43\), \(2\log\Delta_*=10.96\), so \(\kappa_2\ge14.73+\vartheta\) and **\(\kappa\ge32.2+\vartheta\)** for the ST/Prop. N chain (take \(\kappa=33\)); \(\kappa\ge15.5\) suffices for T′ alone ([SI]'s "17.4" is T′ with a margin). (3) \(M\): \(\kappa\le\kappa_{\max}=\delta_0M/(2^{d+6}L^2)\) gives \(M\ge4096\,\kappa/\delta_0\approx1.4\cdot10^5/\delta_0\) for \(\kappa=33\); plus \(M\ge160L^3M_1\); plus the walk-convergence \(M\ge M_0(d,L)\) ([SI]'s crude \(K_w\) makes this \(e^{10^4}\); the true \(M_0\) is unknown but absolute).

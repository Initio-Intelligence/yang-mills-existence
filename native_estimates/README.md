# Analytic estimates for lattice gauge fields

These estimates concern the finite periodic Wilson measure. Hypotheses that rely on a source interpretation or an unproved uniform bound are identified separately. Within a derivation, section and equation numbers refer to that derivation unless a separate work is named.

**Coupling.** In the normalization $Y=g^{-2}=2N/g_{\mathrm{std}}^2$, the proposed decrement is $d_B=11N^2\log L/(12\pi^2)$. The finite Schur identities and the angular average of the Wilson Hessian give the explicit logarithmic shell term. The uniform bound on the remainder, the identification of this calculation with the actual projected coupling, the multiscale boundary restriction and the uniform flat-vertex constants are distinct assumptions.

**Conditional boundary derivatives.** On the normal-form conditional law, integrate the current free coordinates at each T step, the first-class variables and completed preparation stages at an R step, and the auxiliary variables specified in clause (2″). Raw slots and relocated slots differ by a Haar density factor as well as a determinant. The current-rooted result does not bound a pure native derivative, a retained-data derivative, or the outer-law scalar. The single-holed-ancestor estimate uses (BV-Lip) for data-form records; connected products still require the collective crossing estimate (R-T2-H). Dom-PF, V2-R and the local Schur-kernel identification are further inputs wherever invoked.

**Large-field events.** Rarity under the Wilson law cannot yield a pointwise bound after conditioning on the rare event. Joint weights, shape sums and flat insertions must be estimated under the same law. On the local R-test use F(4′), (Sm3′), (Par-g_Z′), A8′ items 3′/4′, and (Lay-W) with $M_W\mid M_1$, $M_1\ge6M_W$. In particular the conditional cluster estimate needs $\kappa_1/2\ge\sqrt d(1+3\beta)\kappa/(1-3\delta)$. The primary geometric condition is CMP 96, (2.59), with $\alpha/4$ as printed. Multiplying the Wilson marginal by an exact coordinate identity does not transport all norms and derivative domains to the amended variables.

**Thermal traces.** The finite-volume comparison uses positive rectangular transfer operators. The Laplace calculation gives a proposed finite-torus estimate at $\beta\ge\beta_*m^{C_0}$. It does not apply at fixed physical terminal coupling. Slab insertion bounds also require the structural transfer realization, not just subexponential scalar coefficient growth.

## Source notation

Brackets with Roman numerals in the renormalization formulas denote Bałaban's papers in *Communications in Mathematical Physics*: [I], vol. 109, pp. 249–301; [II], vol. 116, pp. 1–22; [III], vol. 119, pp. 243–285. [IV] or LFR I, vol. 122, pp. 175–202, and LFR II, vol. 122, pp. 355–392, treat the large-field operations. [P], vol. 99, pp. 389–434, gives the background propagators; [P2], vol. 96, pp. 223–250, the earlier propagator estimates; [V], vol. 102, pp. 277–309, the variational problem; [A], vol. 98, pp. 17–51, the averaging maps; [R], vol. 99, pp. 75–102, regular configurations and gauge fixing. In large-field estimates TM denotes the [terminal law](terminal_law.md), TC the [threshold collar](threshold_collar.md), PE the [joint shape bounds](joint_event_shapes.md), RB the [record bound](native_record_bound.md), IM the [lineage inputs](lineage_inputs.md), RD the [local test](local_test.md), AU the [local chart](local_chart.md), and N in the structured local-test estimates the [fluctuation terms](fluctuation_terms.md). In boundary estimates DN denotes the [deformation](deformation.md), SI the [operator inputs](operator_inputs.md), REF the [operator domains](operator_domains.md), CT the [collar calculus](collar_calculus.md) and Cons the [current-rooted theorem](current_rooted_theorem.md).

# Memory, size and geometry of lineages

The pointwise supremum over retained marked states cannot, in general, be small. The joint event and flat-test estimates below concern integrated insertions. The local R-test requires the [structured cluster estimate and parameter conditions](local_test_estimates.md); transfer of derivative, normalized mark, reset and shell estimates to that same representation is a separate hypothesis. The literal test and the local test cannot be interchanged in a norm estimate solely because the Wilson marginals agree.

## 1. (M): no memory across steps — verified on the printed representation

**What (M) must say.** In RB §3.4 the step-\(k\) live state is \(s_k=(v,\eta_Y)\). Here \(v\) is the new background \(U_k\), the new current field \(V_k\), the unreplaced (Zbar) history data including its old variables, and the second-class geometry. \(\eta_Y\) is the second-class internal state. (M) says that every later selector, domain, test and kernel is a function of \(s_k\) and later variables. In particular, nothing later depends on the internal history \(\eta_X\) of a successfully replaced component \(X\).

**The source, step by step.**

1. *New determining set, variables and background are history-free on \(Z\).* LFR II p.365: "We define \(\mathbf B_k\) as equal to \(\mathbf B_k''\) on \(Z^c\), and to \(\{Z^{(k)}\}\) on \(Z\). More precisely \(\mathbf B_k=\{\Gamma_j\}\), where \(\Gamma_j=\Gamma_j''\cap Z^c\) for \(j<k\), \(\Gamma_k=\Gamma_k''\cup Z^{(k)}\). (1.33) On this determining set we define new gauge field variables \(V\): \(V\restriction_{Z^c}=V''\restriction_{Z^c}\), \(V\restriction_Z=V_k'V_\Lambda\restriction_Z\). (1.34) … The new background field \(U_k\) is defined by \(U_k=U_{\mathbf B_k}(V)\). (1.35)". Inside \(Z\), every old \(\Gamma_j\), \(j<k\), is erased, and the variables are the new current ones. Outside \(Z\) everything is old unreplaced data, which belongs to \(v\).
2. *Localization ends in the new variables.* LFR II p.374: "This is the final localization, because all other functions in the constructed expressions depend on the new gauge field variables, or on the background field \(U_k\), restricted to this domain."
3. *Successful histories occur only inside the braces, and the braces are a function of \(U_k\).* LFR II p.379, (1.72): \((\mathbf R\rho_k)(V_k)=\sum_{Z_k}\sum_{\{Y_1,\dots,Y_m\}}\chi_k(\cdots)\bigl(\sum_{\{\Omega_j^c,Z_j\}}\mathbf T_k''(Z_k)\bigr)\exp A_k'\prod_i\chi_k^c(Y_i^{\sim6})\chi_{k,\Lambda_i}\chi_i\delta_{G_i}(V_k')\mathbf T_k'(Y_i)\bigl\{\sum_{n\ge0}\sum_{\{X_1,\dots,X_n\}}\prod_j\mathbf T_k'(X_j)\exp\sum_Y V(Y,U_k)\bigr\}\), "where \(\Omega_k\) is the new \(k\)th domain defined by the determining set of the configuration \(U_k\). Notice that the terms \(V(Y,U_k)\) depend on the sequence \(\{\Omega_j^c,Z_j\}\) restricted to the components of \(Z\) contained in \(Y\), and that the sum in the definition (1.71) acts also on the last exponential in (1.72)." So \(\Omega_k\) is determined by \(U_k\). The successful family and its internal sequences are summation variables inside the braces, and nothing outside the braces refers to them.
4. *After exponentiation the braces are a function of \(U_k\) and the outer data.* LFR II p.390: "\(\{\cdots\}=\exp\mathbf R'^{(k)}=\exp\sum\mathbf R'^{(k)}(X)\). (1.98) … The expression \(\mathbf R'^{(k)}(X)\) depends on the background field \(U_k\) restricted to \(X\)". The terms meeting the large-field region \(Z_k^\sim\cup\bigcup Y_i^\sim\) become boundary terms \(\mathbf B'^{(k)}(X)\). The new action is (1.101): \(A_k=A_k'+\sum\mathbf R'^{(k)}(X,U_k)+\sum\mathbf B'^{(k)}(X,U_k)\).
5. *The new large-field region contains no successful component.* LFR II p.390: "The new large field region is equal to \(Z_k\cup\bigcup_{i=1}^mY_i\)". In (1.72), \(Z_k\) is the unreplaced (Zbar) region.
6. *Second-class internal states can be integrated at once.* LFR II pp.391–392, source, after (1.103)–(1.104): "hence \(\mathbf T_k(Y_i)\) is the multiplication operation. These functions depend only on the new field variables \(V_k\) restricted to \(Y_i\), therefore they do not participate in operations connected with next renormalization transformations. … The contributions from the previous large field regions is isolated in these boundary terms, and in the functions \(\mathbf T_k'(Y_i)1\)."
7. *The inductive form depends only on the current sequences, and R resets them.* CMP 119 p.257, source, after (2.18): "the effective action \(A_k\) depends on the sequences \(\{\Omega_j\},\{\Lambda_j\},\{S_j\}\)". CMP 119 p.255: "we admit the possibility that \(\Lambda_j=\Omega_j\) for some indices \(j\). Such a situation may arise as a result of an \(\mathbf R\)-operation." CMP 119 p.258, after (2.21): "If the operation \(\mathbf T^{(j)}\) is changed by an \(\mathbf R\)-operation, then the general form (2.21) is preserved, but the characteristic functions are changed".

**Conclusion (c for 1–7; (M) is the conclusion).** After step \(k\), the representation is a function of \(V_k\) (on \(Z\) the new variables \(V_k'V_\Lambda\)), the old data on \(Z^c\), and the second-class factors. With (1.104), the second-class factors are scalar functions of \(V_k\restriction Y_i\). Every later operation acts on this function. So in any path-space lift whose step-\(k\) state contains \(V_k\), the old variables of Zbar and \(\eta_Y\) (or, under (1.104), only \(V_k\restriction Y_i\)), the future is a function of that state. This is (M).

**Remarks.**
* (M) holds even though the fresh field on a successful \(X\) has a law that depends on \(X\)'s descriptor \(\gamma\) (Annex BI (HS20.0a)). The future depends on the realized \(V_k\), which is in \(s_k\). The descriptor affects only the posterior of \(\eta_X\) given \(s_k\), which is (HS20.4).
* What remains assumed is the **existence of the multi-step path-space lift** in which (HS20.4) is the step-\(k\) conditional law of the marks: Annex BI does this for one step; RB §6.1 cites lift α/β. The argument assumes the multi-step construction. It is listed in §9.

## 2. (S): the size bound (1.79) read per internal history

### 2.1 The printed derivation

LFR II p.380: "From the inequality (1.73) it is clear that we have to find a bound of \(\mathbf T_k'(X,(U,0))1\) … At first we bound all the expressions in the exponential, except the Wilson action and the quadratic forms. … In effect we get a bound, to which every large field domain \(Z_j\) contributes the constant \(O(1)\log g_j^{-2}|Z_j|\le O(1)M^dR_j^{d+1}d_j'(Z_j)\)". The page then lists the factors of each large-field characteristic function (\(\chi_j^c(P_j)\), \(\chi_j'^c(Q_j)\), \(\chi^{(j-1)c}(R_j)\)), p.381 those of each preparatory characteristic function, p.382 those of \(1-\chi'\). LFR II p.383: "All the integrals with respect to the group valued variables in the definition (1.71) are estimated by 1. … The integrals with respect to the fields \(A_j\) … are estimated using the positivity properties of the quadratic forms, and we get the factors \(\exp O(1)|Z_j\cap\Omega_j|\) … Finally, the summations over the admissible sequences can be replaced by the factors \(\exp O(1)(MR_j)^{-d}|Z_j|\)." After (1.79): "The supremum is taken over all admissible domains, satisfying all the conditions described in the previous sections".

### 2.2 Reading — additional assumption, strongly supported

\(\mathbf T_k'(X)1\) is the sum over admissible sequences of positive integrals (definition (1.71), p.378). Every factor listed is attached to a set of the sequence. The bound is the product of those factors, and the sum over sequences is replaced by a count. So the derivation bounds each term, i.e. each admissible sequence, by the product. The "sup" over admissible domains in (1.79) is a supremum over the very sequences that index the terms. Hence for every label history \(\mathfrak L\) (summing finer data consistent with \(\mathfrak L\), counted by the same factor),
\[
\sup_v\mathbf T_k'(X;v)(\mathbf 1_{\mathfrak L})\le\exp\Bigl(-\sum_{r\in\mathfrak L}c^{\rm Bal}(r)+c_1\sum_{(n,C)\in\mathfrak L}M^dR_n^{d+1}d_n'(C)\Bigr),
\]
uniformly in the real configuration \(v\) at \(J=0\). This is RB's (S). The one doubt is that Bałaban summarizes ("Here the situation is almost exactly the same as in [16], so we summarize the results", p.380). There is no identified step that needs the full sum before a factor is extracted.

### 2.3 Second-class records — retention source input, failure factor additional assumption: gap (S-Y)

* **Retention.** p.390 (1.102): "the operation \(\mathbf T_k\) for a component \(Y\) of the set \(\bigcup Y_i\) has the form \(\mathbf T_k(Y)=\chi_k^c(Y^{\sim-6})\chi_{k,\Lambda}\delta_G(V_kV_\Lambda^{-1})\mathbf T_k'(Y)\)". \(Y\) belongs to the new large-field region (§1 item 5). So it continues in the same lineage, and its whole earlier history stays inside the lineage's later T-operation, as a nested integral or, by (1.104), as the factor \(\mathbf T_k'(Y)1\). RB's reading "second class = failure of the same lineage" is correct as to bookkeeping.
* **The factor, additional assumption.** Bałaban's failure rule (p.386: "a new large field was introduced in the preparatory operations. Then \(\kappa_j(Z_0)\ge0\), and we do not have a better bound for it, but we have the new factor \(\exp(-p_0(g_j))\)") is printed for preparatory failures. The list of factors on pp.380–383 does not include \(\chi_k^c(Y^{\sim6})\). The natural source is the large plaquette of the new field \(V_k\) on \(Y^{\sim6}\), paid by the Wilson action of \(U_k\) "in the usual way" (p.380). This would give \(\exp(-cA_1^2p_0^2)\gg e^{-p_0}\). Two points are not printed: whether the plaquette may sit in \(Y^{\sim6}\setminus Y\), outside the region that stays large, and where its Wilson action is charged. Bałaban's claim that (1.80) holds for all components needs this factor, so Conjecturally, it to be available. **Until it is checked, (S) and (G-R) for lineages that pass through a second-class failure carry the open item (S-Y).** Lineages with only births and preparatory failures do not.

## 4. (G6) — deduction from

CMP 119 p.254 (2.1): \(\Omega_1\supset\Lambda_1\supset\Omega_2\supset\Lambda_2\supset\dots\supset\Omega_k\supset\Lambda_k\). p.255 (2.2): "\(\Gamma_0=\Omega_1^c,\ \Gamma_j=\Omega_j^{(j)}\setminus\Omega_{j+1}^{(j)},\ j=1,\dots,k-1,\ \Gamma_k=\Omega_k^{(k)}\)"; (2.3): "\(Z_j=\Lambda_j^c\)".

**Lemma 4.1.** Let \(Y\) be a component of the unreplaced region at step \(j\), and \(c\) a cell of \(\Gamma_n\cap Y\), \(n<j\). Then \(c\) lies in a member of \(\mathrm{Anc}_{n+1}(Y)\). Hence \(|Y|_\natural\le L^d\sum_{(m,Q)\in\mathfrak S(Y)}(MR_m)^d\), where each cube is counted at its own step \(m\).

*Proof.* By (2.1)–(2.3), \(\Gamma_n\subset\Omega_{n+1}^c\subset\Lambda_{n+1}^c=Z_{n+1}\), so \(c\) lies in a component \(C\) of \(Z_{n+1}\). Suppose \(C\)'s lineage were replaced at a step \(k\in(n+1,j]\). Then by LFR II (1.33) the determining set inside the replaced component is \(Z^{(k)}\), \(\Gamma_n\) there is empty, and \(c\) is no longer a native scale-\(n\) cell at step \(j\), a contradiction. So the descendants of \(C\) are unreplaced up to step \(j\). Each descendant contains the previous one: \(S(C)\supset C\) and merges only enlarge. So the step-\(j\) descendant contains \(c\in Y\), and since components are disjoint it is \(Y\). Thus \(C\in\mathrm{Anc}_{n+1}(Y)\). Components of \(Z_{n+1}\) are unions of \(MR_{n+1}\)-cubes (G1). An \(MR_{n+1}\)-cube of the step-\((n+1)\) lattice contains \((L\,MR_{n+1})^d\) cells of scale \(n\). Summing gives the bound with \(L^d(MR_m)^d\), \(m=n+1\), per ancestor cube. \(\square\)

So RB's constant \(L^d\) is right when \((MR_m)^d\) is read at the cube's own step. The cells of \(\Gamma_j\cap Y\) add at most \((MR_j)^d\) per cube of slice \(j\).

## 5. One transition per step

The final classification is simultaneous for all components. LFR II p.378: "We introduce the decompositions of unity \(1=\chi_k((\Omega_k^{\sim4})^c)+\chi_k^c((\Omega_k^{\sim4})^c)\) in components of \(Z\). … Thus, the components of \(Z\) are divided into two classes." The successful class enters (1.72) as one sum \(\sum_{n\ge0}\sum_{\{X_1,\dots,X_n\}}\prod_j\mathbf T_k'(X_j)\exp\sum_YV(Y,U_k)\), "We use the fact that these integrations factorize in components of \(Z\)." So the step's successful family has a single joint conditional law, (HS20.4), and RB's treatment of the step-\(k\) R operation as one transition is literal.

The preparatory operations of LFR I are sequential *within* the step (\(n=1,\dots,N\) integrations, then the exterior and fresh-field tests). At each stage the components carrying a large-field function are excluded "for each component separately" and "are not changed by the subsequent operations" (p.181, RB §1.3). These exclusions produce failure records of unreplaced components, never marks. Theorem C needs (RD) only for marks. **No per-sub-step domination is required.** The exclusions do not alter the tests of other components (§3, p.183 quotation).

## 6. Is (HS20.7) uniform next to old unreplaced components?

### 6.1 What Annex BI proves

§3 of Annex BI. (HS20.6): \(|V_C(A)|\le\alpha_ke^{-\kappa_ad_{k,Z_C}(A)}\), \(\alpha_k\le Ce^{-cR}\), "uniformly in the states still integrated. The first part is LFR II (1.68); the fixed decay reserve and the completed-carrier extraction in CR.15/HR18.26 give the second part … Here \(Z_C\) is the union of the completed eligible components involved in the literal operation, not Zbar or an arbitrary collection of older tags." (HS20.7) then follows from a tree count: "at most \(C(1+R)^dA_d^{n+1}\) domains with \(n\le d_{k,Z_C}(A)<n+1\)". It is stated for carriers rooted at a component, at an exterior cube, and after a deletion. The proof never mentions old unreplaced components.

### 6.2 The source

LFR II p.376 (1.67): "\(d_{k,Z}(Y)=M^{-1}\)(the length of a shortest tree graph contained in \(Y\) and intersecting all \(M\)-cubes of the components of \(Y\setminus Z\))". p.377 (1.68): "\(|V(Y,(\mathbf U,\mathbf J))|\le\alpha\exp(-(1+2\beta)\kappa d_{k,Z}(Y))\) for \(Y\setminus Z^\sim\neq\emptyset\), with a constant \(\alpha\), which can be chosen arbitrarily small for \(\gamma\) small enough". The domains are those "\(Y\in\mathbf D_k\) satisfying the property that the intersection \(Y\cap Z^\sim\) is a union of components of \(Z^\sim\), containing at least one component". Here \(Z\) is the region on which R acts: the eligible components of both classes (the class split comes after (1.70), p.378). The unreplaced region is \(Z_k\cap Z^c\). It is **not** contracted in \(d_{k,Z}\). The boundary terms of the inductive action decay in the full length as well (CMP 119 p.261 (2.42): \(|\mathbf B^{(j)}(X,\dots)|<B_0\exp(-\kappa d_j(X))\); p.262 describes the alternative resummed convention with \(d_j(X\setminus Z_j)\) and states that the chosen one is the full \(d_j\)). The exponentiated terms keep the same structure: (1.99) decays in \(d_{k,\cup Y_i}(X)\), which contracts only second-class components.

### 6.3 scope — deduction given (1.68)

**Uniform.** A carrier that meets a candidate \(X\) and extends into an old unreplaced component pays its full tree length there. The number of carriers rooted in \(X\) with \(d_{k,Z}\in[n,n+1)\) is at most \(C(1+R)^dC^n\), whatever the unreplaced region looks like. Distinct components of \(Z_k\) are separated by at least one \(MR_k\)-cube. So a carrier joining \(X\) to an old component has \(d_{k,Z}\ge R_k\) and carries an extra \(e^{-\kappa R_k}\). This is also the visible mechanism behind \(\alpha_k\le Ce^{-cR}\) for such carriers. The old components' own boundary terms are \(O(B_0)\), not small, but they meet \(X\) only across this gap. Terms not meeting a candidate are independent of \(C\) and cancel from (HS20.4). (1.68) is a bound on the analytic extension over \(\widetilde{\mathbf U}^c_k(Y,\tilde\alpha_0,\tilde\alpha_1)\). It is uniform in the old variables \(A,B,V''\) localized in \(Y\), including those of old unreplaced components.

### 6.4 Two corrections to Annex BI's wording

1. **\(Z_C\).** (1.68) contracts all eligible components (both classes), not only those of the successful family \(C\). Since \(d_{k,Z}\le d_{k,Z_C}\), (HS20.6) with \(Z_C\) = successful family is *stronger* than the source. The counting is unchanged with Bałaban's \(Z\): eligible components have diameter \(\le C_dR\) and are mutually separated, so a carrier contains at most \(n+1\) of them and each choice is among \(O(1)\) neighbours. Read \(Z_C\) as Bałaban's \(Z\). (HS20.7) holds as stated.
2. **Scale uniformity of \(\lambda_R\).** (1.68) gives a small constant \(\alpha\) but no visible decay in \(R_k\). \(R_k\) is largest at the ultraviolet end, so \(e^{\lambda_R}\le2\) uniformly in \(k\) rests on Annex BI's \(\alpha_k\le Ce^{-cR}\) (assumed input; construction). **Theorem R′ does not need it.** Every mark has a birth in its lineage with \(c^{\rm Bal}\ge\frac12\gamma_0A_1^2p_0^2\). If \(\lambda_R\le\frac14p_0(g_k)\), then \(e^{\lambda_R}\) is absorbed by a quarter of the record costs (replace \(\frac12\) by \(\frac14\) in RB Step 1). That bound holds with \(\alpha\le1\) as soon as \(br<\) the \(p_0\)-exponent. The root count gives \(b=d=4\): \(X\) has at most \((100R)^d\) \(M\)-cubes. With exponent \(\ge6r\), this holds.

## 7. The terminal hypothesis (T)

### 7.1 What happens at the terminal scale in the construction — assumed input

The RG runs \(K_n\) steps from \(a_n=L^{-K_n}\ell_0\) to the fixed stopping scale \(\ell_0\) on the torus \(T_n=2L^{M_n}\ell_0\) (B-02). No R operation is performed after step \(K_n\). The terminal law is the law of the step-\(K_n\) state, unreplaced components included. The construction's plan is: "S-03 (F-05, M-07, H-05): records alive at the terminal scale go into \(C^{\rm ter}\), whose bound (S.29) is already integrated." S-03 is OPEN, and its criterion ("At fixed ell0, prove the normalized identity/one-point prescription and all-order joint-test terminal distribution bounds uniformly as the torus expands") says nothing about large fields. **Nothing in the construction supplies a normalized statement about terminal large-field lineages.** The terminal integral is not bounded-volume: the terminal lattice has \(2L^{M_n}\) sites per direction.

### 7.2 Which lineages are terminal — source input; additional assumption

LFR II pp.384–385: "Let \(n_0\) be the last index \(n\) such that \(d_n'(Z^{(n-j)})>0\). Then \(S^{n_0+1-j}(Z)\) is contained in a cube of the size \(64MR_{n_0+1}\), hence it satisfies the condition (i), and doing at most \(R_j\) further steps we obtain a domain satisfying both conditions (i), (ii). Thus \(K\le n_0-j+R_j\)". With \(d_n'(Z^{(n-j)})\le2^{-(n-j)}d_j'(Z)\) (p.384), \(n_0-j\le\log_2d_j'(Z)+1\). So a component with no record after step \(t\) becomes eligible within \(\log_2d'_t+R_t+O(1)\) steps (merges extend this by the rule (1.87)–(1.88)). When it becomes eligible it is replaced or acquires a failure record. **A lineage alive at \(K\) therefore has a record in its last \(\approx R+\log_2d'\) steps, or is oversized.** On the physical sequence the terminal coupling is fixed, so \(R_K\) is a fixed number. The terminal window is a bounded number of scales at fixed physical size, but terminal lineages can carry arbitrarily old records.

### 7.3 Why the closure comparison has no terminal analogue

**Proposition 7.1.** Suppose that at the terminal step the live state \(s_K\) determines the terminal label configuration, as the natural state (fields plus labels) does. Then (RD) fails for every finite \(\zeta\): for \(A=\{s:C_*(s)=C\cup\{m\}\}\) with \(\mathbb P(A)>0\), \(\mathbb P(s_K\in A,C_*=C\cup\{m\})=\mathbb P(A)>0=\zeta\,\mathbb P(s_K\in A,C_*=C)\).

At a successful replacement, (RD) is meaningful because both branches share one post-replacement datum \(v\): the fresh small field on \(X\), selected by the exterior test (1.75), maturity (ii) and the fresh small-field test (p.378). For a non-eligible terminal component no such datum exists. Two cases:

* A young component lacks the \(N=R\) small-field layers. In Bałaban's comparison the boundary error is then not small. LFR II p.363 bounds it by \(g_k^2L^{-(d-1)N}N^{(d-1)\beta_0}O(1)A_0^2B_3^2B_5M^{d+4}R_k^{d-1}p_0^2(g_k)\), "small if \(L^{-N}\) is small enough"; at \(N=0\) it exceeds the large-field gain.
* Any version conditional on the field outside a neighbourhood is false. This is PE Prop. F and RB Prop. N′ (rigorous for the Wilson density; transfer to the step-\(K\) density is additional assumption): a near-threshold or flux exterior forces the interior.

So **(T) cannot be the "same comparison"**. The only candidate is an unconditional statement about the terminal law.

### 7.4 Theorem C′ and the weaker terminal input (T_J)

**(T_J) Joint terminal record bound.** Let \(C_*\) be the random set of terminal marks \(m=(X,\mathfrak L)\): \(X\) a component of the unreplaced region at the terminal step, \(\mathfrak L\) its complete record history. There are \(\theta\in(0,1]\), \(c_T<\infty\) such that for every finite set \(M\) of pairwise disjoint terminal marks
\[
\mathbb P(M\subset C_*)\ \le\ \prod_{m\in M}\zeta_T(m),\qquad
\zeta_T(X,\mathfrak L)\le2\exp\Bigl(-\theta\sum_{r\in\mathfrak L}c^{\rm Bal}(r)+c_T\sum_{(n,C)\in\mathfrak L}M^dR_n^{d+1}d_n'(C)\Bigr),
\]
uniformly in volume and depth.

(T) in RB's form (\(\mathbb P(C_*=C\cup\{m\})\le\zeta(m)\mathbb P(C_*=C)\)) implies (T_J): peel one mark at a time as in Theorem C at a single step. The converse fails: if two distinct marks occur together with probability $q^2$ and otherwise neither occurs, the product bound holds with weights $q$, but the one-mark removal bound fails at either singleton.

**Theorem C′.** Assume the setting of RB Theorem C for steps \(1,\dots,K\): (M) and (RD) at every non-terminal step. Let a terminal random finite set \(C_*\) satisfy (T_J), with (M) holding for the pair (future, \(C_*\)) at every step. Then for every finite set of distinct marks \((k_i,m_i)\), each \(k_i\in\{1,\dots,K\}\) or terminal,
\(\mathbb P(m_i\in C_{k_i}\ \forall i)\le\prod_i\zeta(m_i)\).

*Proof.* Induction on the number of non-terminal marks. If there are none, this is (T_J). Otherwise peel the earliest non-terminal mark exactly as in RB Theorem C. The future event \(E_{\rm fut}\) now contains the terminal marks, \(\mathbb P(E_{\rm fut}\mid\mathcal F_{k_1})=\varphi(s_{k_1})\) by (M), and (RD) at \(k_1\) is integrated against \(\varphi\). The induction hypothesis bounds the remaining event. \(\square\)

**Theorem R″ — conditional.** RB Theorem R′ holds with (T) replaced by (T_J). Use Theorem C′ in Step 2 of RB §4.2. In Step 1 replace \(\frac12\) by \(\theta/2\), take \((G\text{-}R)_{c_0}\) with \(c_0\ge2(c_1+c_T+c_2)/\theta\), and absorb \(e^{\lambda_R}\) as in §6.4. The covering sum and Steps 3–4 are unchanged; the constants \(\varepsilon_*,\eta_*,\delta\) change only through \(\theta\).

## 8. Check of Theorems C and R′

* **Theorem C.**  The summation over \(C\supseteq M'\) with \(m_1\notin C\) is over a countable set, \(C\mapsto C\cup\{m_1\}\) is injective, and (RD) as a measure inequality integrates against \(\varphi\ge0\). Nothing else is used. RB's memory counterexample is valid.
* **(RD) with \(s_k=(v,\eta_Y)\).** The lift's step-\(k\) law is \(\propto\) [integrand of (1.72)] × \(G(v,\eta_Y)\), with \(G\) the future weight. The conditional of the marks given \((v,\eta_Y)\) is therefore (HS20.4) conditioned on \(\eta_Y\). The insertion ratio \(e^{H_{C\cup X}-H_C}M_X\) is pointwise in \(\eta_Y\). 
* **Step 2 of RB §4.2.** Lineages are in-trees: components never split, since replacement is of whole components and \(S\) and merges only enlarge. So each step-\(j\) component lies in exactly one lineage, which ends in a mark or at the terminal step. 
* **Correction 1 (scope of (4.1)).** The product bound is proved for events at one step \(j\), or more generally for events whose ancestries \(\mathfrak S(R_i)\) are pairwise disjoint. If \(Y_1\) at \(j_1<j_2\) is an ancestor of \(Y_2\), the product bound is false in general: \(E_2\) nearly implies \(E_1\). The amendment's "pairwise disjoint \(Y_i\)" should be read at one step.
* **Correction 2 (counting).** A lineage is determined by \(\mathfrak S\) and per-cube bits only if marks are recorded at \(MR\)-cube resolution, with finer birth data summed into \(M_X\). Then (S)'s counting factor absorbs them, as in §2.2. Otherwise use PE's \(2^{2\nu_0|\mathfrak S|}\). Constants only.
* **Correction 3.** §6.4 item 2: \(e^{\lambda_R}\le2\) can be replaced by \(\lambda_R\le\frac14p_0\).
* **(G-R) for the lineage at its mark.** (1.80) holds for every component of every admissible history, including the marked one at its step (\(K=0\)), so RB Step 1 is right. The \(c_0\)-scaled version holds given that Bałaban's induction is as printed (RB §1.2).
* **Low-margin sector (RB §5).** Theorem R′ covers events defined by labels. A low-margin configuration "charged to" a component is covered only insofar as it is a record of its lineage. Field conditions that are not labels are outside the theorem, and RB §5 is additional assumption on this point.

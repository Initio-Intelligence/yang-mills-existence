# Separated marked estimates for successful local resets

**Conditional application.** Use the stated source, support and fixed-chart hypotheses. Any application of the complete C05 boundary estimates or the C06/C08 law to all original Wilson histories assumes spatial and native-domain control of the iterated reconstruction with the earlier innovations fixed. The actual composed response required for that application remains open.

## 1. Result and exact scope

This note proves a stronger estimate for the actual successful-component
part of a literal completed source operation. Its two ingredients are:

1. The spatial comparison matrix of the actual mixed reset posterior can
   use a fixed exponential rate in current lattice units. The
   HS20 bound uses a rate of order \(R_k^{-1}\). The source's existing
   \(e^{-cR_k}\) prefactor pays the diameter offset and permits the fixed
   rate.
2. For local polynomial marks in the genuine successful-component energy
   store, the complete normalized finite logarithmic source jet has a
   rare value factor \(e^{-cP_k}\), uniformly in source order apart from
   one fixed factorial power. The factor survives every outer \(L^p\)
   norm because the resulting conditional value estimate is uniform in
   the retained datum.

The source-rooted output bound has the form
\[
 \sup_v\sup_{x_1}\sum_{x_2,\ldots,x_M}
 e^{\gamma\tau(x_1,\ldots,x_M)/M}
 |\Gamma_{\{1,\ldots,M\},k}(v;x_1,\ldots,x_M)|
 \le C^{M+1}(M!)^{s+8}e^{-cP_k}.
 \tag{RS.1}
\]
Here the incoming source coefficients are in the explicitly stated
successful-local energy class in Section 6. All incoming higher
vertices, component states, cross interactions and conditional
normalizers remain in the operation.

The fresh budget on the right of (RS.1) is summable along the retained
AF profile. This closes the rare-value and creation-scale-summability
issue for that class. Corollary RS.6 also retains every outer mean and
outer cumulant in a full-law value bound for the same class. It does
not identify an already transported
microscopic Wilson insertion with the class, bound a hard-selector
current, or give a full physical-carrier norm for the output's dependence
on retained data. Consequently ER.7, M04, M07 and the residual/unreplaced
part of H02 are not declared complete.

## 2. The actual source inputs

Use one fixed source operation LFR II (1.71)–(1.72), with the retained
data denoted by \(v\). Its actual positive mixed posterior is
\[
 \pi_v(dC,d\eta)
 =Z_v^{-1}\mathbf1_{\{C\ {\rm compatible}\}}e^{H_C(v,\eta)}
       \prod_i d\nu_{Y_i}(\eta_i)\prod_{X\in C}dM_X(v,\eta_X).
 \tag{RS.2}
\]
The \(Y_i\) bases are fixed normalized residual bases. The successful
measures \(M_X\) are unnormalized and include their internal old-history
sums. Their empty state has weight one in the geometry sum. The actual
deletion/gluing identity, common rebuilt background, local carrier
family and full conditional normalizers are the established inputs
HS20.1–HS20.14 of the marked-source estimates. This note does not
condition on the presence of a successful component.

Distances are in the fixed source-block units. Every fixed conversion
to ordinary current lattice units is absorbed in the constants. Below,
the symbol \(M\) denotes source order, not the source's fixed blocking
constant. The position-tree length \(\tau\) can be taken to be the
minimum spanning-tree length in the current lattice metric. If the
source convention allows Steiner vertices, its length and this one
differ by at most a factor of two; decrease the fixed spatial rate
accordingly.
Write \(R=R_k\), \(P=P_0(g_k)\). There are fixed constants such that
\[
 \operatorname{diam}X^+\le C_0(1+R),\qquad
 \sup_z\#\{X:z_X=z\}\le e^{C_1(1+R)^4},\qquad
 m_X(v):=M_X(v,1)\le e^{-P}.
 \tag{RS.3}
\]
Here \(X^+\) is the full fixed-collar footprint, and \(z_X\) is its
chosen anchor. Counting all species whose footprints meet a fixed
current cube has the same exponential bound after increasing \(C_1\).
Internal histories are inside \(M_X\), not counted as extra species.
The source hierarchy gives
\[
 P/(1+R)^4\longrightarrow\infty,\qquad
 R\asymp(\log g_k^{-2})^{r_0},\qquad
 P\asymp(\log g_k^{-2})^{p_0},\qquad p_0>1 .
 \tag{RS.4}
\]

The positive energy used here is exactly
\[
 B_X=\mathcal F_X^\natural+D_X\ge1,
 \tag{RS.5}
\]
where \(\mathcal F_X^\natural\) is the actual common native envelope
of the two displayed outer energies and the still-present old Gaussian
precision energies, and \(D_X\) is the corresponding surviving rank.
This is NR20.15 and NM20.7, or SA.6–SA.7 in the successful-sector estimate.
It is not an arbitrary old plaquette bank or the full M03 regulator.
For one fixed \(t_0>0\), every prescribed compatible successful family
\(\mathcal D\) satisfies
\[
 \mathbb E_{\pi_v}\!\left[
   \mathbf1_{\{\mathcal D\subset C\}}
    e^{t_0\sum_{X\in\mathcal D}B_X}\right]
 \le e^{-P|\mathcal D|/2}.
 \tag{RS.6}
\]
All these bounds are uniform in \(v\) on the original admitted real
support. Incompatible families have zero weight.

 J_{DE}\le C(1+R)^b e^{-c_0R}

## 3. A fixed spatial rate for the actual posterior

### Theorem RS.1 — fixed-rate weighted comparison

There exist \(\gamma_0>0\) and a common weak-coupling ceiling,
independent of \(R\), volume and source order, such that the actual
single-coordinate influence matrix obeys
\[
 \sup_i\sum_j c_{ij}\frac{a_j}{a_i}
                      e^{\gamma_0 d(z_i,z_j)}
 \le\varepsilon_R<\frac12,\qquad
 a_Y=1,\quad a_X=\sqrt{m_X(v)} .
 \tag{RS.9}
\]
Zero-mass successful species are omitted. They have no contribution
to the actual law.

**Proof.** The actual conditional comparison HS20.14 gives
\[
\begin{array}{ll}
 c_{Y,Y'}\le C J_{Y,Y'},&
 c_{Y,X}\le C J_{Y,X},\\
 c_{X,Y}\le C m_XJ_{X,Y},&
 c_{X,X'}\le C m_XJ_{X,X'}
       \quad\hbox{for compatible species}.
\end{array}
\tag{RS.10}
\]
For incompatible successful species the last entry is instead
\(c_{X,X'}\le Cm_X\); their anchors have separation at most \(C(1+R)\).
These are estimates for complete normalized kernels on admissible
configurations, with the empty state included.

Choose
\[
 0<\gamma_0<
  \min\{\kappa_0/4,\ c_0/(4C_2),\ 1\}.
 \tag{RS.11}
\]
For \(d\le C_2(1+R)\), the exponential weight in (RS.9) costs at
most \(e^{\gamma_0C_2(1+R)}\). Beyond that radius, (RS.8) leaves
an exponentially summable tail with rate \(\kappa_0-\gamma_0\).
The lattice shell count and the disjoint fixed \(Y\)-anchor count
therefore give
\[
 \sup_D\sum_{Y'}J_{DY'}e^{\gamma_0d(z_D,z_{Y'})}
       \le C(1+R)^{b+4}e^{-c_0R/2}.
 \tag{RS.12}
\]
For sums over candidate \(X'\), multiply the corresponding lattice
count by the shape bound in (RS.3). Conjugating (RS.10) by \(a\)
supplies \(\sqrt{m_{X'}}\) in a \(Y\)-to-\(X'\) row,
\(\sqrt{m_X}\) in an \(X\)-to-\(Y'\) row, and
\(\sqrt{m_Xm_{X'}}\) in an \(X\)-to-\(X'\) row. Thus no inverse
small mass occurs.

For a hard exclusion, the row has finite range \(C(1+R)\), and
its contribution is bounded by
\[
 C(1+R)^4
   \exp\{C_1(1+R)^4+C\gamma_0(1+R)-P\}.
 \tag{RS.13}
\]
Combining all rows, a conservative common bound is
\[
 \varepsilon_R\le
 C(1+R)^{b+4}e^{-c_0R/2}
 +C(1+R)^{b+4}
       e^{C_3(1+R)^4+C_4\gamma_0(1+R)-P/2}.
 \tag{RS.14}
\]
Equation (RS.4) makes this less than \(1/2\) at one common ceiling.
Every choice was made before choosing an insertion order.
\(\square\)

### Corollary RS.2 — true-marginal cut coupling at the fixed rate

For finite coordinate sets \(I,J\), put
\[
 S(I)=\bigcup_{i\in I}D_i,\qquad n_v(I)=\sum_{i\in I}a_i.
 \tag{RS.15}
\]
The true joint marginal and the product of its two true marginals
admit a coupling with failure probability
\[
 \epsilon_{I,J}(v)\le
 \min\{1,\ C n_v(I)e^{-\gamma_0\operatorname{dist}(S(I),S(J))}\}.
 \tag{RS.16}
\]
The same estimate with a fixed changed constant covers overlapping
coordinate sets.

**Proof.** Apply the finite-coordinate heat-bath comparison of
HS20.19a–HS20.21 using (RS.9). The inverse
\((I-C_{FF})^{-1}C_{FJ}\) is bounded by its positive Neumann
series in this weighted norm. Averaging the boundary variables
against their two actual marginals gives disagreement probability
at most \(1\) for a \(Y\) coordinate and at most
\(2\pi_v(X\ {\rm present})\le Cm_X\le Ca_X\) for an \(X\)
coordinate. Summing the resulting bound over \(I\) proves
(RS.16). The permitted deletion/change/addition paths stay inside
the hard-core support, exactly as in HS20.1.
\(\square\)

This improves the rate of HS20.18 on its actual source class.
It does not estimate the response of \(\pi_v\) to an arbitrary
change of the retained datum \(v\).

## 4. Successful local energy stores

For a current source cube \(x\), let
\[
 \mathcal X_x=\{X:x\in X^+\},\qquad
 A_x=\sum_{X\in\mathcal X_x}\mathbf1_{\{X\in C\}}B_X,\qquad
 \eta_k=\sup_x|\mathcal X_x|e^{-P/2}\le e^{-P/3}.
 \tag{RS.17}
\]
The last inequality follows at the same common ceiling from
(RS.3)–(RS.4). For a finite set of anchors \(A\), use the union of
their candidate catalogs, counting each species once, and denote
the corresponding energy sum by \(S_A\).

### Lemma RS.3 — a root-pinned joint exponential bound

For \(0<\theta\le t_0/2\), \(x\in A\), and all admitted \(v\),
\[
 \mathbb E_{\pi_v}e^{\theta S_A}
       \le e^{|A|\eta_k},\qquad
 \mathbb E_{\pi_v}
   [\mathbf1_{\{A_x>0\}}e^{\theta S_A}]
       \le\eta_k e^{|A|\eta_k}.
 \tag{RS.18}
\]

**Proof.** Expand
\[
 e^{\theta S_A}=
 \prod_{X\in\cup_{y\in A}\mathcal X_y}
  [1+\mathbf1_{\{X\in C\}}(e^{\theta B_X}-1)].
\]
For each chosen compatible family, (RS.6) bounds the associated
expectation by \(e^{-P|\mathcal D|/2}\); an incompatible family
has zero contribution. Hence the positive expansion is at most
\((1+e^{-P/2})^{|\cup\mathcal X_y|}\le e^{|A|\eta_k}\).

For the second assertion, use
\(\mathbf1_{\{A_x>0\}}\le\sum_{X\in\mathcal X_x}\mathbf1_{\{X\in C\}}\).
For each such \(X\), factor its already occupied term before
expanding the remaining species. Equation (RS.6), with a fixed
energy coefficient at most \(t_0\), gives
\(e^{-P/2}e^{|A|\eta_k}\). Summing proves the second bound.
The pinned species is not re-expanded, so there is no spurious
factor \(2\) per pinned component.
\(\square\)

In particular, for any integer \(q\ge1\),
\[
 \mathbb E_{\pi_v} A_x^q\le C^q q!\,\eta_k,
 \qquad
 \|A_x\|_{L^q(\pi_v)}\le Cq.
 \tag{RS.19}
\]
For products at distinct or coincident anchors the union store
retains their actual common energy variables. It does not assume
independence.

## 5. Rare spatial cumulants at every order

Let \(F_i\) depend only on the successful component coordinates in
\(\mathcal X_{x_i}\), with \(v\) fixed. Suppose, for positive integers
\(d_i\) and a fixed \(s\ge0\),
\[
 |F_i|\le C_0^{d_i}(d_i!)^s A_{x_i}^{d_i},
 \qquad D=\sum_{i=1}^m d_i .
 \tag{RS.20}
\]
The mark vanishes when no successful footprint in its catalog is
occupied. This is an actual condition on the mark, not a conclusion
about a transported microscopic field.

The class contains literal, nonempty examples. With the outer positive
quadratic form \(Q_X\), the actual inhomogeneous-coefficient outer
Wilson action \(S_X\), or any one of the still-present old positive
Schur forms \(q_{j,X}\), take
\[
 F_x=\sum_{X\in\mathcal X_x}\mathbf1_{\{X\in C\}}Q_X,
 \quad
 \sum_{X\in\mathcal X_x}\mathbf1_{\{X\in C\}}S_X,
 \quad\hbox{or}\quad
 \sum_{X\in\mathcal X_x}\mathbf1_{\{X\in C\}}D_X .
 \tag{RS.20a}
\]
On the actual real slice each summand is bounded by \(B_X\).
These use the forms in NR20.2–NR20.4, with their original
coefficients and free-coordinate measures. Their component-local
polynomials of degree \(d\) give further examples of (RS.20).
They are current completed-operation observables, not replacements
for a previously transported microscopic plaquette.

### Theorem RS.4 — conditional rare-value and spatial bounds

For every \(m\ge1\), uniformly in volume, depth, admitted \(v\) and
all source orders,
\[
 |\kappa_{\pi_v}(F_1,\ldots,F_m)|
       \le C^{D+1}(D!)^{s+1}\eta_k .
 \tag{RS.21}
\]
For \(m\ge2\), there are fixed \(c,\gamma>0\) such that
\[
 |\kappa_{\pi_v}(F_1,\ldots,F_m)|
 \le C^{D+1}(D!)^{s+2}e^{-cP_k}
        e^{-\gamma\tau(x_1,\ldots,x_m)/(m-1)}.
 \tag{RS.22}
\]
The constants and common ceiling are independent of \(m,D\).

**Proof of the value bound.** For a block \(B\) of indices, put
\(D_B=\sum_{i\in B}d_i\). Its product is bounded by its coefficient
factor times \(S_{\{x_i:i\in B\}}^{D_B}\). The product vanishes
unless \(A_{x_i}>0\) for every \(i\in B\), so (RS.18), using one
specified member, gives
\[
 \mathbb E_{\pi_v}\prod_{i\in B}|F_i|
 \le C^{D_B}(D_B!)^{s+1}\eta_k.
 \tag{RS.23}
\]
Without imposing that member it gives the same bound with the
last factor replaced by one. In the cumulant partition formula
use the rare factor only for the block containing index \(1\).
Treat each \(d_i\) as a group of \(d_i\) formal labels. Partitions
which keep those groups intact form a subset of all partitions
of the \(D\) labels. Thus the ordered-partition bound
\[
 \sum_{\pi}(|\pi|-1)!\prod_{B\in\pi}(D_B!)^{s+1}
        \le 2^{D-1}(D!)^{s+1}
 \tag{RS.24}
\]
proves (RS.21).

**Proof of the spatial bound.** For each mark, its coordinate
support is \(I_i=\mathcal X_{x_i}\). The weighted support mass
obeys \(n_v(I_i)\le\eta_k\), by (RS.3) and (RS.17).
Cut a minimum spanning tree on the support sets \(S(I_i)\)
at its longest edge. If its total length is \(T\), every distance
across that cut is at least \(T/(m-1)\).
Equation (RS.16) then couples the true law of these marks to
the product of its two true cut marginals with error at most
\[
 C m\eta_k e^{-\gamma_0T/(m-1)}.
 \tag{RS.25}
\]
The usual moment telescoping proof of HS20.24–HS20.25, with
Hölder exponent \(2\) for the failure event and \(2m\) for each
input, gives
\[
 |\kappa_{\pi_v}(F_1,\ldots,F_m)|
 \le C^m m!\sqrt{m\eta_k}\,
       e^{-\gamma_0T/(2(m-1))}
       \prod_i\|F_i\|_{L^{2m}(\pi_v)} .
 \tag{RS.26}
\]
The true cut product preserves each input marginal; no
conditional-on-presence norm appears.

By (RS.19) and (RS.20),
\[
 \|F_i\|_{2m}\le
 C^{d_i}(d_i!)^s(m d_i)^{d_i}.
 \tag{RS.27}
\]
Use \(d_i^{d_i}\le e^{d_i}d_i!\),
\(m!\prod_i d_i!\le D!\), \(m^D\le e^D D!\),
and \(\prod_i d_i!\le D!\). The product and combinatorial
factors in (RS.26) are bounded by
\(C^{D+1}(D!)^{s+2}\).

Every point in \(S(I_i)\) is within \(C(1+R)\) of \(x_i\).
Choosing witnesses for the edges of the support-set tree gives
\[
 \tau(x_1,\ldots,x_m)\le T+C(1+R)(m-1).
 \tag{RS.28}
\]
The resulting factor \(e^{C\gamma_0(1+R)}\) is paid by a fixed
part of \(\sqrt{\eta_k}\le e^{-P/6}\), using \(P/R\to\infty\).
Reducing the fixed rate and rare exponent proves (RS.22).
\(\square\)

The rare factor in this theorem is not obtained by optimizing
\(p^{-d}\|\mathbf1_EB^d\|_p\). It comes from the occupied-coordinate
mass in the true-marginal cut coupling, or from the root-pinned
joint moment before the finite cumulant sum. The energy growth
only contributes the stated factorial power.

Since (RS.21)–(RS.22) are pointwise uniform in \(v\), the same
bounds hold for every outer \(L^p\) norm of this conditional
cumulant, \(1\le p\le\infty\), under its actual outer marginal.
This statement concerns the conditional cumulant function.
An unconditional cumulant additionally contains outer cumulants
of conditional means and higher conditional vertices.

## 6. The complete finite logarithmic source jet

Let \(I=\{1,\ldots,M\}\) label source positions. Work in the
squarefree finite source algebra. The incoming logarithmic jet is
\[
 \Psi(t)=\sum_{\varnothing\ne B\subseteq I}t_B A_B,\qquad
 t_B=\prod_{i\in B}t_i.
 \tag{RS.29}
\]
Assume a successful-local representation
\[
 A_B=\sum_{\substack{X:\ x_i\in X^+\ (i\in B)}}
     \mathbf1_{\{X\in C\}} A_{B,X}(v,\eta_X),
 \qquad
 |A_{B,X}|\le C_0^{|B|}(|B|!)^s B_X^{|B|}.
 \tag{RS.30}
\]
Every \(A_{B,X}\) is a function of that literal component's
internal variables and its prescribed retained data. Higher
incoming vertices are included; they are not set to zero.
Because \(B_X\ge0\), the sum is bounded by
\(C_0^{|B|}(|B|!)^s A_{x_{\min B}}^{|B|}\).

For physical current-scale coefficients, one may instead assume that
\(h_k^{4|B|}A_B\) satisfies (RS.30). The conclusions then apply to
\(h_k^{4M}\Gamma_I\) and \(h_k^{4M}G_I\). These factors multiply
exactly in the finite partition identities. This is a reset at one
current physical scale; it makes no conversion of a bare
\(a^{-4M}\) factor into \(h_k^{-4M}\).

Define the exact output jet by
\[
 \log\mathbb E_{\pi_v}e^{\Psi(t)}
       =\sum_{\varnothing\ne I'}t_{I'}\Gamma_{I'}(v).
 \tag{RS.31}
\]
This is a finite algebraic jet, so a uniform analytic radius for
sources coupled to high powers of \(B_X\) is not required.

### Theorem RS.5 — the normalized successful finite-jet estimate

At the same ceiling there are fixed \(C,c,\gamma>0\) such that
\[
 |\Gamma_I(v;x_1,\ldots,x_M)|
 \le C^{M+1}(M!)^{s+3}e^{-cP_k}
       e^{-\gamma\tau(x_1,\ldots,x_M)/M}.
 \tag{RS.32}
\]
The rooted bound (RS.1) follows at a smaller fixed spatial rate.

**Proof.** The exact partition identity is
\[
 \Gamma_I=
   \sum_{\pi\in\Pi(I)}
       \kappa_{\pi_v}(A_B:B\in\pi).
 \tag{RS.33}
\]
It retains the one-block conditional mean and every generated
product/cumulant. It uses the complete posterior (RS.2), including
its moving normalizer.

For a partition with \(m\ge2\) blocks, apply (RS.26)–(RS.27)
with total degree \(D=M\). If a block coefficient is nonzero,
all its source positions lie within \(C(1+R)\) of its selected
representative. The support-set tree of length \(T_\pi\) and
these internal connections therefore give
\[
 \tau(x_1,\ldots,x_M)\le T_\pi+C(1+R)M.
 \tag{RS.34}
\]
First weaken \(e^{-\gamma_0T_\pi/(2(m-1))}\) to
\(e^{-\gamma_0T_\pi/(2M)}\). Only then use (RS.34). Its extra
factor is \(e^{C\gamma_0(1+R)}\), independent of \(M\), and is
paid by the same fixed part of the rare factor.
This order avoids a false cost \(e^{CMR}\) for a large
incoming vertex.

The one-block term is covered by (RS.23); its source positions
are within one eligible footprint whenever it is nonzero, so
the same divided-tree conversion applies. Each partition is
therefore bounded by the right side of (RS.32) with factorial
power \(s+2\). The bound
 \(|\Pi(I)|\le C^M M!\) gives (RS.32).

For the root sum, use a fixed smaller rate. The sum of
\(e^{-c\tau/M}\) over \(x_2,\ldots,x_M\), with \(x_1\) fixed,
is bounded by the sum over labeled trees. Each tree edge has
lattice row sum at most \(CM^4\); there are \(M^{M-2}\) trees
for \(M\ge2\). Hence
\[
 \sup_{x_1}\sum_{x_2,\ldots,x_M}
         e^{-c\tau(x_1,\ldots,x_M)/M}
       \le C^M(M!)^5 .
 \tag{RS.35}
\]
The same bound holds on a compatible finite torus in its periodic
metric, with no volume factor. Equation (RS.1) follows.
For \(M=1\), the root sum is empty and (RS.23) applies directly.
\(\square\)

### Corollary RS.6 — the complete outer-law value jet

Let \(\mu(dv,d\eta)=\nu(dv)\pi_v(d\eta)\) be the actual positive
source-free joint law, with any of its actual outer marginals.
For the same incoming jet (RS.29)–(RS.30), define
\[
 \log\mathbb E_\mu e^{\Psi(t)}
       =\sum_{\varnothing\ne I}t_I G_I.
 \tag{RS.35a}
\]
Then
\[
 |G_I|\le C^{M+1}(M!)^{s+3}e^{-cP_k}.
 \tag{RS.35b}
\]
This is a value bound on the full normalized law, with all outer
conditional means and cumulants included. No spatial decay in the
outer marginal is inferred.

**Proof.** The tower identity gives exactly
\[
 G_I=\sum_{\pi\in\Pi(I)}
       \kappa_\nu(\Gamma_B(v):B\in\pi).
 \tag{RS.35c}
\]
For bounded random variables,
\[
 |\kappa_\nu(g_1,\ldots,g_r)|
       \le C^r r!\prod_i\|g_i\|_\infty
\]
by the ordinary finite partition formula. Dropping the spatial
factor in (RS.32), each \(\Gamma_B\) has the bound
\(C^{|B|+1}(|B|!)^{s+3}\varepsilon_k\), where
\(\varepsilon_k=e^{-cP_k}\le1\).
Every nonempty outer partition retains at least one such factor.
For \(r_*=s+3\ge1\),
\[
 \sum_{\pi\in\Pi_M}|\pi|!
          \prod_{B\in\pi}(|B|!)^{r_*}
       \le 2^{M-1}(M!)^{r_*}.
 \tag{RS.35d}
\]
At \(r_*=1\), order the blocks and their internal labels to
obtain exactly \(M!\sum_{r=1}^M{M-1\choose r-1}\).
For larger \(r_*\), use \(\prod_B|B|!\le M!\).
Applying (RS.35d) in (RS.35c) proves (RS.35b).
\(\square\)

The missing spatial estimate for the outer terms is substantive:
products of conditional one-point values may depend on common
retained data. Their uniform amplitude alone gives no decay in
the distance between their source positions. No fixed residual
or unreplaced component is assigned a successful rare factor in
this corollary.

## 7. Creation-scale summability

Under the retained two-sided AF envelope, \(g_j^{-2}\) is comparable
to the terminal inverse coupling plus its ultraviolet age. Therefore,
for every fixed \(c>0\),
\[
 \sup_K\sum_{j\le K}e^{-cP_0(g_j)}
 \le C\sum_{a\ge0}e^{-c'[\log(T+a)]^{p_0}}
 \longrightarrow0\quad(T\longrightarrow\infty).
 \tag{RS.36}
\]
The integral test proves the assertion because \(p_0>1\).
The fixed factorial base in (RS.1) is independent of the
creation scale, so its fresh successful-reset value budgets
are summable at every source order, with the same factorial
power.

This sums values measured in their stated current coordinates.
It does not insert an unproved bound for later physical
reconstruction, a change of carrier mesh, or the full retained-data
current of these coefficients. Those transfer costs must be
included before this sum is used in an all-scale matching theorem.

### Why a simpler finite-moment workaround is insufficient

Keeping a moment \(p\) fixed can preserve a successful-event
penalty \(e^{-cP_k/p}\). For a fixed \(p\) the AF sum converges.
Uniform source-order control requires more care. For large \(p\),
the integral
\[
 \int^\infty e^{s-cs^{p_0}/p}\,ds
\]
has a saddle with logarithmic size of order
\(p^{1/(p_0-1)}\): its exponent is maximal at
\(s_*=(p/(cp_0))^{1/(p_0-1)}\), with value
\((1-1/p_0)s_*\). A fixed relative neighborhood of \(s_*\)
gives the corresponding lower exponential order; scaling
\(s=p^{1/(p_0-1)}u\) gives the upper order.
Thus an independent application of this
scale sum to each of \(M\) factors at Hölder order \(p\asymp M\)
can cost \(e^{CM^{1+1/(p_0-1)}}\). Such a bound is larger than
every fixed factorial power. It would not establish ER.7.

The present argument avoids that loss by retaining the actual
joint posterior and the rare coordinate weight through the
conditional cumulant calculation, before the creation-scale sum.
No multi-age conditional independence is assumed.

### Which coefficient growth can be absorbed at all orders

The scale-independent \(C_0\) in (RS.30) is substantive. There
is a useful permitted extension. Put \(\ell=\log g_k^{-2}\).
For every fixed \(b\ge0\),
\[
 \ell^{bM}e^{-c\ell^{p_0}}
 \le C^{M+1}(M!)^{b/p_0}e^{-c\ell^{p_0}/2}.
 \tag{RS.36a}
\]
Indeed, with \(u=\ell^{p_0}\), maximize
\(u^{bM/p_0}e^{-cu/2}\) and then use
\(M^M\le e^M M!\). Thus a fixed logarithmic polynomial per
source grade changes only the fixed factorial power and spends
a fixed fraction of the rare reserve.

An inverse-coupling power per grade is different. The maximum
of
\[
 \exp\{\alpha M\ell-c\ell^{p_0}\},
 \qquad \alpha>0,
\]
has logarithm of order \(M^{p_0/(p_0-1)}\). This is larger
than every fixed multiple of \(M\log M\).
Consequently the rare factor alone does not turn an arbitrary
\((g_k^{-2})^{\alpha M}\) input into a fixed-factorial,
scale-uniform output. The actual normalization and matching
must be tracked before applying this theorem; choosing a
different coupling ceiling for each source order would not
supply ER.7.

## 8. What is still required for ER.7

The following pieces are now proved for the actual source operation:

- a fixed current-unit spatial rate for its mixed reset comparison;
- an order-independent rare factor for successful-local energy
  cumulants and complete finite logarithmic source jets;
- rooted endpoint sums with one fixed factorial power;
- uniform positive outer averages of those conditional quantities,
  and a full-law value-jet bound retaining all outer cumulants;
- summability of their fresh value budgets along the retained AF tail.

Three actual interfaces remain before this can prove the physically
normalized ER.7 hierarchy:

1. **Microscopic matching.** The exact incoming transported scalar
   and every higher partner must have a decomposition controlled
   by (RS.30), with their physical coefficients and all other
   sectors retained. C05 controls boundary activities; it does
   not assert this observable decomposition.
2. **Residual/unreplaced and outer terms.** A fixed \(Y\) has
   no empty state in (RS.2). Its small unnormalized mass has
   already canceled from its normalized base. The root-pinned
   estimate cannot be assigned to it. Corollary RS.6 controls
   outer value terms for the successful-local class; the outer
   terms of general transported physical sources and the actual
   source-normalization coefficients remain.
3. **Required response topology.** A source-position tree estimate
   is not a full physical-carrier bound on dependence on \(v\).
   The stated M03 current, shifted regulator and hard-selector
   requirements have not been inferred from (RS.32).

For example the exact two-variable full-law identity remains
\[
 \operatorname{Cov}_\mu(F,G)
 =\mathbb E_\nu[\operatorname{Cov}_{\pi_v}(F,G)]
  +\operatorname{Cov}_\nu(\pi_vF,\pi_vG).
 \tag{RS.37}
\]
The first term has the spatial estimate in this note's stated
class. Corollary RS.6 bounds the complete expression in value
for that same class. It does not give spatial decay to the second
term. At higher orders (RS.35c) retains every analogous outer
vertex.

A subsequent argument may use (RS.1) as a proved successful-sector
input. The full ER.7 estimate and the original M04/M07/H02 statements
remain open unless their other actual interfaces are supplied.

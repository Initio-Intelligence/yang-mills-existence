## 6. Native analytic families and the mass-ratio diagnostic

### 6.1 Fixed output atoms and domination are required

Assume the complex family acts on the fixed real
integration space at each cutoff, with the input
environment retained, and

\[
 P_{n,\zeta}1=1,\qquad
 |dP_{n,\zeta}|\le M\,dP_{n,0}.
 \tag{MR.5}
\]

The domination makes every operator well-defined on
the output \(L^\infty\) equivalence class of its real
reference. Uniform Cauchy estimates on a larger common
domain give norm continuity and norm holomorphy in
the product algebra. Thus closure under a countable
dense parameter set implies closure under every
parameter, and all fixed derivatives on strict
smaller domains obey the displayed MK.39 bound.

The inequality

\[
 |P_\zeta f|\le M P_0|f|
\]

passes as an order inequality of continuous functions.
For each input state, it bounds the resulting complex
functional by the \(L^1(\bar P_0)\) norm. Equivalently,
its Riesz measure has a Radon–Nikodym density of
modulus at most \(M\). This proves MK.38, including
support on the same cutoff fiber. Finite words have
the product of their finite operator bounds; local
uniform convergence and fixed derivative convergence
then follow from Cauchy's formula.

For a reconstructed output \(\Psi_\zeta(a)\), an
arbitrary bounded measurable function of the real
physical state need not extend holomorphically to
\(\Psi_\zeta(a)\). The author has made explicit that
the input must be a separately verified bounded
holomorphic family \(F_\zeta(a)\). This resolves the
otherwise invalid promotion of a reference-atom
comparison to all bounded physical-state probes.

### 6.2 Normalized domination alone says nothing about the mass

The following diagnostic is decisive. Let \(\nu\) be
a fixed probability and let

\[
 w_{n,\zeta}(a)=e^{n\zeta}w_0(a),\qquad
 \int w_0\,da=1.
 \tag{MR.6}
\]

Every normalized kernel is exactly \(\nu\), so (MR.5)
holds with \(M=1\), and every normalized derivative
vanishes. After removing the scalar phase
\(e^{in\operatorname{Im}\zeta}\), the density even
lies in a zero-width positive cone. Nevertheless,

\[
 \tau_{n,\zeta}/\tau_{n,0}=e^{n\zeta}
\]

has no uniform upper or lower bound on a fixed
complex neighborhood. One can multiply the real
mass by any small positive scalar as well, without
changing this conclusion.

Consequently the normalized C05 comparison [ND.2](../boundary/primitive_estimates/native_domain_count.md) /
[FC.14](../boundary/primitive_estimates/complete_boundary_estimate.md), especially when a scalar phase was removed,
cannot by itself justify MK.40a. The literal source
comparison LFR II (1.73)–(1.74) is stronger on its
stated component family: the full perturbation
\(\sigma\) has a uniform absolute bound. Its
two-sided comparison of the unnormalized integral
with its real center supplies a bounded holomorphic
ratio \(r_n=\tau_{n,\zeta}/\tau_{n,0}\).

In the final text, the source bound is written concretely as

\[
 r_n=\Pi_{n,0}e^{\sigma_{n,\zeta}},\qquad
 |r_n-1|\le e^s-1,\qquad
 2-e^s\le |r_n|\le e^s,
 \quad s<\log2.
 \tag{MR.7}
\]

These inequalities follow directly from
\(\sup|\sigma_{n,\zeta}|\le s\) and positivity of
the real reference. They are independent of the
possibly very small \(\tau_{n,0}\).

With this separate input, retaining \(r_n\) and
\(\tau_{n,0}\) jointly is valid. Their product gives
the complete mass family and, at a limiting state
with \(\tau_0=0\), this entire complete family and
all its fixed native derivatives vanish. A
stopped-prefix or broader unnormalized branch
requires its own such input; normalized domination
alone does not provide it.

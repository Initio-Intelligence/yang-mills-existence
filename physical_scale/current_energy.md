## 2. Exponential rarity does not pay the stated all-moment weight

### Lemma AC.1 — exact optimized-moment cost of an event

Let \(E\) be an event of probability \(e^{-t}\), with \(0\le t<\infty\). For every \(d>0\),

\[
 \mathfrak m_d(\mathbf1_E)=
 \begin{cases}
 d^{-d}e^{-t},&0\le t\le d,\\[2mm]
 e^{-d}t^{-d},&t\ge d.
 \end{cases}
 \tag{AC.3}
\]

Indeed, the logarithm of the expression to maximize is

\[
 -d\log(dp)-t/p.
\]

Its derivative is \(-d/p+t/p^2\). The maximizer is \(p=1\) for \(t\le d\), and \(p=t/d\) otherwise. This proves (AC.3).

Consequently, even a genuine full-law estimate

\[
 \mathbb P(E_H)\le e^{-\lambda |H|}
\]

does not by itself provide an exponential spatial factor in (AC.2). An indicator with precisely this probability has only order \(|H|^{-d}\) suppression after the moment supremum. Its frozen carrier contribution is then of order

\[
 e^{\kappa T_h(H)}|H|^{-d}.
 \tag{AC.4}
\]

This grows exponentially. A polynomially increasing regulator on \(E_H\) cannot repair the pointwise term either. A much stronger amplitude cancellation or actual physical localization of the marked coefficient is necessary for the original norm.

The distinction is relevant even when a birth event is very rare. A bound on an unnormalized bad-sector mass is weaker still: a small canonical mass cancels from a fixed-sector normalized ratio. C08's conditional gap and moments are uniform statements on that ratio, not posterior bounds on the event selecting the sector. The parity diagnostic SR20.31–SR20.34 of [Annex BK](../history/Annex_BK.md) provides a separate finite-model demonstration that a relative-tree bound plus a conditional gap does not imply a full-volume carrier bound. It is not being asserted to be a Yang–Mills counterexample.

### Theorem AC.2 — current energy and current response

Uniformly over the admitted retained class, for every \(q\ge1\),

\[
 \boxed{\quad

       \le C[\mathcal R_{\wp}(y)+q].
       \quad}
 \tag{AC.10}
\]

Moreover, for either an integrated or a retained same-current direction \(i\),

\[
 \|D_{t_i}\{\beta_k s(U_{\wp})\}\|_{L^q(\nu_y)}
 \le C e^{-c_2\rho(\wp,i)}
                   [\mathcal R_{\wp}(y)+q]^{1/2}.
 \tag{AC.11}

\[
 \left|D_{y_a}(\nu_yF_{\wp})-

On the compact group \(s(P)=\frac12\|P-I\|_{HS}^2\), and the source normalization has \(\beta_k g_k^2\le C\). By (AC.6), (AC.7) and Minkowski,

\[
 \left\|\sum_i e^{-c\rho(\wp,i)}|z_i|\right\|_{2q}
 \le C\sqrt q+C P^{-1}
                  +C\sum_{a\in O}e^{-c_1\rho(\wp,a)}|y_a|.

The square of the last sum is bounded by a fixed constant times the last term of (AC.9), by weighted Cauchy–Schwarz and the four-dimensional shell count. This proves (AC.10), absorbing \(P^{-2}\) in its unit.

Differentiate \(\frac12\|U_{\wp}-I\|^2\) and use (AC.8). The result is bounded pointwise by

 C e^{-c\rho(\wp,i)}

Equation (AC.10) proves (AC.11). RC.13 bounds the new current by a convolution of the fixed exponentially decaying response matrix with the \(L^2\) norm of the integrated gradient. Convolving two exponential kernels at a smaller rate proves (AC.12). The direct term follows from (AC.11) at \(q=1\).

## 4. A random full-carrier norm with moment-dependent rate

At finite cutoff, walk and Taylor expansions can still have countably many records. Work first with a finite truncation of their union of possible labels, padding absent activities by zero. Let \(F_\Gamma(\omega)\) be a coefficient, and let \(X_\Gamma\) be its **full physical carrier**. The carrier can depend on the history label; retain that label in \(\Gamma\). The positive sums below pass to the countable catalog by monotone convergence; no finiteness of the analytic record catalog is inferred from a finite lattice. For a fixed labeled anchor tuple and \(d>0\), define

\[
 \boxed{\quad
 \mathcal N_{d,\kappa}(F)
 =\sup_{p\ge1}(dp)^{-d}
   \left\|\sum_{\Gamma}
        \exp\!\left[\frac{\kappa T_h(X_\Gamma)}{dp}\right]
                       |F_\Gamma|\right\|_{L^p(\mu_h)}.
        \quad}
 \tag{AC.14}
\]

For an anchored family take the supremum over its first anchor. Physical factors \(h^{4m}\) remain attached to a coefficient with \(m\) field labels. Value grade is \(d=m\); a current record may use \(d=m+\frac12\). A forest has additive cost over its components until an actual connector is supplied.

The positive sum is **inside** the probability norm. It is not \(\sum_\Gamma\sup_p\), nor \(\sup_p\sum_\Gamma\|F_\Gamma\|_p\). This order allows the actual probability space to account for incompatible or jointly rare shapes. It does not use cancellation between different activities.

### Lemma AC.3 — exact product rule

Suppose an actual product joins carriers with

\[
 T_h(X_{\Gamma_1\cdots\Gamma_r})
                   \le\sum_{i=1}^r T_h(X_{\Gamma_i}).
 \tag{AC.15}
\]

This holds for intersecting connected covers, or for a forest kept as a forest. A new connecting path must be declared in one of the factors before (AC.15) is used. Let \(d=\sum_i d_i\). Then

\[
 \mathcal N_{d,\kappa}(F_1\star\cdots\star F_r)
             \le\prod_{i=1}^r\mathcal N_{d_i,\kappa}(F_i).
 \tag{AC.16}
\]

Here \(\star\) retains all product labels and sums the corresponding positive activity envelopes. To prove this, at moment order \(p\) choose the Hölder orders

\[
 p_i=\frac d{d_i}p.
\]

Each input weight then has the identical rate \(\kappa/(d_i p_i)=\kappa/(dp)\). Equation (AC.15) bounds the output positive sum by the product of the input sums. Hölder and \(\prod_i(d_i p_i)^{d_i}=(dp)^d\) prove (AC.16).

If the normalization \(p^{-d}\) is preferred, the exact constant is

\[
 \prod_i(d/d_i)^{d_i}.
 \tag{AC.17}
\]

For positive integer source grades this is at most \(e^d d!/\prod_i d_i!\), so it is compatible with ordinary factorial source bookkeeping. The \(dp\) convention avoids this constant entirely. A grade-independent weight \(e^{\kappa T/p}\), without the \(1/d\), does **not** have this product rule at one fixed \(\kappa\).

### Partition rules

For fixed source labels \(I\), form moments, cumulants or exponential-jet coefficients by their exact finite partition identities, retaining the carrier unions and any genuine connecting records. If the moment coefficient of order \(m\ge1\) obeys

\[
 \mathcal N_{m,\kappa}(A_I)\le C^m(m!)^r,
 \qquad r\ge1,
\]

then the cumulant partition sum obeys the same factorial power, with a different fixed base \(C\). Indeed

\[
 \sum_{\pi\in\Pi_m}(|\pi|-1)!
               \prod_{B\in\pi}(|B|!)^r
 \le 2^{m-1}(m!)^r.
 \tag{AC.18}
\]

For \(r=1\), order the blocks and their internal labels; the sum is

\[
 m!\sum_{j=1}^m\frac1j{m-1\choose j-1}
                         \le 2^{m-1}m!.
\]

For \(r>1\), use \(\prod_B|B|!\le m!\) for the extra power. The moment-from-cumulant partition formula has the smaller coefficient (1) and obeys the same estimate. A single differentiated/current block has total grade \(m+\frac12\); (AC.16) applies unchanged and the sum over the marked block contributes only a fixed exponential-in-\(m\) factor.

These are algebraic rules for actual records already defined on one probability space. They do not establish an actual quotient bound or a selector derivative by themselves.

## 5. A sufficient full-law transfer theorem

The next theorem is a usable target for H02. It specifies the normalized joint estimate needed to turn a positive relative-core catalog into (AC.14).

Suppose each actual record has a paid exterior cost \(t_\Gamma\ge0\), and a collection of completed-core footprints with union volume \(v_\Gamma\ge0\), measured in current physical blocks. Include deterministic collars in those footprints. Assume a full connected cover satisfies

\[
 T_h(X_\Gamma)\le C_T(t_\Gamma+v_\Gamma).
 \tag{AC.19}
\]

Do not fill a microscopic core with its number of old fine cells: \(v_\Gamma\) is the actual **current physical** footprint cost. Distinct old labels with the same current geometry are not automatically distinct geometric animals.

Assume the following three inputs.

1. **Conditional positive catalog:** at each real retained configuration,

   \[
    \sum_\Gamma e^{a t_\Gamma}|F_\Gamma|
                           \le K W_x,
    \tag{AC.20}
   \]

   where \(W_x\ge0\) is an actual energy/moment envelope. The sum may depend on the history, and the estimate is pointwise in that history and its real exterior tuple. This is the order used by the C05 positive catalog; for an insertion it must still be proved for that insertion.

2. **Geometric entropy:** after grouping history labels with the same current footprint geometry \(G\), the number of rooted geometries with costs in unit bins \(t,v\) is at most

   \[
    C_0 e^{s_t t+s_v v}.
    \tag{AC.21}
   \]

   A connected current cover of at most \(C(t+v+1)\) cells gives such a bound by the usual bounded-degree animal count, with finitely many cell decorations. This count concerns shapes, not arbitrary historical label strings. If a finer label distinction is retained, its entropy must be paid separately.

3. **Actual joint energy/birth penalty:** for the event \(E_G\) that the corresponding whole core family occurs, after summing all history labels grouped in \(G\),

   \[
    \mathbb E_{\mu_h}\big[W_x^p\mathbf1_{E_G}\big]
          \le (C_W dp)^{dp}e^{-\lambda v(G)},
    \qquad p\ge1.
    \tag{AC.22}
   \]

   The same bound without the exponential is assumed for \(\mathbb E W_x^p\). This is a statement in the **actual normalized full law**. The event must pin every core used by \(G\) jointly. An unnormalized operator norm, independent one-core estimates, a conditional law already forced to be bad, or a penalty for a newly born component alone is not (AC.22).

### Theorem AC.4 — relative-to-averaged-full transfer

If

\[
 a-\kappa C_T/d>s_t,
 \qquad \lambda-\kappa C_T/d>s_v,
 \tag{AC.23}
\]

then

\[
 \mathcal N_{d,\kappa}(F)
        \le K C_W^d C_{\rm geom},
 \tag{AC.24}
\]

where \(C_{\rm geom}<\infty\) depends only on the fixed slack in (AC.23), \(C_0\) and the fixed geometry constants. It is independent of the cutoff, the volume and the number of possible histories.

**Proof.** Set \(\zeta=\kappa C_T/d\). At moment order \(p\), the positive sum in (AC.14) is bounded, using (AC.19)–(AC.20), by

\[
 K W_x\sup_{\Gamma\ \mathrm{active}}
       \exp\{\zeta v_\Gamma/p-(a-\zeta/p)t_\Gamma\}.
\]

Its \(p\)-th power is at most

\[
 K^p W_x^p\mathcal G_x,
 \quad
 \mathcal G_x=
 \max\left(1,\sup_{G\ \mathrm{active}}
                    e^{\zeta v(G)-(a-\zeta)t(G)}\right).
 \tag{AC.25}
\]

The inequality uses \(ap-\zeta\ge a-\zeta\). Bound the supremum by one plus the positive sum over rooted geometries, then use (AC.21) and (AC.22). One obtains

\[
 \mathbb E[W_x^p\mathcal G_x]
 \le (C_W dp)^{dp}
 \left[1+C_0\sum_{t,v\ge0}
      e^{-(a-\zeta-s_t)t-(\lambda-\zeta-s_v)v}\right].
 \tag{AC.26}
\]

The bracket is finite by (AC.23). Take the \(p\)-th root, divide by \((dp)^d\), and take the supremum. This proves (AC.24). \(\square\)

There is no Minkowski sum over rare shapes in this proof. The geometry supremum is taken **after** using the actual pointwise catalog, and its expectation is controlled in the actual joint law. Equivalently, for integer \(p\), expand the positive carrier sum to the \(p\)-th power. A core appearing \(r\le p\) times is charged at most

\[
 \exp\{\kappa C_T r v/(dp)\}
                  \le\exp\{\kappa C_T v/d\}.
 \tag{AC.27}
\]

Thus its birth penalty is spent once per distinct present core, not once per replica. The joint estimate and shape entropy in (AC.22)–(AC.23) are the corresponding exact requirements.

The theorem is nontrivial even for bounded coefficients, taking \(W_x=1\). For general marks, the conditional current estimate in Section 3 supplies one real, fixed-history part of a candidate \(W_x\). It does not establish the outer joint estimate (AC.22). Nor does the theorem supply the marked conditional catalog (AC.20) for the already transported microscopic scalar. Those are concrete remaining estimates.

### 5.1 An actual successful-sector instance of the joint energy input

The accepted H03/NR20 result supplies a restricted, normalized instance of (AC.22). Use exactly the mixed posterior \(\pi_v\) of NM20.1: successful masses remain unnormalized inside its sum, residual component bases and their old states remain present, and \(v\) is the actual retained datum. Let

\[
 B_X=\mathcal F_X^\natural+D_X\ge1
\]

be NM20.2's common native Gaussian-energy envelope plus the actual surviving Gaussian coordinate rank. It controls the stated ordinary birth-coordinate squares, with the source's unit conversion. It contains no arbitrary raw old plaquette energy. For one fixed \(\vartheta>0\), NM20.7 proves

\[
 \mathbb E_{\pi_v}\!\left[
  \mathbf1_{\{\mathcal D\subset C\}}
              e^{\vartheta\sum_{X\in\mathcal D}B_X}\right]
       \le e^{-P_k|\mathcal D|/2}
 \tag{AC.S1}
\]

for every compatible prescribed family of successful components. Its proof deletes those whole components, preserving every remaining component measure, and compares the remaining positive sub-sum with the same full partition function. Incompatible families have zero contribution. Thus no inverse occupation probability occurs. This is the stated normalized deletion result, not a conclusion from an unnormalized source activity alone.

Let \(\mathcal X_x\) be the candidate successful components whose full fixed footprint meets a given current root block \(x\). The source eligible-size and shape count give

\[
 M_x:=|\mathcal X_x|\le e^{C R_k^4},\qquad
 |X^+|+\mathsf t_h(X^+)\le C_F R_k^4.
 \tag{AC.S2}
\]

Internal histories have already been summed in \(M_X\); they are not counted again in \(M_x\). Pad inactive stores by zero and put

\[
 \mathcal B_x=\sum_{X\in\mathcal X_x}
                    \mathbf1_{\{X\in C\}}B_X.
\]

Choose \(0<\theta\le\vartheta/2\). On the pinned event \(\mathcal D\subset C\), factor out the already occupied members of \(\mathcal D\cap\mathcal X_x\), and expand only the other candidates:

\[
 e^{\theta\mathcal B_x}
 =e^{\theta\sum_{X\in\mathcal D\cap\mathcal X_x}B_X}
   \prod_{X\in\mathcal X_x\setminus\mathcal D}
       \left[1+\mathbf1_{\{X\in C\}}
                            (e^{\theta B_X}-1)\right].
\]

For a fixed pinned successful family \(\mathcal D\), the term indexed by
\(\mathcal F\subset\mathcal X_x\setminus\mathcal D\), even with the extra insertion
\(e^{\theta\sum_{\mathcal D}B_X}\), is bounded using (AC.S1) on
\(\mathcal D\cup\mathcal F\). A repeated component has coefficient at most
\(2\theta\le\vartheta\), and the optional family is disjoint from \(\mathcal D\). Consequently

\[
\begin{split}
 &\mathbb E_{\pi_v}\!\left[
   \mathbf1_{\{\mathcal D\subset C\}}
   e^{\theta\mathcal B_x+
                     \theta\sum_{X\in\mathcal D}B_X}\right]\\
 &\quad\le
 e^{-P_k|\mathcal D|/2}
  (1+e^{-P_k/2})^{|\mathcal X_x\setminus\mathcal D|}
 \le C e^{-P_k|\mathcal D|/2}.
\end{split}
 \tag{AC.S3}
\]

The last inequality uses the actual source hierarchy
\(P_k/R_k^4\longrightarrow\infty\) at the common small-coupling ceiling.
It pays the entire candidate-shape count, not just the number of occupied
components.

For any source grade \(d\ge1\), set \(W_x=(1+\mathcal B_x)^d\). The elementary
bound on \(u^{dp}e^{-\theta u}\) gives

\[
 \mathbb E_{\pi_v}\!\left[
     \mathbf1_{\{\mathcal D\subset C\}}W_x^p\right]
 \le (C_\theta dp)^{dp}e^{-P_k|\mathcal D|/3},
 \qquad p\ge1,
 \tag{AC.S4}
\]

after a harmless fixed enlargement of \(C_\theta\). The empty family gives
the unpinned moment bound as well. If \(v(\mathcal D)\) is the union volume
of its full current footprints, then

\[
 v(\mathcal D)\le C_F R_k^4|\mathcal D|,\qquad
 \lambda_k=\frac{P_k}{3C_F R_k^4},
 \tag{AC.S5}
\]

so (AC.S4) is precisely (AC.22) with rate \(\lambda_k\). This rate can exceed
any required fixed shape/weight rate by the same source ceiling. The
estimate is uniform in the remaining admitted \(v\), and therefore remains
valid after integrating its true outer marginal.

This gives a uniform norm bound, not an exponentially rare norm bound.
After taking an \(L^p\) root the penalty is \(e^{-P_k|\mathcal D|/(3p)}\).
For an energy insertion whose degree equals its assigned grade, the
factor \(p^d\) is exactly used by the grade normalization; its supremum
does not retain an exponential rare-event factor. A degree surplus can
give a polynomial gain, as in (AC.3). No stronger conclusion is needed in
Theorem AC.4.

Thus the energy/birth input of Theorem AC.4 is already available for
catalogs dominated by this anchored **successful Gaussian-energy/rank
store**, provided their actual marked relative catalog (AC.20) and support
ledger are established. This is a nonempty class of polynomial energy
insertions. It is not an estimate for a fixed residual \(Y\), an unreplaced
bad region, a law conditioned on occupation, or the arbitrary historical
raw plaquette list in M03. It does not identify a transported microscopic
field with this energy class.

## 6. Four-dimensional endpoint sums and factorial order

The new weight is compatible with the spatial range already proved in C08. For a conditional cumulant of \(m\ge2\) current-layer quadratic observables, C8A.26 gives, with its explicit background envelopes retained,

\[
 |\kappa_\nu(O_1,\ldots,O_m)|
 \le C^{m+1}(m!)^2h_k^{-4m}\prod_i Q_{x_i}
                   e^{-\theta\tau(x_1,\ldots,x_m)/(m-1)}.
 \tag{AC.28}
\]

For a connected finite-stencil carrier formed from a tree joining the anchors, its non-core physical cost is at most \(C_S(m+\tau)\). With grade \(d\ge m\), \(p\ge1\), and fixed \(\kappa C_S<\theta/2\), the weight in (AC.14) uses less than half the tree rate in (AC.28). The factor from \(m/(dp)\) is bounded by a fixed constant. A remaining factor \(e^{-\theta\tau/[2(m-1)]}\) is available uniformly in \(p\). This carrier calculation presupposes an actual localization with the stated support ledger. A spanning tree in a numerical correlation bound is not, by itself, the full dependency carrier of the observable; C08 does not supply that missing localization.

For a fixed labeled tree, successively summing its leaves on the four-dimensional current grid costs at most

\[
 \left[C_\theta(m-1)^4\right]^{m-1}.
\]

There are \(m^{m-2}\) labeled trees. Since \(m^m\le e^m m!\),

\[
 \sup_{x_1}\sum_{x_2,\ldots,x_m}
            e^{-\theta\tau/[2(m-1)]}
          \le C_\theta^m(m!)^5.
 \tag{AC.29}
\]

Combining (AC.28) and (AC.29) gives factorial power seven, exactly as in C8A.28, after division by the stated \(Q\) envelopes. Finite field labels and fixed local stencils change only \(C^m\). The outer moments of those envelopes and pinned bad-core events are still separate.

This conclusion uses the **conditional \(1/(m-1)\) tree estimate before the outer moment supremum**. A bound with only rate \(1/(dp)\) would instead produce a factor \(p^{4(m-1)}\) from endpoint summation. The norm (AC.14) alone does not remove that loss. One must retain the stronger conditional connector estimate, prove the joint rooted geometry estimate of Section 5, or increase the moment grade explicitly. No fixed-factorial claim follows from a bare moment-dependent decay statement.

## 7. Hard selectors remain a genuine obstruction

Changing the carrier weight does not regularize a jump. On a local real coordinate \(u\), take the selector \(\chi(u)=\mathbf1_{u>0}\), and suppose the ambient law has a positive continuous density near \(u=0\). For a shift \(t>0\),

\[
 \delta_t\chi(u)=t^{-1}\mathbf1_{-t<u\le0}.
\]

For every fixed \(p>1\),

\[
 \|\delta_t\chi\|_{L^p}
           \asymp t^{-1+1/p}\longrightarrow\infty.
 \tag{AC.30}
\]

Multiplying the threshold neighborhood by a fixed small probability factor does not change this divergence. The M03 pointwise supremum over divided differences is more demanding still. A different event tail cannot supply a finite all-direction current norm at such a boundary.

A complete sum over physical branch labels can cancel a selector boundary term when every branch has first been pulled back to the same raw variables and carries exactly the same physical insertion. The exact identity is now verified in [Survival estimates for the unreplaced history](unreplaced_history.md), §9, and [Microscopic source transport and contact neutrality](microscopic_source_transport.md), CS.12–CS.13, with the pointwise/integrated distinction of LFR I p.193 and LFR II p.378 retained. It does not apply to an event-marked subset, a label-dependent native insertion, or the absolute sum of separate selector-current records. No conditional posterior estimate is inferred from that full-label identity here.

Another possible framework would use a positive smooth local partition subordinate to overlapping admitted source charts. To replace the hard augmented history law one would have to prove the common raw marginal identity, support admission for every new branch, locality of the partition and a summable derivative budget. Neither the stated M03 convention nor the new norm grants these properties automatically.

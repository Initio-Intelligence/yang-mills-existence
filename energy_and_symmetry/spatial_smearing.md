# Spatial smearing of energy insertions

Throughout, use the ordinary compact finite Wilson transfer, \(\beta=2Nx_0\), an even spatial period \(n\ge4\), spacing \(0<a\le1\), and the reserve \(\log(1+\beta)/n\le B\). Set \(P=e^{-aH}\) on the gauge-invariant Hilbert space. Translations of the spatial torus commute with \(H\). Write \(M_x\) for the two-step insertion of the specified dimensionless Euclidean clover at site \(x\). Fixed Wick phases do not affect the norm. The conclusions use the complete Wilson law under these hypotheses. 

## The zero spatial mode of the actual pseudoscalar survives

Sum the signed identity of the corresponding estimate over sites. Each spatial edge from \(x\) to \(x+e_i\) acquires both a left derivative generated by \(B_i(x)\) and a right derivative generated by \(B_i(x+e_i)\), with the same sign. On a commuting configuration their generators add. Let \(D=\sum_x D_{B(x)}\). Consider the periodic links
\[
 U_i(x)=\exp(i\epsilon A_i(z)T),\qquad
 (A_1,A_2,A_3)=(\cos kz,\sin kz,0),\quad z=x_3,\quad k=2\pi/n,
\]
where \(T=\operatorname{diag}(1,-1,0,\ldots,0)\). Write \(F\) for the total spatial Wilson action without its coupling. Its value is \(c_n\epsilon^2+O_n(\epsilon^4)\), with \(c_n>0\) because the two transverse differences do not vanish simultaneously. The centered clover curl gives \(B_1=-\epsilon T[A_2(z+1)-A_2(z-1)]/2+O_n(\epsilon^3)\), \(B_2=\epsilon T[A_1(z+1)-A_1(z-1)]/2+O_n(\epsilon^3)\), and \(B_3=0\). The two end generators on the \(i=1,2\) edges agree, since \(z\) is constant along the edge. The tangent on this field is therefore \(-2\sin(k)\) times its leading link logarithm, with a cubic remainder. Consequently
\[
 DF=-4c_n\sin(k)\epsilon^2+O_n(\epsilon^4)\ne0
 \tag{1}
\]
for sufficiently small nonzero \(\epsilon\).

This also gives an operator statement. The full sum of vector fields is smooth and divergence free on the compact link manifold. Its complete flow defines a closed skew generator preserving physical functions. \(P\) has dense range: each normalized Wilson convolution has strictly positive coefficients on all irreducible representations, since the positive exponential series contains characters of tensor powers of the fundamental representation and its dual, which together generate them all. Endpoint multiplication by the strictly positive magnetic half-weight is invertible at fixed cutoff. The same injectivity and dense range hold after restriction to the invariant space. If \(PDP=0\), then \(DPf=0\) for every \(f\), by injectivity and smoothing of \(P\). The closed kernel of the generator would contain a dense set, contradicting (1) on the smooth invariant \(F\). Thus the operator \(\sum_xM_{p(x)}=(2\beta)^{-1}PDP\) is nonzero. This establishes nonvanishing at fixed cutoff without a uniform lower estimate.

An independent pathwise example also tests a possible time divergence. On an ordinary periodic spacetime torus take \(U_2(x)=\exp(iT b(x_1))\), \(U_3(x)=\exp(iT c(x_0))\), \(U_0=U_1=I\), with consecutive forward differences of \(b\) equal to \((v,v,-2v,0,\ldots)\) and of \(c\) equal to \((w,w,-2w,0,\ldots)\). Both functions start at zero and are periodic. Only the 12 and 03 plaquettes are nontrivial. For each of those planes the based clover is \(T\) times the average of the sines of the current and previous forward differences. Each of these two plaquettes occurs twice in its respective four-term clover. The complete actual pseudoscalar sum is
\[
 \sum_{x_0,\mathbf x}p(x_0,\mathbf x)
 = n_2 n_3\,\operatorname{Tr}(T^2)
 (2\sin v-\sin(2v))(2\sin w-\sin(2w)).
 \tag{2}
\]
It is nonzero for all sufficiently small positive \(v,w\). The links can lie in an arbitrarily small global chart, with zero flux in both planes. Nonvanishing persists on an open set of positive compact Wilson measure at every finite coupling. An exact periodic spatial or spacetime divergence of the original clover would have zero sum. Equation (2) also excludes a configurationwise exact endpoint difference after summing the spatial slice. Its leading defect is of degree six in this example. Estimates after integration, local perturbative matching and relative bounds on the nonlinear remainder are separate questions.

## High spatial momentum transfer can be removed at arbitrary order

For a Schwartz spatial test \(g\) periodized on the spatial torus set \(Q_a(g)=z a^{-4}a^3\sum_xg_{\rm per}(x)M_x\). For a finite channel mixture sum the resulting bounds with its actual coefficient moduli. A constant identity term has only zero momentum transfer. Let \(S_1,S_2\) be arbitrary spatial momentum sets of the physical representation, with spectral projections \(\Pi_1,\Pi_2\). Use the shortest Euclidean distance modulo the reciprocal mesh \(2\pi\mathbb Z^3/a\). Suppose the distance between the sets is at least \(s\), where \(1\le s\le a^{-1}\).

For each positive integer \(k\) and either seed there is a bound
\[
 \|\Pi_1(1+H)^{-1/2}Q_a(g)(1+H)^{-1/2}\Pi_2\|
 \le C_{k,B,N}\frac{|z| a^{-4}}{\beta}\,
       b_a\,s^{-k}\,\mathcal N_k(g),\qquad
 b_a=\begin{cases}\sqrt a&\text{pseudoscalar},\\1&\text{magnetic seed}.\end{cases}
 \tag{3}
\]
One choice is \(\mathcal N_k(g)=\max_{|\alpha|=k}\sup_x(1+|x|)^4|\partial^\alpha g(x)|\). For the heat-sandwiched version use \(b_a=\min(1,\sqrt{a/t}+\sqrt{a/t'})\) in the odd channel and \(1\) in the even channel. Constants absorb the finite stencil and fixed trace conventions.

Choose a real Schwartz function \(\phi\) whose Fourier transform equals one near zero and is supported on \(|\xi|<1/2\). For \(\phi_s(x)=s^3\phi(sx)\), \(g_< =\phi_s*g\) has only frequencies of length less than \(s/2\). All positive-order moments of \(\phi\) vanish, and its integral is one. Taylor expansion with integral remainder at order \(k\) and \(1+|x|\le(1+|x-y|)(1+|y|)\) give
\[
 |g(x)-g_<(x)|\le C_k s^{-k}(1+|x|)^{-4}\mathcal N_k(g)
\]
for \(s\ge1\). Summing samples of the periodized remainder with \(a^3\) preserves this upper bound on its lattice \(L^1\) norm, since \(a^3\sum_{x\in a\mathbb Z^3}(1+|x|)^{-4}\le C\). For \(g_<\), Poisson summation on the mesh and periodization give only momentum transfers of length less than \(s/2\) modulo \(2\pi/a\). Equivalently each block between two translation characters picks out exactly the Fourier coefficient at their difference. Thus \(\Pi_1 Q_a(g_<)\Pi_2=0\). Apply the corresponding estimate to the remaining sampled test, using commutation of translations and heat or graph weights, to obtain (3). This proof uses no operator-norm estimate based on the number of momentum eigenvalues. Its constants are independent of the spatial period. For tests supported inside one central box, periodization agrees with the original samples there.

For example, \(s=a^{-\theta}\), \(0<\theta\le1\), earns the factor \(a^{k\theta}\). This absorbs any fixed engineering power on the displayed blocks, separately from the actual renormalization multiplier. More generally, the whole smeared operator is approximated in the same norm by one with transfer below \(s/2\), with the error in (3) and with both projections omitted on that *difference*. Small transfer can connect arbitrarily energetic states. Neither a dispersion assumption nor a continuum field domain enters this estimate.

## Why time reversal alone gives no magnetic gain

At a single center an off-diagonal magnetic tensor is time even. The reversal used in the corresponding estimate then symmetrizes its endpoint pairing. The sum of endpoint values survives when both endpoint functions are constant. Spatial reflection reversing this magnetic tensor does annihilate its ground expectation. Combining that reflection \(J\) with time reversal instead produces an endpoint difference satisfying
\[
 \tfrac12\mathbb E|f(X)-f(JY)|^2
    =\langle f,(I-JP^2)f\rangle .
\]
The identity holds for a site-centered lattice reflection, using the ground state's invariance and commutation with \(P\). On the reflection-odd subspace the multiplier is \(I+P^2\). An opposite-parity pair is exactly where the off-diagonal matrix element can survive. Thus the two-step reversal proof supplies no small energy factor on unrestricted magnetic matrix elements. This is an obstruction to that argument, not a physical lower bound for the magnetic field.

The estimates above leave the uniform physical bounds for low spatial transfer, including the zero mode, and the continuum domains in E-03 and E-04 open.

## Uniform control of the nonlinear clover error, including zero transfer

One part of the nonlinear lattice correction can be estimated on the complete physical Hilbert space, even at low spatial transfer. For a plaquette \(U\), let \(A(U)\) be its Hermitian spectral angle, using \(-\pi<\theta<\pi\) away from \(-1\), and assigning angle zero on the \(-1\) eigenspace. With this convention the Borel function is covariant under conjugation, odd under inversion, obeys \(\sin A(U)=(U-U^\dagger)/(2i)\), and satisfies \(\|A(U)\|_{\rm HS}\le C_N\sqrt{s(U)}\) globally. The last estimate follows from the elementary scalar chord bound. No link gauge or common logarithm of the configuration is chosen.

For each fixed \(m\ge0\), replace the sine inside every clover by \(b_m(A)=\sum_{j=0}^m(-1)^j A^{2j+1}/(2j+1)!\), retaining every orientation, average, trace and traceless projection. Let \(R_m\) be the original product of two clovers minus the resulting product. In particular \(m=0\) compares the original field to its angle clover. Write \(S=\sum_{p\in X}s_p\) for the finite union of plaquettes in the two-step stencil and its time reflection. Taylor's theorem and the fixed matrix dimension give
\[
 |R_m|\le C_{m,N,X} S^{m+2}.
\]
Indeed the error of one plaquette entry is \(O_m(s_p^{m+3/2})\), while every unchanged or polynomial clover has size \(O_m(\sqrt S)\). For an unchanged Wilson trace repair its contribution to the difference is zero. These estimates concern two fields evaluated in the same Wilson distribution.

Let \(d=m+2\). The two-step exponential majorant of the corresponding estimate bounds \(\|M_{R_m}\|\le C_m\beta^{-d}\). For the pseudoscalar, \(R_m\) is reversal odd. For \(h=R_m{\bf1}_{\beta^d|R_m|\le u}\), the same exponential majorant gives
\[
 \|M_{R_m-h}\|\le C_m\beta^{-d}e^{-c_m u^{1/d}},
 \qquad \|M_{h^2}\|\le C_m\beta^{-2d}.
\]
The first bound uses \(\beta S\ge c_m u^{1/d}\) on the tail and reserves half the exponential for the polynomial moment. The second follows by pointwise domination on the two-step path. Repeating the corresponding estimate (5)–(6) yields the normalized-vector bound \(C_m\beta^{-d}(\delta+u\delta^2+e^{-c_m u^{1/d}})\), with \(\delta=\sqrt{D(f)}+\sqrt{D(g)}\) for unit vectors. Choosing \(u=\max(1,(c_m^{-1}\log(1/\delta))^d)\) for \(0<\delta<1\) absorbs the final two terms into \(C_m\delta\); use the direct norm at \(\delta\ge1\) and send \(u\) to infinity at zero. In particular the spatially smeared error obeys
\[
 \left\|(1+H)^{-1/2}z a^{-4}a^3\sum_x g_{\rm per}(x)M_{R_m(x)}
                 (1+H)^{-1/2}\right\|
 \le C_{m,B,N}\,|z|a^{-4} b_a\,\beta^{-(m+2)}
              \sup_x (1+|x|)^4|g(x)|.
 \tag{4}
\]
For the odd seed \(b_a=\sqrt a\). For the time-even magnetic seed \(b_a=1\) follows directly from the norm bound. The heat versions use the factors in (3). For an off-diagonal seed both the compact and polynomial-component means vanish by the same spatial symmetry; for other fields a prescribed scalar subtraction would have to be accounted for. At \(m=0\) the error gains a full inverse coupling relative to the estimate for the original field. Equation (4) gives no cancellation for the angle-clover product itself. Constants here are for each fixed \(m\), and iterating \(m\) on a physical trajectory would require tracking them.

There is also a simple uniform-in-order alternative for the remainder alone. Since \(\|A\|_{\rm op}\le\pi\), the polynomial clovers are uniformly bounded (by the absolutely convergent exponential series), and the matrix sine tail gives \(\|R_m\|_\infty\le C_{N,X}\pi^{2m+3}/(2m+3)!\). Endpoint Cauchy–Schwarz in the stationary path gives this same bound on its arbitrary-vector operator norm. For the odd remainder, reversing the endpoints before applying Cauchy–Schwarz multiplies this bound by \(C\delta\) on normalized vectors, with no clipping. Thus in (4) one can replace \(C_{m,B,N}\beta^{-(m+2)}\) by \(C_{N,X}\pi^{2m+3}/(2m+3)!\) independently of \(\beta,m\). In particular \(m=\lceil\log(1/a)\rceil\) gives \(O_j(a^j)\) for every fixed \(j\), by the factorial lower bound. This permits a cutoff-dependent polynomial representation of the same engineered insertion to errors smaller than any mesh power before the factor \(|z|\). It gives no bound on the remaining polynomial observable or on its own low-transfer matrix elements.

# Low spatial momentum and the magnetic energy exponent

There are two different lower bounds. The first is a contact estimate valid under the compact reserve. The second concerns the actual Wilson transfer at positive lattice-time separations on a selectable, arbitrarily weak comparison family. At bounded outgoing spatial momentum the latter rules out any energy exponent \(s\le5/2\) on the vacuum side of the dimension-four magnetic insertion on that family. It allows larger exponents and fixed positive physical heat times. The family has terminal coupling tending to zero.  · Signed and unsigned two-step insertions

## A microscopic variance at zero spatial transfer

Use the centered real trace clovers \(B_1=C_{23}, B_2=C_{31}, E_i=C_{0i}\). The actual (F.12), (F.33) normalized magnetic readout before engineering is
\[
 V(x)=\alpha\,\operatorname{Tr}(B_1B_2)(x)
             +\gamma\,\operatorname{Tr}(E_1E_2)(x),\qquad
 \alpha=(z_{T_o}+z_{W_s})/2>0,\quad |\gamma|\le\alpha.
\]
The orientation sign of \(\gamma\) is immaterial below. A fixed common trace conversion changes the constants. The pure magnetic case uses \((\alpha,\gamma)=(1,0)\). Fix a central time slice. In an even periodic spatial cube select at least \(c n^3\) sites \(y\) mutually separated by at least eight bonds in periodic distance, for sufficiently large \(n\). At each selected site leave unobserved its outgoing \(1\)-link and \(2\)-link at that time; condition on all other links. The Wilson conditional law factors over these pairs since no plaquette touches two pairs. Every central-time observable \(V(x)\) also meets at most one pair. A sum over all spatial sites therefore has conditional variance equal to the sum of the conditional variances of the affected local sums.

For each pair, route a fixed exterior tree through the visible bonds of its incident faces and affected stencils; either hidden link can be bypassed via the preceding time slice. Every visible loop in its tree coordinates has a bounded plaquette filling, possibly involving hidden links. By the unconditional rectangular reserve and the triangle inequality each visible loop displacement has squared expectation at most \(C/\beta\). Thus, with probability at least \(1/2\) under the original marginal of the conditioning, all its visible tree-gauge logarithms have size \(\le K/\sqrt\beta\), for a common fixed \(K\). This event concerns one pair at a time. Uniformity holds in \(n\) and in even temporal lengths \(m\ge n\) when \(\log(1+\beta)/n\) is bounded.

Here are details of the local nondegeneracy on that event. At a flat collar the compact two-link action has the unique minimum \(U_1=U_2=I\) in the rooted tree gauge; each live link belongs to faces containing no other live link. The Hessian of the action in their Lie coordinates is strictly positive, because these faces include separate singleton contributions. For scaled visible parameters in the compact ball \(|\sqrt\beta\,b|\le K\), the scaled hidden variables \(h_i=\sqrt\beta\,\operatorname{arg}(U_i)\) in the identity chart converge in all fixed polynomial moments, uniformly in these parameters, to a Gaussian with that fixed positive inverse Hessian and a bounded, parameter-dependent mean. This follows by Taylor expansion about the flat configuration in all variables simultaneously, keeping the bounded scaled visible parameter in the quadratic. The complement of a fixed chart has an exponentially small mass by the isolated compact minimum; inside it the positive quadratic bound yields uniform Gaussian tails after a bounded shift. The same argument applies to the first two moments of \(\beta\sum_{x\ {\rm affected}} V(x)/\alpha\), whose limiting polynomial in \(h\) has degree at most two. Borel choices of angle outside the identity chart do not affect these estimates.

In the linearized clovers, the centered \(23\) curl of the hidden \(2\)-link has a nonzero coefficient at the centers in its two transverse layers \(y_3\pm1\), with magnitude \(1/4\); at the central layer the forward and backward differences cancel. The \(31\) curl of the \(1\)-link has the opposite sign on the respective layers. Their lists of centers intersect just at \(y\pm e_3\). Thus the mixed quadratic term of the local sum is \(-\operatorname{Tr}(h_1h_2)/8\) in Hermitian coordinates. Both centered \(0i\) curls have zero first variation at every central-time center under any spatial-link variation confined to precisely that slice: the two temporal difference contributions cancel. Their product therefore has no mixed quadratic term in the scaled hidden variables for any allowed \(\gamma/\alpha\). Visible parameters produce at most affine terms in these variables. For a real Gaussian with positive covariance \(\Sigma\), the variance of its quadratic-plus-affine polynomial is at least \(2\|\Sigma^{1/2}A\Sigma^{1/2}\|_{\rm HS}^2\) for symmetric quadratic matrix \(A\), by orthogonality of centered linear and second Wick polynomials after shifting the mean. The nonzero mixed term gives a strictly positive lower bound, uniform in visible parameters and mixing. The same lower bound, reduced by a fixed factor, holds in the finite weak Wilson fiber by the uniform moment comparison.

Average these conditional variances and use total variance. For all sufficiently large \(\beta,n\),
\[
 \operatorname{Var}\left(n^{-3}\sum_x V(x)\right)
       \ge c\,\alpha^2\beta^{-2}n^{-3}.                         \tag{1}
\]
This is the exact spatially constant mode. The same proof yields, for a fixed nonzero smooth compact spatial \(g\) inside the period, a lower bound \(c_g\alpha^2\beta^{-2}a^3\) on the variance of \(a^3\sum_xg(ax)V(x)\) when its support is resolved and the torus contains it. Use pairs in a box of fixed physical size on which \(g\) has constant sign and is bounded away from zero. At a given pair the two terms in the mixed coefficient carry the weights \(g(a(y+e_3))\) and \(g(a(y-e_3))\); the entire finite list of coefficients is uniformly bounded. The fixed smooth test does not change the conditional nondegeneracy there. The constants depend on the compact reserve and stencil. For the pure magnetic multiplication field the same estimate holds in the true finite spatial ground state by taking even temporal length to infinity, as the positive transfer has simple top eigenvalue. In the mixed temporal case (1) is an ordinary same-time contact with both insertions using their original stencil. It does not directly lower-bound the squared norm of the two-step inserted transfer.

## A lower bound on the actual normalized transfer at bounded spatial momentum

Fix one nonzero real spatial Schwartz function \(g\) with Fourier transform supported in the unit ball in physical units. Use its periodization, without a support cutoff. At each fine spacing take the calibrated cubic coefficients from (F.33) and take the vacuum of the corresponding finite spatial Wilson transfer \(P=e^{-aH}\). Write
\[
 v_a=Q^R_a(g)\Omega,\qquad
 Q^R_a(g)=a^{-4}a^3\sum_x g_{\rm per}(ax)M_{V(x)} .
\]
Here \(M\) is the normalized two-step insertion, with support on its central time and two neighbors, and \(\Omega\) is normalized. This even insertion is self-adjoint. Periodization and translation covariance put \(v_a\) exactly in spatial momenta of shortest physical length \(\le1\) modulo the reciprocal mesh. On a cofinal family of genuine Wilson finite spatial Hamiltonians one has
\[
 \|(1+H)^{-s}v_a\|^2 \ \ge\
 \begin{cases}
 c_{g,s}\,a^{2s-5}, &0\le s<5/2,\\[2pt]
 c_g\log(1/a), &s=5/2 .
 \end{cases}                                                    \tag{2}
\]
All coefficients in \(V\) here are those measured on the cubic Wilson torus. Finite-cutoff boundedness makes the vector and the nonpositive power of the positive Hamiltonian well-defined without a continuum domain assumption.

**Rectangular limit used to select the parameters.** In the local proof of R2 §11 take side lengths \(n,n,n,m\), all divisible by its auxiliary \(l\), all \(\ge3l\), and \(m\ge n\) even. Its free/pinned pressure bracketing (40) uses disjoint \(l^4\)-vertex cubes. There are \(n^3m/l^4\) of them; the number of interbox and boundary coordinates is at most \(C n^3m/l\). All Taylor integrals and compressed determinants belong to a single such cube. Dividing by \(n^3m\) gives (40) with precisely the same constants, and the difference quotient at \(\beta\) gives (41) uniformly in \(m\). The random-offset side-\(l\) conditional boxes likewise fit each coordinate exactly. The compact single-stencil reserve is uniform in \(m\) by the corresponding estimate (1); the single-box centered Laplace moments use those same local coordinates. Thus the proof by summing the positive conditional-mean excess over all plaquettes and averaging the offset applies in a rectangle. It only uses the total mean in (41), translations and positivity to bound each specified orientation; no permutation symmetry involving the longer time direction is required. On a fixed finite lifted stencil with sufficiently large \(n,l\), a random offset places it in one interior except on \(O({\rm diam}/l)\) of placements. The conditional curl projection converges to the infinite-lattice one, by the inner-box exhaustion or the averaged rank deficit. This proves the fixed finite list of two-clover-product moment comparisons of the corresponding estimate uniformly in even multiples \(m\) tending to infinity. Taking \(m\to\infty\) at each fixed spatial cutoff gives the same bounds in its transfer ground state, by compactness, positivity and the trace-with-insertions identity. For the seven normalization measurements use the original cubic law, not these rectangular moments. **Discrete Gaussian bound.** For dimensionless field \(\beta\operatorname{Tr}(B_1B_2)\) the Gaussian limit is the product of two spatial centered clover curls, contracted over independent colors. In spatial momentum \(p\in[-\pi,\pi]^3\), put \(\hat p_i=2\sin(p_i/2)\), \(E(p)=2\operatorname{arsinh}(|\hat p|/2)\). The matrix of Gaussian time-separated spatial magnetic propagators at fine-time gap \(r>0\) is
\[
 C_B(p,r)=D(p)\big(|\hat p|^2 I-\hat p\hat p^t\big)D(p)
                \frac{e^{-rE(p)}}{2\sinh E(p)} .
\]
Here \(D_{ii}=\prod_{j\ne i}\cos(p_j/2)\), from averaging the four offsets at the clover center. This is the midpoint curl symbol followed by the elementary time Fourier coefficient of \((2-2\cos k_0+|\hat p|^2)^{-1}\), obtained by solving its scalar second-order recurrence. \(C_B\) is real positive semidefinite. For the off-diagonal bilinear let \(A\) be its nonzero real symmetric magnetic matrix, with its only nonzero entries at \(12,21\). The spatial Fourier covariance of two such bilinears is a positive color constant times the integral of \(\operatorname{tr}(A C_B(p,r) A C_B(\zeta-p,r))\). The integrand is nonnegative since it is the squared Hilbert–Schmidt norm of the appropriately square-root-sandwiched \(A\). On a small cone around \(e_3\) with \(c_1/r\le|p|\le c_2/r\), both transverse \(12\) polarizations have covariances of order \(1/r\). For \(|r\zeta|\) sufficiently small, \(\zeta-p\) lies close to the opposite cone, and continuity after rescaling gives a uniform positive lower bound \(c/r^2\) for that trace. Integrating over the cone gives \(c r^{-5}\) for \(r\ge4\). The constants can be chosen using small fixed \(0<c_1<c_2<1\), so the same statement includes the finitely many smaller integer gaps \(r\ge4\).

For the mixture one also needs an absolute upper estimate. For \(r\ge4\) any centered electric or magnetic two-curl propagator at spatial \(p\) is bounded by \(C|\hat p|e^{-(r-2)E(p)}\). Indeed spatial differences carry \(\hat p_i\). Each temporal difference, evaluated away from the contact, gives \(O(|\hat p|)\) by the above exponential formula at neighboring times and \(e^{E(p)}-1=O(|\hat p|)\) on this compact momentum range. Clover averages use only bounded half or whole shifts. Two curls give two derivatives in total. Division by \(2\sinh E(p)\) leaves the stated power. Cauchy–Schwarz in spatial momentum bounds each Wick convolution in absolute value by \(C\int |\hat p|^2e^{-2(r-2)E(p)}dp\le Cr^{-5}\), uniformly in \(\zeta\). The magnetic coefficient of the dimensionless normalized Gaussian stencil tends to \(1/2\) and the electric coefficient tends to zero as \(a\to0\): at each fixed mesh they are the half-sum and signed half-difference of \(t_{T_o}(a),t_{W_s}(a)\) in the corresponding estimate. They do not depend on \(r\). The strict \(cr^{-5}\) lower bound therefore survives the actual calibration's Gaussian limit uniformly on the stated small external momentum range.

By Poisson summation on the infinite spatial lattice the dimensionless two-point function of \(a^3\sum g(ax)V(x)\) is \(a^3\int|\widehat g(k)|^2\widehat C(ak,r)d^3k/(2\pi)^3\) in this normalized Gaussian comparison. There is no sampling alias for sufficiently small \(a\) with the stated Fourier support. Hence it is bounded below by \(c_g a^3r^{-5}\) for \(4\le r\le 2a^{-1/2}\). The means of both off-diagonal insertions vanish by reflection.

**Selecting the actual Wilson bound.** At each fixed \(a\), first truncate the spatial samples at a finite large radius in physical units so that their omitted \(a^3\)-weighted absolute sum is as small as desired. Such a truncation approximates the two-point functions uniformly on the large tori, by the compact two-stencil moment reserve, bounded \(z_h/\beta\) on the weak tail, and bounded Gaussian moments. Take the spatial period large enough to include the truncation. Periodizing the entire original \(g\) changes only this controlled tail. This truncation is solely in the proof of the value estimate; the actual operator uses the full band-limited periodization. Now the stencil pairs and the gaps \(4\le r\le2a^{-1/2}\) are finite lists. Use the rectangular fixed-stencil result for the ground-state pairs and the cubic fixed-stencil result for the seven coefficients. At this fixed mesh choose their weighted comparison errors and the initial tail so that their sum is smaller than \(\tfrac12 c_g a^3(2a^{-1/2})^{-5}\). At the sequence of meshes this can be imposed as additional finite requirements in the corresponding estimate, including all candidate terminal depths before the suffix stop. No quantitative rate on \(\beta(a)\) or physical matching is asserted.

The trace/ground-state insertion formula places the centers of two two-step insertions \(j+2\) fine-time units apart when \(P^j\) lies between them. Restoring both engineering factors now gives, for all integers \(2\le j\le a^{-1/2}\) on the selected family,
\[
 \langle v_a,P^j v_a\rangle\ \ge c_g\, a^{-5}(j+2)^{-5}.
 \tag{3}
\]
The extra two steps come from the two central-stencil half widths and do not affect the exponent.

For \(s=0\), (3) and contractivity suffice. For \(s>0\) the norm squared in (2) is the integral of \(\langle v_a,e^{-tH}v_a\rangle e^{-t}t^{2s-1}/\Gamma(2s)\). This expectation is decreasing and nonnegative by its positive spectral measure. For \(2\le j\le\lfloor a^{-1/2}\rfloor\), its value on \([(j-1)a,ja]\) is bounded below by (3). The contribution of these intervals is at least \(c_{g,s}a^{2s-5}\sum j^{2s-6}\) over that range. The first few terms suffice for \(s<5/2\); the harmonic sum diverges logarithmically at \(s=5/2\). This proves (2), also for the two-sided graph norm since the right graph factor fixes \(\Omega\).

This obstruction concerns a time-local two-step measurement that is smeared only in space on the specified weak comparison family. It does not rule out a physical positive-time heat bound, exponents \(s>5/2\), time smearing, or a different physical trajectory. E-03 and E-04 remain open.

## Matching upper bound on the vacuum for every spatial test

The comparison family in (2) can simultaneously satisfy
\[
 \|e^{-tH}Q^R_a(g)\Omega\|
    \le C\,[1+(a+t)^{-5/2}]\,\mathcal N(g),\qquad t\ge0,
 \qquad
 \|(1+H)^{-s}Q^R_a(g)\Omega\|\le C_s\mathcal N(g)
             \quad(s>5/2),                                      \tag{4}
\]
where \(\mathcal N(g)=\sup_{x\in\mathbb R^3}(1+|x|)^9|g(x)|\). The same one choice of bare parameters works for every spatial Schwartz test. These are estimates for the actual mixed, calibrated, two-step Wilson insertion and the finite spatial ground state. In particular dual pairing against arbitrary vectors in the finite-cutoff graph domain obeys \(|\langle f,Q^R_a(g)\Omega\rangle|\le C_s\|(1+H)^s f\|\mathcal N(g)\).

To obtain the uniformity, enlarge the finite requirements at each \(a\) to all central-site pairs \(|ax|_\infty,|ay|_\infty\le R_a=a^{-2}\) and center gaps \(2\le r\le\lceil1/a\rceil+3\). Require absolute error \(\le a^9\) for each dimensionless normalized two-point entry, compared with its infinite-lattice Gaussian value with the fixed calibration \(t_h(a)\). The same fixed-mesh rectangular selection and separate cubic calibration prove this for the finite list. Keep the spatial period more than \(4R_a\) in physical units. Extend the Wilson kernel periodically to the infinite mesh, which implements periodization of the test exactly. Both dimensionless pair kernels have a uniform absolute bound, from the compact reserve and bounded \(z_h/\beta\) in the vacuum limit or the Gaussian moments. The omitted region of the two-site sum costs at most \(C\mathcal N(g)^2 R_a^{-6}\) since \(a^3\sum_{|ax|_\infty>R_a}(1+|ax|)^{-9}\le C R_a^{-6}\). Including the central entries, the absolute comparison error is consequently bounded by \(C(a^9+a^{12})\mathcal N(g)^2\) before the \(a^{-8}\) engineering factor.

For every external momentum, the Gaussian absolute Fourier upper bound \(C r^{-5}\) for \(r\ge4\) was proved above. For \(r=2,3\) use boundedness of the underlying fixed-lag spatial Fourier curl matrices: spatial-spatial entries follow by the displayed \(C_B\) formula, differences in time at positive separation by the same scalar recurrence, and any zero-lag second-difference contact is bounded by its constant Fourier coefficient. Equivalently integrate the uniformly bounded four-dimensional curl-projection multiplier in temporal momentum, keeping the bounded clover symbols. The finite Wick convolutions are bounded uniformly in external momentum. Thus \(C(1+r)^{-5}\) holds for \(r\ge2\). Discrete spatial Parseval for the convolution of the two-point kernel with sampled \(g\) bounds the dimensionless Gaussian covariance by
\[
 C a^6 (1+r)^{-5}\sum_{x\in\mathbb Z^3}|g(ax)|^2
       \le C a^3(1+r)^{-5}\mathcal N(g)^2 .
\]
Together with the uniform comparison and \(r=j+2\), this gives
\(\langle v_a,P^j v_a\rangle\le C[1+(a+ja)^{-5}]\mathcal N(g)^2\) for all integers \(0\le j\le\lceil1/a\rceil\), including the exact squared norm at \(j=0\). Monotonicity of the vacuum spectral integral fills the intervals, using the previous mesh point for upper bounds, and bounds all later times by the value at a time of order one. Taking the square root at \(2t\) proves the heat estimate in (4). The same Mellin identity now gives the graph bound because \(\int_0^1 t^{2s-1}t^{-5}dt\) is finite for \(s>5/2\). At \(0<s<5/2\) it gives the matching upper growth \(O_s(a^{2s-5})\) for the squared norm; at \(s=5/2\) it gives \(O(1+\log(1/a))\). At \(s=0\) use \(j=0\) directly. The physical heat and graph estimates here have one vacuum input. They do not supply a uniform estimate between two general vectors.

## The odd seed has the same vacuum threshold on this family

Equations (2)–(4) hold also for the actual prescribed pseudoscalar with its fixed Wick phase and \(z_P\) from (F.33), on the same sequence. For its Gaussian lower bound it suffices to use fine-time separations above a fixed large constant \(r_0\). The continuum bilinear matrix \(A_P\) is a nonzero multiple of the Hodge star on two-forms. For every unit vector \(u\), the matrix \(J(u)=\Lambda^2(I-2uu^t)\) is orthogonal, squares to the identity and anticommutes with that star. Consequently the scalar trace in the separated Gaussian Wick pair is proportional to \(\operatorname{tr}(A_P J A_P J)=-\operatorname{tr}(A_P^2)\) in every direction. The shifted discrete curl asymptotic for each pair, with bounded stencil shifts, has error \(O(|z|^{-5})\) and leading term \(\pi^{-2}|z|^{-4}J(\hat z)\). Multiplying the two factors gives the direction-uniform covariance
\[
 C_P^{G}(r,x)=-c_N(r^2+|x|^2)^{-4}
                        +O\big((r^2+|x|^2)^{-9/2}\big),
 \qquad (r,x)\in\mathbb Z^4,\quad |(r,x)|\gg1 ,
\]
up to its positive squared calibration, bounded away from zero for all small \(a\). The minus sign is exactly compensated in \(\langle v_a,P^j v_a\rangle\) by the adjoint of the skew Euclidean insertion (equivalently the square of the self-adjoint insertion with its Wick phase). In spatial Fourier the compensated value at \(\zeta=0\) is at least \(c r^{-5}\) for \(r\ge r_0\), by summing the leading term on \(|x|\le r\) and bounding the total error by \(C r^{-6}\). Its first absolute spatial moment is at most \(C r^{-4}\). Thus the same lower bound, with a smaller constant, holds at \(|r\zeta|\le c'\) by \(|e^{i\zeta x}-1|\le|\zeta|\,|x|\). This handles exactly the range in the band-limited lower argument once \(a\) is small. The positive lower Mellin integral can start at \(j=r_0\) and has the same exponents. The uniform upper on every test uses the previous absolute curl convolution estimate. Adding the pseudoscalar throughout the finite lists leaves the selection unchanged in form. The central-time hidden link contributes zero to every central-time linearized electric clover, and the future hidden link never enters a central-time magnetic clover. Hence the mixed quadratic coefficient of the complete spatial sum of \(\sum_i\operatorname{Tr}(E_iB_i)\) has magnitude \(1/16\) times the color inner product. The two-link Hessian is again positive. The exterior routing, moment comparison, second-chaos bound and conditional-variance sum prove (1) for this Euclidean contact with \(\alpha\) replaced by \(|z_P|\) times its fixed convention constant. This last argument concerns the square in the original path measure; the graph assertion above uses separate time slabs and the correct adjoint.

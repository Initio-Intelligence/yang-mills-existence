### Lemma C8A.2 — the required covariance inequality

For the actual law and functions in the indicated Sobolev spaces,
\[
 |\operatorname{Cov}_{\nu}(F,G)|
 \le\sum_{i,j}\mathsf G_{ij}
       \|\nabla_iF\|_{L^2(\nu)}
       \|\nabla_jG\|_{L^2(\nu)},\qquad
 \mathsf G_{ij}=\frac2{\lambda_0}e^{-\gamma\rho(i,j)}.
 \tag{C8A.12}
\]
For localized products one also has the weighted scalar bilinear estimate used in Section 7 below.

**Proof.** First approximate each literal ball from inside by smooth symmetric convex bodies, with indicators converging almost everywhere, and use their product. The product can have corners; its faces belong to distinct coordinate blocks. The scalar Neumann form is
\[
 \mathfrak l(u,u)=\int|\nabla u|^2\,d\nu,\qquad
 L=-\Delta+\nabla V\cdot\nabla .
\]
On a product of smooth factors, the differentiated form on vectors \(w\) tangent to each own-block face is
\[
\begin{split}
 \mathfrak a(w,w)={}&
 \int\sum_\alpha|\partial_\alpha w|^2\,d\nu
 +\int\langle w,V''w\rangle\,d\nu\\
 &+\sum_i\int_{\partial D_i\times D_{\ne i}}
       \mathrm{II}_i(w_{i,\tan},w_{i,\tan})\,d\nu_{\partial i}.
\end{split}
 \tag{C8A.13}
\]
The last term is nonnegative. This is C07's exact facewise Neumann identity. It follows by integration by parts in the factors successively; intersections of different faces add no negative term. Tensor products of smooth functions satisfying the factor Neumann conditions give the initial core; the closed form supplies the solution. Thus one need not pretend that the whole product boundary is a smooth hypersurface.

At a finite cutoff the scalar Neumann problem on the bounded connected product has a solution on mean-zero functions. An initial finite-dimensional Poincaré constant suffices for existence; no uniform interacting spectral gap is assumed at this step. For \(Lu=G-\nu G\), differentiating that form gives the gradient solution
\(w=\nabla u=\mathcal A^{-1}\nabla G\) on each approximation. Scalar integration by parts gives
\(\operatorname{Cov}(F,G)=\langle\nabla F,\mathcal A^{-1}\nabla G\rangle\).
This is the differentiated diffusion operator, not the multiplication matrix \(V''\).

Let \(M_i=e^{\gamma f_i}\), with \(f\) real and 1-Lipschitz in the spatial bond metric. \(M\) is constant in the integration variables and scalar on each Lie-algebra block. It preserves the face tangency constraints. Testing the vector equation with \(M^2w\) leaves a nonnegative differential and boundary part; the conjugated principal matrix costs \(a_K(\gamma)\) and the perturbation costs \(\|A\|_\gamma\). Therefore
\[
 \|M\mathcal A^{-1}M^{-1}\|_{L^2\to L^2}
       \le c_0^{-1}\le2/\lambda_0 .
 \tag{C8A.14}
\]
Choosing \(f_i=\rho(i,j)\) and restricting the two vector endpoints gives the block estimate with kernel \(\mathsf G\). Splitting the two gradients into blocks proves (C8A.12) on every approximating product. The same argument with an anchor weight on each gradient gives the localized scalar bilinear estimate.

At each finite cutoff the actual potential is smooth on a neighborhood of the compact closure. Its density is positive and bounded above and below there, and the approximation normalizers converge. For smooth marks, dominated convergence passes the covariance and weighted gradient norms to the actual product. For general marks, truncate and approximate in the required weighted Sobolev norms. Such density follows, for example, by a small homothety into the convex product followed by mollification; all spatial weights are finite constants at that cutoff. The uniform inequality survives this limit.

Only these scalar inequalities are passed to the literal nonsmooth domain. No assertion that the arbitrary-vector operators themselves converge to a particular boundary-domain realization is required. All constants in the inequalities are independent of the approximation, cutoff and volume. \(\square\)

## 4. Means and all-order coordinate moments

Fix \(0<a<\gamma/4\). The positive scalar kernel satisfies
\[
 \|\mathsf G\|_a\le (2/\lambda_0)S_{\gamma-a}
       =:\mathsf G_a.
\]
By (C8A.10), one final fixed ceiling gives
\[
 \|\mathsf G A\|_a\le\mathsf G_a\|A\|_a\le\tfrac12 .
\]
Define
\[
 \mathsf H=(I-\mathsf G A)^{-1}\mathsf G,\qquad
 \|\mathsf H\|_a\le2\mathsf G_a .
 \tag{C8A.15}
\]
This is a nonnegative numerical comparison kernel, not the interacting covariance or the inverse pointwise Hessian.

### Theorem C8A.3 — the actual retained-force mean and moments

For every \(p\ge1\) and \(0\le s,t\le1\),
\[
 |\nu_{s,t}z_i|
 \le C\{P^{-1}+P e^{-c d_O(i)}\}=:M_i,
\]
\[
 \|z_i-\nu_{s,t}z_i\|_{L^p}\le C\sqrt p,\qquad
 \|z_i\|_{L^p}\le M_i+C\sqrt p .
 \tag{C8A.16}
\]
More precisely, if \(d_{\mathfrak g}\) is the fixed block dimension and
\(\sigma=(d_{\mathfrak g}/\lambda)^{1/2}\),
\[
 \sup_{s,t}|\nu_{s,t}z_i|
 \le\sum_j\mathsf H_{ij}
       \left(|b_j|+d_j+\sigma\sum_lA_{jl}\right).
 \tag{C8A.17}
\]

**Proof.** Unweighted coercivity in (C8A.13) gives variance at most
\(\lambda^{-1}\) for every scalar unit projection of \(z_i\), under every real linear tilt. Thus its centered logarithmic moment-generating function has second derivative at most \(\lambda^{-1}\), and
\[
 \nu e^{t u\cdot(z_i-\nu z_i)}
 \le e^{t^2/(2\lambda)},\qquad |u|=1,\ t\in\mathbb R .
\]
The Chernoff bound and tail integration give the \(C\sqrt p\) estimate, with only the fixed block dimension in its constant.

The law \(\nu_{0,0}\) and the product support are even, so their mean is zero. Differentiating the normalized expectation first in \(t\), at \(s=0\), gives
\(\partial_t\nu_{0,t}z_i=\operatorname{Cov}_{0,t}(z_i,b\cdot z)\).
Equation (C8A.12) bounds its integral by \((\mathsf G|b|)_i\).

Convexity places the segment from zero to \(z\) inside \(D\), and the fixed majorant gives
\[
 |D_jW(z)|\le d_j+\sum_lA_{jl}|z_l|.
\]
Differentiate next in \(s\). The exact derivative is
\(\operatorname{Cov}_{s,t}(z_i,W)\), including the normalizer response. Apply (C8A.12), and use
\(\|z_l\|_2\le|\nu_{s,t}z_l|+\sigma\).
For \(M_i^*=\sup_{s,t}|\nu_{s,t}z_i|\),
\[
 M^*\le\mathsf G|b|+\mathsf Gd
              +\mathsf G A(M^*+\sigma\mathbf1).
\]
All vectors are finite at a finite cutoff. The positive convergent series in (C8A.15) proves (C8A.17), without an extensive force norm.

The nonforce input is bounded by \(C/P\). For the force, take
\(0<c<\min(a,\delta)\) and use
\(d_O(i)\le\rho(i,j)+d_O(j)\). Then
\[
 \sum_j\mathsf H_{ij}e^{-\delta d_O(j)}
 \le e^{-c d_O(i)}\sum_j\mathsf H_{ij}e^{c\rho(i,j)}
 \le2\mathsf G_a e^{-c d_O(i)}.
\]
This proves the mean profile. \(\square\)

The boundary mean may be of order \(P\). If \(O=\varnothing\), it is at most \(C/P\). No lower comparison between the literal cutoff and \(P\), and no rare-collar estimate, has been used.

             \sup_z\|D_iD_j a_{v,e}\|\le Cg^2 .
 \tag{C8A.19}
\]
Here the derivatives are in the integrated \(z\) coordinates at \(t=y+\iota_Iz\). This is the checked RC18.4 chart on the heterogeneous class admitted by C06.

Define
\[
 \mathcal A_e(z)=g^{-1}
      [a_{v,e}(y+\iota_Iz)-a_{v,e}(y)],\qquad U_{\rm ref}=U(y).
\]
For the fixed covariant current-lattice difference stencil \(\nabla_{\rm ref}\),
\[
 \|\mathcal A_e-\nu\mathcal A_e\|_p+
 \|\nabla_{\rm ref}\mathcal A_e
              -\nu\nabla_{\rm ref}\mathcal A_e\|_p
 \le C\sqrt p,
\]
\[
 |\nu\mathcal A_e|+|\nu\nabla_{\rm ref}\mathcal A_e|
 \le C\{P^{-1}+P e^{-c d_O(e)}\}.
 \tag{C8A.20}
\]
Indeed, integrate the first row in (C8A.19) between \(m=\nu z\) and \(z\), then use Minkowski and (C8A.16). For the mean, expand about \(m\): its linear expectation vanishes, while the second row divided by \(g\) bounds the expected quadratic remainder by \(Cg\). The value at \(m\) is an exponential convolution of its mean profile. Since \(g\le C/P\), the same bound follows. A fixed unitary covariant stencil preserves these estimates.

This is a difference of logarithmic chart coordinates, not an identification of noncommuting products with the exponential of their difference. The full field \(g^{-1}a_v(y+\iota_Iz)\) adds its fixed reference value explicitly. A canonically normalized physical potential and its physical derivative acquire the factors \(h_k^{-1}\) and \(h_k^{-2}\), respectively.

The displayed \(\nabla_{\rm ref}\) is exactly the reference-covariant stencil in the stated RC18.45–RC18.46. The same bounds also hold for a fixed finite covariant stencil whose transporters use \(U(z)\). Indeed a finite transporter \(T(z)\) has localized first and second derivatives bounded by \(Cg\) and \(Cg^2\), by (C8A.19) and finite group multiplication. On the actual support \(|\mathcal A|\le CP\), so the product rule for \(T(z)\mathcal A(z)\) has a localized first row bounded by \(C(1+gP)\) and second row by \(Cg(1+gP)\). These are \(C\) and \(Cg\) at the chosen ceiling. Its value at \(z=0\) is zero. The identical expansion about \(\nu z\) therefore proves (C8A.20) for this field-dependent finite stencil as well. No variable-length transport path is included in this assertion.

## 6. Native scalar/tensor values and gradients

For the prescribed finite curvature-bilinear and Wilson scalar/tensor stencils, including their parallel transports, write
\[
 O_{\alpha,x}(z)=h_k^{-4}g^{-2}P_{\alpha,x}(U(y+\iota_Iz)).
 \tag{C8A.21}
\]
Their entire fixed stencil and actual averaging footprint lie in the admitted current collar. These are native fields of the conditional law; no projected microscopic mark is identified with them.

Let \(B_x\) be \(g^{-1}\) times the required reference plaquette-distance sum over that footprint. The retained source regularity and actual uniform-depth averaging theorem BM.20–BM.21 give \(B_x\le CP\). The local application is RC18.49–RC18.49a: put the whole fixed footprint in one axial-gauge cube, use the fine plaquette bound \(C\varepsilon_k\eta^2\), and then the actual \(k\)-fold average with a depth-independent constant. Its output plaquettes are \(C\varepsilon_k\); the finite exterior correction adds \(CgP\). This does not replace the actual average by a one-step surrogate or bound an arbitrary old retained field by \(P\).

Fix a sufficiently small positive \(\theta\), and put
\[
 Z_x=\sum_i e^{-2\theta\rho(x,i)}|z_i|,\quad
 M_x=\sum_i e^{-2\theta\rho(x,i)}M_i,\quad
 Q_x=1+B_x+M_x .
\]
The actual first chart derivative and finite group-product telescoping give
\[
 |O_{\alpha,x}|\le Ch_k^{-4}(B_x+Z_x)^2,
\]
\[
 \left(\sum_i e^{2\theta\rho(x,i)}
                  |D_iO_{\alpha,x}|^2\right)^{1/2}
 \le Ch_k^{-4}(B_x+Z_x).
 \tag{C8A.22}
\]
A differentiated parallel transporter initially contributes
\(Cg(B_x+Z_x)^2\); the real support gives \(B_x+Z_x\le CP\) and \(gP\ll1\), so it fits the displayed bound. Thus all tensor components, not merely the scalar trace, are included.

Minkowski, (C8A.16) and (C8A.22) imply
\[
 \|O_{\alpha,x}\|_p
 \le Ch_k^{-4}(B_x+M_x+\sqrt p)^2,
\]
\[
 \left\|\left(\sum_i e^{2\theta\rho(x,i)}
                  |D_iO_{\alpha,x}|^2\right)^{1/2}\right\|_p
 \le Ch_k^{-4}(B_x+M_x+\sqrt p).
\]
Integrating the first derivative on the segment from \(\nu z\) to \(z\), and using the centered coordinate moments, gives
\[
 \|O_{\alpha,x}-\nu O_{\alpha,x}\|_p
 \le Ch_k^{-4}(Q_x\sqrt p+p).
 \tag{C8A.23}
\]
The explicit envelopes satisfy
\[
 M_x\le C\{P^{-1}+P e^{-c d_O(x)}\},\qquad Q_x\le CP.
\]
They are not absorbed into volume-uniform constants.

## 7. Spatial cumulants with fixed factorial powers

For marks \(F_i\) anchored at \(x_i\), \(m\ge2\), define their actual norms
\[
 B_{i,m}=\|F_i-\nu F_i\|_{2m},\qquad
 D_{i,m}=\left\|
 \left(\sum_b e^{2\theta\rho(b,x_i)}
                         |\nabla_bF_i|^2\right)^{1/2}
                    \right\|_{2m}.
 \tag{C8A.24}
\]
Require these norms to be finite; this is not a regularity theorem for an arbitrary transported mark.

For a nontrivial cut \([m]=E\sqcup F\), the scalar weighted covariance argument of Section 3 gives
\[
 |\kappa_\nu(F_1,\ldots,F_m)|
 \le C c_{m-1}
   \sum_{i\in E,j\in F}e^{-\theta\rho(x_i,x_j)}
       D_{i,m}D_{j,m}\prod_{l\ne i,j}B_{l,m},
\]
\[
 c_n=\sum_{\pi\in\Pi_n}(|\pi|-1)!
       \le n!2^{n-1}.
 \tag{C8A.25}
\]

Here is the proof, retaining the nonsmooth-domain qualification. On each product approximation the weighted vector estimate (C8A.14) implies
\[
 \|W_i^{-1}\mathcal A^{-1}W_j^{-1}\|
       \le C e^{-\theta\rho(x_i,x_j)},\qquad
 (W_i)_b=e^{\theta\rho(b,x_i)}.
\]
Factor it as
\((W_i^{-1}\mathcal A^{-1}W_i)(W_i^{-1}W_j^{-1})\).
The first factor uses the same weighted form estimate; the second is bounded by the triangle inequality. Expand the gradients of two products and apply Hölder with the \(L^{2m}\) norms. This gives a scalar covariance-of-products estimate with one differentiated crossing pair and the remaining \(B\) factors. Pass that scalar estimate to the actual domain as in Section 3.

Center the marks. Subtract from the cumulant partition formula the formula for the product of the two marginal laws across the chosen cut; its mixed cumulant vanishes. Telescope each product of block moments. Each differing block is the covariance just estimated. For a fixed crossing pair \(i,j\), only partitions putting them in that block occur. Merging the pair gives coefficient sum \(c_{m-1}\), with no extra independent block-count factor. Ordered partitions can be encoded by permutations with cuts, proving the bound on \(c_n\). Smooth approximation in the displayed Sobolev norms proves (C8A.25) in its stated domain.

For the native family, (C8A.23) at \(p=2m\) gives
\[
 B_{i,m}\le Ch_k^{-4}Q_{x_i}m,\qquad
 D_{i,m}\le Ch_k^{-4}Q_{x_i}\sqrt m.
\]
Delete a longest edge of a minimum spanning tree of the anchors. Every pair crossing the resulting cut has length at least the deleted edge, which is at least \(\tau/(m-1)\). The cut has at most \(m^2\) pairs. Using \(m^m\le e^m m!\) in (C8A.25) yields
\[
 |\kappa_\nu(O_{\alpha_1,x_1},\ldots,O_{\alpha_m,x_m})|
 \le C^{m+1}(m!)^2 h_k^{-4m}
       \prod_i Q_{x_i}
       e^{-\theta\tau(x_1,\ldots,x_m)/(m-1)}.
 \tag{C8A.26}
\]
Hölder and (C8A.23)'s uncentered bound also give
\[
 \left|\nu\prod_{i=1}^mO_{\alpha_i,x_i}\right|
 \le C^m m!h_k^{-4m}\prod_iQ_{x_i}^2.
 \tag{C8A.27}
\]

The order-dependent spatial range remains summable. Dominate the minimum-tree exponential by the sum over labeled trees and sum leaves successively. Each edge costs at most \(C_\theta(m-1)^4\) in four dimensions, and there are \(m^{m-2}\) trees. Hence
\[
 \sup_{x_1}\sum_{x_2,\ldots,x_m}
 e^{-\theta\tau/(m-1)}
 \le C_\theta^m(m!)^5 .
 \tag{C8A.28}
\]
Combining (C8A.26)–(C8A.28), after division by the displayed \(Q\) envelopes, gives anchored cumulant factorial power seven, uniformly on periodic volumes and admitted subsets. The finite tensor-label count changes only \(C^m\).

## 9. Quantitative quadratic regulators on the actual conditional law

This section strengthens the moment output without introducing a Gaussian comparison law for the field. It uses an auxiliary finite-dimensional Gaussian only to evaluate an exponential identity.

Let \(X=z-\nu z\), and let \(\lambda\ge3\lambda_0/4\) be the unweighted coercivity constant. For every real vector \(u\), apply the scalar covariance theorem to \(u\cdot z\) under all real linear tilts. Its variance is at most \(\lambda^{-1}|u|^2\). Integrating the second derivative of the centered log moment-generating function gives the joint bound

\[
 \nu e^{u\cdot X}\le
               e^{|u|^2/(2\lambda)}.           \tag{C8R.1}
\]

There is no independence assertion between coordinates.

### Theorem C8R.1 — linear and quadratic regulator bounds

For a real finite matrix \(T\), and \(\theta\ge0\) with \(2\theta\|T\|^2<\lambda\),

\[
 \nu e^{\theta|TX|^2}
 \le \det\!\left(I-\frac{2\theta}{\lambda}TT^*\right)^{-1/2}
 \le \exp\!\left[
 \frac{\theta\,\operatorname{Tr}(TT^*)}
       {\lambda-2\theta\|T\|^2}\right].       \tag{C8R.2}
\]

**Proof.** Let \(\xi\) be a standard Gaussian in the range space of \(T\). The elementary Gaussian integral identity gives \(e^{\theta|Tx|^2}=\mathbb E_\xi e^{\sqrt{2\theta}\,\xi\cdot Tx}\). Tonelli and (C8R.1) imply

\[
 \nu e^{\theta|TX|^2}
 \le\mathbb E_\xi
 e^{(\theta/\lambda)\xi^TTT^*\xi}.
\]

Diagonalizing the fixed positive matrix \(TT^*\) evaluates this integral as the displayed determinant. For its eigenvalues \(s_j\), use \(-\log(1-x)\le x/(1-x_*)\) for \(0\le x\le x_*<1\), and sum. This proves (C8R.2). All quantities refer to the same actual conditional expectation. \(\square\)

For an uncentered linear readout, \(\epsilon>0\) gives

\[
 \nu e^{\theta|Tz|^2}
 \le e^{\theta(1+\epsilon^{-1})|T\nu z|^2}
 \det\!\left(I-\frac{2\theta(1+\epsilon)}{\lambda}TT^*\right)^{-1/2},
                                                        \tag{C8R.3}
\]

whenever the matrix is positive. The retained mean cost is explicit. For a projection onto many current coordinates, the trace cost is proportional to their number; it is not a volume-independent constant.

### Corollary C8R.2 — the actual nonlinear current field

Let \(F_e\) be any fixed component or finite tuple of the normalized chart field \(\mathcal A_e\), its reference-covariant gradient, or its actual-field finite covariant gradient from Section 5. Then there are fixed \(\theta_*>0,C<\infty\) such that

\[
 \nu\exp\{\theta_*|F_e-\nu F_e|^2\}\le C.     \tag{C8R.4}
\]

**Proof.** The actual chart derivative estimates give numbers \(a_i\le C e^{-c\rho(e,i)}\) such that

\[
 |F_e(z)-F_e(m)|\le\sum_i a_i|z_i-m_i|,
 \qquad m=\nu z.
\]

Both the segment and its limiting endpoint \(m\) lie in the closure of the convex product, with the same analytic margin. By Cauchy–Schwarz,

\[
 |F_e(z)-F_e(m)|^2
 \le\left(\sum_i a_i\right)\sum_i a_i|X_i|^2.
\]

The first sum, the operator norm of the diagonal quadratic weight, and its trace are uniformly bounded, including the fixed Lie-algebra block dimension. Equation (C8R.2) bounds the resulting exponential uniformly. The second moment also bounds \(|\nu F_e-F_e(m)|^2\) by a fixed constant. Apply \(|u+v|^2\le2|u|^2+2|v|^2\) and reduce \(\theta_*\) once. This proves (C8R.4) for the nonlinear field itself, rather than for a substituted linearization. \(\square\)

Equation (C8A.23) also gives, for a native quadratic observable, a fixed \(c>0\) with

\[
 \nu\exp\!\left\{c\,\frac{h_k^4|O_{\alpha,x}-\nu O_{\alpha,x}|}
                              {Q_x}\right\}\le C.
                                                        \tag{C8R.5}
\]

Indeed its \(p\)-th norm divided by \(h_k^{-4}Q_x\) is at most \(Cp\), since \(Q_x\ge1\). Expand the exponential and use \(p^p/p!\le e^p\) for integer \(p\ge1\). Choosing \(cCe<1\) sums the geometric majorant.

These are finite-chart regulators. A regulator depending on transported microscopic marks, several scales, shifted external configurations or a changing selector requires its own identification and comparison. C8R.2 explicitly records the rank/trace cost and C8R.3 records the mean cost; neither is silently dropped in such an application.

## 10. Same-current exterior response, with its denominator retained

### 10.1 Actual source chart and mixed derivative

CMP119 (3.15), (3.25) gives \(t=(z,y)\), \(q=g_kCt\), \(a=q-hD(q)\). The identities \(QC=0\), \(Qh=I\), \(D(q)=N(a)\) show \(Qa+N(a)=0\) for every admitted \(t\). The old operations and owned raw slots lie in \(Z_k\), whereas the new current chart lies in its shielded \(\Omega_{k+1}\) region. The fixed stencil enlargement is contained in the source collar. Thus these directions do not vary an arbitrary old raw argument; the induced finer-field response is an admitted native response.

The current endpoint calculation preceding the I/O restriction therefore gives, at a smaller fixed rate,

\[
 \begin{gathered}
 A^{IO}_{ia}\ge\sup_{z,y}\|D_{z_i}D_{y_a}W^{\rm pre}(z,y)\|,\\
 \max\!\left\{\sup_i\sum_a e^{c\rho(i,a)}A^{IO}_{ia},
       \sup_a\sum_i e^{c\rho(i,a)}A^{IO}_{ia}\right\}\le CP^{-2}.
 \end{gathered}                                                \tag{C8Y.1}
\]

The two sums are obtained by the entrywise bound and current shell count, with a fixed rate loss. This uses the complete current contour in FP.2–FP.3 and the same disjoint E/R and local sectors; no analyticity of the later smooth cutoff is asserted.

On this fixed active chart the inner support \(D\) is independent of \(y\), and

\[
 H(z,y)=-\tfrac12z^TK_{II}z-z^TK_{IO}y+W^{\rm pre}(z,y),
 \qquad d\nu_y=Z(y)^{-1}\mathbf1_De^{H(z,y)}dz.
                                                        \tag{C8Y.2}
\]

Every factor independent of \(z\) cancels only from this conditional ratio. It retains its derivative in the outer marginal.

### 10.2 Normalized score, signed measure and spatial response

For a unit direction in the retained current block \(a\), put

\[
 S_a=-z^T(K_{IO})_{\cdot a}+D_{y_a}W^{\rm pre},
 \quad \widehat S_a=S_a-\nu_yS_a,
 \quad C_{ia}=\|(K_{IO})_{ia}\|+A^{IO}_{ia}.     \tag{C8Y.3}
\]

The source precision and (C8Y.1) give fixed exponential row and column bounds for \(C\). Direct differentiation of the same fixed-support integral gives

\[
 D_{y_a}\nu_y=\widehat S_a\nu_y,
 \qquad
 D_{y_a}(\nu_yF)=\nu_yD_{y_a}F+
                         \operatorname{Cov}_{\nu_y}(F,S_a).
                                                        \tag{C8Y.4}
\]

Since \(|D_{z_i}S_a|\le C_{ia}\), integration on the segment from \(m=\nu_yz\) to \(z\), Minkowski and the centered coordinate moment bound prove

\[
 \|\widehat S_a\|_{L^p(\nu_y)}\le C\sqrt p,
 \qquad \|D_{y_a}\nu_y\|_{\rm TV}\le C.        \tag{C8Y.5}
\]

The possibly large deterministic score at \(m\) cancels under centering. The principal contribution to the derivative is order one and is not declared small.

Define \(T_{ia}=\sum_j\mathsf G_{ij}C_{ja}\). Weighted row and column multiplication gives \(T_{ia}\le C e^{-c'\rho(i,a)}\) at a fixed smaller rate. The scalar covariance theorem then yields

\[
 |D_{y_a}(\nu_yF)-\nu_yD_{y_a}F|
 \le\sum_i T_{ia}\|D_iF\|_{L^2(\nu_y)},
 \qquad |D_{y_a}\nu_yz_i|\le C e^{-c'\rho(i,a)}.
                                                        \tag{C8Y.6}
\]

For an inner support \(X\), one may sum retained directions with a smaller exponential weight \(e^{c''d(a,X)}\). The result is bounded by \(C\sum_{i\in X}\|D_iF\|_2\), using the weighted row of \(T\). These are actual normalized-kernel currents, not merely bounds on the unnormalized numerator.

The source's real exterior relocation \(y=\mathcal R(a;H)\) has a fixed bounded first derivative on the actual running cutoff. Therefore the ordinary chain rule transfers (C8Y.5)–(C8Y.6) through it, with the same spatial rate and a fixed prefactor. Its Jacobian cancels from the conditional ratio and remains in the outer score. The cutoff is only assumed smooth with a fixed first-derivative bound; no all-order Gevrey bound or complex continuation of that cutoff follows.

### 10.3 The outer and moving-support boundaries remain explicit

For the actual disintegration \(\mu(dy,dz)=\pi(dy)\nu_y(dz)\),

\[
 \operatorname{Cov}_\mu(F,G)
 =\mathbb E_\pi\operatorname{Cov}_{\nu_y}(F,G)
  +\operatorname{Cov}_\pi(\nu_yF,\nu_yG).       \tag{C8Y.7}
\]

Only the first term is controlled by the regular conditional theorem alone. The second requires an estimate for the actual outer law. Its density contains \(Z(y)\), all outer masses and the exterior Jacobian; their derivatives are not removed by (C8Y.4). An arbitrary retained native direction also differentiates the actual precision and force, producing the quadratic and linear score terms in RC.19–RC.21. A changing selector produces a support current, which a fixed-support score cannot represent.

Thus the same-current conclusion above supplies the smooth retained-current portion of the tracked C08-to-M03/M06 interface. It is not a proof of the general selector-support estimate SS, the full physical marked numerator, or the outer-history covariance theorem.

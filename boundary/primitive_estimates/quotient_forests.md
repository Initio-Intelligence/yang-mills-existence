## 1. The exact metric mismatch

CMP119, p. 262, expressly permits a resummation in which a carrier either contains an entire large-field component or misses it, with a weakened decay written as `d_j(X \\ Z_j)`. That assertion is valuable, but its exceptional set is the whole current large-field set `Z_j`. In the repeated completed-component problem an old completed core `H_m`, born at `m<j`, can be a proper subset of a much larger component of the later `Z_j`. Replacing the old core by that larger component is not innocuous: its diameter can be arbitrarily large, and it contains points of grades higher than `m`.

The usable exceptions are therefore the actual surviving completed cores, with their actual physical locations. Their native-field support collars may be stored separately as deterministic decorations. A decoration is not a zero-length edge and is not, solely by its presence in a displayed carrier, a slot of a current normalized operation.

This distinction is also necessary for entropy. One should sum old scalar atoms using their established rooted coefficient norm; one should not enumerate their fixed decorations a second time as arbitrary new polymer cells.

## 2. Three geometric facts available before source transfer

### 2.1 A quotient path cannot erase a newer native grade

For an actual history, CMP119 (2.1), (2.3) gives

`Z_m = Lambda_m^c subset Omega_(m+1)^c`.

Every point of an actual completed core born at `m` consequently has native grade at most `m`. The preparation used in LFR II preserves these low-grade regions, including the borderline retained region `Z_h=Z_h'' subset Z_(h+1)''`, where `h=k-N`.

Consider an old birth-`b` quotient connector which is allowed to move freely only inside actual cores `H_m`, `m<=b`. Fill each zero jump by a path inside its core. No part of this inserted path has grade greater than `b`. A path from the actual old owner to an evaluation point, followed by the outer reconstruction path to `C_1`, therefore pays every grade above `b` either in the charged old exterior connector or in the actual reconstruction distance. This proves the quotient version of the annular bound (H37) of [the history-mass note](history_masses.md). It does not require any halo surface-volume estimate.

### 2.2 Bounded support decorations cost one largest diameter

Let the actual cores satisfy the relative separation used in the CR.1 geometric lemma, and let `D(H)` be fixed support footprints with `diam D(H)<=C diam H`. For a connected quotient graph `G`, charge each traversed core diameter except the largest to its preceding exterior gap. Then

`diam_phys(supp G union all D(H)) <= C [M h_b (ell_b(G)+1) + max_H diam H]`.

Adding the decorations changes the diameter by at most twice their largest radius. This does not claim that a tree visits every decorative cube. It is precisely the weaker diameter conclusion needed for physical current localization.

Making the decorations themselves free is invalid without a new separation theorem. Equal boxes with gaps `epsilon` times their side length have overlapping enlarged collars when the enlargement exceeds `epsilon/2`; an arbitrarily long chain then becomes free. No single largest-diameter remainder can cover that chain.

### 2.3 A logarithmic grade allowance for a fixed decoration

Suppose the determining-region geometry supplies the ordinary scale-separation implication: a point of grade `n` is at distance at least `c M h_n` from the retained large-field set of grade at most `b`, for `n>b+c_0`. If `D(H_b)` stays within distance `C M R_b h_b` of its true core, then every point of the decoration has grade at most

`b + c_1 + ceil(log_L(1+R_b))`.

Indeed `c M h_n <= C M R_b h_b` implies `L^(n-b)<=C'R_b`. In an age exponential, this allowance loses only a fixed power of `(1+R_b)`, not an uncontrolled exponential in `k-b`. The existing AF profile comparison can convert this into a fixed current-profile power and an age polynomial. This fact is useful if the actual source footprint is slightly larger than its true low-grade core. It does not authorize a current coarse cover to retain an obsolete fine birth grade.

## 8. The deep-incidence graph lemma

The following is the exact geometric statement needed by the decorated quotient norm. It uses the endpoints established in Section 7, rather than final halo cells.

Let the current attempted components `H` be disjoint, and choose deep sets `K_H` satisfying (Q1). The actual old completed cores which lie in `H` are contained in `K_H`; every other old completed core is disjoint from `H`. Give old cores zero traversal cost, and charge all other graph edges in current `M`-units. Let `G` be a connected pre-completion graph consisting of old quotient connectors, ordinary localization-domain trees, and any marked reconstruction/history-difference legs. Suppose that for each genuine current incidence `H in I(G)`, the graph visits `K_H`. Suppose also that the atom is not a self atom: for each such `H`, the graph has a terminal outside `H`.

Then

`length_paid(G) >= a' R_k |I(G)|`.                         (Q3)

**Proof.** Fix `H in I(G)` and follow a path in `G` from its deep terminal to a terminal outside `H`. Take the part between the last crossing of the inner boundary of the guard annulus and the first subsequent exit from `H`. Its physical length is at least a fixed fraction of `dist(K_H,H^c)`. No old zero-cost core intersects this annulus: resident old cores lie in `K_H`, and external old cores miss `H`. Thus this whole required displacement is charged. The annuli for distinct current `H` are disjoint, so summing their charged edge lengths does not count an edge twice. Dividing by `M` proves (Q3). Branches, multiple visits, and arbitrarily many old fine cores inside `K_H` do not affect the argument. QED.

There is no hypothesis that the decorative supports are separated. They never enter the zero-cost relation.

The nonself premise is checked before decoration. Terms whose entire pre-completion carrier is contained in one `H` are among the terms absorbed in the source self action on p. 377. A declared saturation footprint cannot convert such a term into a nonself term. For a genuine nonself graph with one incidence, a real exterior carrier/field terminal supplies the second endpoint. With two or more incidences, a deep terminal in another disjoint component supplies it.

## 9. Deterministic completion has no new domain entropy

Keep each pre-completion positive atom until after the rooted sums. Its decoration is deterministic: attach the fixed support footprint of each genuinely used completed component. Do not re-enumerate the decoration as an arbitrary connected set.

Let `D(G)` be the decorated output. Under the AF physical-size comparison all footprints relevant at current scale have diameter at most `C M R_k`. Consequently

`x in D(G)  =>  dist(x,G) <= C M R_k`,                     (Q5)

where old-core vertices may be represented by any one physical point in that core. There are at most `C'(1+R_k)^4` current cells in this neighborhood of a fixed current cell. Therefore, for any nonnegative weights `p_G`,

`sum_(G: x in D(G)) p_G`

` <= sum_(z: dist(z,x)<=C M R_k) sum_(G: z in G) p_G`

` <= C'(1+R_k)^4 sup_z sum_(G: z in G) p_G`.               (Q6)

This is a physical-cell sum. It does not count historical tags. If arbitrarily many tiny old cores lie in one cell, their decorations do not create additional terms in the first sum over `z`; each atom is counted once by a deterministic tie-breaking choice of a witnessing cell. The old fine-cell density inside the second sum is the actual `L^{4(k-b)}` density already paid by the scalar age lemma.

For a fixed component root `H`, an endpoint in the actual `Lambda_H` lies in the source's fixed `100M` cube, giving a fixed `O(100^4)` cell factor. A general history-disagreement endpoint lies in `K_H`, which can be larger; use the bound `O((1+R_k)^4)` in that case. The guard (Q4) pays this polynomial as well. No uniform `100M` bound is asserted for the entire changed old-history region.

The factor in (Q6) is absorbed by any remaining share of (Q4): for fixed `p`, `(1+R)^p exp[-cR]` tends to zero. Thus completion itself need not have a norm smaller than one; it has a fixed polynomial loss which is paid by the proved deep-incidence guard. This is the concrete weaker alternative to paying every final halo cube by an FH-type full-tree exponent.

The remaining analytic verification is now more narrowly located: the source-preserving record refinement must supply the positive `P(G)` bound simultaneously with every genuine deep endpoint, including the old/new structural pairs and the inherited completed scalar records. ### 16.9 A geometric one-time budget for the linear transport

A factor `M_*` per fine historical raw slot is **not used** in the final proof. A resummed ordinary `B^beta` can retain many finer slots, and its coarse full-tree bound alone does not provide that mark budget. The following geometric payment avoids that assumption.

Fix one genuine creation event of an inherited relative scalar, and retain its raw-slot creation label `b` and its original small-tail budget. `ell_in^(r)-ell_out^(r) >= c sum_(P crossed) R_r`.        (Q31g)

The released input connector weight pays the bounded operator norm, new component-tag weight, the fixed support-root polynomial and a fixed physical-diameter reserve for each such component. Its bounded operator/root factor is reserved once in the initial small tail; the preparation-window profile comparison makes the required polynomial uniform. A fixed spare part of that tail pays the largest possible new component diameter. The reserve is not renewed at subsequent attempts.

Why can the same old paid segment not be charged repeatedly? Along a surviving lineage, two direct partial components at different times cannot overlap. Whole-component nesting would make the later one contain the earlier failed component. Its failure clock would force the later lower endpoint `h'` to be at least the earlier global attempt, hence at least `b`; it could not directly integrate any slot of grade below `b`. At a successful consumption the old lineage ends. Any new outside dependency generated by the kernel is already separated as the new guarded response Q31f. Thus the crossed collars assigned to a fixed creation lineage are disjoint, and (Q31g) is a cumulative, one-time payment.

This argument also gives the necessary label convention. The new protected footprint may have geometric/port label `r`, because all its points have grade at most `r`. Its original small-tail generation and its raw-slot label remain attached to the old creation event. One must not use an age-`b` assertion through a newly free `P_r` when `r>b`. Nor is the transported coefficient added again as a new forcing `f_r`. The global induction sums the original reserved creation budgets and separately tracks the current geometric/native labels used for age and root transport.

### 16.10 Original active-block ownership gives the norm's integration reserve

There is a sharper finite mark construction which **does** apply to an inherited full-tree atom with many fine old slots. Mark its original active source blocks, not the fine coordinates. ## 17. The positive forest calculation after the new-record estimate

This section gives the combinatorial calculation explicitly. It does not manufacture a factorial for localization histories, and it does not infer a sum of analytic suprema from a signed scalar estimate. ### 17.1 Actual vertices, weights and primitive load

Work first at finite cutoff and volume. A primitive record `e` has a finite set `U_e` of actual incidence vertices: paid native cells or blocks, genuinely used fixed component slots, and any actual covariance/walk contact needed for factorization. A whole old scalar stays one atom. Its fixed component decorations are metadata. Purely decorative overlap of two external native supports is not an integration edge: the corresponding functions commute pointwise, and disjoint normalized integrations still factor. If a new localizer joins two pieces, its physical joining cost is the paid cost in Section 14.

Use a weight

`w(e)=exp[nu n(e)+a q(e)+lambda ell(e)]`,

with fixed positive floors for `nu,a,lambda`. Here `n` counts the actual admitted cells/blocks used in the record, with old blocks kept in their old grading, and `q` counts the fixed component vertices. The output weight is submultiplicative on unions by (Q9). For a new source interaction `v_e`, let

`z_e = w(e) M_comp^(|I_e|) ||exp(v_e)-1||_D`

`    <= w(e) M_comp^(|I_e|) ||v_e||_D exp(||v_e||_D)`.     (Q32)

`M_comp` bounds the literal canonical completed operation. Its factor is assigned once per actually used slot; replacing `M_comp^(|union I_e|)` by `prod_e M_comp^(|I_e|)` is an upper bound. For the auxiliary prefix use `M_pref` in the same finite-slot combinatorics, with its linear factor paid by Q31h. Include `M_pref^(u(e))` in the carried activity weight. The centering factor of Q12, fixed bridge costs, and fixed analytic block constants are included in `z_e` before root summation.

After reserving a part of the **new** volume weights and a summable per-step part of the inherited count weights for the subsequent forest sums, the needed primitive estimate is

`sup_x sum_(e: x in U_e) z_e exp[4s |U_e|] <= delta`,     (Q33)

uniformly for each kind of root `x`. This is a sum of the individual `D`-supremum norms. In a repeated history, `s=s_r` is **not a fixed loss charged anew to old counts**. Choose, for example, `s_r=c sigma_r`, with `sigma_r=C poly(ell_r) epsilon_r^(1/72)`, and decrease the auxiliary old volume/tag weights by a fixed multiple of `sigma_r` at the global step. The positive rate floors remain fixed because `sum sigma_r=o(1)`. The eventual fresh loads are `O(poly(ell_r) epsilon_r^(1/18))=o(s_r)`, so the criterion below holds. The slower `1/72` reserve also leaves room for the quadratic recent-input estimate in Section 17.6. New localizer volumes have their independent fixed large reserve. The raw set cardinality in (Q33) counts actual block/slot vertices only; it does not count every point in a deterministic halo as a separate free incidence.

For physical current roots, first keep the literal old density `L^{4(k-b)}` and sum it with the restricted scalar age row, choosing `Gamma>4 log L+lambda_age`. For a source prefix localizer whose grade is finer than an exterior incoming block, retain that whole coarse input block and the fine new pieces separately. It is not assigned a fictitious finer birth port. Resident inputs have old births at most `h` and therefore at most every preparatory localization grade. The old and new graded row measures are used before taking the current-root sum. The deterministic support completion then costs only Q6's polynomial in `R_k`.

### 17.1.1 The mixed-prefix root count uses the actual strip

Here is the explicit count for the seemingly dangerous prefix case `b>j`. For a fixed admitted history and attempted component `P`, let `S_j(P)` be the actual native integration/forcing support at stage `j`. The eligible ancestral rectangles give

`C D_j A_b (1+j-b)^p exp[-(Gamma-4 log L)(j-b)]`.         (Q33a)

The `4 log L` is the literal fine-root density. Summing `b<=j` is geometric when `Gamma>4 log L` with the stated fixed slack.

If `b>j`, a genuine directly used strip point is outside every protected old footprint; it is a paid active-support point. ### 17.2 A rooted tree majorant with its actual counting factors

Let `b_e>=0` and let two primitive records be adjacent if their actual incidence sets meet. Suppose

`sup_x sum_(e: x in U_e) b_e exp[2s |U_e|] <= delta<=s`.  (Q34)

The sum of the weights of all finite connected primitive families with union containing `x`, with the exact unordered-family convention, is at most `delta` after a harmless fixed enlargement of `b_e` and reduction of `s`. Here is the precise usual majorant. Root each connected family at one of its members incident to `x`, and dominate its connected overlap graph by the sum over spanning trees. Enlarge the sum to allow repeated child labels. The rooted-tree functions are bounded by the monotone solution of

`T(e)=b_e exp[ sum_(e' adjacent e) T(e') ]`.               (Q35)

To verify convergence, start with `T_0(e)=b_e` and iterate. If `T_n(e)<=b_e exp[s |U_e|]`, then

`sum_(e' adjacent e) T_n(e')`

` <= sum_(x in U_e) sum_(e': x in U_e') b_e' exp[s|U_e'|]`

` <= delta |U_e| <= s |U_e|`.

Thus `T_(n+1)(e)<=b_e exp[s|U_e|]`, and the iteration converges. Summing roots incident to a fixed `x` gives at most `delta` by (Q34). The extra `s` in (Q34) pays the output union's cardinality weight.

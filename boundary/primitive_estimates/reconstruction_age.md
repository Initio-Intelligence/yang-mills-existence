# Age dependence of the outer reconstruction

The bounds involving an actual Wilson boundary derivative require the same chart, conditional law and configuration of fixed earlier innovations as the derivative being estimated. In particular, the all-history application requires the [anchored composed-response estimate](../../continuum/partial_gauge_transport.md); the [two-age estimate](../ordinary_oscillation.md) concerns the identical subtracted quantity at both endpoints. Exact finite-dimensional identities do not depend on these two application bounds.

This note identifies a concrete response channel in the printed maps that is not explicit in the CR.22 proof. It proves the algebraic channel and a conditional geometric implication. The complete all-history ownership certificate must accompany an application.

## 1. The exact first map

LFR II, printed p.373, (1.61), reconstructs the original field occurring in an action term \(E(X,U_k^0)\) by
\[
U_k^0=\mathcal R_1(D_1;\eta_1),
\qquad
\mathcal R_1=U(\mathfrak B''_k(Y_1),\cdot),
\tag{A1}
\]
where \(Y_1\) contains the original carrier \(X\) and every attempted component of \(Z\) intersecting it. The localized determining set is
\[
\mathfrak B''_k(Y_1)=\mathfrak B(Y_1)\cup\mathfrak B''_k.
\]
It agrees with \(\mathfrak B''_k\) on \(Y_1^0\); its disagreement set
\[
C_1=Y_1\setminus Y_1^0
\tag{A2}
\]
is a boundary layer of width at most \(2M_1\) at the corresponding native scale.

The varied boundary datum \(D_1\) is the first entry in (1.61): the product involving \(u_k^0\), \(\exp i\widetilde Q(\eta H''_k)\), and \(M'(U_1)\), restricted to \(C_1\). The remaining data \(\eta_1\) are the displayed restrictions of \(V''\) and \(M'(U_0^{(AL)})\) to the interior pieces. For the three selective localization operations under discussion, those interior data are held fixed.

In particular, the gauge \(u_k^0\) is **inside the determining input** of \(\mathcal R_1\). It is not a gauge factor acting on the final output field. A variation of that gauge on \(C_1\) still passes through the determining-data derivative
\[
K_1(x,y)=D_{D_1(y)}\mathcal R_1(x).
\tag{A3}
\]

### Proposition A1 (retained outer derivative)

In the localizations of (1.61)–(1.64), every nonconstant response of \(E(X,U_k^0)\) to one of the three selective field localizations contains the outer determining-data response \(K_1\) from a point of \(X\) to \(C_1\). Specifically:

| First nonempty localization | Response channel |
|---|---|
| \(Y'_2\ne\varnothing\) | \(DE\,K_1\,D D_1[\Delta H''_k]\) |
| \(Y'_2=\varnothing,\ Y'_3\ne\varnothing\) | \(DE\,K_1\,(D M'\,K_2)\,D D_2[\Delta H_{\mathfrak B_1}]\) |
| \(Y'_2=Y'_3=\varnothing,\ Y'_4\ne\varnothing\) | \(DE\,K_1\,(D M'\,K_2)\,(D M'\,K_3)\,D D_3[\Delta H_k]\) |

Each row denotes the exact chain-rule channels, with the displayed boundary gauge and averaging factors included in the corresponding datum derivative; it is not a replacement of those factors by identities. If an earlier localized field has a nonzero unperturbed value, its derivative with respect to later native fields adds channels, but all still enter the first reconstruction only through \(C_1\).

**Proof.** All dependence on each selective localization parameter enters \(\mathcal R_1\) through its boundary datum \(D_1\). This is explicit in (1.61). Apply the chain rule to \(E\circ\mathcal R_1\). For the later localizations apply the chain rule again to \(U_1\) and \(U_{k,\Lambda}\) using (1.62) and (1.63). Setting an earlier localization increment to zero does not remove the earlier reconstruction map. The other interior variables in (1.61) are held fixed under these selective interpolation parameters. Their subsequent substitution by functions of the final native \((U,J)\) changes the final argument family, but does not remove a factor already generated by a nonempty localization difference. \(\square\)

For a finite difference, integrate the first derivative along a native interpolation. To preserve the spatial decay in higher localization coefficients, establish the first-variation estimate uniformly on the admitted localization polydisc and then use the same localization/Cauchy construction as the source. One must not differentiate only a scalar supremum bound after discarding \(K_1\).

## 4. The specific geometric certificate needed for a resident old tag

Let \(H_b\) be a resident completed tag born at \(b\le h\). A sufficient certificate consists of the following facts about the actual history:

1. Its raw output remains retained, so a chosen exterior halo port has native grade at most \(b+c_0\), with fixed \(c_0\).
2. The original hole and its halo have not been replaced by a promoted current component in the old record's metric.
3. If the tag has been consumed, the whole old factor is assigned to the new exact component integral/output before another birth-root estimate; it is not carried as an untouched old scalar.
4. The first reconstruction agrees with the final prepared history until its outer layer \(C_1\). Since \(Y_1\) contains the attempted component, every path from the resident tag to \(C_1\) reaches the terminal prepared annulus. Localizing the determining set can remove only a fixed number of native collars near the final boundary.

Under these facts, (A7) gives the full age \(k-b-O(1)\), not merely \(h-b\). There is no need to assign the running collar \(R_k\) to the recent one-cell prepared layers.

If the original carrier \(X\) already reaches \(C_1\), then \(d(X,C_1)\) can be small. In that case use the original full connecting tree from the birth port to the reconstruction output point \(x\in X\), followed by the \(K_1\) path. For each crossed grade above \(b\), either:

- the crossing occurs on the old tree, where its physical collar is at least \(M h_{\rm grade}\ge M h_b\), so it has a fixed cost in birth-scale tree units; or
- the crossing occurs on the native reconstruction path, where (A7) supplies \(M/M_1\).

Earlier surviving holes of birth at most \(b\) cannot create a free positive-grade jump to \(k\) if their own determining endpoints remain at their actual old grade. Interior attachment ports need their explicit paid connectors, such as those provided by the full-halo invariant; they cannot be replaced by a zero-cost distance to a set.

This yields a sufficient joint-cost inequality of the form
\[
c_1\beta\kappa\,t_b^{\mathcal H}(X)
+c_2\delta\,d_{\mathfrak B''_k(Y_1)}(X,C_1)
+\operatorname{paid\ port\ cost}
\ge \Gamma(k-b)-C,
\tag{A8}
\]
for some fixed rate \(\Gamma\) that can be chosen above the root-entropy threshold if the stated geometric certificate is valid with the actual source constants.

Equation (A8) is a geometric implication from the certificate. It must not be cited as an unconditional printed bound for all inherited scalar records.

Two further bookkeeping restrictions are indispensable. The free holes are the actual bounded completed tags; an arbitrary unreplaced ordinary large-field component retains its original full-tree cost. Also a reconstructed old first term can keep its old birth label only if its actual raw/boundary anchor survives. If that anchor is consumed, the source partition of the action must put its complete contribution into the fresh component integral/difference before a new rooted norm is assigned. A changed label without this exact reassignment proves no estimate.

## 5. The exact remaining count after that certificate

At a fixed current root, birth-\(b\) positions have density at most \(C L^{4(k-b)}\). If the remaining native-radius and preparation factors are bounded by \(C(1+k-b)^A\), the contribution is summable provided the reserved rate obeys
\[
\Gamma>4\log L.
\]
Indeed
\[
\sum_{b\le k}
L^{4(k-b)}(1+k-b)^A e^{-\Gamma(k-b)}
\le\sum_{a\ge0}(1+a)^A
e^{-(\Gamma-4\log L)a}<\infty.
\tag{A9}
\]
If the precise map conversion leaves an additional fixed exponential scale factor, its rate must be included in this threshold rather than called polynomial.

The mixed-mesh coarsening lemma by itself does not prove (A9): a tiny old carrier can fit in one current cell, making its coarsened tree cost zero. The retained \(K_1\) channel and the actual birth-grade certificate are what can supply the missing age factor in that case. The algebraic channel is present for all \(i_0=2,3,4\), not just for the first localization.

## 7. Scalar near/far lemma with an absolute input row

### 7.1 Precise setting and the near branch

Fix the actual history, the real older arguments, and one incoming scalar record \(E_{b,X}\) with its original scalar birth \(b\). At a fresh attempted component let \(h=k-N\). Its actual resident tag has a birth \(m\le h\) and \(m\le b\), as in the ownership certificate. **The scalar birth \(b\) need not be at most \(h\)**: an older resident tag can enter a newer scalar. Write \(X^+\) for a fixed scalar-birth-scale collar containing every old-field basepoint, retained raw endpoint, nodal interpolation ball, and normalized-gauge dependency block used by this scalar. Every surviving slot used by this birth-\(b\) scalar has its actual mesh at most a fixed multiple of \(h_b\). The collar therefore has fixed physical thickness \(C h_b\); it is not enlarged by the number of old fields or the number of later preparation steps.

Use the actual outer strip where the localized and prepared determining histories can disagree, enlarged by those same fixed local dependency neighborhoods. If \(X^+\) reaches this strip, the old full connector from the record's retained port to \(X^+\) already reaches the outer current component collar. The geometric proof of Section 13 of `Conditioning, deletion and mass factors for the retained history` pays all grades from \(b\) to \(k-O(1)\) with a reserved part of that connector. Appending or deleting the fixed birth-scale collar changes its cost by a fixed constant. One simply uses
\[
 |E_{b,X}(s)-E_{b,X}(s_0)|\le 2\|E_{b,X}\|.
 \tag{A10}
\]
No Landau-to-axial tangent estimate at a refined boundary output is used in this branch. The same observation covers the all-empty localization branch whenever the term is nonself: its carrier must itself reach the relevant exterior. The terms wholly self-contained in the attempted component are assigned to the full self action and its component operation, as in the source decomposition.

### 7.2 The interior absolute row

In the other branch, \(X^+\) is contained in the region where the localized history agrees with the prepared history. All output weights and terminal blocks used on \(X^+\) are consequently the actual prepared ones. Preparation inside the attempted component coarsens the older determining history. If the original scalar norm at an output point uses grade \(i\) and its prepared output grade is \(n\), then \(n\ge i-O(1)\). Comparing a field, gradient or current row of physical order \(p=1,2,3\) gives the factor
\[
 (h_i/h_n)^p=L^{-p(n-i)}
 \tag{A11}
\]
up to the fixed endpoint allowance. It gives no adverse fine-mesh factor. The full connector pays any part of the age crossed before the selected output point; \(K_1\) pays the remaining prepared collars from that point to \(C_1\).

Use CMP102 (172), which is an **unweighted** determining-bond condition. On the common smaller absolute chart let \(B_1\) denote the logarithmic determining datum relative to a fixed real reference, and let \(v=\dot B_1\) be supported on \(C_1\). There is no claim that \(v/\alpha_s\) is bounded at a newly refined boundary grade. Use the source's actual interpolation with its **full admitted localization disks**. The analytic estimates for those datum maps and their interpolation derivatives give \(\|v\|_\infty\le C_{\rm loc}\varepsilon_{\rm abs}\). All fixed source disk factors, including the \(e^{16\kappa_1}\)-type factors in the chosen localization estimates, belong in \(C_{\rm loc}\). The native/core reserve is chosen after these constants; no localization disk is shrunk or enlarged by this argument.

CMP102 (190), including the native determining-data measure, gives on \(X^+\)
\[
 h_n^p|D_{B_1}\mathcal U_{{\rm L},p}v|
 \le C\|v\|_\infty
     \int_{C_1}e^{-\delta d(x,y)}\,d\nu(y),
 \qquad p=1,2,3,
 \tag{A12}
\]
where \(\delta\) is a reserved positive share of \(\delta_0/8\). The physical-current row includes the local chart and Wilson-remainder terms in addition to the displayed curl row; these are controlled by the value/gradient rows and the admitted background bounds, exactly as in AD17.25–AD17.27. Here \(d\nu\) is the paired native measure: the displayed \(h_s^{-4}\) kernel density is canceled by its actual \(h_s^4\) determining-cell measure. It is not a counting-matrix estimate over every fine bond. The source row-sum bound, after reserving another share of \(\delta\), changes the right side to \(C\|v\|_\infty e^{-\delta' d(x,C_1)}\).

The AF comparison permits
\[
 \alpha_i^{-1}\le C g_k^{-1}(1+k-i)^a
 \tag{A13}
\]
with one fixed exponent \(a\). Combining (A11)–(A13) yields the actual strong scalar-output row, with at most one fixed coupling loss \(g_k^{-1}\). The polynomial in the grade difference is paid by a reserved share of the joint connector/native-distance decay. This is where the stronger assertion in the initial version of Section 13.8 is weakened: it is unnecessary to prove a coupling-independent normalized boundary row or a compatible coarse trace for every fine datum.

### 7.3 The actual inverse gauge also applies on a nonzero corrected fiber

There is a useful exact extension of the stated zero-fiber version of RM.3. CMP102 Proposition 2, printed p.281, treats the constraint
\[

 \tag{A14}
\]
for general admitted \(B\), and identifies its Landau configuration with the raw-data axial configuration using the normalization \(\overline{R_0u}^{\,j}=1\). The blockwise construction RM.26–RM.36 does not use \(B=0\): its logarithmic means, hierarchical transports, normalized root factor and derivative estimates depend on \(U_0,A\), and impose precisely that same normalization. Thus the same proof gives, on every actual terminal block,
\[
 |u^{-1}D u|(x)
 \le C h_n\sup_{\Delta_n(x)}|D A_{\rm L}|.
 \tag{A15}
\]
The reference can remain fixed when the determining data vary. There is no extra term from a moving right inverse for the constraint, and no direct datum response bypasses the Landau row.

For real determining data, \(\mathcal R_1\) in (1.61) is the literal source minimum \(U(\mathfrak B''_k(Y_1),D)\). In the interior branch all terminal blocks and their fixed transport dependency neighborhoods used in (A15) lie in the agreement region. Their scaled diameters are bounded, so (A15) preserves the endpoint exponential in (A12).

### 7.4 Which older slots survive at this scalar stage

The needed node statement is about the scalar entering (1.61), after the preliminary integrations, not every hidden variable in an earlier representation.

For \(j\ge h\), the original adjoint fluctuation has support in \(\Omega_{j+1}\cap Z_{j+1}\). LFR I (1.10)–(1.11) puts \(Z''_{j+1}\) outside \(\Omega_{j+1}\) on the attempted component. Therefore this entire old fluctuation support lies in the preparation strip \(Z_{j+1}\setminus Z''_{j+1}\). LFR I (1.22) onward integrates its \(A_j,V_j\) variables, and (1.71) is the result after all \(N\) integrations. The variables left in the recent layers are raw variables on the actual determining pieces \(\Gamma''_j\), as in (1.72), (1.76). A fine old \(A_j\) has not survived there with an artificially coarser terminal grade.

For the deeper explicit adjoint slots, preparation leaves levels \(\le h\) unchanged. The borderline slot \(A_{h-1}\) is supported in \(\Omega_h\cap Z_h\); the retained deep region satisfies \(Z_h=Z''_h\subset Z''_{h+1}\), so that support lies outside \(\Omega''_{h+1}\). Its terminal grade is \(h\), with the fixed bond-star allowance. Earlier old adjoint slots retain the AD17.19–AD17.19c grading. Outside the attempted component the original history is unchanged. The surviving raw slots use the one-sided CR.8 relation on their actual determining grids. Merging repeated endpoints, the same separation and bounded-overlap proof therefore applies to the scalar's entire surviving node list.

After a completed component's exact scalar expansion, its hidden variables have already been integrated and are not included in this list. Adding them again as frozen old slots would change the object being estimated and would reintroduce the distinct pointwise-kernel problem.

## 8. Complete scalar integration versus arbitrary operator marks

For the action problem one may first perform the full normalized component integrations and their scalar cluster expansion on the published native \((U,J)\) chart. LFR II (1.103)–(1.104), pp.391–392, does exactly this. Its \(B'^{(k)}(X)\) is a scalar activity; the full boundary exponential and every moving normalizer occur inside its defining convergent expansion. Its remaining old arguments are the surviving raw/adjoint fields. The source states that this representation can be used in the induction; the simultaneous real scalar gauge covariance is part of that source invariant, as in CMP119 (2.41).

One can consequently apply the scalar nodal argument and native/current Cauchy to this completed scalar. Its derivatives include the derivatives of every normalized component factor by definition. No separate claim that a pointwise kernel can be gauge-pulled through an arbitrary bounded test function is needed for this application. Such a claim would be stronger and is relevant to independently marked posterior quantities, not supplied by the scalar source invariant. The completion of the full C-05 target still requires FH, or a quantitatively sufficient weaker positive-majorant pushforward, and the unsubtracted pure-native rooted estimate. Those remain open; the scalar lemma above does not replace them or change the status of C-05.

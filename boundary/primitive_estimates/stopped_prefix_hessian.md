# Stopped ordinary prefixes and the heterogeneous Hessian

The bounds involving an actual Wilson boundary derivative require the same chart, conditional law and configuration of fixed earlier innovations as the derivative being estimated. In particular, the all-history application requires the [anchored composed-response estimate](../../continuum/partial_gauge_transport.md); the [two-age estimate](../ordinary_oscillation.md) concerns the identical subtracted quantity at both endpoints. Exact finite-dimensional identities do not depend on these two application bounds.

**Summary.** The ordinary one-step, bulk old-birth estimate and a remote-walk estimate can be identified at the level of the integrand. The available equations leave a gap for the complete stopped-prefix action on the joint contour. Below are two sufficient tests with proofs. The first permits cancellation within the ordinary density. The second tests the real Hessian of the actual marginal using its hidden-variable scores. Neither test is established here for every history required by C-05/C-06. C-05 and the actual-model application of C-06 remain open.

times the summable path and row factors of (1.7). The unchanged monomials cancel. Restricting the list of multiplied cubes to the exterior of a larger excluded region can only decrease \(m\). ## A scalar substitute for [ND.14](native_domain_count.md) in the common-reference step

Keep the full fixed-support ordinary density \(w_\zeta(a)\) of [ND.6](native_domain_count.md), including every discrete label in the support. Assume a positive real reference \(w_0\), finite mass, and nonzero holomorphic weights on that support. Choose a reference tuple \(a_*\) with those properties. Define
\[
 B_\zeta(a)=\log\frac{w_\zeta(a)w_0(a_*)}{w_0(a)w_\zeta(a_*)},
 \qquad B_0(a)=0 .
\]
The simply connected parameter disk fixes the branch. Every external-only term and every parameter-independent raw action cancels algebraically. In particular raw Gaussian energies of arbitrary magnitude at the center disappear. If \(\sup_{\zeta,a}|B_\zeta(a)|\le d<\pi/2\) on the desired chart, the normalized measure obeys
\[
 |p_\zeta(a)|\le e^{2d}\sec(d)\,p_0(a).
\]
Indeed \(p_\zeta/p_0=e^{B_\zeta}/\mathbb E_0 e^{B_\zeta}\). The real part of the denominator is at least \(e^{-d}\cos d\). This includes its parameter dependence and supplies [ND.26](native_domain_count.md) for arbitrary bounded measurable insertions by Tonelli. Cancellation is permitted inside the ordinary log-density. The positive forest weights still require their own spatial estimates.

At unchanged reference localizers suppose \(\sup_{++,a}|B_z(a)|\le K_r\) on the external disk. On its homothetic subdisk of ratio \(q_r\), Schwarz's lemma on each radial line gives \(|B_z(a)|\le q_rK_r\). If changing all remote localizers from the reference values to their full disk values costs at most \(\delta_r\) in this same cross-difference on the working chart, take \(d=q_rK_r+\delta_r\). Thus \(K_r=C(Q_2)\ell_r^{D_0}\) with \(D_0\) fixed before the enlargement, \(q_r\le C\ell_r^{D_{\rm map}-Q_2}\), and \(\delta_r=o(1)\) suffice for a comparison constant tending to one when \(Q_2>D_0+D_{\rm map}\). The comparison must include pairs of tuples in different internal labels if the ordinary law sums those labels. Bounding variations only within each label permits an arbitrary relative change of their masses.

The integrated output alone cannot supply this test. Let \(a\) be an angle with normalized Haar measure and \(w_z(a)=\exp[M\cos(a-z)]\). Its integral is a positive constant independent of complex \(z\), by periodicity for real shifts and holomorphy. At \(z=it\ne0\), \(p_{it}(0)/p_0(0)=\exp[M(\cosh t-1)]\). Continuity yields arbitrarily large essential domination ratios as \(M\) increases. All real input weights are positive and each integrand is entire and nonzero. This tests the inference from an integrated scalar. For the Wilson law, one sufficient step would be to anchor the stages of [ND.8](native_domain_count.md) at \(a_*\), insert the old bank with its own marginal subtraction at each birth and bound the resulting four evaluations on the same enlarged disk. ## A real marginal test for the C-06 coercivity application

At a real current-unit free-bond point \(x\) on an admitted fixed-support chart, let \(I(x)=\int e^{-S(x,a)}\,d\nu(a)>0\) be an exact hidden-variable factor of the physical law, with all \(x\)-dependent Jacobians incorporated in \(S\) and \(d\nu\) fixed. Assume real \(S\) and dominated first and second derivatives on the chart. This includes a finite sum over discrete histories. Put \(\mu_x=e^{-S}d\nu/I\), \(s_u=\partial_u S\), \(t_{uv}=\partial_{uv} S\). Differentiating the same normalizer twice gives
\[
 \partial_u[-\log I]=\mu_x s_u,\qquad
 \partial_{uv}[-\log I]=\mu_x t_{uv}
 -\tfrac12(\mu_x\otimes\mu_x)
 [(s_u(a)-s_u(a'))(s_v(a)-s_v(a'))].
\]
The replicas are independent whole hidden tuples. Their internal components can be correlated. For a single hidden factor take its reference matrix \(T^0\), including zero if the entire factor is a boundary perturbation. With \(\Delta s_u=s_u(a)-s_u(a')\), a sufficient real weighted Hessian error bound is \(A_\gamma+B_\gamma\), where
\[
 A_\gamma=\sup_u\sum_v e^{\gamma\rho(u,v)}|\mu_xt_{uv}-T^0_{uv}|,\qquad
 B_\gamma=\tfrac12\sup_u(\mu_x\otimes\mu_x)
 \left[|\Delta s_u|\sum_v e^{\gamma\rho(u,v)}|\Delta s_v|\right].
\]
For symmetric \(T^0\) and distance, symmetry gives the column estimate. One can instead keep the absolute value outside each replica expectation for a weaker sufficient test. If several masses occur in a ratio, apply the identity to each actual signed logarithm, or first combine the exact factors and subtract the already budgeted terms once. A real score bound follows from the first identity after the same reference-force subtraction. An affine exterior force has zero Hessian.

If the sum of the resulting boundary errors is below the budget of HC.28 and the other accepted bounds hold, the perturbation argument gives HC.29–31 on the heterogeneous admitted class. This real spectral test involves two derivatives at the physical law. The full C-05/C-06 statement also requires native-domain and derivative estimates. A convexity lower bound on the hidden microscopic action in the visible directions alone leaves the conditional score covariance uncontrolled. For example \(I(x)=\tfrac12\sum_{a=\pm1}\exp[-\lambda x^2/2+axK]\) has \(-(\log I)''(0)=\lambda-K^2\). A score constant in the hidden variables contributes zero covariance, even if it is large. The model input is the weighted fluctuating score and the remaining averaged curvature through the retained and failed components, with their proper distance. No all-history estimate of that pair is available from the matched one-step inputs.

# Primitive interactions with shared integration slots

The bounds involving an actual Wilson boundary derivative require the same chart, conditional law and configuration of fixed earlier innovations as the derivative being estimated. In particular, the all-history application requires the [anchored composed-response estimate](../../continuum/partial_gauge_transport.md); the [two-age estimate](../ordinary_oscillation.md) concerns the identical subtracted quantity at both endpoints. Exact finite-dimensional identities do not depend on these two application bounds.

The shared-slot part of the two forest sums admits a direct estimate on the corrected complete-atom norm. The unverified part is narrower. It includes the reverse spatial root of the first newly exposed dependence of a normalized prefix kernel on its exterior, after all earlier ordinary substitutions. Below, a bound on a single propagated covariance kernel is proved at both roots. A different, many-support bound is required for that prefix response. Neither C-05 nor C-06 is completed by these calculations.

## Shared integration slots

Fix a finite admitted history and its exterior real tuple. Write $I_e$ for the actual independent integration sites or component-operation slots of an interaction, and $U_e$ for all its incidence and readout roots. Thus $I_e\subset U_e$, after identifying each integration slot with its root. In different grades these are labeled vertices. An overlap of readout footprints alone does not couple two integrations. Take a product reference of normalized component kernels at fixed external data, each dominated on the analytic chart by $M_i\mu_i$ for a **common probability** $\mu_i$. In particular each $\mu_i$ is independent of the chart point and the other integrated slots. This argument applies after the actual product decomposition. CMP116 (2.5)–(2.10), pp. 12–14, conditions and transforms the correlated Gaussian before factorization. Whole-prefix domination for the actual ordinary tilt is an additional application hypothesis.

Let $b_e(a)$ bound the analytic supremum of the Mayer factor at fixed complete real tuple $a$, multiplied by its output weight and its allocated $M_i$ factors. Take only terms with actual integration incidences. For $0<s\le1$, assume
\[
 \sup_{a,x}\sum_{e:x\in U_e} b_e(a)e^{2s|I_e|}\le\delta\le s.
 \tag{1}
\]
When measures combine, the factor $\prod_{i\in\cup I_e}M_i$ is at most the product of per-interaction factors for $M_i\ge1$. Each direct old incidence spends its own original-block mark under ND.49. For repeated input occurrences the product retains the sum of the mark counts. When the shared slot is a retained component, its actual core can join the incident physical graphs. A successful component disappears and requires the separate restored-interior volume budget of LFR II (1.89), (1.94)–(1.99). Distinct exterior ports in a decorative footprint still require a physical join. Weight submultiplicativity below requires those joins to be paid first.

Expand the product of $1+f_e$. Group each nonempty set of selected factors by the connected components of the graph $I_e\cap I_f\ne\varnothing$. The integrations of disjoint components factor exactly when all integrated dependencies have been included. The remaining gas has hard incompatibility through shared integration sites. Write $p_H$ for the activity of a connected family $H$ and $U_H=\bigcup_{e\in H}U_e$, $I_H=\bigcup_{e\in H}I_e$. Then
\[
 \sum_{H:x\in U_H}w_H\|p_H\|e^{s|I_H|}
 \le \int\sum_{H:x\in U_H}\prod_{e\in H}
          b_e(a)e^{s|I_e|}\,d\mu(a)\le\delta ,
 \qquad \mu=\bigotimes_i\mu_i .
 \tag{2}
\]
For the last inequality choose a root interaction containing $x$, and dominate connected sets by rooted spanning trees. At fixed $a$ set $c_e=b_e(a)e^{s|I_e|}$. Starting with $c_e$, iterate $T_e=c_e\exp(\sum_{f:I_f\cap I_e\ne\varnothing}T_f)$. If $T_f\le b_f(a)e^{2s|I_f|}$, the child sum is bounded by $\delta |I_e|$, so the induction holds. The unordered-child factorial counts distinct children correctly. Allowing repetitions gives a positive majorant. The tree with a specified root bounds each connected set with that root. A function depending on a subset of the real coordinates has the same integral against the full probability product, which justifies the single integral in (2). In particular no supremum over each interaction's raw data was moved inside the catalog sum.

The hard-core coefficient has absolute value bounded by the number of spanning trees of its incompatibility graph (the usual alternating connected-graph sum, obtained by partitioning connected graphs into intervals indexed by spanning trees). For the logarithm repeat the tree induction with $c_H=w_H\|p_H\|$ and target $c_He^{s|I_H|}$. Equation (2) supplies its child sum. The final rooted norm is at most $\delta$. Empty-slot analytic factors instead pass directly through the logarithm as scalars. The proof applies both at genuine component roots and at physical readout roots. It needs (1) at both. The exponential reserve in (1) counts only the slots that can create incompatibility. A physical readout root needs a rooted sum but adds no tree exponent merely by belonging to a decorative footprint. If a particular source gas imposes additional hard exclusions, include their labels in the compatibility count before applying this improvement. The change addresses tree entropy only. It gives no free path between distinct readout points. No additional smallness hypothesis on every *pair* of old atoms sharing an actual integration slot is required once their individual transferred activities obey (1).

There is also a quantitative bound for the nonlinear sector. Scale each of the marked recent interactions by $z$ at the activity level. If their contribution to (1) is $K$, the other contribution is at most $s/2$, and $0<K<s/4$, take $R=s/(2K)>2$. On $|z|=R$ the two tree sums give norm at most $s$. Cauchy and the geometric sum give at most $s/[R(R-1)]\le8K^2/s$ for the Taylor degrees at least two at $z=1$. If the mark is instead inserted *before* the Mayer exponential, the same argument needs a bound on the marked exponential on that disk. For a primitive exponent $v$, $|e^{zv}-1|\le |z|\,|v|e^{R|v|}$; a rooted bound $K$ for the positive linearized exponents with weights at least one implies $R|v|\le s/2$, so a fixed reduction of $R$ absorbs this extra factor. This covers ordinary products over a common integration slot, with the original single-atom linear transport subtracted.

## Opening one new spatial dependence

A useful way to test (1) is to leave the attachment port labeled. For one insertion on an old complete atom $G$, let $v$ range over its actual ports. Let $J(v,W)$ be the positive bound for a new localized piece on $W$, including the Cauchy-radius division, new-volume reserve and output-connector cost already spent on that piece. Mark a readout root $x$ on the new piece. For each fixed raw tuple, positivity gives
\[
 \sum_{G,v,W:x\in W}a_G {\bf1}_{v\in G}J(v,W)
 \le A\sum_{v,W:x\in W}J(v,W),\qquad
 A=\sup_v\sum_{G:v\in G}a_G .
 \tag{3}
\]
Multiple occurrences of the same port require its actual multiplicity in this formula. A bounded multiplicity is a fixed factor. A large native footprint must be counted at its own birth grade before using $A$, as in ND.42–ND.47. Conversely, if the root is old, the sum over first insertions is bounded by the weighted old-root sum with factor $\sum_{v\in G}\sum_W J(v,W)$. The latter is paid from a decrement on the actual attachment count, or the connector release of (16) for exposed old cells. Further unordered Taylor attachments with nonnegative patch intensity $u_v<1/2$ contribute at most $\prod_v(1-u_v)^{-1}\le e^{2\sum_vu_v}$, by (18). If the distinguished new piece is chosen inside an ordered sequence of occurrences at one patch, summation over its possible positions replaces one geometric series by $(1-u_v)^{-2}$, costing at most a factor of two relative to the original series. Thus a marked new root needs the **reverse** sum in (3), with the same paid geometry as the forward estimate.

For a concrete physical subclass take the current-grid propagator in CMP99, Theorem 3.15, (3.185)–(3.187). Suppose a single insertion has the bound $D|C^{(k)}(v,y)|$, with a real guard $d(v,y)\ge r$ in that mesh, and charge the output endpoint length at rate $0<\lambda<\delta_0$. Equation (3.187) gives, in either direction,
\[
 \sup_v\sum_{y:d(v,y)\ge r}D e^{\lambda d(v,y)}|C^{(k)}(v,y)|
 \le C_{\delta_0-\lambda} D e^{-(\delta_0-\lambda)r/2}.
 \tag{4}
\]
Use the four-dimensional shell bound $C(1+n)^3$, also valid uniformly in the torus size and on subsets. The reversed bound holds by the same pointwise estimate. All distances in (4) are in the metric of the source bound (or in an equivalent lattice metric after reducing its rate). Two serial endpoint kernels compose with the product of the two weighted sum bounds using the triangle inequality. This gives a volume-uniform summable guard payment when $r\ge cR_k$ and $D$ has a fixed inverse-coupling and logarithmic prefactor. This calculation treats the whole endpoint kernel as one coefficient where its remaining arguments are fixed. It supplies no localization-support norm for its dependence on those arguments. [Bałaban, CMP 99, 389–434](https://doi.org/10.1007/BF01240355)

The missing case already occurs for an exterior density response of one normalized partial operation. In assembly §6.6 and (27) the insertion is the complete $V_{\rm hi}$ in a covariance. Writing $V_{\rm hi}=\sum_W V_{\rm hi,W}$, with the corresponding exact real-slot and analytic domains fixed, reduces its *physical-root* bound to (3) for $J(v,W)$ built from the **absolute individual** $V_{\rm hi,W}$ and their source-to-root joins. The integrated endpoint is on a proper strip and the marked new root can be anywhere on $W$, including an internal localization cube. A bound only at the integrated endpoint, including an exponentially small unweighted total variation of the two normalized laws, does not bound (3). For example a positive matrix $J(v,\{x_0\})=\epsilon$ for $n$ different $v$ has forward sum $\epsilon$ and reverse sum $n\epsilon$. The physical application can rule out that matrix by proving decay on each actual connecting support and paying the mixed-grade root conversion. The matrix only tests what follows from a forward total.

CMP116 (1.10)–(1.14), pp. 4–6, controls localized propagators and an analytic nonlinear map on the source disks. Its (2.8)–(2.10), pp. 13–14, constructs the localized Gaussian. LFR II (1.67)–(1.69), pp. 376–377, bounds an individually **resummed** source output on its carrier. Assembly §5.1 already applies that bound to the ordinary forcing atom. To apply it to (3) one would need to identify the inserted $V_{\rm hi,W}$ as those outputs, or perform the proper-mesh estimate on the composed pre-integration coefficients. Their actual source and output grids may differ, and opposite-root counting can cost $L^{4(j-b)}$ for an unresolved grade difference $j-b$. Assembly §6.7 specifies an age payment $e^{-\Gamma(j-b)}$ with $\Gamma>4\log L$. The endpoint proof (4) does not establish that payment for a substituted historical prefix. Installing an intersecting native decoration as a zero-length connector would change the metric of the frozen test.

## Marking a physical cube after source resummation

There is a direct two-root consequence of LFR II (1.67)–(1.69). Take two native perturbations supported in individual mesh cubes $i,j$ outside $Z$, in a bidisc of admitted local native radius $r$. Fix the independent external arguments of the printed $V(Y)$, and use only directions whose action on this printed function is local in its stated domain $Y$ (enlarge by the fixed stencil if needed). A mixed derivative vanishes unless $Y$ meets both supports. For such $Y$, its outside tree intersects both exterior cubes and $d_{k,Z}(Y)\ge M^{-1}\operatorname{dist}(i,j)-C$. Cauchy applied directly to the one resummed source atom yields
\[
 |\partial_i\partial_j V(Y)|\le \alpha r^{-2}
                 e^{-(1+2\beta)\kappa d_{k,Z}(Y)} .
\]
An equivalent finite difference follows by integrating the derivative on an inner bidisc. One can split the exponent to retain pair decay and a support-enumeration reserve. A self atom (1.69) can contribute when both directions meet its support, and needs its own size estimate. This identifies exactly the nonself, direct-native derivative to which marking after resummation applies. In particular, one should not claim that differentiation of a finite resummation itself fails to commute.

The first extra operation in applying that estimate to an arbitrary earlier marked insertion is normalization against a **moving law**. For two real external-cube settings, let $\Pi_t$ be the normalized prefix operation, $r_1=w_1/w_0$ the unnormalized density ratio on common support, and $F_t$ the marked old block after substitution. Direct division gives
\[
 \Pi_1F_1-\Pi_0F_0
 =\Pi_1(F_1-F_0)
   +\frac{\operatorname{Cov}_{\Pi_0}(r_1,F_0)}{\Pi_0 r_1}.
\]
The second term is the commutator of differencing the external cube with applying the fixed-center normalized operation. Both source masses, even if each mass is known exactly on the native chart, determine $\Pi_0 r_1$ but do not determine this covariance. A minimal algebraic test uses a mean-zero variable $A=\pm1$ with probabilities $1/2$. Set $w_t=1+tA$, $|t|<1/2$, and $F=A$. Then $\int w_t=1$ identically, while $\Pi_tF=t$. With a second source $h$, $\int w_t(1+hA)=1+ht$, whose mixed derivative is one. The marks $t,h$ can formally be labeled by cubes at any separation. One cannot obtain a uniform distance factor from the unmarked mass. This calculation tests the inference and does not model a Wilson kernel. If both marks genuinely live on the same native family controlled by (1.68), the first calculation above applies instead.

Thus the proposed exact-real-partition-function method would bypass Q43 for the Hessian if the mixed response with both physical insertions were bounded on the same Wilson family. The printed source estimate controls the displayed nonself $V(Y)$ after fixing the old arguments. In the partial-prefix application $F$ may be an independent marked complete old block and the source representation provides no (1.68) estimate on a disk for its mark $h$ through the entire ordinary tilted prefix. The covariance in the displayed identity is the first missing term even when the differentiated test has no explicit native response. Its reverse-root sum or an independent spatial mixing bound on those same normalized kernels is needed. LFR II p. 376 specifies the resummation over $Y'_j,X$ and grades, p. 377 states the arguments and bounds of $V(Y)$, and pp. 378–380 discuss the completed component operation. Assembly §6.2 distinguishes its auxiliary ordinary partial-prefix reference from this canonical completed operation.

## Testing a direct C-08 argument

Common positive domination by itself does give moment comparisons for an already controlled reference marginal. If $d\pi/d\pi_0\le M$, then $\|X-c\|_{L^p(\pi)}\le M^{1/p}\|X-c\|_{L^p(\pi_0)}$ for real $p\ge1$; centering at the new mean costs at most another factor of two. The actual whole-prefix common measure is an *ordinary tilted* measure, while the requested C-08 assertion concerns the marginal of the entire C-01 regular law. Moreover even a uniformly positive bounded analytic density relative to a product law can retain a nondecaying two-point cumulant.

For an explicit test take a symmetric nondegenerate probability on $[-1,1]$, use its product at all sites and choose distinct sites $i,j$. Multiply its density by $1+\epsilon X_iX_j$, $0<\epsilon<1/4$. Its normalizer is exactly one, its real density ratio is between $1-\epsilon$ and $1+\epsilon$, and the ratio and its logarithm are bounded on a fixed complex polydisc about the support (choose, for example, a disc of radius 3/2 centered at zero). All coordinate means vanish, all their moments are bounded by one, and
\[
 {\rm Cov}_\pi(X_i,X_j)=\epsilon(\mathbb E_0 X_i^2)^2 .
\]
The distance $d(i,j)$ is arbitrary. This test is independent of Wilson dynamics. It identifies the missing input even in order two for a route using only regular support, local moment bounds and scalar normalization. One could replace the forest bounds with spatial control on real conditional influences throughout the actual marginal, but none of the scalar comparisons above establishes such control for the remote prefix term.

# Two attachments to one normalized component: the missing physical decay

The bounds involving an actual Wilson boundary derivative require the same chart, conditional law and configuration of fixed earlier innovations as the derivative being estimated. In particular, the all-history application requires the [anchored composed-response estimate](../../continuum/partial_gauge_transport.md); the [two-age estimate](../ordinary_oscillation.md) concerns the identical subtracted quantity at both endpoints. Exact finite-dimensional identities do not depend on these two application bounds.

**Status:** a proved counterexample to an abstract implication, followed by a proved collar repair. This is not an admitted Wilson-history counterexample and does not disprove the intended C-05 estimate.

## 1. The implication tested

The following conditions, by themselves, do not give a uniform Hessian row weighted by a fixed positive exponential of physical separation:

- a component diameter of order \(R(g)=(\log g^{-2})^r\), with \(r>1\);
- uniformly small individual interaction amplitudes and small unweighted component incidence;
- a positive normalized internal integration operator with uniform norm one;
- a bound for each input activity using a connected tree required to visit only that activity's exterior cubes.

The missing issue is the connection generated by integration between **different** exterior attachments to the same component. The example does not remove any length from an input tree that already has to connect both endpoints. Rather, the two endpoints begin in two separate input activities.

## 2. Common metric and carriers

Use the ordinary nearest-neighbor graph metric \(\rho\) on the unit lattice \(\mathbb Z^4\). All lengths in this note are in these same units. A large finite box containing the construction gives the same example; there is no change of mesh or hidden volume normalization.

Fix \(r>1\), and take \(0<g<\pi/8\). Let

\[
\ell_g=\log g^{-2},\qquad
R_g=\ell_g^r,\qquad
n_g=\left\lceil R_g/4\right\rceil.
\]

Let \(H_g\) be the rectangular union of unit cubes corresponding to the lattice sites

\[
\{0,1,\ldots,n_g\}^4.
\]

Its lattice-site diameter is \(4n_g\), hence lies between \(R_g\) and \(R_g+4\). Choose exterior sites

\[
x_g=(-1,0,0,0),\qquad
y_g=(n_g+1,0,0,0),
\]

and let \(Q_x,Q_y\) be their exterior unit cubes, attached to the opposite first-coordinate faces of the rectangular component. Then

\[
\rho(x_g,y_g)=n_g+2\ge R_g/4.
\tag{T1}
\]

The two input carriers are

\[
X_x=H_g\cup Q_x,
\qquad X_y=H_g\cup Q_y.
\tag{T2}
\]

Each is connected and contains the **whole** internal component. Each contains only its own prescribed exterior cube. To avoid irrelevant boundary conventions, one may represent unit cubes as half-open cells when assigning fields and take their closures when measuring continuous tree lengths. All estimates change by at most a fixed additive constant under another consistent unit-cell convention.

## 3. The normalized operator and the input activities

The internal variable is a single fair spin \(\sigma\in\{-1,1\}\), assigned to the component. Define

\[
\Pi f=\tfrac12 f(1)+\tfrac12 f(-1).
\tag{T3}
\]

The operator is positive, normalized, independent of current parameters, and has norm one on bounded complex functions. The component law is permitted to correlate its interior perfectly; no internal mixing assumption is imposed in the abstract implication being tested.

On the common current polydisc \(|z_x|,|z_y|<1\), define

\[
V_x(z,\sigma)=g z_x\sigma,
\qquad
V_y(z,\sigma)=g z_y\sigma.
\tag{T4}
\]

Both functions are entire and localized to their declared native carriers. Their sup norms are at most \(g\). There is one integrated component, touched by two interactions, so their total unweighted component load is \(2g\), tending to zero.

For comparison with the unweighted condition (MC4) of [the multi-component theorem](connected_component_expansion.md), \(M=1\), \(|I_x|=|I_y|=1\), and that condition becomes \(4eg\le1\). It therefore holds for all sufficiently small \(g\). The corresponding unweighted current-root load is also of order \(g\).

For an individual relative connected-tree cost, a tree in \(X_x\) is required to visit only the exterior cube \(Q_x\). It need not visit every cube of \(H_g\). Such a tree has length bounded by an absolute constant independently of \(R_g\); allowing a one-point tree makes the infimum zero. The same is true for \(X_y\).

If the chosen convention instead imposes a fixed positive upper bound \(c_0\) on those minimal costs, replace \(g\) in (T4) by \(g e^{-\kappa c_0}\). Then both activities obey an input bound of the form \(g e^{-\kappa d(X_e)}\), while the argument below is multiplied only by a fixed nonzero constant. Thus the phenomenon does not depend on a zero-length-tree convention.

## 4. Exact reduced scalar and divergent physical weighted response

Direct summation gives

\[
Z_g(z_x,z_y)=\Pi e^{V_x+V_y}
=\cosh(g(z_x+z_y)).
\tag{T5}
\]

On the stated polydisc,
\(|g(z_x+z_y)|<2g<\pi/4\). The zeros of \(\cosh w\) have modulus at least \(\pi/2\), so \(Z_g\) has a holomorphic logarithm on this polydisc, with the branch fixed by \(F_g(0,0)=0\):

\[
F_g(z_x,z_y)=\log\cosh(g(z_x+z_y)).
\tag{T6}
\]

This can also be checked without a zero classification: for sufficiently small \(g\), the elementary estimate
\(|Z_g-1|\le e^{2g}-1<1\) supplies the logarithm by its power series. Either argument gives a uniform common analytic neighborhood.

Differentiating (T6),

\[
\partial_x\partial_y F_g(0,0)=g^2.
\tag{T7}
\]

For every fixed \(\chi>0\), the physical weighted row at root \(x_g\) is therefore at least

\[
\begin{aligned}
\sum_j e^{\chi\rho(x_g,j)}
|\partial_x\partial_jF_g(0)|
&\ge g^2e^{\chi\rho(x_g,y_g)}\\
&\ge\exp\left(-\ell_g+\frac\chi4\ell_g^r\right)
\longrightarrow\infty.
\end{aligned}
\tag{T8}
\]

The final limit follows because \(r>1\). A fixed multiplicative factor from the alternate convention in Section 3 does not change it.

Thus even a diameter bounded by the usual superlogarithmic scale, small unweighted component incidence, uniformly bounded normalized kernels, and well-controlled analytic logarithms do not imply the required fixed-exponent physical weighted Hessian estimate.

## 5. Why this does not contradict a connected-tree input bound

The output cumulant joining the two attachments has carrier

\[
X_{xy}=H_g\cup Q_x\cup Q_y.
\]

A connected tree in this output carrier required to visit **both** exterior cubes has length at least their separation, up to a fixed cell-diameter correction. Its length is consequently of order \(R_g\). That length would be charged by the full connected relative-tree definition.

Neither input activity had this two-endpoint carrier. The covariance of their common internal spin creates the connection:

\[
\operatorname{Cov}_\Pi(gz_x\sigma,gz_y\sigma)=g^2z_xz_y.
\]

The example therefore proves that a theorem preserving the stronger connected output tree norm across normalized component integration needs an additional estimate. It does **not** show that the initial source norm fails to charge a tree path it actually requires.

This distinction matters for LFR II (1.67). Its connected relative tree charges its full length, including interior crossings. The size expression used later on a possibly disconnected exterior set must be checked separately. Neither expression should be silently replaced by another metric.

## 6. A genuine collar estimate repairs this example

Suppose instead that two active current roots reach the component through corridors of length \(R\), and the actual endpoint bounds supply a factor \(e^{-\kappa R}\) on **each** interaction. Assume the component diameter is at most \(C_DR\) and the total physical span of the two roots is at most

\[
(C_D+2)R.
\tag{T9}
\]

For the same fair-spin law, let

\[
V_x=g e^{-\kappa R} z_x\sigma,
\qquad
V_y=g e^{-\kappa R} z_y\sigma.
\]

The mixed Hessian is now \(g^2e^{-2\kappa R}\). Its physical weighted contribution is at most

\[
g^2\exp\{[\chi(C_D+2)-2\kappa]R\}.
\tag{T10}
\]

Hence the explicit condition

\[
\chi(C_D+2)<2\kappa
\tag{T11}
\]

makes it small. Fixed polynomial prefactors in \(R\) and fixed inverse powers of \(g\) can also be absorbed when \(R\asymp(\log g^{-2})^r\), \(r>1\), and (T11) has a fixed positive margin.

The two factors in (T10) must come from actual current-to-component decay, or another proved estimate. If the roots are placed behind long exterior corridors, assigning the old amplitude \(g\) while retaining an input connected-tree bound would omit the cost of those corridors. The original obstruction deliberately uses boundary attachments of fixed width and does not make that omission.

## 7. Source scope and conclusion

The toy law is a finite positive probability model. Its component size is of the same **order** as an eligible completion scale, but that fact is only one numerical condition. The example does not verify, or claim to verify, the construction's determining sets, histories, preparation conditions, active current collars, or native-to-current map. It is therefore **not a Wilson-history counterexample**.

What it establishes is the logical need for an extra input at the post-component stage: a current collar with quantitative decay, a component-sensitive physical extent norm, or another estimate controlling correlations transmitted across the component. The source-free kernel norm and component diameter alone cannot replace that input.

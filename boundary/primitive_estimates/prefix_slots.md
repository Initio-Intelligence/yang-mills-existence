# Slot lineage in the ordered integration

The bounds involving an actual Wilson boundary derivative require the same chart, conditional law and configuration of fixed earlier innovations as the derivative being estimated. In particular, the all-history application requires the [anchored composed-response estimate](../../continuum/partial_gauge_transport.md); the [two-age estimate](../ordinary_oscillation.md) concerns the identical subtracted quantity at both endpoints. Exact finite-dimensional identities do not depend on these two application bounds.

This note analyses the argument lists needed to apply a bounded partial normalized operation without accumulating a constant at every later preparation. The phase estimate for the ordinary-tilted kernel is handled separately in Complex radius reserve for the phase cone. A pure Gaussian bound alone is not substituted for that estimate.

## 1. Three labels must remain distinct

For a record retain:

* the births of its actual protected geometric cores;
* its old scalar/transport label \(b\), and the ceiling \(b-1\) on its explicitly occurring old adjoint coordinates when that ceiling is used;
* the current attempt \(r\) and each genuinely new localization grade \(m\).

A core born at \(a\le b\) may occur in a scalar created later. A scalar localized during an attempt \(r\) need not live on the \(r\)-grid. A recent exterior \(b\)-atom may have \(b>m\). The old atom stays whole, while the new \(m\)-pieces retain their native measures. For a mixed native-root count its effective old/new transport label is \(\max(b,m)\), after separating roots on the old block and on new pieces. No \(L^{4(b-m)}\) enumeration of copied old atoms is permitted.

## 2. What the source argument lists actually imply

CMP119 (2.23) writes the effective action as a function of the background \(U_k(V)\) and a separate independent fluctuation system \(A\). Its \(B\)-terms have the arguments displayed in (2.40)–(2.42). The background is determined by the retained raw \(V\)-data, not by declaring the separately retained \(A_j\) to be determining data.

The ordinary transformation, CMP119 (3.15) and (3.25)–(3.28), inserts the newly introduced fluctuation through the old-background argument. Subtracting the zero-new-fluctuation reference leaves the old scalar with its old explicit \(A\)-arguments; the nonzero response belongs to the new correction. An explicit new \(A_{b-1}\) can first occur in the new scale-\(b\) correction. This can be recorded as an exact annotation before the later resummation. It is not inferred just from the name of a final signed coefficient.

LFR I (1.60) preserves the distinction:

\[
 F\!\left(
 U_k^{(n)}
   \big(e^{i[g_jC B_j-h\widetilde D(g_jCB_j)]}V^{(j)}\big),
 A\right)
 -
 F(U_k^{(n+1)},A_j=0).
\tag{SL1}
\]

The displayed \(B_j\) enters through the native background map. The directly occurring \(A_j\) is integrated as an independent real coordinate and is explicitly removed in the reference. The successful prefix correction (1.63) is then a scalar of \(U_k^{(n+1)}\), its selected independent-current extension, and the prescribed older variables. Its historical localization parameters have been integrated or put at their endpoints; the source does not retain them as additional arbitrary raw variables.

For a **pure** conditional quadratic/coarea operation through stage \(t\), the coefficients depend on the native pair. They do not introduce a new independent \(A_l\) of grade \(l>t\). Thus, if the incoming record has no explicit old slot with grade at least \(b\), pure integration through its last directly integrated slot \(j_*\le b-1\) preserves that ceiling. The remaining dependence on later raw determining data is represented by the actual new native background. Old completed-kernel raw arguments remain separately stored on their exact footprints; the actual halo-exclusion/deep-footprint lemma is required before claiming that none of them lies in the integrated strip.

The pure statement is not automatically true for an **ordinary-tilted** operation.

## 3. The higher-slot issue in an ordinary tilt

An ordinary interaction may depend jointly on an integrated \(A_j\) and an external \(A_l\), \(l\ge b\). Even if \(F\) has no such \(A_l\)-argument,

\[
 \frac{\int e^{V(A_j,A_l)}F(A_j)\,d\mu(A_j)}
      {\int e^{V(A_j,A_l)}\,d\mu(A_j)}
\tag{SL2}
\]

can depend on \(A_l\). Therefore the claim that normalization only eliminates slots and never creates a higher explicit slot is false for a general tilted operation. The moving denominator does not cancel this dependence.

There is a source-based way to classify it. Remove from the density terms independent of all integrated coordinates; they cancel in the normalized quotient and remain in their external action position. In each remaining ordinary term, fix every higher external real \(A_l\), \(l\ge b\), at one admissible real reference value. The reference may be chosen in its actual cutoff support; setting it to zero is unnecessary. Denote the resulting interaction by \(V_{\rm lo}\), and the exact finite difference by \(V_{\rm hi}\):

\[
 V_{\rm ord}=V_{\rm ext}+V_{\rm lo}+V_{\rm hi}.
\tag{SL3}
\]

This operation needs no \(A_l\) derivative or complex \(A_l\) extension. The low part carries no new higher slot. Every nonzero high part records its actual higher slot and its existing source carrier.

The split must also preserve component factorization. An ordinary term containing integration slots in two different simultaneously attempted components is not included in either single-component normalized reference. It remains a marked connecting interaction. The native pair may be held fixed as a common external parameter, but another component's actual integrated raw/adjoint variable is not such a parameter. The original full-tree connector, or its own-scale port plus the new map connector, pays these cross-component terms by the same guard argument below.

If an ordinary term sees the integrated variable only through its native background, first remove the integration-independent native reference. The relevant expression is the exact double difference

\[
\begin{split}
 W_\gamma={}&
 G_\gamma(\Phi(B),A_{\rm hi})
 -G_\gamma(\Phi(0),A_{\rm hi})\\
 &-G_\gamma(\Phi(B),A_{\rm hi}^*)
 +G_\gamma(\Phi(0),A_{\rm hi}^*).
\end{split}
\tag{SL4}
\]

It is a nonempty native-map response with a real higher-slot mark. It is not treated as a freely transmitted old reference.

## 4. The guard is measured at the carrier's own scale

Suppose an original ordinary full-tree term \(B^{(\beta)}(X)\) carries an explicit \(A_l\), \(l\ge b\), and couples to an integrated \(A_j\), \(j\le b-1\). Its creation list gives \(\beta\ge l+1\), while the source support theorem gives

\[
 X\cap\Omega_\beta\ne\varnothing,\qquad
 A_j\hbox{ is supported in }Z_{j+1}\subset Z_{\beta-1}.
\tag{SL5}
\]

The ordinary construction of \(\Omega_\beta\), CMP119 (3.2)–(3.5), places a proper-scale collar between \(Z_{\beta-1}\) and \(\Omega_\beta\). Consequently

\[
 d_\beta(X)\ge cR_\beta-C.
\tag{SL6}
\]

This is the required source-metric statement. It would be incorrect to take only an \(l\)-scale buffer and call it \(R_l\) units of a much coarser \(\beta\)-carrier.

For (SL4), keep the exact localized background-response record. If the old carrier reaches the integrated region, its own full tree crosses the \(\beta\)-collar. Otherwise the retained response path from the integrated region to the carrier crosses that collar. Reserving parts of the old tree and the normalized native endpoint kernel gives the same exponential gain. This is the ordinary near/far attachment argument, with its actual input measure; no real old-\(A_l\) derivative is introduced.

For a pure prefix forcing term of localization grade \(m\), LFR I (1.63) gives

\[
 X\subset\Omega_{m+1}^c,\qquad X\cap\Omega_m\ne\varnothing .
\tag{SL7}
\]

The unintegrated higher \(A_l\) lies on the unchanged higher layer \(\Omega_{l+1}\). With the literal independent-bond convention (SL7) forces \(m\ge l+1\). Fixed crossing-bond or dependency collars can produce only a bounded adjacent exception. In that exception the direct distance from \(Z_{j+1}\) to the \(A_l\)-support is measured at a scale within a fixed number of grades of \(m\), so it still gives \(cR_m-C\). In the main case the \(m\)-port in (SL7) and the inclusion of the old strip in \(Z_{m-1}\) give

\[
 d_m(X)\ge cR_m-C .
\tag{SL8}
\]

This argument applies to the **ordinary full-tree** sector. An inherited already completed relative scalar is not promoted to (SL7)–(SL8); it remains one marked incoming atom and uses its own positive record norm.

All the new grades \(\beta,m,l+1\) in this higher-slot branch lie in the current preparation window \(h<\beta,m,l+1\le r\), since \(j\ge h\). Their profiles are polynomially comparable to \(R_r,P_r,\ell_r\). Rooting at a genuine integrated native coordinate and then summing the actual source tree/map rows gives a polynomial number of stage/grade choices. The integration-coordinate count is the native rectangular-history count in (P10) of A phase cone for a partially integrated prefix. Thus the strong source bound for these higher-slot records has the form

\[
 \|V_{\rm hi}\|_{\rm positive,rooted}
 \le Cg_r^{-a}\ell_r^D e^{-cR_r}.
\tag{SL9}
\]

There is no \(L^{4N_r}\) count: a coarse old input is not refined into new independent fine-grid inputs. If a carrier is rooted at a newer \(m\)-piece, its intermediate-coordinate sum uses the actual native row; if rooted at the old \(b\)-block, use that block's original rooted norm.

## 5. Exact response to the higher-slot marks

Assume the ordinary-tilted phase estimate has been established uniformly with
\(V_{\rm lo}+tV_{\rm hi}\), \(0\le t\le1\), and on the required common native/localization chart. Write \(\Pi_t\) for that exact normalized operation and assume \(\|\Pi_t\|\le M\), with fixed \(M\). Then

\[
 \Pi_1F-\Pi_0F
  =\int_0^1\left[
       \Pi_t(FV_{\rm hi})-\Pi_tF\,\Pi_tV_{\rm hi}
             \right]dt ,
\tag{SL10}
\]

and hence

\[
 |\Pi_1F-\Pi_0F|
 \le (M+M^2)\|F\|_\infty\|V_{\rm hi}\|_\infty .
\tag{SL11}
\]

The identity is differentiation of the fixed-support quotient. It includes the moving normalizer. It invokes neither mixing nor a posterior spectral gap.

For spatial bookkeeping the components of \(V_{\rm hi}\) are retained as marks before applying the connected expansion. Equation (SL9) supplies their new small tail; (SL10) identifies the mandatory nonempty higher-slot record. Its creation ceiling is raised to at least the actual newly introduced slot index plus one. One may assign the outer attempt \(r\) to a new small failed-prefix record only **after** this bound and its component-root sum have been obtained.

This is a repair of the no-new-slot assertion, not a reason to drop the high-slot response.

Within the same global attempt a new high-slot mark must not be recursively hidden inside another arbitrary old-\(A\) test. It can include a top \(A_{r-1}\), for which the deep last-direct-slot localization lemma is inapplicable. Preserve its explicit new source map/polynomial record and use the ordinary distinguished-insertion expansion. LFR I (1.2), p. 178, states that the last \(N_r\) operations in the eligible component have the unchanged simplest form: no large-field characteristic functions are included, by condition (ii). A recent failed-selector bump on that same strip would violate this eligibility. This is why the new higher slot in this branch belongs to the ordinary current-response construction, while an arbitrary inherited old real-\(A\) dependence still uses the bounded partial operation. It is not a claim of holomorphy for every historical old \(A\)-slot.

## 6. What failure prevents from repeating

LFR I p. 181, after (1.22), says that a failed selector introduces a new large field and excludes the whole component from the attempted operation. The later selector exclusions repeat this rule. The event is indexed by the current attempt \(r\), even if it tests an older field grade. LFR I p. 201, after (1.99), factors the remaining operation on the original external set \(Z_r\setminus Z\), where \(Z\) contains only components that passed every selector. Thus the original failed component \(P\) survives whole; it must not be replaced by its smaller prepared determining complement \(Z''_r\).

Ordinary growth may merge surviving components but cannot split this original \(P\). A later complete R operation consumes a whole component or misses it. If a later attempt \(r'\) contains any point of the failed \(P\), its no-new-large-field waiting condition forces

\[
 h'=r'-N_{r'}\ge r
\tag{SL12}
\]

up to the fixed endpoint convention. Every explicit old fluctuation coordinate already present at attempt \(r\) has grade at most \(r-1\). Thus this same failed-component lineage cannot subject those coordinates to another unguarded direct integration with \(j\ge h'\). The preserved inner set and old complete-kernel footprint are then behind the PF inner collar.

An old scalar may nevertheless have direct slots in several **disjoint** attempted components. No claim says that a single creation label permits only one operator factor in the whole lattice. Each disjoint component yields a distinct genuine record incidence, paid by the existing component/quotient-tour reserve. Likewise, a tilted operation can create a new higher slot only through the marked response of Sections 3–5; that event has its new small tail and new creation annotation. These two qualifications are necessary to avoid a hidden \(M^N\) accumulation.

## 7. Adding the failed component as a protected core

A new small failed-prefix response may declare the original bounded component \(P_r\) as a protected geometric core. Remove from that record's core list every older core contained in \(P_r\), and keep a maximal antichain. No unchanged old scalar is relabeled in this way.

For an older surviving core \(H_b\) exterior to \(P_r\), the current ordinary construction covers its retained ancestor and adds the actual proper-scale layers. That cover lies in a distinct current component \(Q\). It follows that

\[
 \operatorname{dist}(H_b,P_r)\ge cMR_rh_r.
\tag{SL13}
\]

Both \(P_r\) and \(H_b\) have diameter at most a fixed multiple of \(MR_rh_r\): the first by eligibility and the second by the physical scale comparison \(R_bh_b\le C R_rh_r\). Thus

\[
 \operatorname{dist}(H_b,P_r)
    \ge c'\max\{\operatorname{diam}H_b,
                       \operatorname{diam}P_r\}.
\tag{SL14}
\]

Two disjoint newly protected components at the same current scale obey the corresponding grid/component separation with the same fixed-factor conclusion. The source component convention and its fixed dependency collars must be retained; bare disjointness of arbitrary sets would not prove (SL14).

This preserves the CR.1 relative-separation hypothesis with a smaller fixed constant. The next ordinary step's compatible cover and four proper-scale layers contain the whole \(P_r\) and its prescribed finite halo/stencil. Therefore the next-step footprint protection applies to this new auxiliary core exactly as to a surviving original core. The detailed geometric proof is recorded independently in the current history/quotient note.

Its geometric birth is \(r\), not the old input birth \(b\). The new record keeps its actual \(m\)-grid pieces and its already paid old-to-current transport. A component-root norm is summed before the support is decorated by \(P_r\); the declaration does not make a fine family into one coarse coefficient without an estimate.

## 8. Result of this analysis

The source argument lists justify conditional integration and native analyticity after the last direct slot, with the prescribed surviving older variables. The literal pure kernel does not create higher old adjoint slots. An ordinary tilt can, and that creation must be assigned its actual nonempty full-tree/native-map response; the own-scale port in (SL5) or (SL7) supplies its guard. A whole-component failure then prevents repeated unguarded averaging along that lineage, while disjoint incidences and newly created higher slots remain explicitly charged.

The remaining input to this part of the proof is the actual ordinary-tilted common-domain phase bound. It must include the full ordinary interactions, their masses, and the full permitted external localization contour. The pure bound of A phase cone for a partially integrated prefix alone does not supply it.

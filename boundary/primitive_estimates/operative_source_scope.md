# Operative source domains and the ancestral dimension bound

Throughout, Q denotes [Positive attachment bounds and the quotient forest](quotient_forests.md) and M denotes [Majorants for marked atoms and composition of prefixes](marked_atoms.md).

## 1. The statement

The C-05 statement of the catalogue reads:

> Establish the same local derivative bounds for the actual B and boundary-induced activities, including collars, constraints and current-coordinate dependence.

Its accompanying clause is:

> Boundary constants do not grow with total volume or hidden carrier distance; unrecovered primary formulas remain premises until checked.

The only predecessor is C-01. C-01 identifies the actual conditional density term by term. Its scope explicitly excludes smallness, convexity and decay. Those conclusions cannot be obtained just by treating C-01 as an estimate.

The C-04 comparison supplies the meaning of “same local derivative bounds”: a displayed small weighted-Hessian budget together with fixed-factorial local native source jets. The unsubtracted native scalar is also necessary: a term depending on the native variable but independent of the current disappears from every current derivative and still needs a native estimate. This is also the point needed for the transfer to C-06 and C-08.

## 2. Scope restrictions which are actually permitted

The C-01 construction, admitted positive histories and stipulated coupling trajectory are retained as inputs. The target is not an independent construction of the trajectory or the complete physically normalized observable hierarchy.

In particular:

1. C-08 assigns its remaining all-history application to H-04.
2. F05 physical normalization and nondegeneracy are not additional C05 clauses.
3. The original native extensions keep raw old variables and discrete selector labels in their prescribed fixed roles. Arbitrary derivatives of a hard selector or a separately chosen moving retained-data map are not silently included in native Cauchy estimates.
4. “Retained C01/source scope” therefore is not, by itself, a proof defect. A defect must be an estimate not proved on the full class which that scope actually permits.

This distinction prevents replacing the C05 problem by a different, larger problem. It also prevents treating an extra unproved restriction on the admitted class as though it were already part of C-01.

The boundary proof uses the normalized completed operation only on the literal source self law. This particular use is supported on the printed pages.

- The printed theorem controls the literal canonical unmarked self operation on its admitted source family.

## 5. A concrete admission point requiring resolution: ancestral dimension

The whole-prefix phase estimate ([FC.13](complete_boundary_estimate.md)) uses the bound that the total number of genuine stage-j integration coordinates in one attempted component obeys

\[
D_j\le C(LMR_{j+1})^4.
\tag{CT.1}
\]

M §21.2 calls the corresponding hypothesis a “bounded nested rectangular history per attempted component.” The C-01 statement does not itself state this stronger geometric condition.

The relevant source text has to be read precisely:

- LFR I p.177 defines an eligible component by a current size bound and the condition that no new large-field region was created inside it over the preceding N steps, with previous regions satisfying the size condition on their corresponding scales.
- LFR I p.179 says the components of the relevant domains are rectangular parallelepipeds.
- LFR I p.192 uses (1.73) to bound the final reduced domain by a 100M cube.
- LFR I p.195 uses a nested rectangular sequence in the gauge construction.
- CMP119 p.255, after (2.3), imposes rectangularity on each sufficiently small component. Its p.269 construction rectangularizes the new component at the current step.

The phrase “previous regions” in the p.177 eligibility rule must apply to the entire ancestral intersection with the chosen attempted component, or an additional argument is required to derive (CT.1). A size bound on each member of an arbitrary collection of separated ancestors gives only

\[
D_j\le C\,n_j(P)(LMR_{j+1})^4,
\tag{CT.2}
\]

where n_j(P) is the number of ancestral pieces used at that stage. Individual rectangularity does not remove this factor. Merely calling the stronger whole-intersection interpretation “C01” is not a derivation.

This is an admission check, not a claimed violating Wilson history. The source's later nested-domain usage may settle the intended whole-intersection convention. To complete this check rigorously one must either exhibit the exact source implication to (CT.1), or give a branchwise estimate that retains the factor n_j(P) and pays it in the same actual history norm.

### Why it matters quantitatively

The prefix phase proof estimates the exact quadratic and local scalar phase by a quantity containing

\[
\sum_{j=h}^{r-1}D_jP_j^2\,\alpha_r^+
\]

and comparable local scalar sums. Polynomially many genuine coordinates are paid by the chosen logarithmic radius margin. An uncontrolled ancestral multiplicity cannot be dropped from those estimates; it would also affect the claimed near-one domination constant used for the one-time integration marks.

The final source-stage dimension is therefore a concrete place where the scope and the numerical argument meet. Resolving it is preferable to recasting all downstream open problems as C05 requirements.

## 7. Resolution using the literal operative source family

This section settles the admission question raised in Section 5. It uses the source's actual complete per-component domains. It does not define a new age clock, restrict the schedule of attempted operations, or infer admission from individually small unrelated ancestors.

### 7.1 Source convention, stated explicitly

Fix one component P in the class on LFR I p.177. On that page, immediately following conditions (i),(ii), the paper asserts that all the regions connected with the last N steps for such a component are rectangular parallelepipeds. It then says that intersections of the previous regions Z_j with the attempted union Z are denoted again by Z_j. Equation (1.1) factorizes the operation over the components X_i of that union. Consequently the domains in the component-P factor are the complete intersections

\[
Z_j[P]=Z_j\cap P.
\tag{CT.3}
\]

The earlier-region size condition in this local convention is imposed on these preceding regions on their corresponding scales. It is not a license to replace Z_j[P] by an arbitrary collection of components, apply the bound to each component independently, and forget their number. The operative geometry is confirmed by (1.10)–(1.13), the complete-domain assertion following (1.73) on p.192, and the nested complete rectangular sequence used in (1.81) on p.195.

These are source admission assertions for the very operation being used, not consequences of the generic C-01 density identification alone. Their role must be stated when the actual C-01 construction is invoked. All components on which the source performs the attempted R prefix satisfy those assertions. Components outside that eligible class retain their ordinary operations; they have not been removed from the C05 history class.

### 7.2 Proper-scale strip dimension

**Lemma CT.1.** For every attempted source component P, every admitted successful prefix of that attempt, and every stage j in that prefix, the number D_j(P) of independent real Lie-algebra coordinates in its newly integrated pair (A_j,B_j) obeys

\[
D_j(P)\le C_G\,[1+100LMR_{j+1}]^4
\le C'_G(LMR_{j+1})^4.
\tag{CT.4}
\]

The constants depend on the fixed gauge group, dimension and free-bond convention. They do not depend on the outer scale, cutoff depth, component, or number of earlier histories.

**Proof.** The source begins the preparation by specifying integration in the part of T^(j)(Z_{j+1}) supported on Z_{j+1} minus Z''_{j+1}; see LFR I p.179 after (1.13). The old A_j variables occur on the portion of that set belonging to the old regular region. Their retained cutoff is (1.9), completed by (1.28)–(1.29). Thus every integrated A_j free bond belongs, up to the fixed bond-boundary convention, to Z_{j+1}[P] on the j-grid.

The newly integrated gauge variables V_j are on the complementary part of the same source set. Equations (1.53)–(1.60), pp.187–189, introduce B_j there, linearize the one-step constraint, remove the exceptional bonds by C, and perform the scaling by g_j. None of these operations adds independent variables outside that set. Consequently every free B_j bond also lies in Z_{j+1}[P] on the j-grid. Gauge fixing and constraint elimination can only reduce the number of independent real coordinates.

By the complete preceding-region size clause in the source convention (CT.3), Z_{j+1}[P] lies in a cube of side 100MR_{j+1} on the (j+1)-grid. One such cell contains L^4 j-grid cells. A cube enlarged by the fixed bond boundary convention therefore contains at most C[1+100LMR_{j+1}]^4 free bonds. Each bond carries dim(G) real coordinates, and there are at most two field species A_j and B_j. Absorb these fixed factors into C_G. Since M and the lower bound on R are fixed positive source parameters, the second inequality follows. This proves (CT.4).

The proof counts the actual source integration strip. It does not count the whole final component P at the j-mesh. In particular no factor L^{4(r-j)} has been discarded. The deep operation T_h and fields not integrated by the prefix remain external to this count. Internal discrete histories are subsequently averaged by the normalized source operation; they do not multiply D_j. ∎

### 7.3 Consequence for the phase ledger

The source choice of N_r after LFR I (1.94), p.198, is a fixed logarithmic power. The retained AF comparison makes the profiles at stages in that preparation window comparable. Hence, for each fixed d,

\[
\sum_{j=r-N_r}^{r-1}D_j(P)P_j^d
\le C\ell_r^{D(d)}
\tag{CT.5}
\]

with D(d) fixed before choosing the extra analytic radius exponent. This is the actual polynomial required in the prefix phase calculation. It is uniform for every source-eligible attempted component, including attempts that stop after any successful initial segment.

No inference from the birth index j(Z) in LFR II is needed. That index is expressly the first-large-field index and can remain the index of one child after a merger. The chronological discussion (1.83)–(1.87) is consistent with the source family but is not substituted for an unprinted eligibility-age formula.

### 7.4 What this resolves

The phrase “bounded nested rectangular history” in M §21.2 is to be read as the complete source convention (CT.3), with the actual strip proof above. At that literal source scope the dimension estimate is established and no extra C05 hypothesis is introduced. A broader abstract class formed by admitting arbitrary branching ancestral unions solely from separate component-size bounds is not the input family specified by the printed prefix construction. That weaker reinterpretation cannot be used either to prove (CT.4) or to refute its application to the actual source operation.

This settles the specific CT.1/CT.2 admission concern. It does not certify the separate positive-family propagation estimates merely by resolving geometry.

## 8. Verification of the positive catalog

This section verifies PC.4–PC.6 of [Coarsened protected atoms and the positive catalog](positive_catalog_coarsening.md), including its fixed-representation convention and Lemma PC.6a. The conclusion concerns the specific cancellation, Gaussian-family, self-restriction and geometric-payment interfaces.

### 8.1 The statement permits one fixed source representation

The C05 statement asks for local bounds on the actual complete boundary scalar and its boundary-induced responses. It does not prescribe an absolute norm on a particular unresummed list of localization summands. The original compatible-extension convention likewise does not prescribe independently chosen extensions for every historical summand after a different representation has been selected.

CMP119 p.262 expressly allows component-saturated boundary domains: resumming the terms which meet a given large-field component produces domains which contain that component or miss it; the decay measure becomes the outside-region measure \(d_j(X\setminus Z_j)\); the text states that the constructions and proofs can use these assumptions. This is a source permission for a representation convention, not a theorem saying that an arbitrary new positive norm is invariant.

The proof selects this source convention **once, before iteration**. At each step it carries a finer decomposition into complete protected blocks, and performs the internal sum before the next native Taylor expansion or Gaussian conditioning. This retains the exact scalar density. It does not replace a previously chosen independent-variable extension after taking estimates, and it does not identify Gaussian inverse families constructed from different special domains.

Consequently the complete-block norm PC.12, in place of a norm on the unresummed microcatalog, is within the C05 statement. What is not available is the stronger assertion

\[
 \Bigl\|\sum_i F_i\Bigr\|\text{ small}
 \quad\Longrightarrow\quad
 \sum_i\|F_i\|\text{ small}.
 \tag{CT.6}
\]

Neither the source scalar theorem nor a common later self status proves (CT.6). PC.1–PC.2 use an exact sum before any absolute value and therefore do not make that inference.

### 8.2 The actual algebra needed for later self extraction

Work inside the one selected source representation. A protected zero-remote linear block has a common old carrier \(X\), a genuine deep owner in each directly used failed component \(P\), and carriers satisfying

\[
 X\subset Y_i\subset\operatorname{cover}(X\cup D),
 \qquad D=\bigcup_P D(P).
 \tag{CT.7}
\]

Here \(D(P)\) uses at most two birth-scale proper layers. The source ordinary four-layer enclosure contains the whole old failed component and this footprint; physical layer monotonicity is Q15. Until whole-component consumption, an old component and its footprint therefore belong to one surviving current component. A deep old endpoint cannot enter a different attempted component through its outer padding.

Let \(H^{\sim}\) be a later literal source self region. If \(X\not\subset H^{\sim}\), no \(Y_i\) is self. If \(X\subset H^{\sim}\), every deep owner lies there; the previous whole-component inclusion places each associated \(P\), and hence \(D(P)\), in \(H^{\sim}\). The compatible cover in (CT.7) is then also contained there. Thus

\[
 \mathbf 1_{\{Y_i\subset H^{\sim}\}}
 =\mathbf 1_{\{X\subset H^{\sim}\}}
 \quad\hbox{for every member of the block},
 \tag{CT.8}
\]

and, on the admitted common analytic family,

\[
 \sum_i\mathbf 1_{\{Y_i\subset H^{\sim}\}}F_i
 =\mathbf 1_{\{X\subset H^{\sim}\}}\sum_iF_i.
 \tag{CT.9}
\]

This proves the required compatibility with the canonical self projection. It does not follow from equality of the physical total sum alone. At an interior R substitution, the first completion of LFR II (1.61), p.373, fills the touched component. It therefore gives the same completed domain and determining set for every member of (CT.7). Holding subsequent outside localization labels fixed gives the same later unions and maps in (1.62)–(1.65). The analytic block identity consequently survives that substitution as well.

The literal complete self coefficients of the selected representation remain the input to the canonical operation in LFR II (1.73)–(1.75). No canonical self action is replaced by an arbitrary bounded test law. The claim does not compare these self coefficients with the coefficients of another, independently localized historical representation. PC.5 makes exactly this necessary distinction.

Sources: CMP119 pp.264–265, ordinary whole-region enclosure; LFR II pp.373–380, (1.61)–(1.75); Q §§12 and 18.7, with (CT.8) supplying the explicit commuting projection.

### 8.3 Why the common Gaussian family is exact

After internal coarsening, let \(F=\sum_i F_i\) on the common native chart. Write the new response as \(v(A)=\sum_\gamma v_\gamma(A)\), with the old raw variables fixed and the Taylor center \(U_0\) independent of the newly integrated Gaussian field \(A\). For a fixed degree and a fixed tuple of new records,

\[
 \sum_i D^mF_i(U_0)
       [v_{\gamma_1}(A),\ldots,v_{\gamma_m}(A)]
 =D^mF(U_0)
       [v_{\gamma_1}(A),\ldots,v_{\gamma_m}(A)].
 \tag{CT.10}
\]

The coefficient on the right is external to the new Gaussian integral. Let \(J\) contain the actual \(A\)-supports of these fixed new records and the other explicit Gaussian primitive factors in the marked term. This \(J\) is a sufficient conditioning support. An old native site in \(D(P)\) which carries no new \(A\) coordinate is not a coordinate of this conditional integral.

Gaussian conditioning on \(J\) is first performed as the exact unlocalized Fubini/Schur identity. One then chooses **one** special domain \(X_0\) and the associated generalized-walk family for that fixed support, following CMP116 pp.12–14, (2.5)–(2.10). The source construction allows its connected-domain choices; disconnected supports use the same component convention before final factorization. Its covariance, precision and square-root localizations are subsequently those of this one chosen family. No identity between independently localized inverse matrices is required at nonphysical parameter values.

The finite exact identity (CT.10), the source holomorphy, and the integrable Gaussian majorant extend the calculation to the common independent native/current chart. Compact selector data remain in their prescribed fixed roles. The distinguished-insertion normalization then removes the denominator clusters which do not touch the insertion, as in CMP119 (3.44)–(3.47), pp.277–278.

The trace/determinant cost is consequently measured on genuine new Gaussian coordinates and their new localization records:

\[
 |J_0|\le C\,[\ell_{\mathrm{paid,current}}+1+V_{\mathrm{new}}].
 \tag{CT.11}
\]

An exposed old-carrier Gaussian vertex belongs to the paid old connector, while a new localization vertex belongs to \(V_{\mathrm{new}}\). The protected native footprint remains in the coefficient's dependency and current-derivative graph; absence from the Gaussian trace rank does not make that footprint geometrically free. PC.4 preserves both sides of this distinction. In particular it introduces no factor \(\exp(CR_r^4)\) merely because \(D(P)\) was used as native support metadata.

### 8.4 The newly declared root support is paid

Coarsening enlarges the declared native footprint and can add roots. The positive norm must pay that enlargement separately from Gaussian integration. For an original creation at \(b\) directly used at \(r\), the actual old raw endpoint lies in the complete source ancestor \(Z_b[P]\). Lemma CT.1 bounds that ancestor's \(b\)-block cover by \(CR_b^4\). Counting all \(b\)-cells of the final coarse rectangle would insert an erroneous \(L^{4(r-b)}\); PC.6a does not do so.

The direct-use window \(r-b<N_r\), the fixed AF window comparison and the proper-scale ancestor count give one polynomial allowance in \(\ell_b\) for the possible transport grades and root witnesses. An added footprint has current diameter \(O(R_r)\). For \(m\) endpoint directions, its divided-tree diameter cost is bounded by

\[
 \exp\!\left(\gamma\frac{CmR_r}{m}\right)
 =\exp(C\gamma R_r).
 \tag{CT.12}
\]

This is an exponential diameter cost, not a polynomial. PC.6a explicitly pays it. A crossed component releases a distinct segment of the old paid collar, of length at least \(cR_r\); that segment pays its new component tag and the fixed diameter/root cost. A whole-carrier-contained event has no crossing and instead uses the spare collar share reserved at its original creation. There is at most one such event on that unchanged creation lineage. Original-block integration marks pay the common-measure operator factor separately.

The failure/preservation rule prevents direct reintegration of the same old block at a later containing prefix. Whole successful consumption terminates the lineage. A new raw dependence outside the protected footprint belongs to the separately marked normalized numerator-and-denominator response, with its own paid creation. Hence neither the diameter reserve nor the polynomial transport count is renewed for the same unchanged atom at every step. These are precisely the obligations addressed by Q31g–Q31i and PC.6a.

The outside physical tree is retained after this payment. Overlap of two footprints alone is not a zero-cost connection between their protected cores.

### 8.5 Result of the interface verification

The complete-block construction avoids the invalid inference from a degree-one scalar cancellation to a positive norm on separately localized summands. It supplies a legitimate stronger intermediate norm for the original C05 target, without narrowing the source history class or replacing the Wilson density.

PC.6's four transfer cases are exhaustive for this interface: unchanged scalar transport; new native-map forcing with external old raw data; direct integration by an admitted prefix; and complete component integration. Nonlinear occurrences are subsequently estimated by the positive connected expansion with these complete blocks as its atoms. The forest bounds must therefore be read with PC.12 as their input norm, and not with a microcatalog interpretation.

The cancellation, common Gaussian family, self projection, and once-paid root support hold at the explicitly cited source scope. This is a narrower conclusion than a verification of every map, native-jet, phase and endpoint estimate in C05. Those estimates are the separately identified clauses of Section 3; the present section contributes the interface proofs needed to combine them.

### 9.4 Readout of all clauses of the statement

The fixed representation is admissible for the actual complete scalar and the compatible native extension. Coarsening changes how an exact analytic sum is represented. It does not drop a native-dependent constant or move a scalar between a canonical mass and its normalized law to gain a bound. [FC.5](complete_boundary_estimate.md) covers the unsubtracted scalar, including \(B_k(0,\xi)\); this is the clause which a current-only estimate would miss.

The remaining adverse fixed coupling powers in [FC.22](complete_boundary_estimate.md) have superlogarithmic collars. Hence every displayed budget is bounded at one ceiling, and division by \(P_k^2\) gives the required small Hessian row. The all-order moment estimate uses the already paid physical diameter reserve, so no hidden distance-dependent constant is concealed in its factorial coefficient.

The local native count depends only on the local first-direction support. The spatial root sums precede the creation-capacity scale sum; one does not take a separate norm supremum for each spatial component and then sum those suprema over the torus. Together with [FC.15](complete_boundary_estimate.md), this proves the required volume and history uniformity at the same supremum order as the target statement.

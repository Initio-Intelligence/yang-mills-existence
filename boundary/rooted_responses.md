# Rooted reconstruction responses and linear transport

**Composed-response hypothesis (CR).** Each complete boundary conclusion below assumes the displayed native-chart and spatial response estimates for the actual iterated reconstruction, with the prescribed earlier innovations and raw variables fixed, uniformly over the admitted histories. A sufficient estimate for the latent contribution is (A.8) in [Conditional-law estimates and the continuum application](../continuum/conditional_to_continuum.md), refined for the anchored exterior derivative in PG.18–PG.20. The two endpoint bounds concern the identical Wilson-subtracted primitive, its actual cutoff and its independently extended current. A direct minimizing-field derivative alone does not establish this hypothesis. The application to every required original Wilson history remains open. Complete boundary estimates and their C06/C08 applications in this chapter are conditional on CR and the other displayed source, AF, domain and support assumptions. Exact density identities and abstract inequalities retain their individually stated hypotheses.

In the projective density formulation, a sufficient comparison is
\[
 \sup_{\zeta,x,a}\left|
 \log\frac{W_\zeta(x,a)W_0(x,a_*)}
               {W_0(x,a)W_\zeta(x,a_*)}
 \right|\le\delta_r<\pi/2,
\]
on the stipulated chart with the required uniform rates and actual fixed support. The composed ordinary response is the unresolved input in its derivation. The exact partial-gauge transport and the surviving fixed-exterior derivative are given in the chapter [Gauge covariance of partial integrals and exterior scores](../continuum/partial_gauge_transport.md), equations PG.2–PG.6 and the subsequent anchored-score criterion.

**Scale convention.** The Ward estimate uses the evaluation age $n-b$. When $b\le n<j$, one also needs decay in the forcing age $j-n$. The latter response for the actual composed field, with earlier innovations fixed, is conditional on CR. The two-age interpolation SR.12–SR.24 is used here in place of the one-age calculation of OR.19.

This note isolates the source calculation needed before the
forest estimates. It uses complete protected atoms, with every creation and

The useful new step is to center the **actual preintegration density**, with
all old real variables fixed, before counting roots. A native-only old atom

Neither count fills a later coarse box with all possible fine origins.

## 1. Norm, labels, and induction inputs

Let \(h_i=L^i h_0\). The outer capacity label is a creation scale and operation
sector \(c=(b,s)\). Positions, carrier shapes, component identities, and
localization records remain inside its catalog. At a fixed real exterior
tuple \(a\), write

\[
 S_{c,m}(y;a)=
 \sum_{\substack{G\in\Gamma_{c,m}\\y\in U(G)}}
 W(G)\sup_{\xi\in\mathcal D^+}|F_G(\xi;a)| .                 \tag{RL.1}
\]

Here \(m\) is the actual geometric/native grade of the retained record, which
need not equal its original creation label \(b\). The norm of \(F_G\) is the
norm of the complete scalar; its canceled internal localizer pieces are not
individual summands in (RL.1). \(U(G)\) contains actual active blocks and
actual component incidences. A protected footprint is dependency data, not a
separate list of integration slots and not a free ball.

The induction carries capacities which dominate the actual graded catalogs
after the once-reserved transport costs. Thus, after spatial root sums,

\[
 \sum_c \|S_{c,\bullet}\|_{\mathrm{root,res}}\le A_{r-1}.
                                                               \tag{RL.2}
\]

The symbol on the left includes the one-time proper-grade pushforward of
[ND.43](primitive_estimates/native_domain_count.md)–[ND.49](primitive_estimates/native_domain_count.md), not a new supremum for each spatial component. Its only role
here is linear transport. Those incidence formulas are not estimates for a
new map or a normalized-density score.

The analytic supremum remains inside the catalog sum; the real exterior
supremum is taken afterward. In particular no step below replaces

\[
 \sup_a\sum_G\sup_\xi |F_G(\xi;a)|
 \quad\hbox{by}\quad
 \sum_G\sup_{a,\xi}|F_G(\xi;a)| .                            \tag{RL.3}
\]

The incoming norm is the norm before the present global attempt. There is
no appeal to the result of the current prefix integration.

## 2. Exact centering of the relative part

Fix one admitted history branch, an eligible attempted component \(P\), and
all earlier real/raw and exterior variables. Let \(a_P\) be its whole tuple of
genuinely integrated prefix variables, including the actual \(A_j,B_j\)
variables. Choose one admissible reference \(a_P^*\) on that branch. Zero is
not required to belong to a failed support.

Choose these reference points as restrictions of one global tuple
\(a^*=(a_P^*)_P\) on the product of the actual disjoint component supports.
The local laws are formed only after classifying the complete actual
integrated support. A raw atom using two components stays in the remote
interaction catalog, with its old connecting graph, even if a subsequent
reference projection makes one displayed term independent of one slot.
Its genuine crossed-collar reserve is not thrown away.

For native-map terms, Taylor-expand the complete scalar at the common
native reference with all prefix variables at \(a^*\). The coefficient is
independent of these new variables, except for its genuine direct old raw
arguments. A local monomial has every direct raw slot and every new
map-factor slot in the same \(P\). Its raw coefficient is evaluated at the
restriction of the same global actual tuple, or the same global \(a^*\).
Mixed-component monomials remain guarded interactions. This convention
prevents separately chosen raw tuples for different atoms in one root sum.

For the incoming relative action, use the exact identity

\[
 V_{\rm rel}(\xi,a_P)
 =V_{\rm rel}(\xi,a_P^*)+
   [V_{\rm rel}(\xi,a_P)-V_{\rm rel}(\xi,a_P^*)].            \tag{RL.4}
\]

The first term is independent of every variable being integrated and factors
out of both numerator and denominator of a normalized expectation. It stays
in the outer action. Only the second term belongs to the relative tilt of the
prefix law. This is algebraic centering; no canonical mass is removed.

For a complete old atom there are two disjoint counting cases.

* If it directly uses a variable in \(a_P\), retain it as one bounded raw test.
  Its difference is bounded by the sum of its two values, hence by twice its
  incoming bound at the two common real tuples. No raw Cauchy derivative is
  used.
* If all its explicit raw arguments are fixed, the difference is through the
  actual native reconstruction map. Apply native Cauchy only after summing
  the protected atom. Every nonzero difference contains an actual input
  forcing leaf from a proper prefix strip.

If the source records vary with a discrete selector in the current tuple,
first keep the selector on its literal fixed support. The corresponding
changed local record is a direct slot/history incidence at that selector's
actual block. An old history label held fixed in the conditional law is not
summed as a new independent index.

For an actual stopped law, (RL.4) has to be applied to its **unrolled joint
density**. One cannot put a relative \(C_i\) produced earlier in the same
attempt into an independently bounded ordinary reference and then count the
incoming relative atom again. The exact cancellation of these stage masses
is a density identity, treated in [Stopped densities and projective comparison](stopped_densities.md); it is not a
root-counting assertion.

\[
 (1+j-i)^vL^{-p(n-i)}
 \le C_p(1+j-n)^v\quad(n\le j),\qquad
 (1+j-i)^vL^{-p(n-i)}\le C_p\quad(n>j).

exponential share has absorbed its polynomial; only then weaken the

## 5. Pointing a genuinely new root in a Taylor catalog

For one old complete scalar of majorant \(a_G\), let \(u_y\) be its
dimensionless positive new-map intensity at a native patch \(y\). The exact
multihomogeneous Banach Taylor argument gives

\[
 \sum_{\text{new records}}\!\text{majorant}
 \le a_G\prod_{y\in U(G)}(1-u_y)^{-1}.                    \tag{RL.12}
\]

The coefficients already include the actual Taylor factorials. This formula
does not assign a factorial to a localization-domain enumeration.
Here a protected patch is one bounded native Banach block on its actual
footprint, not its list of fine coordinates. Start with the source smooth
proper-mesh partition and assign its patches to the relevant protected
blocks or exposed paid blocks. Group all patches assigned to one
protected block before Taylor expansion. Grouping disjoint families of
the original partition preserves its bounded overlap and derivative
bounds. Even when stored footprints overlap, the partition patches are
assigned once. The resulting product ball has the source affine margin.
Internal map operators in a protected block have already been summed as
in §3, so neither a fine-coordinate coefficient norm nor a new fine-site
incidence sum appears in \(u_y\).

Suppose the persistent patches have \(u_y\le C\epsilon_r\), and spend a
summable count decrement \(\rho_r=K\sqrt{\epsilon_r}\). For a root already
on the old graph,

\[
 e^{-\rho_r n}
 \left[\prod_y(1-u_y)^{-1}-1\right]
 \le C\sqrt{\epsilon_r}.                                \tag{RL.13}
\]

The proof uses \(\prod(1-u_y)^{-1}\le e^{2C\epsilon_r n}\)
and \(\sup_{n\ge0}ne^{-\rho_r n/2}=2/(e\rho_r)\).
An (n+1) convention only adds \(O(\epsilon_r)\).

For a root \(x\) which belongs to a newly added branch, let

\[
 u_y(x)=\sum_{\gamma:\,x\in\gamma}u_{y,\gamma}.
\]

Point one occurrence of the new record containing \(x\). Differentiating
the positive geometric product, or summing its marked monomials directly,
gives

\[
 \sum_{\text{records containing new }x}\!\text{majorant}
 \le a_G\prod_y(1-u_y)^{-1}
                 \sum_y\frac{u_y(x)}{1-u_y}.              \tag{RL.14}
\]

After the count decrement, summing \(G\) yields the convolution

\[
 S^{\rm new}(x)\le C\sum_y u_y(x)S^{\rm in}(y).           \tag{RL.15}
\]

This is the missing direction of the root calculation: an unpointed scalar
map norm is insufficient. The pointed source-walk estimate of §§3–4 bounds
the row \(\sum_y u_y(x)\), including the actual fine-root density. Old roots
and new roots must not be conflated.

Exposed paid patches may have a non-summable bare intensity. Their count
is controlled by the old physical connector. The exact coarsening identity

\[
 \ell_r=L^{-(r-m)}\ell_m
\]

releases \(\lambda(1-L^{-1})\ell_m\) while retaining the same output rate

\[
 \lambda.
\]

That released length pays exposed Taylor entropy and actual Gaussian
insertion volume. It does not create a fresh fixed loss on unchanged tags.

# History labels in the normalized Wilson return operator

The frozen H-02 and H-03 tests distinguish the probability of inherited events, locality at a reset and the outer retained-mean problem. This note proves estimates for a one-cut posterior which actually sums possible returning components. Bounds are conditional on the admitted unmarked histories and primary weak margins. They do not complete H-02 or H-03.

## 5. F-03 and the seven native mean coefficients

There is a normalization issue which can be settled independently of F-04's uniform error. At a fixed physical cutoff assume the seven X (F.33) multipliers exist; the finite fallback can also be used wherever it is invertible, with its own meaning. Write D=∑_h z_h P_h. Centering removes the scalar additive identity. Applying the same D at both ends of a linear one-field step conjugates its channel matrix. Hence it leaves each coefficient of an H₄-equivariant endomorphism on these seven multiplicity-one sectors unchanged. A scale-dependent sequence D_j instead gives two different endpoint factors in each step, and conditioning at a fixed non-invariant exterior does not by itself give an equivariant map. This argument concerns the centered one-field projection only.

On the admitted whole-lattice translation-averaged regular family, T9 (T9.10)–(T9.11),(T9.32)–(T9.34) calculate a genuine coefficient of the exact frozen transport. For the spatially uniform source and its *classical stable descendants*, the trace uses the actual constrained Wilson covariance and stationary source Hessian. Its critical coordinate is A_j¹+B_j¹ S_j⁰, not just the critical-to-critical block. Native N9 (N9.19)–(N9.26) give its seven coefficients, prior to higher-order remainders, as the universal vector plus a finite-part difference F_j−F_{j+1} and the stated cutoff error. The corresponding backward graph uses its own bounded shift of F_j. With u_j=1/Y_j and Y_j=Nβ_W, matching β_W=2/g_can² in the half-trace generator convention gives g_can²=2N u_j. Thus the universal constant entries in the dimension ordering (1,1,3,6,2,2,6) are

    (0, 0, −d_B, −d_B, −18d_B/11, −18d_B/11, −18d_B/11),
    d_B=2N²·11 log L/(24π²).

If the B-03 coordinate is x_j^{-1}=2N²/Y_j, divide the displayed entries by 2N². The finite-part differences must be converted by the same convention. Bounded finite parts need not have small adjacent differences, so discarding them would not calculate the raw one-step coefficient.

There is an endpoint-preserving change of intermediate mean coordinates, if the chosen scheme permits one. Write the forward matrix as M_j=I+u_j(Γ+F_j−F_{j+1})+r_j in these common units. Choose bounded diagonal c_j with c₀=F₀, c_K=F_K, and Q_j=I+u_j(F_j−c_j). Then Q₀=Q_K=I, and for a uniformly weak interval and |u_{j+1}−u_j|≤C u_j² the exact coordinate change obeys

    Q_{j+1}M_jQ_j⁻¹ = I+u_j Γ +u_j(c_j−c_{j+1})
                        +O(u_j²)+ Q_{j+1}r_j Q_j⁻¹.                 (7)

Take c_j=F₀+(F_K−F₀)∑_{i<j}u_i/∑_{i<K}u_i. When the total sum is bounded below uniformly, the displayed extra term is O(u_j²). Linear interpolation in j also works when K⁻¹≤C u_j on the whole interval. On a short ultracold interval the extra term need not satisfy that pointwise bound; with linear interpolation its total norm is O(max_j u_j). Neither choice changes the endpoint matrices. Equation (7) is an identity on the matched diagonal mean transport in its specified coordinates. An intermediate chart change must be declared if used: it does not identify the unmodified entries of the fixed reference basis at every cut.

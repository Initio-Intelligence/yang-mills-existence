# Positive history kernel extensions and selector interfaces

**Catalogue items addressed:** H-02 and H-03.\
**Scope.** These finite positive-kernel estimates concern the declared native extension. The full physical marked norm requires additional bounds.

The new positive result is a construction of a positive extension of the actual normalized old-component kernel on each verified native chart. It removes the artificial divided-current singularity caused by setting that kernel equal to a fixed point mass immediately outside its admitted chart. It retains the previously proved spatial tail and an all-order source-jet budget, with explicit radius costs and no reciprocal small component mass.

A second result identifies precisely why the same repair cannot remove a genuine two-sided selector jump. Such a jump makes the strong M-03 current norm infinite. Exponential rarity of the selector, or an estimate of its thin boundary layer, does not repair that failure. An explicit test uses the original Wilson $P_{0}$ selector. It concerns a specified erasure of that selector while retaining its determining links; it is not claimed to describe the eligible native $R$ operation, which also integrates old fields and may retain outer geometry.

These two results distinguish a removable extension defect from a genuine current obstruction. Neither supplies the missing physical coordinate pullback, complete carrier cost, or normalized unreplaced-event estimate.

## 1 Actual source input and the role of the native chart

Fix a source-admitted outer component geometry and the other live component records. Let $Q(z)$ be the normalized old-component posterior obtained from the real positive operation $T'_{k}(X)$ of LFR II (1.71), including its nonsmall self action, old-history sums, graph choices, constraints and normalizers, and tilted by the cross action in (1.72).

The source fact that matters here is quite specific. At printed p.379, immediately after (1.72), LFR II identifies the $B$ quadratic form and $V\left( \widetilde{X} \right)$ as the parts of (1.71) depending on the extended native variables $(U,J)$⁠. The other factors use the common integration support of that native representation. The comparison (1.73)--(1.75) is consequently a comparison of the old component operation with its real positive center, including the denominator. It is not a density estimate for a freshly sampled reference law.

On a verified one-parameter native chart, Annex AG, Theorem HL.6 gives

$$Q(z)F = \frac{Q(0)\left( e^{\vartheta(z)}F \right)}{Q(0)e^{\vartheta(z)}},\quad\quad|z| < r,\quad\quad \parallel Q(z) \parallel_{var} \leq 3,$$

(PH20.1)

where one may use

$$r = \frac{\rho\log(3/2)}{s_{i} + \lambda_{i} + \log(3/2)}.$$

(PH20.2)

Here $\rho$ is the actually admitted native radius, $s_{i}$ bounds the recentered component exponent, and $\lambda_{i}$ is the full cross-action majorant. These quantities have the meanings of HL.21--HL.22. The total-variation convention is the dual norm over tests of supremum at most one, so the distance between two probabilities is at most two.

When $Q_{E}$ is the posterior with the remote cross carriers deleted as in HL.18, the same common chart gives

$$\sup_{|z| < r} \parallel Q(z) - Q_{E}(z) \parallel_{var} \leq 12\lambda_{i}(E).$$

(PH20.3)

This is the bounded-test version of HL.34. The bound applies because the deleted cross exponent is bounded on the admitted chart. It does not replace any physical-coordinate image check.

For the positive construction below, the real segment is a segment on which the component representation is an actual positive normalized law. This includes its real regular-background source charts. An independently extended current argument is not silently declared to be a physical positive law: there one retains the analytic estimate on its stated domain, and verifies reality/positivity separately if it is needed.

**Primary source:** Bałaban, *Large Field Renormalization II*, CMP 122 (1989), pp.378--380, (1.71)--(1.75). The source-domain map is (1.65), pp.374--375. The exact current-coordinate application still required is recorded in Annex AG.1 of the main manuscript.

## 2 A positive extension with bounded divided currents

### Theorem PH20 1 a positive collar extension of the actual native kernel

Suppose $Q(z)$ has the common-support representation (PH20.1), is holomorphic as a finite-measure-valued function on $|z| < r$⁠, and $Q(t)$ is a probability for real $|t| < r$⁠. Define

$$\theta_{r}(t) = \left\{ \begin{matrix}
1, & |t| \leq r/4, \\
2 - 4|t|/r, & r/4 < |t| < r/2, \\
0, & |t| \geq r/2,
\end{matrix} \right.\ $$

and define, on the entire real parameter axis,

$$\widetilde{Q}(t) = \left\{ \begin{matrix}
\theta_{r}(t)Q(t) + \left( 1 - \theta_{r}(t) \right)Q(0), & |t| < r/2, \\
Q(0), & |t| \geq r/2.
\end{matrix} \right.\ $$

(PH20.4)

Then it is a positive normalized kernel, agrees exactly with $Q$ on $|t| \leq r/4$⁠, and satisfies

$$\parallel \widetilde{Q}(t) - \widetilde{Q}(s) \parallel_{var} \leq \frac{14}{r}|t - s|\quad\quad(s,t \in {\mathbb{R}}).$$

(PH20.5)

In particular every divided kernel current against a fixed bounded test $F$ is bounded by $14 \parallel F \parallel_{\infty}/r$⁠, including differences crossing the admission collar. No lower bound on $T'_{k}(X)1$ appears.

**Proof.** A convex combination of probabilities is a probability. On $|t| \leq r/2$⁠, Cauchy's estimate applied to (PH20.1), in the measure norm, gives

$$\parallel Q'(t) \parallel_{var} \leq 6/r.$$

The cutoff has Lipschitz constant $4/r$⁠, and $\parallel Q(t) - Q(0) \parallel_{var} \leq 2$ on the real segment. Away from its finitely many corners,

$$\widetilde{Q}'(t) = \theta'_{r}(t)\left( Q(t) - Q(0) \right) + \theta_{r}(t)Q'(t),$$

whose norm is at most $8/r + 6/r$⁠. At $|t| = r/2$ the expression tends to $Q(0)$⁠; hence there is no jump. Integrating on the finitely many smooth subintervals proves the global divided-difference estimate. Positivity at the center and the normalized representation (PH20.1) have already canceled the possibly small old component mass. ∎

This construction does not alter the actual kernel on its declared smaller chart. It is an explicit choice of ambient version, of the kind allowed in M.1. It is valid only when that smaller chart contains the actual data at which it is used. Covering the entire physical support with a compatible extension is a further application task; a chart centered at one datum is not such a cover.

### Theorem PH20 2 the extension retains the discarded carrier current tail

Apply the same cutoff to $Q$ and $Q_{E}$⁠, using their own center probabilities and a common verified radius $r$⁠. Write $\lambda = \lambda_{i}(E)$⁠. Then

$$\sup_{t} \parallel \widetilde{Q}(t) - {\widetilde{Q}}_{E}(t) \parallel_{var} \leq 12\lambda,$$

(PH20.6)

and

$$\parallel \left\lbrack \widetilde{Q}(t) - {\widetilde{Q}}_{E}(t) \right\rbrack - \left\lbrack \widetilde{Q}(s) - {\widetilde{Q}}_{E}(s) \right\rbrack \parallel_{var} \leq \frac{120\lambda}{r}|t - s|.$$

(PH20.7)

**Proof.** Set $\Delta(z) = Q(z) - Q_{E}(z)$⁠. The extended difference is $\theta_{r}(t)\Delta(t) + \left( 1 - \theta_{r}(t) \right)\Delta(0)$⁠. Its norm is at most $12\lambda$⁠. Cauchy applied to (PH20.3) gives $\parallel \Delta'(t) \parallel_{var} \leq 24\lambda/r$ for $|t| \leq r/2$⁠. Differentiating the extended difference costs at most

$$(4/r)(24\lambda) + 24\lambda/r = 120\lambda/r.$$

The expression is continuous at the collar endpoints, so integration proves the finite-difference bound. ∎

The cutoff's determining variables and center must be included in its footprint. In particular this theorem does not make the cutoff local to the insertion anchor merely because the insertion is there. For two different localization truncations it is essential to use a common cutoff; independently changing the extension rule can create an unrelated tail.

## 3 All source orders and the separate new kernel current record

Let $I$ be a set of $m$ source labels. Freeze the incoming vertices $a_{B}$⁠, for all nonempty $B \subset I$⁠, while varying the kernel. Assume

$$\parallel a_{B} \parallel_{\infty} \leq C^{|B| + 1}\left( |B|! \right)^{p}b^{|B|},\quad\quad C \geq 1,\quad p \geq 1.$$

(PH20.8)

The complete reset coefficient is

$$A_{I}^{Q} = \sum_{\pi \in \mathcal{P}(I)}^{}\kappa_{Q}\left( a_{B}:B \in \pi \right).$$

(PH20.9)

This includes the higher incoming vertices. Treating it as a cumulant of first vertices alone would omit part of the exact jet.

### Proposition PH20 3 the positive extension preserves a fixed factorial budget

Set

$$K_{m} = C^{m + 1}(1 + 2C)^{m - 1}(m!)^{p}b^{m}.$$

(PH20.10)

Then

$$\left| A_{I}^{\widetilde{Q}(t)} \right| \leq K_{m},\quad\quad\left| A_{I}^{\widetilde{Q}(t)} - A_{I}^{\widetilde{Q}(s)} \right| \leq (14/r)K_{m}|t - s|.$$

(PH20.11)

For the common truncated extension,

$$\left| A_{I}^{\widetilde{Q}(t)} - A_{I}^{{\widetilde{Q}}_{E}(t)} \right| \leq 12\lambda K_{m},$$

(PH20.12)

and its divided-current tail is bounded by

$$\frac{288\lambda}{r}C^{m + 1}(1 + 8C)^{m - 1}(m!)^{p}b^{m}.$$

(PH20.13)

**Proof.** For $k$ bounded variables, the moment-cumulant formula and telescoping products of moments give

$$\left| \kappa_{Q}\left( F_{1},\ldots,F_{k} \right) \right| \leq k!2^{k - 1}\prod_{a}^{} \parallel F_{a} \parallel_{\infty},$$

and the same right-hand side multiplied by $\parallel Q - P \parallel_{var}$ bounds the difference of the two cumulants. One way to check the constant is

$$\sum_{\sigma \in \mathcal{P}\left( \lbrack k\rbrack \right)}^{}|\sigma|! \leq k!2^{k - 1}.$$

The derivative version has the same bound multiplied by $\parallel Q' \parallel_{var}$⁠. The extra factor $|\sigma|$ comes from choosing the differentiated moment, converting $\left( |\sigma| - 1 \right)!$ to $|\sigma|!$⁠.

For a pair of differentiable probability curves, with $\parallel Q - P \parallel_{var} \leq \delta$⁠, $\parallel Q' - P' \parallel_{var} \leq \delta_{1}$ and $\max\left( \parallel Q' \parallel_{var}, \parallel P' \parallel_{var} \right) \leq T$⁠, a second telescoping gives

$$\left| \left( \kappa_{Q} - \kappa_{P} \right)' \right| \leq \left( \delta_{1} + \delta T \right)k^{2}2^{k - 1}k!\prod_{a}^{} \parallel F_{a} \parallel_{\infty}.$$

Use $\delta = 12\lambda$⁠, $\delta_{1} = 120\lambda/r$⁠, $T = 14/r$ and $k^{2} \leq 4^{k - 1}$⁠. Thus the last bound is at most $(288\lambda/r)8^{k - 1}k!$ times the product of the test bounds.

Finally, for an outer partition with $k$ blocks, $\prod_{B \in \pi}^{}\left( |B|! \right)^{p} \leq (m!)^{p - 1}\prod_{B \in \pi}^{}|B|!$⁠, and the exact ordered-block count is

$$\sum_{\substack{\pi \in \mathcal{P}\left( \lbrack m\rbrack \right) \\ |\pi| = k}}^{}k!\prod_{B \in \pi}^{}|B|! = m!\binom{m - 1}{k - 1}.$$

(PH20.14)

Insert (PH20.8) and sum the resulting binomial series, with 2 or 8 respectively. The curves are piecewise differentiable and continuous, so the derivative bounds imply the displayed finite-difference bounds. ∎

The current in (PH20.11) is the **new kernel current with the incoming vertices frozen**. It is not the sum of direct input derivatives, previous currents and the new current. Those are separate records in M.10--M.12 and still require their own estimates.

For one certified direction and a declared carrier $S$⁠, let $d = m + 1/2$⁠. Because the native regulator is at least one, the contribution of this direction to the M.11 current seminorm is at most

$$\frac{28h^{4m}}{rd^{d}}K_{m}.$$

(PH20.15)

Indeed the pointwise current is bounded by $14K_{m}/r$⁠, and each of the two terms in M.11 is bounded by that constant divided by $d^{d}$⁠. The carrier contribution still multiplies (PH20.15) by

$$\exp\{\kappa\left( |S| + \mathsf{t}_{h}(S) \right)\}.$$

(PH20.16)

Every physical direction must be included in M.7. A jointly compatible extension on a verified polydisc can use a common rectangular cutoff and pays the corresponding sum of inverse directional radii. Separately extending unrelated one-dimensional slices does not by itself construct such a common kernel.

Thus the new result removes an artificial admission-boundary jump, with an explicit native current cost. It does **not** pay the whole owner/body volume, convert source directions to physical retained directions, or control the regulator and path costs of the incoming coordinate pullback.

## 4 Genuine two sided interfaces cannot be repaired by an ambient extension

### Theorem PH20 4 a nonzero kernel jump forces an infinite M 03 current norm

Use a finite-cutoff retained-coordinate chart $(u,y)$⁠, with $u$ one allowed normalized continuous direction. A tangential graph may first be subtracted from $u$⁠; at fixed $y$ this leaves the same elementary direction. Suppose a hypersurface is locally $u = 0$⁠, both sides are actual admissible output fibers, and the scalar $Q_{(u,y)}F$ extends continuously to each closed half of a compact local chart. Denote its one-sided values by $Q_{y}^{-}F$ and $Q_{y}^{+}F$⁠. Suppose there is a fixed admissible bounded incoming mark $F$ and a tangential patch on which

$$\left| Q_{y}^{+}F - Q_{y}^{-}F \right| \geq a > 0.$$

(PH20.17)

Assume the actual M.11 regulator envelope ${\widehat{R}}_{h,X}$⁠, including its full elementary-perturbation supremum from M.6, is bounded on a compact neighborhood of that patch. Then the new kernel current of this mark has infinite pointwise part in M.11, for every finite source grade. No choice of values outside the actual support changes this conclusion.

If, additionally, the actual output density in this chart is bounded below by $c|u|^{b}$ on one side, on a tangential subpatch of positive measure, with $0 \leq b < \infty$⁠, then the current envelope has infinite $L^{q}$ norm for every $q \geq b + 1$⁠. In particular a strictly positive continuous marginal density gives divergence already at $q = 1$⁠.

**Proof.** Shrink the patch so the one-sided scalar traces of $QF$ differ by at least $a$ and their approaching values have errors at most $a/8$⁠. For $0 < u < \epsilon$ choose a permitted increment taking $u$ to $- u$⁠, with length comparable to $2u$ in the fixed normalized coordinate. The mark is frozen, so its direct record is zero and the kernel divided difference has magnitude at least $c_{0}a/u$⁠. The supremum over increments in M.7 is therefore at least $c_{0}a/u$ at these points. A bounded regulator cannot absorb this divergence in the pointwise supremum.

For the moment assertion, integrate its $q$-th power against the lower density. The normal integral dominates a positive constant times

$$\int_{0}^{\epsilon}u^{b - q}\, du,$$

which diverges for $q \geq b + 1$⁠. The normalization of the elementary parameter only changes a finite nonzero comparison constant at this fixed cutoff. All compared points lie in actual admissible fibers; altering off-support values cannot remove the jump. ∎

If the two kernel traces are distinct probabilities on the finite padded old-state space, a bounded regular test separating them can be chosen. On its finite compact charts, smooth cylinder tests together with discrete sector indicators determine probability measures. Thus the obstruction need not use an irregular incoming test. Whether such a test is generated by the particular physical source family is a separate question from boundedness of an operator on the whole M-03 domain.

### Consequence for low margin estimates

Suppose a hard selector is exponentially rare, or its thickness-$\epsilon$ layer has probability bounded by $C\epsilon\exp( - P)$⁠. That estimate controls a boundary measure or an integrated undivided jump. It does not imply a finite M.7 envelope: taking a supremum over step sizes first produces the inverse-distance singularity in Theorem PH20.4. The supremum part of M.11 does not use the rare-event probability at all.

Likewise, replacing a selector threshold by a nearby one, even with exponentially small displacement, creates two jump interfaces unless their marked traces match. A uniform sup-norm approximation of the smooth determining field is not a strong divided-current approximation of its hard threshold.

This is a requirement on the actual coefficient representation. Cancellation of jumps after summing separately measured activity records does not bound each record in M.12. A proof must establish the required matching before taking the separately specified absolute norms, or explicitly give a valid different carrier representation.

## 5 A concrete test using the original Wilson selector

The initial partition (CMP119, printed p.246, (1.1)) attaches to each diagnostic cube $D$ the hard indicator

$$H_{D}(U) = \mathbf{1}\left\{ \max_{p \subset D^{test}} \parallel U(dp) - 1 \parallel \geq \epsilon_{0} \right\},$$

(PH20.18)

with its complete finite test stencil. The exact thickening and plaquette convention are retained in $D^{test}$⁠. The initial Wilson law at a finite coupling has a strictly positive smooth density with respect to product Haar measure. The deterministic positive history lift of this law includes the label $H_{D}$⁠.

Consider the specific projection whose retained output is exactly the original compact link field $U$⁠: it erases this label and every equivalent selector flag, while retaining all its determining links. One may also adjoin a newly drawn independent seed; it does not change the old conditional law. The normalized old-label kernel is exactly

$$Q_{U}(dh) = \delta_{H_{D}(U)}(dh),\quad\quad Q_{U}\lbrack h\rbrack = H_{D}(U).$$

(PH20.19)

The incoming mark $F(h) = h$ has a finite M-03 norm at this cutoff: it is bounded, its continuous differences at fixed $h$ vanish, its discrete differences are bounded, and its full footprint is the test stencil. Its output new kernel current, however, has infinite strong norm.

To verify that the interface needed by Theorem PH20.4 actually exists, use the Hilbert--Schmidt convention for the physical plaquette defect recorded in T1, including its identity $\parallel I - U \parallel_{HS}^{2} = 2s(U)$⁠, and choose small commuting link angles in a fixed nonzero Cartan direction. Each unoriented plaquette angle is a nonzero linear functional of these link angles. Choose the angles generically so a single tested unoriented plaquette has the strictly largest absolute angle; the finitely many equality conditions can be avoided. Scale the angles until that maximum reaches the small positive threshold $\epsilon_{0}$⁠. All other tested plaquettes remain strictly below it. Varying an incident link changes the distinguished plaquette angle with nonzero derivative, and its norm is smooth and strictly increasing at this sufficiently small nonzero angle. The boundary is therefore a smooth transversal hypersurface. A neighborhood has positive smooth Wilson density. The traces of $Q_{U}\lbrack h\rbrack$ are zero and one. At the finite cutoff, compactness also bounds the full M.6 plaquette regulator envelope. Theorem PH20.4 applies. If a different primitive norm is retained, the same construction uses a smooth transversal point of that actual norm rather than silently replacing the selector.

This example can be arbitrarily rare in the usual weak-coupling sense. The verified original Wilson plaquette estimate of T1, Proposition 7.96, gives a small union bound for (PH20.18) when its stated even-tiling and partition-ratio hypotheses hold. Nevertheless the current norm is infinite at each finite cutoff: a small positive density near the interface still multiplies a divergent inverse-distance integral, and the pointwise supremum is independent of that density.

**Scope of this example.** It rules out the proposal that taking the original physical conditional measure is, by itself, a universal way to extend every erased-history kernel smoothly. It is an actual source-defined selector and an exactly specified physical projection. It is not asserted that the eligible native $R$ operation performs this projection. $R$ integrates old fields as well as histories and can retain outer geometric data. Keeping $H_{D}$⁠, or an equivalent determining discrete flag, changes the output coordinate space; integrating enough of its determining links can smooth the conditional probability. Both possibilities require their actual kernels, and neither is excluded here. No geometry erasure is imposed on an eligible-only reset.

## 6 Exact matching condition and the remaining source application

Let $r_{v}$ be the unnormalized old measure, $Z_{v} = r_{v}(1)$⁠, and suppose both sides of an actual interface have positive limits. Its normalized kernel traces agree precisely when

$$\frac{r^{+}}{Z^{+}} = \frac{r^{-}}{Z^{-}}\quad\text{as measures on the common padded old-state space}.$$

(PH20.20)

For one incoming mark, only the corresponding equality of normalized marked traces is needed. Equality of the unmarked total weights alone does not imply it. The centered finite-difference formula

$$\left( Q_{v'} - Q_{v} \right)F = \frac{\left( r_{v'} - r_{v} \right)\left( F - Q_{v}F \right)}{Z_{v'}}$$

(PH20.21)

keeps the correct denominator, but does not eliminate a nonzero jump in (PH20.20).

For the fixed-geometry native $T'_{k}$ chart the common-support analytic representation supplies matching automatically inside the chart. Theorems PH20.1--PH20.3 now give a positive collar extension beyond its smaller version. To use this for H-03 one still has to establish, on the actual full physical support:

1.  The physical normalized coordinate curves, including every independent current and all retained stencils, enter the complete domain of LFR II (1.65) with quantitative simultaneous radii.
2.  The chart center, cutoff, source-coordinate map and normalized current have correctly bounded physical footprints, including the entire owner and collar whenever they are consulted.
3.  The sum over all physical directions, the actual radii, and the carrier factor (PH20.16) are paid in the stated M-03 norm.
4.  Direct differences of every higher incoming vertex, previous-current records, and their actual regulator/path pullbacks satisfy the same current-scale input budget. Freezing the vertices only isolates the new current; it does not prove these other estimates.
5.  Any genuine two-sided sector change either retains its determining discrete data, integrates them through a verified smoothing kernel, or satisfies the actual marked matching condition (PH20.20).

A capped-minimum construction may address the first item. Even a successful source-domain transfer does not automatically supply the other four.

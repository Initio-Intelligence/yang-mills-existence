### 1. What the primary formulas do and do not say

CMP119 p.258, (2.19)–(2.21), describes the support and component factorization of the old operation \(T_k\). Its within-component quadratic form contains the background-dependent operators
\[
 C^*\Delta^{(j)}C,\qquad
 C^*\Delta^{(j)}C\,C^{(j)}(\Lambda_{j+1})\,C^*\Delta^{(j)}C.
 \tag{OT.1}
\]
An integration supported on an old component need not have coefficients independent of a new current coordinate. The old background is itself a function of the retained and current data.

The issue remains after R. The complete operation \(T'_j(X)\) is LFR II (1.71), pp.378–379. The source explicitly states on p.379 that its quadratic form in the R fluctuation and the self-component expression \(V(X^\sim)\) depend on the native pair \((U,J)\). It then controls their variation. This is a statement of retained dependence, not exact constancy.

The local representation in (1.61)–(1.65) does not remove that dependence. Printed p.374 explicitly notes that an intermediate localized background still depends nonlocally on the gauge variables \(V''\). Its final extension substitutes the local averages of \(U\) into that representation. Keeping the current raw determining data fixed on the old large-field set does not, by itself, keep every new finer collar average in a local determining splice fixed.

Consequently the implication
\[
 \text{inner current support misses }X
 \quad\Longrightarrow\quad
 \partial_z T'_j(X)=0
 \tag{OT.2}
\]
has not been proved and is not used here. An exact fixed-data identity for every coefficient, or an actual response estimate, is required.

### 3. A genuine directional disc gives an ordinary density response

Let \(\mathfrak N_X\) be a native norm on the dependency set of the component expression. Assume a ball of radius \(r_T>0\) about each point of an inner chart lies in the source domain of (OT.3), with the same old variables and integration support. Assume (OT.4) on that larger ball. Let the **actual** current/native argument map be \(\Phi(z,\xi)\), holomorphic for
\[
 \|z\|_\infty<2P_k,\qquad \xi\in\mathcal D^+,
 \tag{OT.6}
\]
and suppose its image and every old argument used in (OT.3) are in the preceding inner chart.

For a current coordinate \(i\), suppose
\[
 \|\partial_i\Phi(z,\xi)\|_{\mathfrak N_X}
 \le \frac{\theta_k}{P_k}\,
       a_{j,k}\,e^{-c\,d(i,X)}.
 \tag{OT.7}
\]
A fixed enlargement of \(X\) and fixed conversion constants may be included in this notation. If the operation uses transformed old fields, their full tangents belong on the left side; they are not silently frozen.

#### Proposition OT.1 — fixed-old-variable log-density response

On the active common support of \(M_0\), the ordinary logarithmic density change
\[
 F_X(z,\xi;\eta)
 =\sigma(\Phi(z,\xi),\eta)
       -\sigma(\Phi(0,\xi),\eta)
 \tag{OT.8}
\]
obeys
\[
 |\partial_i F_X|
 \le \frac{C\epsilon_{T,j}}{r_T}
        \frac{\theta_k}{P_k}
        a_{j,k}e^{-c\,d(i,X)}
 \tag{OT.9}
\]
uniformly in the old variables. The same statement holds for differences of two numerator or denominator log contributions if each is retained with its actual signed coefficient and admitted disc.

**Proof.** At the actual argument value, use the affine native line through that point in direction \(\partial_i\Phi\). A disc of radius \(r_T/\|\partial_i\Phi\|_{\mathfrak N_X}\) is admitted. Cauchy applied to the scalar analytic function \(\sigma(\,\cdot\,,\eta)\), with bound (OT.4), gives
\[
 |D\sigma(\Phi,\eta)[\partial_i\Phi]|
 \le C\epsilon_{T,j}r_T^{-1}
          \|\partial_i\Phi\|_{\mathfrak N_X}.
\]
The subtraction in (OT.8) has zero current derivative. Insert (OT.7). No derivative of a moving hard support is taken: the common support is a premise, just as in the source representation. \(\square\)

On the current support \(\|z\|_\infty\le P_k\), all higher current derivatives follow from the full current contour:
\[
 |\partial_{i_1}\cdots\partial_{i_m}F_X|
 \le \frac{C\epsilon_{T,j}}{r_T}\theta_k a_{j,k}
      (m-1)!(2/P_k)^m
      e^{-c\max_s d(i_s,X)}.
 \tag{OT.10}
\]
This is the finite-dimensional Cauchy argument of BC16.1. It differentiates the full composition, including every higher derivative of the current-coordinate map. Mixed native derivatives use the remaining positive native radii, not an unverified raw-coordinate radius.

### 4. The component mass has the same endpoint response

The scalar unmarked mass must also retain its denominator. Under the hypotheses above, choose the common ceiling so that, after recentering on the whole contour,
\(\sup|\sigma|\le\log(3/2)\). Put
\[
 Z_X(z,\xi)=M_0(e^{\sigma(\Phi(z,\xi),\cdot)}),\qquad
 \mathcal F_X=\log\frac{Z_X(z,\xi)}{Z_X(0,\xi)}.
 \tag{OT.11}
\]
Use the logarithm determined continuously from the real positive reference.

#### Proposition OT.2 — no inverse-small-mass loss

The normalizer is nonzero on the stated chart, and
\[
 |\partial_i\mathcal F_X|
 \le 3\sup_\eta
       |\partial_i\sigma(\Phi(z,\xi),\eta)|.
 \tag{OT.12}
\]
Thus (OT.9)–(OT.10) hold for \(\mathcal F_X\) with a further fixed constant.

**Proof.** Let \(m_0=M_0(1)>0\) and \(\pi_0=M_0/m_0\). Then
\[
 \left|\pi_0(e^\sigma)-1\right|
 \le e^{\|\sigma\|_\infty}-1\le\tfrac12.
\]
Hence \(|Z_X|\ge m_0/2\), and a holomorphic logarithm exists on the corresponding ball. Differentiation under the finite positive integral gives
\[
 \partial_i\log Z_X
 =\frac{\pi_0(e^\sigma\partial_i\sigma)}
        {\pi_0(e^\sigma)}.
\]
Its modulus is at most \(3\sup|\partial_i\sigma|\). The factor \(m_0\) cancels exactly. Apply the current Cauchy estimate afterward. \(\square\)

This proposition is a response estimate for the scalar mass. It does not permit replacing the conditional law with its mass when an inherited activity or a mark depends on the old integration variables. At fixed old variables, use Proposition OT.1. If a complete old integral is actually performed, retain the dependence of every remaining factor in it.

### 5. Which ordinary spatial sum would suffice

The fixed-chart propositions require much less than the full M-03 marked carrier norm. For example, if one proves on the actual current map
\[
 a_{j,k}\le C(1+k-j)^b e^{-\Gamma(k-j)}
 \tag{OT.13}
\]
with enough geometric reserve, and the eligible component footprints have their source bound
\(\operatorname{diam}_j X\le CMR_j\), their contribution can be summed by the same endpoint and old-root mechanism as BC16.

The old components in a fixed history are not an unrestricted sum over every possible large polymer. At a fixed age their chosen disjoint roots obey the old-grid count \(CL^{4(k-j)}\). Their diameter factors must still be paid. If
\[
 R_jL^{-(k-j)}\le C_R R_k
 \tag{OT.14}
\]
and the actual current guard supplies distance at least \(c_RMR_k\), one can choose the fixed spatial exponent small enough that a fixed part of that guard pays \(e^{\gamma\operatorname{diam}_k X}\). The remaining guard pays the polynomial diameter factors in the higher-order counting integrals. The estimate
\[
 \sup_{s\ge0}s^N e^{-as}\le N!a^{-N}
 \tag{OT.15}
\]
then gives the same fixed factorial type as BC16, rather than a constant depending on the diameter or on derivative order.

The factors \(R_j^{d+2}p_1(g_j)^2q_0(g_j)\) in (OT.4) are fixed polynomials in \(\ell_j\) at the stated source scope. AF comparison bounds their ratios by a fixed polynomial in age and \(\ell_k\). They can be retained explicitly in the age sum and in a prefactor \(C g_k\ell_k^{N_T}\), with one fixed exponent \(N_T\). The common ceiling makes this prefactor tend to zero.

These are sufficient ordinary component-summation conditions. They must be verified with the actual old component footprints and current-to-component map; this section does not claim their application from a bare scalar bound. In particular an enlarged footprint caused by an unaccounted old gauge transformation must not be discarded.

### 6. The exact remaining application issue

The source bound (OT.4), the ordinary input of (OT.5), and Propositions OT.1–OT.2 are available. The decisive remaining issue is to match the **actual fixed-old-variable current pullback** to that native chart and prove (OT.7), without changing its meaning.

For a primitive \(B^{(j)}(X)\), AD17.3/JI.39 compensates the actual gauge at the surviving old adjoint-field basepoints. The full \(T'_j(X)\) kernel also contains raw \(V''\), graph/delta and gauge-fixing factors and hidden older fields. Primitive-B covariance alone is not pointwise simultaneous covariance of this complete kernel.

There are several logically valid ways to finish the ordinary application, each requiring its own proof:

1. Show that the literal actual axial input is admitted to the ordinary source domain. A bounded axial gauge matrix does not suffice, because a fine-edge potential can still violate the old physical scale.
2. Prove a pointwise fixed-old-variable analytic replacement for the entire variable-dependent part of the kernel. Every old raw endpoint and adjoint slot affected by that replacement must be matched or retained with a quantified analytic radius. If a nodal interpolation is used, its spacing and physical weights must be checked for the actual post-R hidden histories, not borrowed from only the surviving primitive-B slots.
3. Prove an exact joint source-free coordinate change, with its full Jacobian and selector/gauge-fixing transformation, that represents the same conditional density in a common admissible chart. Merely changing variables in \(T'_j1\) does not prove this assertion when the rest of the integrand depends on those old variables.
4. Find an exact primary reassignment of the complete current-dependent coefficient difference into the ordinary B activity and prove its current budget there.

At present this note does not supply one of these matching results. Consequently it does not conclude that \(\mathcal K_{T,k}=0\), nor that the ordinary old-operation contribution meets the complete C-05/C-06 current budget.

This ordinary matching issue is narrower than H-03: it requires no renormalized marked input, regulator pushforward, moving-selector finite difference, or strong exponential-volume carrier norm. H-03 remains open even if this ordinary issue is resolved. Conversely deferring all marked reset estimates to H-03 does not erase a current-dependent old factor from a present unmarked conditional density.


### 7. The fully integrated alternative is a different exact representation

LFR II pp.391–392 supplies an alternative that was not used in Sections 1–6. It should not be conflated with the pointwise operation (1.71). The following records its exact scope and the changes in the localization norm.

#### 7.1 Which components and which factors are integrated

At a fixed R step \(j\), let \(Y_1,\ldots,Y_m\) be the **second-class eligible components** in LFR II p.378: components of the selected set \(Z\) for which the final large-field characteristic function is retained. Write
\[
 \tau_i=T'_j(Y_i)1,\qquad
 \Pi_i=\tau_i^{-1}T'_j(Y_i).
 \tag{OT.16}
\]
At real admitted data, \(\tau_i>0\) and \(\Pi_i\) is a probability kernel on the complete old variables in this component. On the primary complex domain, the same analytic normalized operation is defined by the small-tilt argument of Section 4. Its uniformly bounded total variation is precisely the input used on p.391.

Let \(B_{\rm in}\) be the sum of the boundary activities whose localization domains meet \(\bigcup_iY_i\). The source identity (1.103) is
\[
 \Bigl(\prod_iT'_j(Y_i)\Bigr)e^{B_{\rm in}}
  =\Bigl(\prod_i\tau_i\Bigr)
       \Bigl(\prod_i\Pi_i\Bigr)e^{B_{\rm in}}.
 \tag{OT.17}
\]
The bracket on the right, **including every occurrence of the old variables in \(B_{\rm in}\)**, is then integrated and expanded:
\[
 \Bigl(\prod_i\Pi_i\Bigr)e^{B_{\rm in}}
     =\exp\!\left(\sum_X\widehat B^{(j)}(X)\right).
 \tag{OT.18}
\]
This is the second use of the convergent expansion (1.98), as prescribed on p.391. It is an exact identity of the source-free density. It is not the replacement \(T'_j(Y_i)F\mapsto\tau_iF\).

The final density is (1.104). In that representation the factors associated with \(Y_i\) are their retained characteristic functions, their gauge-fixing delta functions, and \(\tau_i\), all multiplying the density. There is no remaining integration operation belonging to these \(Y_i\). Printed p.392 states that \(\tau_i\) depends only on the retained raw field \(V_j\) on \(Y_i\), and therefore is not involved in subsequent RG integrations. Thus, for the actual next inner-current operation, these raw variables and these multiplicative factors are fixed.

This fixed-data conclusion belongs to **(1.104)**. It does not assert that the earlier \(T'_j(Y_i)\) kernel, with its old variables unintegrated, was independent of the native field or current.

#### Proposition OT.3 — exact removal of the completed-kernel response

Adopt the alternative (1.104) at every completed R step. Then the completed eligible-component sector contributes no inner-current derivative through its multiplicative masses, cutoffs or delta functions. Its entire remaining dependence is in the new boundary activities \(\widehat B^{(j)}(X)\) produced by (OT.18). The hidden old variables integrated in \(\prod_i\Pi_i\) are not arguments of these activities.

**Proof.** Equations (OT.17)–(OT.18) are the source’s exact factorization and cluster expansion. The dependency statement for the factors \(\tau_i\) is the explicit final statement on p.392. The next RG step integrates raw fields only in the new small-field region; the retained raw fields on \(Y_i\) lie outside it. The characteristic and delta factors in (1.104) use those same retained raw arguments. Therefore all their current derivatives are zero at fixed retained data. Every other contribution of the old variables has already been included in (OT.18). \(\square\)

The operators denoted \(T''_j(Z_j)\) in (1.104), for **unreplaced components**, are still present. Proposition OT.3 makes no assertion of their independence. Their ordinary Gaussian/Schur or other coefficient response must be handled separately.

The argument list is obtained by actually applying the kernels to the entire exponential. An integrated hidden field cannot then be treated as a surviving external variable. Conversely a raw retained parameter is not discarded merely because the old histories inside its component have been integrated.

       (X,\widetilde\alpha_0,\widetilde\alpha_1)
 \tag{OT.19}

used in (1.65), (1.68) and (1.99), with the remaining old arguments fixed in their admitted support. This is a property of the complete normalized integrals, not an inference from a real mass bound alone.

For simultaneous real gauge transformations of all surviving external arguments, the resulting activities have the effective-action covariance required by the source induction. This is the scoped primary import of the final p.392 statement that (1.104) may be used in the inductive description of the effective action and proves Theorem 1. #### 7.4 Eligible-component geometry and the ordinary current application

At its birth scale every \(Y_i\) is a whole component of the selected pre-R large-field set. LFR I p.177, condition (i), bounds its containing cube by \(100MR_j\). \[
 \operatorname{diam}_jY_i\le C_d100MR_j,
 \qquad
 \operatorname{dist}_j(Y_i,Y_{i'})\ge c_d MR_j
 \quad(i\ne i').
 \tag{OT.27}
\]
The fixed constants allow the Euclidean/supremum norm convention. The corresponding periodic statement is used on a torus.

A contracted tree visiting \(N\) different components consequently pays a connector cost at least \(cR_j(N-1)\), in \(M\)-cube units. An ordinary path through a component costs at most \(CR_j\). Replacing the zero-cost passages along a contracted tree by actual paths therefore gives
\[
 \operatorname{diam}_j X
   \le CM\bigl(\ell_{\mathcal Y}(X)+R_j\bigr),
 \tag{OT.28}
\]
for its connected carrier. Only **one** component-diameter term remains unpaid. This estimate concerns the diameter and current endpoint tube; it does not pay the volume \(R_j^d\) of all interior \(M\)-cubes.

In the actual later current step, the growing current-to-old-region guard can pay that one remaining \(R_jL^{-(k-j)}\) term. The outside-carrier decay pays the other distances. ### 8. Repeated use: active tags and preparatory failures

The final sentence of LFR II p.392 permits repeated use of (1.104). There is also a relevant explicit primary statement in CMP119 p.262: the boundary induction can be written after resumming each carrier to contain or miss whole large-field components, with \(d_j(X)\) replaced by \(d_j(X\setminus Z_j)\), and the constructions and proofs remain valid. Thus a relative-carrier induction is a primary option, not an invented interpretation of one scalar identity.

For the present current theorem, however, contracting **all** of an arbitrarily large unreplaced component would lose too much spatial information. A stronger bookkeeping choice keeps the particular bounded components that have actually been integrated, or are being used as bounded over-carriers. Call these the active tags. The geometric assertions below are proved; preservation of the complete quantitative tagged activity norm must be checked in the assembly and is not inferred from the weaker all-\(Z_j\) statement alone.

#### 8.1 All-or-none consumption and uniform mixed-age separation

An ordinary next T step has \(\Omega_{r+1}\subset\Lambda_r\), so every retained tag stays inside the next pre-R large-field region. A selected current R component is a whole connected component of that region. Thus it contains each older tag completely or is disjoint from it.

When a new eligible component \(X\) is processed, old tags inside it may be replaced in the carrier bookkeeping by the single over-carrier \(X\). Its physical diameter is at most \(C100MR_rh_r\). Every older tag outside \(X\) lies in another current component, separated from \(X\) by at least \(cMR_rh_r\). AF comparison gives
\[
 R_jh_j\le C_RR_rh_r\quad(j\le r),
 \tag{OT.29}
\]
so the older tags have no larger physical diameter than a fixed multiple of this same scale.

Starting with no tags and applying this observation inductively proves that the active tags form an antichain, and that
\[
 \operatorname{dist}(Y,Y')
 \ge c_{\rm tag}\max\{\operatorname{diam}Y,
                         \operatorname{diam}Y'\}
 \quad(Y\ne Y').
 \tag{OT.30}
\]
This also holds for tags of different birth ages. The constant may depend on the fixed geometric factors \(100,M,L\) and the AF comparison, but not on the age or cutoff.

A tree crossing such separated atoms pays each atom diameter through an adjacent outside connector, apart from one starting atom. The ordinary diameter comparison (OT.28) therefore extends to mixed-age tags, with one remaining largest physical tag radius. This is the geometric input to a tagged ordinary current sum.

#### 8.2 What a failed R preparation actually leaves

LFR I p.181, (1.22)–(1.23), pp.182–183, (1.25)–(1.28), and p.188, (1.55), exclude **whole originally selected components** when a newly introduced large-field alternative occurs. A failed part of an original component is not silently declared a new regular current region.

The density representation nevertheless has changed: preliminary integrations may already have been performed. LFR I p.191 explicitly retains the terms created before stopping as boundary terms. Pages 200–201 state that the operations on the remaining components have several intermediate forms, ranging from the original operation to partly prepared operations. They are not claimed to be the unchanged original \(T\).

Safe carrier bookkeeping is therefore to keep the whole attempted eligible component \(X\) as an over-carrier on every failed branch. Its old sub-tags have been absorbed into that same bounded carrier. This preserves (OT.30), never removes an unpaid hole, and is valid even when some inner variables have already been integrated. The final unremoved large-field region in LFR II (1.101)–(1.104) still contains these excluded components.

This geometric statement does not settle their remaining density response. The source’s localized selectors, Gaussian forms, prepared backgrounds and any assigned boundary/Jacobian terms still have to be checked in the actual intermediate representation.

#### Proposition OT.5 — old canonical masses survive every preparatory prefix

Suppose the alternative (1.104) has been used at earlier completed steps. Fix a newly selected eligible component at scale \(r\), and put \(h=r-N\), as in LFR I p.178. Every older canonical mass in this component belongs to the untouched deep factor of that preparation. It is not partially integrated by a successful or interrupted preparatory prefix.

**Proof.** The last \(N\) steps have no newly created large-field region in the selected component, by eligibility condition (ii). LFR I p.178, (1.2), therefore splits the operation into the last \(N\) ordinary one-step operations and \(T_h(Z_h)\), with the displayed raw \(\chi_h\) factor. Canonical masses born at an earlier index \(b<h\) are contained in that deep operation. A mass born at \(b=h\) is a multiplication factor depending on \(V_h|Y_i\), with \(Y_i\subset Z_h\).

The actual determining-set formulas (1.15)–(1.17), p.180, integrate the successive shell variables and keep their remaining inner restrictions. Formula (1.11), p.179, reads
\(Z''_s=(\Omega_s^{\sim5})^c\cap Z\) in its stated index range; the index is \(s\), not \(s-1\). There is no step integrating a deep \(V_h|Z_h\) coordinate into those shells.

The decisive final factorization is printed in (1.76), p.194:
\[
 \int dV_h|Z_h\,
       \chi_h(\Omega_h\cap Z_h)\,T_h(Z_h)
 \quad\hbox{and a separate integral over the remaining }V'' .
 \tag{OT.31}
\]
The source expressly states that these two integration regions are disjoint and that the first integral is left unchanged in the following analysis. This is an exact variable factorization, not an inference from the support of a background-dependent coefficient. In particular the endpoint mass \(\tau_h(V_h|Y_i)\) remains in the first factor. The same is true before the full prefix has finished: stopping a sequence of shell integrations cannot have consumed a variable that is explicitly retained throughout (1.15)–(1.17). The failed-branch rule keeps that deep factor.

Eventually the **full** R component operation integrates it along with all the other old factors. That is precisely the complete \(T'_r\) entering (OT.17), not a partial hidden kernel. \(\square\)

Thus the canonical choice does not resurrect an arbitrary normalized hidden component kernel inside a failed preparation. It leaves fixed raw canonical multipliers, the unchanged deep operation, the actual remaining ordinary/prefix Gaussian factors and fixed selectors, and the boundary activities generated by the completed shell integrations. Their Gaussian/native-domain and selector-relocation responses are still the distinct ordinary-prefix analysis. No independence of those coefficients is inferred from Proposition OT.5.

#### 8.4 The remaining quantitative induction check

The tagged norm is preserved by a localization or product graph whenever all its new connecting edges carry the source’s physical decay, since contracting a tag only shortens a connecting distance. It is preserved by an overlapping cluster union because contracted tree size is subadditive. A completed normalized old integration can add a zero-cost connection only inside its actual eligible component; that is precisely the new tag just recorded.

A successful removal of an eligible component is different: its interior is no longer a free atom. The source pays this cost explicitly. LFR II p.389, before (1.95), combines the component factor with
\(2\kappa(100R_j)^d\) and requires the remaining negative \(p_0(g_j)\) reserve. This is the appropriate place to pay a full interior tree/volume if that atom is removed. A failed branch does not have that payment and retains its tag instead.

These observations specify the proof that has to be carried through the actual repeated source expansion. They do not license replacing the original full-tree boundary estimate by an unspecified weaker scalar bound, nor do they prove a marked posterior theorem. The completed C-05 assembly must record the tagged carrier majorant, the successful-removal payment, the prefix/failed-operation response, and the current endpoint sum explicitly.

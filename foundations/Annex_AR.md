# The complete primitive domain and fixed older slots

**Annex AR.** The complete Wilson application assumes CL (compatibility of the conditional representation) and CR (uniform composed reconstruction response with the prescribed earlier innovations and raw variables fixed). The ordinary and completed sectors must control the same subtracted primitive, with its actual support, tags, connectors, stopped prefixes and repeated histories. The two-age estimate must retain both the evaluation and forcing ages. These are uniform hypotheses for the all-history application. [Boundary induction](../boundary/boundary_induction.md) · [Conditional response and the continuum application](../continuum/conditional_to_continuum.md).

The normalized coupling and geometric hypotheses are retained throughout. Whenever the Wilson trajectory is identified through Gaussian calibration, GC includes the error bounded uniformly in depth. [Theorem catalogue](../THEOREMS_AND_OPEN_PROBLEMS.md).


This chapter distinguishes the independent-current extension of the primitive domain from the physical variation of an older raw slot. Applying these estimates to complete Wilson histories requires the composed-response and compatibility hypotheses above.

### 1. Results and the remaining source question

The direct printed product-domain shortcut has not been justified. CMP119 (3.43) contains a current radius \(C_1p_1(g_k)\), whereas the checked cutoff definitions use \(p_0(g_k)\) and the ratio \(A_1/A_0\). The function \(p_1\) in that formula has not been identified by a checked definition in the source chain. The later LFR primed preparation polynomial is a different threshold and is not imported. This note also records a separate printed scaling inconsistency in (3.16); it is not used to shrink the frozen full support.

Two actual argument-map facts can nevertheless be established without this radius identification:

1. In the CMP119 next-step substitution, all earlier fluctuation coordinates \(A_j\), \(j<k\), remain fixed inputs. The new current enters the old action through its displayed background-field argument. A gauge conversion used only to bound a reconstructed field need not be applied to those old arguments.
2. The relocation on \(S_{k+1}\) changes only exterior current-scale coordinates. Its Jacobian is exactly independent of the inner current variables and cancels from their normalized conditional density. Its full exterior weight remains present. The relocated inherited action may be estimated directly as a composed primitive function; a Taylor estimate in an earlier fluctuation coordinate is not algebraically necessary.

The source also supplies a useful fixed-fraction reserve at the actual real centers. It follows from the physical-current part of the minimizing-field regularity theorem, not from a curvature bound alone. Section 5 states the exact numerical reserve that the native-ball construction of Annex AQ must meet.

#### 2.2 The source chain outside the isolated formula

CMP119 carries forward the definitions of its preceding small-field papers. CMP109 Theorem 3 and its fluctuation construction use fixed small constants \(\varepsilon_0,\varepsilon_1\). CMP116 (1.20)–(1.22) and Lemma 1 use the absolute current radius \(g_k|B|<\varepsilon_1\); its displayed product domain (1.34) has \(\varepsilon_1/g_k\), not a defined logarithmic \(p_1(g_k)\). Thus those preceding statements provide genuine analytic-map information but do not identify the new notation in CMP119 (3.43).

The earlier reference [16] in that source chain is the three-dimensional stability paper, CMP102 (1985), 255–275, DOI 10.1007/BF01229380. It defines \(p(g)=b_0(1+\log g^{-1})^{p_0}\) near (7), uses enlarged fluctuation cutoffs near (18), (22) and (51), and obtains the smooth minimizing-field representation only modulo a gauge in (52). It does not supply a checked identification of the \(p_1\) in CMP119 (3.43) with the later unprimed cutoff. Several small printed cutoff subscripts or superscripts are ambiguous. No definition is inferred from those ambiguous characters.

CMP119, pp. 247, 250, 261, 267–269, 276 and 278, uses different symbols for the regular radius, the cutoff exponent and the older fluctuation threshold.

The later LFR polynomial bearing the same subscript belongs to the primed preparation threshold and has a lower logarithmic exponent than the unprimed cutoff. Inserting it into (3.43) would make the ratio in (AD17.2) tend to zero. Increasing a fixed coefficient cannot repair that mismatch. This is a reason to reject that proposed identification, not a proof that the published construction fails.

We distinguish three assertions:

- The source's complete construction is intended to evaluate the localized expressions throughout the relevant integration domain.
- Its displayed domain provides a quantitative radius if \(p_1\) is identified.
- The required numerical relation (AD17.2) has been proved.

The first assertion does not, by itself, establish the third. Defining the undefined symbol ourselves to mean \(P_k\) would not verify the analytic estimates that the printed domain is supposed to summarize.

#### 2.3 What (3.43) would supply if its radius were matched

The source statement is substantive. On p.276 it transfers the completed localization construction to the multiscale boundary, refers to the full generalized random-walk expansion, and places the remaining localized terms on (3.43). The current dependence is localized to \(Y\cap\Lambda_{k+1}^{(k)*}\). Inherited B terms are explicitly included by the formulas corresponding to CMP109 (3.6)–(3.7); p.277 retains the shrinking multiscale margins, heterogeneous Wilson coefficient and complete relocation action.

Consequently a verified radius (AD17.2), together with the actual enlarged background/current native margins and the printed complete carrier bound, would allow ordinary Cauchy differentiation of those localized functions. A new theorem about arbitrary prepared minimizing operators would then be unnecessary for this direct application. The already completed localization must be used with its actual carrier majorant and its full variable scope; ordinary boundedness of an unlocalized scalar function does not replace it.

At present the direct source shortcut stops at the exact missing relation (AD17.2). The alternative native-ball proof is a way to establish the required radius independently, without deciding the typographical intention of \(p_1\).

### 3. Earlier fluctuation coordinates in the actual next-step map

#### 3.1 The source argument list

CMP119 (2.20)–(2.23), printed p.258, distinguishes the outer integration operation from the effective action. The operation \(T^{(j)}\) retains and integrates \(A_j\) on \(Z_{j+1}\cap\Omega_{j+1}\), at its own lattice scale. The action is written as

\[
 A_k(U_k,\mathbf a)
 =-A(g_k^{-2}(\cdot),U_k)
       +E_k(U_k)+R_k(U_k)+B_k(U_k,\mathbf a)-E_k^{\rm vac},
 \tag{AD17.3}
\]

where \(\mathbf a=(A_0,\ldots,A_{k-1})\) records the earlier fluctuation inputs. The history sets are suppressed in the source notation but are retained here as fixed parameters.

In (3.15), printed pp.267–268, the new fluctuation \(A_k\) enters through

\[
 U_k\!\left(\exp i\,[g_kCA_k-hD(g_kCA_k)]\,V^{(k)}\right).
 \tag{AD17.4}
\]

The same inherited action \(E_k+R_k+B_k\) is evaluated at that argument and at \(U_{k+1}\). There is no replacement of an earlier \(A_j\) in this step. The only coordinate rescaling and constraint elimination described there concern the newly introduced \(A_k\).

#### Proposition AD17.1 — the old slots are fixed in the direct inherited pullback

For the actual current substitution (AD17.4), before the subsequent exterior relocation, a primitive inherited boundary term has the form

\[
 F_{j,X}(z;U_{k+1},a,\mathbf a,\mathbf S)
 =B^{(j)}\!\left(X,U_k(I_k(z,a)),\mathbf a,\mathbf S\cap X\right),
 \tag{AD17.5}
\]

where \(z\) and \(a\) are the inner and exterior pieces of the new \(A_k\), and \(I_k\) is the exact source map in (AD17.4). All earlier coordinates \(\mathbf a\) remain fixed. Therefore a current derivative of this direct function differentiates the displayed field map and any source-native independent-current substitution; it does not differentiate an old fluctuation argument.

**Proof.** The old variables are already integration variables of \(T_k\) in (2.18)–(2.23). The construction before (3.15) integrates and changes coordinates only in the new \(V_k\) variables, introduces their logarithm \(A'_k\), eliminates their new constraints, and rescales \(A'_k=g_kA_k\). The old operation \(T_k\) remains outside this new integral. Its earlier fluctuation coordinates are not included in any of those substitutions. Expanding the suppressed argument list of \(B_k\) using (2.23) therefore gives exactly (AD17.5). The final split into \(z\) and \(a\) is a restriction of \(A_k\) to disjoint coordinate sets and leaves the old list unchanged. $\square$

This is a statement about the actual fixed-source representative used in (3.15). It does not replace \(U_k\) by an arbitrary representative of its gauge orbit. A gauge converter used solely to estimate a reconstructed field/current can be kept inside that norm calculation. A gauge change used to replace the whole primitive B input is different: its action on the earlier fields must be accounted for. Sections 3.3–3.4 make that distinction explicit.

Fixed old coordinates do not imply that the earlier \(T_k\) weights, their coefficients or their constraint factors are independent of the native background. Their actual parameter dependence remains in the complete density and conditional assembly. Proposition AD17.1 removes only old-coordinate chain-rule terms from this direct inherited B pullback; it does not remove those weights or their background derivatives.

#### 3.2 Why fixing determining roots is not a general substitute

CMP119 (1.17) gives the adjoint law at the initial endpoint:

\[
 (R(u)A)(x,x')=u(x)A(x,x')u(x)^{-1}.
 \tag{AD17.6}
\]

CMP102 (4) fixes a variational gauge at the determining sites. Those facts alone do not make every old fluctuation invariant. An earlier \(A_{j-1}\) lives at \((j-1)\)-lattice sites in \(\Omega_j\cap\Lambda_j^c\), whereas the determining data on the surrounding \(j\)-layer use \(j\)-lattice sites. A noncentral \((j-1)\)-site need not be one of the fixed roots. A gauge can equal the identity at the roots and have a nontrivial value at that site, changing (AD17.6).

Thus a proof which actually gauge-transforms a primitive scalar must carry the actual transformed old arguments or prove a stronger identity for that particular gauge. Root fixing by itself is insufficient. The LFR II collar construction makes the same caution concrete: around (1.61), printed pp.373–374, it keeps the splice gauge because two representations meet at the collar. That gauge is not globally removable. Proposition AD17.1 avoids an additional change of old slots in the next current insertion; it does not delete a gauge already present inside the inherited primitive boundary function or inside an actual R-operation.

No analyticity in arbitrary complex old fluctuation coordinates is asserted here. The source primitive B bound is used with its actual old coordinates as fixed parameters, including its stated uniformity over their admitted real values.

#### 3.3 The actual input-gauge issue

The first sentence before CMP119 (2.34) specifies analyticity in \((U,J)\). The space in (2.34)–(2.39) contains those variables and their reconstructed fields. Property (ii) after (2.41) is an extension to that space; it does not state a complex product radius for the older fluctuation variables. Property (iii) does state simultaneous **real \(G\)-gauge covariance** of \(U,J,\mathbf a\).

CMP109, pp.265–267, first converts the relative axial minimizing field into a smooth Landau representative. It uses gauge invariance to expand the Wilson action. The inherited effective action in (2.10) is still written at the original \(U_k(\exp iB'V^{(k)})\); subsequent scalar gauge invariance is used in Section 3. In CMP119 the inherited B term also depends on \(\mathbf a\), so the corresponding replacement rotates those variables. Neither (3.15) nor Proposition AD17.1 proves that its axial input itself satisfies the strong complex relative-field condition (2.39).

This matters even when curvature and physical current are well controlled. Axial representations can put a coarse link increment onto a fine boundary bond. The relative potential on that bond then has size proportional to the increment divided by the fine mesh, whereas (2.39) demands the old physical layer scale. A bound on the gauge matrix alone does not remove this difference.

The literal estimate (3.19) is also narrower than such a claim: it bounds the ratio between \(V^{(k)}\) and its localized background average, using (3.18) and the cited gauge estimates. Its gauge compares two background minima. The formula does not by itself bound the different current-dependent converter in (3.15).

For real variables, simultaneous covariance gives the exact identity

\[
 B(U_{\rm AX},J_{\rm AX},\mathbf a)
   =B(U_{\rm L},J_{\rm L},\operatorname{Ad}_{u}\mathbf a),
 \qquad
 U_{\rm L}=U_{\rm AX}^{u}.
 \tag{AD17.18}
\]

A uniformly bounded complex \(u\) does not make the right side an admitted analytic function merely because the real older fields had a norm reserve. A complex old-field domain would have to be proved. The following construction avoids needing one.

#### 3.4 Smooth interpolation of the gauge values on old fluctuation sites

The construction uses the actual terminal-layer geometry of the old variables, not a new gauge fixing of the measure. It is performed separately for each primitive \(B^{(j)}(X)\), using only its actual older slots. Those slots were introduced by its creation step, so their indices are at most \(j-1\); subsequent dependence on newer fields enters its displayed background argument. This termwise choice is an analytic-extension identity for a scalar activity, not a change of integration variables.

Let \(h_j\) be the physical mesh of the \(j\)-lattice, \(h_{j+1}=Lh_j\). Let \(\mathcal N_j\) consist of the initial sites of oriented bonds carrying an older \(A_j\) on which the primitive in question actually depends. By CMP119 (2.20)–(2.21), these bonds are in

\[
 \Omega_{j+1}\cap Z_{j+1},
 \qquad Z_{j+1}=\Lambda_{j+1}^{c}.
 \tag{AD17.19}
\]

The interlacing (2.1) places the point support inside
\(\Omega_{j+1}\setminus\Omega_{j+2}\). The bond-star convention can move an initial endpoint by one \(h_j\)-step. The source's admitted layer buffers therefore put its actual terminal level \(n(x)\) in the fixed range
\[
 j\le n(x)\le j+2,\qquad x\in\mathcal N_j.
 \tag{AD17.19a}
\]
Here level zero denotes the fine exterior when it occurs. The slightly generous range retains both boundary conventions. No buffer between \(\Lambda_{j+1}\) and \(\Omega_{j+2}\) is needed for this assertion: a second layer boundary cannot be crossed in one \(h_j\)-step because the \(\Omega\)-history itself is buffered.

This point requires care after R. The sentence on p.256 about domains determined by a renormalization transformation is not a statement that R leaves the geometry unchanged. Indeed p.255 allows \(\Lambda_j=\Omega_j\) after R. LFR I, printed p.179, (1.10)–(1.13), explicitly rebuilds the history and states that the resulting \(\Omega''\) is admissible; its construction separates the newly introduced successive layers by an \(M\)-cube in their corresponding scale. We use this preserved **grading**, rather than assume that the old domains remain fixed.

For the admitted histories, fix a positive \(c_{\rm gr}\) such that two sites of terminal levels differing by at least two satisfy
\[
 |x-y|_\infty\ge c_{\rm gr}\max(h_{n(x)},h_{n(y)}).
 \tag{AD17.19b}
\]
This follows directly from the consecutive-domain separation. For example, if \(n(y)\ge n(x)+2\), then \(y\in\Omega_{n(y)}\), whereas \(x\notin\Omega_{n(y)-1}\); the separating layer has thickness at least the admitted fixed number of cubes at mesh \(h_{n(y)-1}\). The endpoint convention costs at most one step. With the already large block parameter, the conservative choice \(c_{\rm gr}=1/(4L)\) is available for the one-\(M\)-cube version as well as the larger original buffers. Empty layers do not produce old nodes.

Merge repeated nodal sites; their target is the same matrix \(u(x)\). For two distinct sites whose terminal levels differ by at most one, the nested odd-block grids and (AD17.19a) give
\[
 |x-y|_\infty\ge
  h_{\min(j(x),j(y))}
  \ge L^{-3}\max(h_{n(x)},h_{n(y)}).
\]
Together with (AD17.19b), this proves uniform separation with
\[
 c_{\rm sep}=\min\{c_{\rm gr},L^{-3}\},\qquad
 r_x=(c_{\rm sep}/8)h_{n(x)}.
 \tag{AD17.19c}
\]
The open nodal balls of radii \(r_x\) are disjoint. Each meets only a fixed adjacent range of terminal layers, so its physical weights and those at its center differ by fixed powers of \(L\) and the adjacent AF comparison. This handles the surviving explicit old fluctuation slots on the final admitted history, including the possibility that some slot sets are empty after R.

Fix the real baseline \(U_*\). On each such ball choose one of its real local regular gauges from (2.38), fixed throughout the analytic variation. Choose once a smooth scalar bump \(\psi\), supported in the unit ball, with \(\psi(0)=1\) and fixed first and second derivative bounds. At the very finest scale, a ball smaller than a fine lattice spacing is interpreted by sampling this bump; direct finite-difference bounds give the same conservative constants, and no local smooth gauge outside the fine exterior is needed.

**Proposition AD17.3 — fixed-old-field replacement by nodal gauge interpolation.** Suppose the actual current/background contour supplies a holomorphic gauge \(u(z)\), real \(G\)-valued on its real slice, relating the original primitive input to a smooth admitted source family as in (AD17.18). The selected formal family agrees with the minimizing family on its physical real slice; it need not be a minimum for arbitrary independent native parameters. Criticality is not required for the interpolation argument. Normalize the baseline converter to the identity using a fixed real gauge. Suppose its logarithm at the old sites obeys

\[
 \|\log u(z,x)\|\le r_u\alpha_{j+1},
 \qquad x\in\mathcal N_j,
 \tag{AD17.20}
\]

uniformly on that contour. The adjacent-layer \(\alpha\) ratios are bounded by the admitted AF comparison. Then there is a holomorphic fine-lattice gauge \(v(z)\), real \(G\)-valued on the real slice, with

\[
 v(z,x)=u(z,x)\quad(x\in\mathcal N_j,\ \text{all }j),
 \qquad
 \|\log v\|+h_n\|D_{U_*}\log v\|
       +h_n^2\|D_{U_*}^2\log v\|
       \le C_{\rm node}r_u\alpha_n
 \tag{AD17.21}
\]

on every actual layer \(n\). The constant depends on the fixed block size and regularity constants, not on the fine mesh or the number of layers. In particular it has no factor \(h_n/\eta\).

If the smooth family \(U_{\rm L}\) lies in a sufficiently small strong-native ball and the actual primitive domain certificate admits that ball, then the function

\[
 \mathcal F_{\rm node}(z)
 =B\!\left(U_{\rm L}(z)^{v(z)^{-1}},
           \operatorname{Ad}_{v(z)^{-1}}J_{\rm L}(z),
           \mathbf a\right)
 \tag{AD17.22}
\]

is a holomorphic extension of the original real inherited pullback. It has the primitive B value bound on its carrier. Every current or retained-background derivative of the original real function is the corresponding derivative of (AD17.22). No extension of B to arbitrary complex \(\mathbf a\) is required.

**Proof.** Put \(\lambda_x(z)=\log u(z,x)\). Conjugate it by the fixed real local regular gauge at \(x\), multiply by \(\psi((y-x)/r_x)\), and conjugate back by that local gauge at \(y\). Let \(\lambda(z,y)\) be the sum of these expressions over the distinct old sites and set \(v(z,y)=\exp\lambda(z,y)\). Disjoint supports make this a pointwise local sum with at most one nonzero term. It is holomorphic in \(z\), and unitary on the real slice. Its value at each old site is exactly \(\lambda_x\), hence \(v=u\) there.

In the local regular gauge, two finite differences of the bump cost at most \(C r_x^{-2}\), and one costs at most \(C r_x^{-1}\). The background covariant differences add terms bounded by its regular local potential and derivative. The common smallness ceiling makes these additions harmless. By (AD17.19a)–(AD17.19c), the normalized factors \(h_n/r_x\) and \(h_n^2/r_x^2\) are fixed constants depending on \(L\), and not on the number of levels. Equation (AD17.20) and the adjacent-layer comparison therefore give (AD17.21). Sampling the bump on the fine lattice has the same finite-difference bounds, including the case \(j=0\). This is the reason a fine axial jump is avoided.

Multiplication by \(v^{-1}\) changes the relative potential by a covariant derivative of \(\lambda\), plus the usual analytic products with the preexisting relative potential. One additional covariant difference uses \(D^2\lambda\). For \(\lambda\) uniformly small, the local logarithm and its first two differentials are bounded on a fixed neighborhood. Thus the value and first-gradient norm of the transformed relative field is at most \(C\) times the original norm plus \(C_{\rm node}r_u\).

The sufficient strong-native norm used here has exactly the source-required second-order **curl row**:

\[
 \|E\|_{\mathfrak N_{\rm curl}}
  =\sup_n\alpha_n^{-1}\max\{
       h_n|E|,\ h_n^2|\nabla_{U_*}E|,
       h_n^3|D_{U_*}^{*}D_{U_*}E|\}.
 \tag{AD17.24}
\]

We do not assert a component-Laplacian bound for the interpolated relative potential; that optional row would require an additional argument. The curl row follows from physical-current covariance. Put \(W=U_{\rm L}^{v^{-1}}=e^{i\eta E}U_*\). The current obeys
\(\mathscr J(W)=\operatorname{Ad}_{v^{-1}}\mathscr J(U_{\rm L})\).
The derivative of the link exponential is an invertible, uniformly bounded local chart matrix. The exact chart identity therefore reads

\[
 J_*+\Delta_*E+V_{0,*}'(E)
       =\mathsf T(E)\mathscr J(W).
 \tag{AD17.25}
\]

Here \(\Delta_*=D_{U_*}^{*}D_{U_*}\) plus the local curvature multiplier, with the source convention, and \(\mathsf T(E)\) is the bounded chart matrix. The value/gradient Wilson remainder estimate, used in RM.23, bounds the nonlinear term by
\(C r^2\alpha_n^2h_n^{-3}\) when the first two rows of (AD17.24) are bounded by \(r\). The real baseline current, the original smooth current, and their difference have their stipulated strong layer bounds. Subtract their values at the center in (AD17.25), and use bounded adjoint matrices. This gives
\[
 h_n^3|D_{U_*}^{*}D_{U_*}E|
       \le C(r_{\rm L}+C_{\rm node}r_u)\alpha_n
\]
after one common smallness ceiling. The same formula accommodates a nonzero strong real-center current: the chart-factor difference is bounded by the perturbation times that small baseline current and is absorbed in the displayed linear bound. This establishes the curl row without taking a third spatial derivative of \(\lambda\). The independent-current slot transforms by the same adjoint representation. The reconstructed clauses still use the actual whole-ball primitive domain certificate; they are not inferred from a first-response estimate.

On the real slice, (AD17.18) holds. At every old bond basepoint \(\operatorname{Ad}_{v}\mathbf a=\operatorname{Ad}_{u}\mathbf a\). Applying the source's simultaneous gauge covariance a second time gives

\[
 B(U_{\rm L},J_{\rm L},\operatorname{Ad}_{u}\mathbf a)
 =
 B(U_{\rm L}^{v^{-1}},
       \operatorname{Ad}_{v^{-1}}J_{\rm L},\mathbf a).
 \tag{AD17.23}
\]

The last expression has fixed real older fields and analytic native field/current arguments. Its analyticity and value bound are exactly the primitive source statement once those arguments are admitted. The real equality proves that it is an extension of the actual real pullback and fixes all its jets. One does not first assert that B is defined at the complex axial field, and no integration contour or outer measure has been changed. \(\square\)

The construction preserves endpoint locality. A marked derivative of a nodal logarithm has the same bound as that of the actual \(u\), up to the uniform logarithm constant. Interpolation uses only sites within the radius in (AD17.19c) of the output. For a primitive born at index \(j\), the relevant node meshes are at most a fixed multiple of \(h_j\). There is no unnecessary later coarse node whose bump reaches the primitive from a distant scale. Thus a source one-endpoint estimate changes by a fixed spatial connector, with the same layer normalization and a fixed decrease of exponential decay. On a primitive carrier this enlarges only its local collar; its cost is absorbed by the available carrier decay. There is no factor proportional to the number of old fields because the interpolation supports have bounded overlap. The number of old nodes per terminal block is bounded by a fixed power of \(L\), by the same separation argument.

More explicitly, suppose the actual smooth family and converter satisfy on the common complex contour

\[
 \alpha_n^{-1}\!\left(
 h_n|\partial_i A_{\rm L}|+
 h_n^2|\nabla_{U_*}\partial_i A_{\rm L}|+
 h_n^3|\partial_i\mathscr J(U_{\rm L})|\right)
 \le E_i e^{-c d(i,x)},
 \qquad
 \alpha_n^{-1}|\partial_i\log u(x_j)|
 \le E_i e^{-c d(i,x_j)}
 \tag{AD17.26}
\]

for the nearby old nodes \(x_j\), with the corresponding independent-current bound. Bump differentiation gives the same bound for
\(\partial_i\lambda,h_nD\partial_i\lambda,h_n^2D^2\partial_i\lambda\).
Differentiating the analytic relative-potential formula gives the first two rows of
\(\partial_iE\) with the same majorant. Differentiate the exact covariance of \(\mathscr J(W)\), then (AD17.25). The new current term is the conjugated original current derivative plus a commutator with \(\partial_i v\); the commutator is bounded by the strong current times the already bounded nodal derivative. The curvature multiplier and the differentiated local remainder have the same normalized estimates. Thus

\[
 \|\partial_i(E,J_{\rm aux})\|_{\mathfrak N_{\rm curl},X}
     \le C E_i e^{-c' d(i,X)}.
 \tag{AD17.27}
\]

This proves the direct one-endpoint tangent estimate in the fixed-old-field input; it is not a tensor-product bound inferred only from a scalar value bound.

If the complete primitive ball has radius \(r_{\rm nat}\), it suffices to take the smooth-family radius and converter reserve so that
\[
 C(r_{\rm L}+C_{\rm node}r_u)<r_{\rm nat}/4.
\]
The converter estimate \(r_u\le C_g r_{\rm L}\), when supplied by the actual normalized inverse-axial gauge, reduces this to the single fixed choice
\[
 r_{\rm L}<
 \frac{r_{\rm nat}}{8C(1+C_{\rm node}C_g)}.
 \tag{AD17.28}
\]
This reserve is independent of age and derivative order. At any resulting point, a genuine affine directional disc of radius at least \(r_{\rm nat}/2\) in the chart remains in the full primitive domain. Cauchy and (AD17.27) give the source-activity first-derivative bound with its unchanged value majorant. Subsequent current derivatives and the old-root/carrier sums are then the already proved BC16 estimates. The complete-domain premise, rather than a bound on the derivative of a domain predicate, is what licenses this last Cauchy step.

**Actual inputs identified in Annexes AP and AQ.** RM.3 and (RM.26)–(RM.36) in Annex AP construct the normalized inverse-axial gauge in the single chosen symmetric source family. Its unambiguous endpoint row is
\[
 |u^{-1}\partial_i u|(x)
 \le C h_s\sup_{\Delta_s(x)}|\partial_i A_{\rm L}|.
\]
No extra factor \(\alpha_n\) is inserted into this row. JI.32–JI.40 in Annex AO apply it to the actual full \(2P_k\) current contour, retain the fixed real reference transformation, and compare with the running native radii. This supplies (AD17.20) and (AD17.26) with their actual coupling/age factors. The raw \(M^j\) gauge covariance uses the restriction of the fine gauge at \(j\)-grid sites; it is not the averaged gauge appearing in corrected relative coordinates. Thus its action on each old \(A_j\) is precisely the action used in (AD17.18).

NP.5 in Annex AQ supplies the genuine strong ball for the U-dependent reconstructed clauses, with the independent-current extension specified there. ### 4. The current-scale exterior relocation

#### 4.1 Its exact support and parameter dependence

CMP119 (3.20), p.269, defines

\[
 \Lambda_{k+1}
 =\Omega_{k+1}^{\sim-2}\cap(R'_{k+1}{}^{\sim})^c,
 \qquad
 S_{k+1}=(\Omega_{k+1}\setminus\Omega_{k+1}^{\sim-1})\cup R_{k+1}.
 \tag{AD17.7}
\]

Here the enlargement uses the stated \(LMR_{k+1}\) cubes. The extra layer between \(S_{k+1}\) and \(\Lambda_{k+1}\) also separates their free-bond coordinate sets; it is much wider than the fixed bond endpoint convention. The later rectangular enlargement is of components of \(\Lambda_{k+1}^c\), so it can only shrink \(\Lambda_{k+1}\) and preserves this separation.

The source defines \(H^{(k)}\) from two background averages in (3.22). It depends on \(U_{k+1}\) and the fixed geometry, restricted to the stated exterior region \(\Lambda_{k+1}^c\cap\Lambda_k\). It is independent of the new inner integration coordinate \(z=A_k|_{\Lambda_{k+1}^{(k)*}}\).

For \(b\in S_{k+1}^{(k)*}\), the change of variables is (1.22) with \(H^{(k)}(b)\) in place of \(H_{1,\Lambda_1}(b)\). In current notation it has the form

\[
 a_b^{\rm old}=\mathcal R_b(a_b;H_b)
 =\frac1{ig_k}\log\!\left(\exp(ig_ka_b)
                 \exp[-i\chi_b(a_b)H_b]\right),
 \tag{AD17.8}
\]

where \(\chi_b\) is the smooth real cutoff used by the source on the specified subset, and is replaced by (1) on the remaining bonds. It is not a source variable and is not analytically continued as an arbitrary complex function. For each fixed real exterior coordinate \(a\), its value and the derivatives appearing in the Jacobian are fixed coefficients. The source current radius and the logarithm convention ensure that this is the indicated local change of variables.

#### Proposition AD17.2 — exact cancellation of the relocation Jacobian in the inner conditional law

Let \(\rho_k(z,a;U_{k+1},\mathbf a,\omega)\) be the full density at this step, with all action terms, nonlinear constraint factors and history information \(\omega\) retained. The exterior relocation (AD17.8) produces

\[
 \widetilde\rho_k(z,a;\cdots)
 =J_{\mathcal R}(a;U_{k+1},\omega)
       \rho_k(z,\mathcal R(a;H^{(k)});\cdots).
 \tag{AD17.9}
\]

The Jacobian \(J_{\mathcal R}\) is independent of \(z\). Wherever the conditional normalization is nonzero,

\[
 \frac{\widetilde\rho_k(z,a;\cdots)}
      {\int\widetilde\rho_k(z',a;\cdots)\,dz'}
 =
 \frac{\rho_k(z,\mathcal R(a;H^{(k)});\cdots)}
      {\int\rho_k(z',\mathcal R(a;H^{(k)});\cdots)\,dz'}.
 \tag{AD17.10}
\]

In particular the relocation Jacobian's current gradient and current Hessian vanish identically, before any estimate. The cancellation is an identity in the admitted retained/background parameters as well, so every existing mixed current/native jet of this conditional Jacobian contribution is zero. This does not assert that the unnormalized outer Jacobian has zero native derivatives.

**Proof.** The support statement (AD17.7) shows that the change does not act on an inner free coordinate. Its displacement uses \(a\), \(U_{k+1}\) and fixed history information, and none of these is an inner coordinate. Thus the derivative matrix of the full change in ((z,a)) is block triangular with an identity inner block; its nontrivial determinant depends only on the exterior variables. Substitution gives (AD17.9), and the common factor cancels in the quotient. This is an exact algebraic cancellation. It is not an assertion that the Jacobian is one. $\square$

The source's induced \(V^{(k)}(S_{k+1})\) includes more than this Jacobian. It also includes the change in every original action term. Those changes do not generally cancel. Nor does the Jacobian disappear from the outer marginal: the integral in the denominator of (AD17.10), multiplied by \(J_{\mathcal R}\), remains its weight. Native background derivatives of that outer weight are not estimated by the zero inner Hessian.

#### 4.2 Direct treatment of the relocated inherited action

Rather than estimate a Taylor expansion of a primitive activity in the displacement, one can retain its exact composition:

\[
 \widetilde F_{j,X}(z;\eta)
 =B^{(j)}\!\left(
       X,U_k(I_k(z,\mathcal R(a;H^{(k)}))),
       \mathbf a,\mathbf S\cap X\right).
 \tag{AD17.11}
\]

This is the source activity after the actual change of variables. It retains every induced term in that activity. Conditional normalization subtracts its value at a fixed inner reference coordinate only after the whole transformed action has been assembled.

If its actual field/current arguments are admitted to the primitive B domain, the primitive value majorant remains \(B_0e^{-\kappa d_j(X)}\). No inverse displacement radius is introduced merely by representing the changed activity as a Taylor difference. Its derivative with respect to \(z\) is the original exact current-map derivative evaluated at the shifted exterior datum. The derivative of the displacement itself with respect to \(z\) is zero. The constraint map \(I_k\) still contains its full nonlinear dependence on \(z\) and the exterior datum; it is not replaced by its linearization.

This observation removes an unnecessary argument, not the domain requirement. The shifted coarse datum must remain in the common original-map contour, and its entire primitive U-dependent output must pass the native B admission test. The same condition is needed when the native background is complex, because \(H^{(k)}\) then has its actual analytic continuation. No holomorphy of the smooth real cutoff in an arbitrary complex exterior coordinate is used.

For the conditional quadratic form, exterior relocation changes the retained linear force and constant term. It does not change its current-current block, because the background precision is fixed in that conditional split. The force must remain in the Gaussian mean. Nonquadratic classical, constraint, measure and E/R terms are likewise retained at the shifted datum; they are not removed by (AD17.10).

Thus the complete source \(V^{(k)}\) may either be estimated in its published localized Taylor representation, or handled by the exact fully relocated action plus the explicit exterior Jacobian. Both expressions represent the same density. The second route is sufficient for current derivative estimates if the full shifted-data domain is proved, and does not require first constructing analytic derivatives in an earlier fluctuation variable.

### 5. Actual real-center reserve for the native B ball

#### 5.1 The physical current is part of the primary regularity theorem

CMP102 (2), p.278, defines regular configurations by both a plaquette bound and

\[
 |D_U^*\partial U|\le \varepsilon_0\eta^2h_n^{-3}
 \quad\text{on an actual layer of proper mesh }h_n.
 \tag{AD17.12}
\]

The physical current used in the later expansion is \(J_U=\eta^{-2}\operatorname{Im}D_U^*\partial U\), so (AD17.12) gives \(h_n^3|J_U|\le\varepsilon_0\). Theorem 1, (8), places the actual minimum in this space with \(\varepsilon_0=B_3\varepsilon_1\). Its (9)–(10) also supplies the local value, first-difference and second-order chart bounds. Thus a strong physical-current bound is a primary result, not an inference from the plaquette bound alone.

CMP119 p.276 states that on a multiscale layer \(\Omega_n\setminus\Omega_{n+1}\), the current background has the regularity properties and bounds of an \(n\)-scale configuration. At the admitted source-free history scope this gives

\[
 h_n^2\|F(U_*)\|+h_n^3\|J(U_*)\|
       \le C_{\rm hist}\varepsilon_n,
 \qquad \varepsilon_n=A_0g_n\ell_n^{p_0},
 \tag{AD17.13}
\]

with the corresponding source local chart bounds. This import retains the actual history geometry and primary smallness conditions. It is not a new proof of the full source-free construction.

#### 5.2 The numerical reserve is fixed, not dyadically shrinking

Every coefficient in the source restrictions (2.34)–(2.39) is at least

\[
 s_*=1-\beta>0.
 \tag{AD17.14}
\]

Choose the already proposed normalization radii

\[
 \alpha_{0,n}=C_0g_n\ell_n^q,
 \qquad \alpha_{1,n}=C_1g_n\ell_n^q,
 \qquad q>p_0,
 \tag{AD17.15}
\]

with the fixed ratio required by the source local gauges. Then the real-center ratio in every basic layer norm is bounded by \(C\ell_*^{p_0-q}\), where \(\ell_*=\inf_n\ell_n\). The current and curvature comparisons between an actual layer \(n>j\) and the innermost region of an old \(j\)-domain have factors \(L^{-3(n-j)}\alpha_{0,n}/\alpha_{0,j}\) and \(L^{-2(n-j)}\alpha_{0,n}/\alpha_{0,j}\), respectively. The admitted AF comparison bounds their remaining ratio polynomially in \(n-j\); hence both have a fixed finite embedding constant \(C_{\rm emb}\).

The reconstructed-current restriction (2.37), after physical unit conversion, allows the weight \(h_n^{-2}h_{\min(p,n)}^{-1}\). The stronger physical profile \(h_n^{-3}\) therefore fits it. This is the same unit conversion checked in the argument; it does not prove a reconstructed-current estimate for a new map.

Suppose the actual joined projection proves, on its entire strong-native ball of radius \(r_b\), the output estimate required for those reconstructed profiles,

\[
 \text{normalized output size}
 \le C_{\rm map}\left(C_{\rm hist}\ell_*^{p_0-q}+r_b\right),
 \tag{AD17.16}
\]

including its actual source gauge and every relevant real/complex relative-field clause. Then the specific numerical choices

\[
 r_b\le\frac{s_*}{16C_{\rm emb}C_{\rm map}},\qquad
 C_{\rm emb}C_{\rm map}C_{\rm hist}\ell_*^{p_0-q}
       \le\frac{s_*}{16}
 \tag{AD17.17}
\]

leave at least a fixed fraction of every threshold in (2.34)–(2.39). The first choice is fixed; the second is achieved by one common coupling ceiling. They do not depend on age, cutoff depth or derivative order. Independent-current additions use the fixed common norm and triangular extension already proved in BC16.4.

The actual mathematical issue is (AD17.16) on a whole ball, not the numerical reserve afterward. A bound at its center or only for its first response does not suffice. The \(B=0\) joined-projection approach is relevant because it seeks this whole-ball bound directly. If proved for the actual determining average and source gauge, it could provide the primitive directional-disc certificate without first establishing an identity between arbitrary prepared maps.

The exterior relocation datum must be included in that same map-admission argument. Its source displacement is \(O(\varepsilon_ke^{-R_k})\) before division by \(g_k\), so it is far inside a fixed absolute coarse-data ceiling at real admitted histories. A uniform complex-background version still needs the common analytic map estimate; a real displacement bound alone is not that theorem.

### 6. Current application conditions

The exact old-slot and relocation reductions above are available for the actual C-05 application.

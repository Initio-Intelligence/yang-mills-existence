# Actual regular product support and the remaining R and boundary estimates

**Annex AA.** The conclusions below hold on the stated source domains. Applying them to the complete Wilson law requires compatibility of the conditional representation (CL), including the retained variables, densities, and marked transport. See [the theorem catalogue](../THEOREMS_AND_OPEN_PROBLEMS.md).


### 0. Result and precise scope

This annex distinguishes two different conditional laws that the abstract notation `D_{k,v}` does not separate.

1. A fiber conditioned on every primitive archived selector label can have a complicated selected domain. It must not be declared a convex product merely because the effective potential is regular.
2. The **source-normal-form regular marginal**, obtained after the exact integrations and resummations prescribed by the source, has a product of fixed free-bond norm balls as its inner integration domain. This remains true after the complete R operation has restored the inductive form. The full marked integral can be written on this marginal by conditional expectation of the entire marked jet. That replacement is exact; it is not a pointwise identification of marks on the two different fibers.

The main result below proves the support assertion for the second law, supplies the exact marked disintegration, and checks the hard-boundary argument needed by the weighted covariance theorem. This is the natural regular law to use for C-08. All E, R, B, Jacobian, heterogeneous-coupling and retained-mean terms remain in its density.

The proof does **not** make the complete positive history law convex. The responses generated by averaging archived labels and by crossing live-history activation boundaries are retained explicitly. They are inputs to H-03/H-04 and M-04/M-06, rather than silently deleted from the marked induction. Likewise, H1 regularity and the size of derivatives of transported marks still require their own estimates.

Under CL, the support and integration-by-parts statement for the regular conditional law applies to the chosen Wilson representation. The R and B derivative estimates have separate hypotheses.

No complete C-08 estimate or Yang-Mills existence theorem is asserted.

### 2. C-07: exact regular product support after the complete R operation

#### 2.1 Notation and the conditional law

Fix an admitted finite scale and an output of the complete source-free construction in the inductive form of CMP119 (2.18). Fix its live geometry, gauge graphs, determining sets and outer variables. Call these retained data `v`; in particular the source coupling labels are fixed, rather than varied with the integration variable.

Let `Lambda=Lambda_{k+1}` be the regular interior of CMP119 (3.20). Let `I_Lambda` be the remaining free bond coordinates on that interior after the constraint elimination in (3.15). Each free coordinate takes values in a copy `E_b` of the finite-dimensional real Lie algebra. Denote the norm used by the literal source cutoff on that bond by `||.||_b`.

This identification of free coordinates is substantive. CMP119 p.249 explicitly defines the starred bond set by deleting one exceptional bond `b_0(c)` for each eliminated constraint, names the remaining bond values `A`, and writes the constrained field as `A'=C A`. CMP99 p.428 says that `C` is the identity except at the solved exceptional bonds. The general step on CMP119 p.267 invokes exactly this elimination; its exponent contains `C A_k`, whereas (3.16) tests `A_k` on the starred free-bond set. The cutoff is therefore a product in independent coordinates. Had it instead tested all components of `C A_k`, this product conclusion would not follow merely from convexity of the resulting intersection.

Define

\[
 K_{k,\Lambda}
 =\prod_{b\in I_\Lambda}
       \{A_b\in E_b:\|A_b\|_b<r_k\}.
 \tag{C7.1}
\]

Repeated good-cube tests in (3.16) do not produce a new constraint: a product of repeated indicators `1_{||A_b||<r_k}` is that same indicator. Some tests may contain already fixed exterior coordinates. On an active exterior fiber those factors equal one. Thus the restriction on the remaining inner variables is exactly (C7.1).

Let `L_reg(A;v)` be the complete logarithmic density furnished by the source identity (3.25), including the full terms identified in the C-01 density identity. Constants independent of `A` can be kept in the outer weight. The conditional law is

\[
 q_{k,v}(dA)
 =Z_{k,v}^{-1}\mathbf1_{K_{k,\Lambda}}(A)
       e^{\mathscr L_{\rm reg}(A;v)}\,dA,
 \qquad
 Z_{k,v}=\int_{K_{k,\Lambda}}
       e^{\mathscr L_{\rm reg}(A;v)}\,dA.
 \tag{C7.2}
\]

The retained Gaussian cross term belongs to `L_reg`. It is a linear term in `A` and changes the conditional mean. Its presence does not change the support in (C7.1).

#### Theorem C7.1 -- the support of the actual regular marginal

For the exact source-normal-form regular marginal just specified, on every active nonzero-mass retained fiber:

1. the normalized inner law is (C7.2);
2. `K_{k,Lambda}` is a convex product domain in the free coordinates;
3. this domain is independent of the values of the retained continuous variables within the fixed live geometry and fixed coupling chart;
4. no inherited R, E or B term has been discarded to obtain this assertion;
5. this assertion applies after every admitted complete R step, not only before the first replacement or in the homogeneous no-R model.

**Proof.** Begin with the exact normal form (2.18), not an arbitrary conditional law obtained by fixing extra latent labels. The source applies the next blocking operation, inserts the new coarse and approximate-fluctuation partitions, and performs the exact constraint linearization and exceptional-coordinate elimination. Formula (3.15) is the result in free coordinates. Consequently the old group and nonlinear averaging constraints are not additional delta surfaces inside the `A` integral. Their coordinate factors belong to the density already identified by C-01.

The remaining approximate-fluctuation selector in (3.15) depends on the coarse field through the constrained background. The new decomposition (3.16) tests the supremum of the individual free-bond norms on its specified cubes. On a good cube, the source comparison (3.19) makes the earlier selector exactly one. This is an exact identity on the stated cutoff, not an estimate which replaces a nonzero boundary contribution by zero.

The geometry in (3.20) chooses the regular interior with the indicated collars. The text immediately after (3.20) states that only the good free-field tests remain there, in fact on the indicated enlargement of that interior. Their product is `chi^(k)(Lambda)`. All complementary bad tests and the surviving approximate-fluctuation test occur in the exterior factor (3.21).

The coordinate relocation (3.22), referring to (1.22), acts on the exterior set `S_{k+1}`. The source states that the other characteristic functions are unchanged. The complete change of variables, including its Jacobian and its effects on every old action term, generates the boundary correction `V^(k)` in (3.25). It does not shift the inner ball or remove that correction from the exponent.

Formula (3.23) puts the exterior integration, its selectors and its retained quadratic factor into the new outer operation. Formula (3.25) then has exactly the inner factor

\[
 \int dA\big|_\Lambda\,
       \chi^{(k)}(\Lambda)
       e^{\mathscr L_{\rm reg}(A;v)}.
 \tag{C7.3}
\]

Conditioning the outer variables in this equality gives (C7.2). The cutoff in (C7.3) is a product of norm inequalities, hence a convex product as in (C7.1). It contains neither `v` nor the old selectors as an additional constraint on `A`. The dependence on `v` remains in the full potential and in the outer weight.

For inherited operations the argument uses their actual source-supported form: CMP119 (2.19)-(2.22), rather than an assertion that every background-dependent expression is local. These old operators are supported in the large-field region and remain outside the inner integral in (3.25). Their smooth dependence, if present in the particular expression for the regular exponent, is retained there. The exact formula is the justification for separating the support; no additional operation is commuted across a mark or a selector.

Finally LFR II (1.101)-(1.102) reconstructs the new action including the new R and B terms and states that the resulting density has precisely the form (2.18), with all inductive hypotheses. Induction therefore permits the same argument at the next regular step. The stronger representation (1.104) gives another explicit check: the remaining large-component factors depend only on variables restricted to those components, and the source states that they do not enter the next regular renormalization integration. This does not assert that those factors are small. It locates their support.

All integrations and domain assertions above are identities of the admitted source construction. This proves the theorem. \(\square\)

 V'(b)=\exp(i g_k B'(b)),

LFR I (1.101) gives the fixed cutoff

 \prod_{b\notin G}

### 3. Exact marked passage to the regular marginal

Theorem C7.1 concerns an unmarked marginal. To use it in the proposed route, one must check that no observable is lost when archived histories are integrated out.

Let `Xi` denote the variables retained in (C7.2), including `A,v`, and let `omega` denote archived primitive histories and any latent old variables. On the positive finite source construction there is a joint probability measure `P(d omega,d Xi)` whose `Xi` marginal is the source-normal-form measure. This is the positive lift of B-01/H-01; no signed polymer activity is used as a probability. When the source removes a gauge delta by integral equivalence rather than pointwise equality, as on LFR II p.378, use its normalized gauge disintegration/averaging as a Markov step on this enlarged space. Carry the mark through that same step. The requested field family is gauge invariant at the original Wilson level. This procedure preserves its marked integrals without multiplying a merely gauge-equivalent final density by an untransported mark.

For a finite source jet `G(omega,Xi;t)` with integrable coefficients, define coefficientwise

\[
 \overline G(\Xi;t)
 =\mathbb E[G(\omega,\Xi;t)\mid\Xi].
 \tag{C7.6}
\]

#### Proposition C7.2 -- full-jet disintegration without support identification

For every retained test function `f(Xi)` and every coefficient of the finite jet,

\[
 \mathbb E[f(\Xi)G(\omega,\Xi;t)]
 =\mathbb E[f(\Xi)\overline G(\Xi;t)].
 \tag{C7.7}
\]

Truncation in source degree commutes with this operation. If the original constant coefficient is one, the new constant coefficient is one. If `Psi=log G`, the logarithm of `Gbar` includes the conditional covariance, higher cumulants and all pre-existing higher source responses. In particular,

\[
 \overline\Psi_i=\mathbb E[\Psi_i\mid\Xi],
 \qquad
 \overline\Psi_{ij}
 =\mathbb E[\Psi_{ij}\mid\Xi]
  +\operatorname{Cov}(\Psi_i,\Psi_j\mid\Xi).
 \tag{C7.8}
\]

**Proof.** Equation (C7.7) is the defining identity for conditional expectation. At finite cutoff there are finitely many jet coefficients, so it applies to all of them and commutes with truncation. Differentiating the finite algebraic identity `Gbar=exp(Psibar)` proves (C7.8) and the corresponding partition formula at higher orders. \(\square\)

Thus the regular covariance theorem may be applied to `Gbar` on the domain of Theorem C7.1 **once its gradient and current budgets have been proved**. It may not instead be applied to the original mark on a finer selector-conditioned fiber, nor may `Gbar` be replaced by a freshly inserted coarse plaquette.

This exact change of conditioning is allowed by the frozen-source-free route: it uses the same positive source construction and conditional expectation of marks. It requires no source-dependent background. It also shows exactly where archived-selector responses go. They become derivatives and cumulants of (C7.6), which remain part of the obligations M-04/M-06 and H-03/H-04.

### 4. The hard boundary in the actual regular covariance argument

Let

\[
 V(A;v)=-\mathscr L_{\rm reg}(A;v),\qquad
 q_v(dA)=Z_v^{-1}e^{-V(A;v)}\mathbf1_K(A)\,dA,
 \tag{C7.9}
\]

where `K=K_{k,Lambda}` is the domain just proved. Suppose the complete potential satisfies the derivative and coercivity hypotheses being sought in C-04-C-06. This subsection checks the support and boundary part of the ensuing estimate, rather than assuming those potential estimates.

Write

\[
 V=\tfrac12\langle A,K_0A\rangle+\langle \ell,A\rangle+W(A),
 \qquad K_0\ge\lambda_0 I,
 \tag{C7.10}
\]

and, for the spatial metric on free bonds, set

\[
 a_0(\gamma)=\sup_i\sum_j
 \|(K_0)_{ij}\|(e^{\gamma d(i,j)}-1),
 \quad
 \eta_\gamma=
 \sup_i\sum_j e^{\gamma d(i,j)}
           \sup_{A\in K}\|\nabla_i\nabla_jW(A)\|.
 \tag{C7.11}
\]

If `c_0=lambda_0-a_0(gamma)-eta_gamma>0`, the existing weighted covariance argument is valid on the actual domain and gives

\[
 |\operatorname{Cov}_{q_v}(F_x,G_y)|
 \le c_0^{-1}e^{-\gamma d(x,y)}
 \left(\sum_i e^{2\gamma d(i,x)}q_v|\nabla_iF_x|^2\right)^{1/2}
 \left(\sum_i e^{2\gamma d(i,y)}q_v|\nabla_iG_y|^2\right)^{1/2}.
 \tag{C7.12}
\]

Here the marks must belong to the indicated weighted Sobolev spaces; (C7.12) does not prove that transported marks have those norms.

For completeness the boundary check is repeated explicitly. On a smooth convex product approximation to `K`, solve the weighted Neumann equation

\[
 Lu=F-q_vF,
 \qquad L=-\Delta+\nabla V\cdot\nabla,
 \qquad \partial_nu=0.
 \tag{C7.13}
\]

Set `w=grad u`, and let `M_i=exp(gamma d(i,x))`, scalar on each Lie-algebra block. Differentiating (C7.13) gives the interior equation

\[
 (L\otimes I+\nabla^2V)w=\nabla F.
 \tag{C7.14}
\]

After multiplication by `M^2w`, the boundary term on a face belonging to block `i` is

\[
 M_i^2\int_{\partial K_i\times\prod_{j\ne i}K_j}
    \mathrm{II}_i(w_{i,\tan},w_{i,\tan})\,
    Z_v^{-1}e^{-V}\,d\sigma_i\,dA_{\ne i}\ge0.
 \tag{C7.15}
\]

All other block directions have zero second fundamental form on that face. The scalar-by-block weighting preserves the tangent/normal condition. Consequently the boundary has a favorable sign and may be retained or discarded in the lower bound; it is not set to zero by pretending that the sharp cutoff has no derivative.

The conjugation cost of `K_0` is at most `a_0(gamma)`, by the symmetric Schur majorant. The conjugated perturbation Hessian costs at most `eta_gamma`. This yields

\[
 c_0\|Mw\|_2^2\le\langle Mw,M\nabla F\rangle,
 \qquad
 \operatorname{Cov}_{q_v}(F,G)=q_v[\nabla u\cdot\nabla G],
 \tag{C7.16}
\]

from which (C7.12) follows by Cauchy-Schwarz and the triangle inequality for `d`.

Norm balls need not have smooth boundaries. At each finite dimension one approximates each convex ball from inside by smooth convex bodies, uses the same Hessian bounds on their product, and passes to the limit for smooth marks by dominated convergence. Their weighted gradients have the same dominating integrable functions on the compact product. Density in the weighted Sobolev space gives the general case. Corners of product domains produce no additional negative boundary term. Thus the argument applies to the literal bond norm in the source, without replacing that norm by an unrelated spherical cutoff.

The lower bound `c_0>0` is a remaining actual-potential premise; the theorem above supplies the complete support justification for using it on the source regular marginal.

### 5. Retained derivatives and history interfaces are different from inner IBP

#### 5.1 Within one active live-history chart

Since `K` in (C7.9) is independent of the retained field values, a derivative within one active fixed-geometry chart gives, when the differentiated integrands are integrable,

\[
 \partial_{v_a}q_vF
 =q_v(\partial_{v_a}F)
  -\operatorname{Cov}_{q_v}(F,\partial_{v_a}V).
 \tag{C7.17}
\]

All coordinate-map, Haar, coarea, inherited-activity and boundary-relocation derivatives belong to `partial_v V` and `partial_v F`. The product cutoff itself has zero `v` derivative. This is now a source-specific support fact, not an assumption about every primitive history kernel.

If the source parameters, cutoff radius or coupling label are varied instead of held fixed, (C7.17) is no longer the full formula. A radius derivative produces the ordinary surface term. This statement concerns fixed source-free parameters, as required by the proposed frozen-map route.

#### 5.2 Exact interface formula for live activation changes

Suppose in a common retained chart the live-history branch density is

\[
 w_h(v)=\mathbf1_{D_h}(v)z_h(v),\qquad
 m_h(v)=q_{h,v}F_h,
 \quad
 w(v)=\sum_h w_h(v),\quad
 m(v)=\frac{\sum_h w_h(v)m_h(v)}{w(v)}.
 \tag{C7.18}
\]

For piecewise smooth activation domains, the distributional derivative of the unnormalized numerator in a retained direction `a` is exactly

\[
 \partial_a\sum_h\mathbf1_{D_h}z_hm_h
 =\sum_h\mathbf1_{D_h}\partial_a(z_hm_h)
   -\sum_h n_{h,a}z_hm_h\,\sigma_{\partial D_h}.
 \tag{C7.19}
\]

The minus sign uses the outward normal and the convention
`partial_a 1_D=-n_a sigma_boundary`. The denominator has the same formula with `m_h` replaced by one. A singular quotient derivative must be computed from the two actual traces, rather than by dividing a surface measure by an unspecified value of a discontinuous function.

At a regular interface `Sigma`, let `n` point from its minus side to its plus side, write `N=sum_h w_h m_h`, and suppose both traces `w^-,w^+` are positive. Then the exact singular derivative is

\[
 (D_am)_{\Sigma}
 =\left(\frac{N^+}{w^+}-\frac{N^-}{w^-}\right)
       n_a\,\sigma_\Sigma
 =\frac{[N]-\overline m\,[w]}{\overline w}
       n_a\,\sigma_\Sigma,
 \qquad
 \overline m=\frac{m^++m^-}{2},\quad
 \overline w=\frac{w^++w^-}{2}.
 \tag{C7.20}
\]

Here `[f]=f^+-f^-`. The second equality is the elementary product identity
`[wm]=wbar [m]+mbar [w]`; it specifies the arithmetic trace convention completely. If a side has zero marginal weight, the normalized conditional mean on that side has no canonical value, and no quotient distribution across it is asserted. The exact finite-difference kernel below remains the correct formulation on nonzero fibers.

For a nonsmooth selector one uses the exact finite signed-kernel difference of Annex Y rather than assuming a classical surface density exists. Equations (C7.19)-(C7.20) apply whenever the indicated boundary measures and traces are defined. No regularity of every source selector is assumed merely to display them.

Across an interface separating two primitive cells, their boundary terms cancel only if the two inward limits of the **unnormalized marked density** agree. Equality of the sum of the unmarked weights is not sufficient for this marked cancellation. In particular, the conditional means on two branches need not agree. This is why the live-history current survives in H-03/M-06.

Equation (C7.20) is outside the inner `A` integration by parts of Section 4. It has not been bounded here. The distinction is mathematical: it differentiates the marginal history/retained-data law, while (C7.15) is the reflecting boundary form of the conditional regular field law. The statement support result does not identify these two derivatives or erase either one.

#### 5.3 Centered finite differences and integrated denominator cancellation

The same separation is available without differentiability or surface geometry. Let `r_v` be the finite unnormalized conditional measure on a common padded input space, `Z_v=r_v(1)>0`, and `q_v=r_v/Z_v`. Then, for a finite increment `v->v'`,

\[
 q_{v'}F-q_vF
 =\frac{(r_{v'}-r_v)(F-q_vF)}{Z_{v'}}.
 \tag{C7.21}
\]

If the mark itself changes, add `q_{v'}(F_{v'}-F_v)` to the right-hand side. This follows by subtracting the two fractions and uses
`(r_{v'}-r_v)(1)=Z_{v'}-Z_v`.

On multiplying by the true marginal factor `Z_{v'}`, the displayed fiber denominator cancels exactly. Integration against the corresponding output marginal uses its actual underlying measure and any coordinate-change Jacobian. This is useful for an integrated-current estimate, but does not establish its spatial localization or a uniform pointwise retained-data norm. It also does not permit cancellation against a different reference normalizer.

### 6. C-03: R activity norms and the required quantitative pullback (Annex AE)

#### 6.1 A nonvanishing reference normalization is already part of the source

LFR II (1.10)-(1.11), printed p.358, represents the localized reference integral after removal of its large background Wilson factor as the exponential of an analytic function `I(U_0)` plus the stated constant normalization terms. On its source-defined complex domain,

\[
 |I(U_0)|\le C\log(M)\,|\Lambda|.
 \tag{C3.1}
\]

In particular the normalization is nonzero on that **specified** domain. An additional generic argument trying to derive zero-freeness from a real lower bound is unnecessary there and would be invalid in general. What remains necessary is admission of the actual differentiated current-coordinate contour to that domain.

For example, if a particular one- or two-coordinate pullback disc of radius `r` is verified to lie in that source domain, Cauchy's formula gives

\[
 |\partial_a I|\le C\log(M)|\Lambda|/r,
 \qquad
 |\partial_a\partial_b I|
 \le 2C\log(M)|\Lambda|/r^2.
 \tag{C3.2}
\]

The full logarithmic derivative of the original reference denominator also contains the derivative of the extracted background action and of any retained normalization factors. It is not equal to the derivative of `I` alone. At the frozen quotient, the reference factor cancels from the old conditional mark, but it still contributes to the output reference density and to later transformations.

#### 6.2 Primitive R norm versus current marginal norm

CMP119 (2.31), p.260, gives the primitive complex bound

\[
 |R_X^{(j)}(U,J)|
 \le g_j^{\kappa_0}e^{-\kappa d_j(X)}
 \tag{C3.3}
\]

on the specified old multiscale space. LFR II (1.99)-(1.100), p.390, proves the new activity bound on its own complex space, with the stronger small factor `exp[-p_0(g_k)]` away from the surviving large components.

LFR II (1.65), pp.374-375, supplies an analytic old-field preparation map from its new `(U,J)` domain into the enlarged old domain of LFR I (1.64)-(1.69). This is a real source theorem. It cannot be replaced by the unsupported claim that arbitrary fluctuations around an admitted real point remain in its domain with one fixed current-coordinate radius.

The exact missing C-03 estimate can be expressed without an unnamed constant. For every actual current real point `A` and every contributing old carrier, one needs a quantitative admitted current bidisc, including independently extended currents and all decoupling parameters, and a localized marginal transfer of the form

\[
 \sup_i\sum_{Y\ni i}\sum_{l\in Y}e^{\gamma d(i,l)}
 \sup_{\zeta\in\mathbb D_{i,l}(A,r_k)}
 |V^R_{j,k,Y}(\zeta)|
 \le C\ell_j^Dg_j^{\kappa_0}
                  \frac{Q_k^2}{a_j^2}.
 \tag{C3.4}
\]

The notation and the necessity of the marginal scale gain are as in Annex G: `a_j` is an admitted old radius, `Q_k` controls the physical amplitude on the same tube, and `ell_j=log(g_j^-2)`. The four marginal scale powers must compensate the old-cell count before the old-scale sum. The two current Cauchy derivatives then cost `r_k^-2`.

The existing semisimple first-jet cancellation and old-scale summation do not prove the contour or (C3.4). The fresh denominator source theorem (C3.1) removes a possible extra zero-free subproblem on its admitted domain, but does not establish (C3.4). **C-03 remains open.**

with localization meeting the appropriate large-field region, and analytic extension on the specified multiscale spaces. Its old fields and history labels remain arguments of the activity. with a budget compatible with the complete coercivity margin, together with the relevant retained-variable/source derivative estimates. The pullback `Phi_{j,k}` includes the boundary relocation and all old fluctuation arguments; its second derivative cannot be omitted.

The value bound (C5.1) and the extensive real bound (2.48) do not imply (C5.2). Two Cauchy radii, all old-layer gains, the collar geometry, and the radius costs of the boundary relocation must be retained. In particular a boundary component whose distance grows with the volume cannot be charged by an unrecorded constant. Neither the product support result nor the normalized interface identity supplies that missing current-carrier budget. **C-05 remains open.**

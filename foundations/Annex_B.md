# Regular-action Hessians and homogeneous coercivity

**Annex B.** All estimates have the hypotheses stated below. Applications to the complete Wilson conditional law use the common assumptions in [the theorem catalogue](../THEOREMS_AND_OPEN_PROBLEMS.md).


### Scope

Uniform real weighted coercivity of the full conditional kernel requires bounds on the complete boundary part of CMP119 (3.27) throughout its real cutoff support.

**Scope.** The fixed-domain CMP109/CMP116 inherited regular-action estimate is valid, including its old-scale resummation. Its extension with a fixed radius and prefactor to all complete CMP119 interior histories is not justified. CMP119 uses running multiscale analytic domains. Both the admissible free-coordinate radius and the constants in the activity estimate must be tracked through that transfer. Section 3 separates the fixed-domain theorem from this still-conditional extension; Annex G gives the source analysis and explicit running-radius formula.

A sharper vanishing Hessian estimate is proved for the explicitly cubic localized classical summands in CMP116 (1.38)–(1.39). It is **not** promoted to all of the classical fluctuation action: total cubicity does not, by itself, prove cubicity of every localized carrier. Section 4 explains the distinction.

The direct calculation in Section 9 closes the **complete homogeneous classical-action** Hessian without localizing its Taylor remainder. It uses the scale normalization in CMP102 (190), converts fine-link variations to physical curvature variations, and retains the compensating \(\eta^4\) volume factor. Combined with the regular E estimate and the new coarea bound, this proves full conditional coercivity for the explicitly specified homogeneous **no-R** density (9.9) on its stated regular window. It is not identified with the complete RG/R homogeneous sector, because inherited R activities can survive there.

Finally, recentering the exact native constraint chart gives complex tubes about every point of its real regular cutoff. Thus a comparison of two radii measured from zero is not a necessary obstruction. The unfinished issue is admission of the full inherited multiscale pullback, including the current and localized activity bounds, into these tubes. Section 5 proves the native-chart statement and identifies this remaining step precisely.

Labels: **P** means proved here, with stated source inputs; **L** means the precisely scoped statement imported from a primary paper; **O** means not proved in this argument.

### 1. The exact measure being estimated

Use the source-free chart and all the retained history/exterior data. On a regular current interior the positive conditional density has the form

\[
 d\nu(z)=Z^{-1}\chi(z)\exp\left[-\tfrac12\langle z,H_0z\rangle+W_k(z)\right]dz.
\]

Here \(H_0\) is the **actual constrained classical quadratic form**. In particular the quadratic contribution generated by the nonlinear averaging constraint belongs to \(H_0\); replacing it by an unconstrained curl Hessian would change the measure. The complete \(W_k\) retains the new coarea logarithm once, all inherited \(E_k,R_k,B_k\), exterior dependence, nonconstant coupling, and the coordinate-localization corrections. The selector is fixed at zero source. A Hessian statement below concerns the interior of its regular product-cutoff support, not a derivative of its discontinuous boundary.

The desired estimate is

\[
 \eta_\gamma(W_k):=
 \sup_z\sup_i\sum_j e^{\gamma d(i,j)}
       |\partial_i\partial_jW_k(z)|<\lambda_0,
 \qquad H_0\ge\lambda_0 I.
 \tag{1.1}
\]

The available covariance/precision input is the source's constrained one. CMP99, Section E and Theorem 3.15, supplies uniform exponential bounds for the current fluctuation covariance, with the positivity statement for \(C^*\Delta_kC\) on printed p.428. This is not a substitute for estimating \(W_k\).

#### 2.2 The cutoff margin that is actually proved

Set

\[
 \delta_k=g_kA_1(\log g_k^{-2})^{p_0},\qquad
 P_k=\delta_k/g_k.
 \tag{2.2}
\]

CMP119 p.267 makes the scaling \(A'_k=g_kA_k\). Formula (3.16) on p.268 literally prints the good condition \(\sup|A_k|<\delta_k\); it does not print the corresponding factor \(g_k^{-1}\). Either the literal displayed condition, for \(g_k\le1\), or the intended physical condition \(\sup|g_kA_k|<\delta_k\), implies the conservative inclusion

\[
 \|A_k\|_\infty\le P_k.
 \tag{2.3}
\]

For every fixed admissible \(\epsilon_1>0\),

\[
 \frac{P_k}{\epsilon_1/g_k}=\frac{\delta_k}{\epsilon_1}\longrightarrow0.
 \tag{2.4}
\]

This provides a genuine complex margin on the fixed-domain CMP116 free-field analytic ball \(\|B\|_\infty<\epsilon_1/g_k\). It supplies no margin on a running radius \(\epsilon_{1,k}/g_k\) without a separate comparison. The complete CMP119 transfer does not establish that \(\epsilon_{1,k}\), or its activity prefactor, stays fixed.

#### 2.3 Complete-action transfer, with its scope retained

CMP119 p.276 describes generalized random-walk localization on the full sequence of domains, applies CMP99 Theorems 3.7–3.10, and resums into current-scale carriers \(Y\in D_k\). It then explicitly states that the summands whose carriers satisfy

 Y\subset\Lambda_{k+1}

satisfy exactly the small-field lemmas from [I]. Those lemmas are CMP116 Lemmas 1 and 2 in the split publication. This identifies the already-renormalized full inherited \(E_k\) fluctuation action on these carriers. The domains and parameter dependence of those lemmas still have to be retained. In particular, their fixed-domain constants cannot be imported unchanged into running CMP119 domains. The identification is stronger than a bare-action statement, but it is not yet the required uniform derivative bound.

For the remaining carriers the displayed analytic space is instead

 \widetilde U^c_{k+1}

See CMP119 (3.43). The printed radius is \(C_1p_1(g_k)\). No checked definition in that paper currently identifies it with \(P_k\), with \(p_0\), or with the later LFR-I sharper selector also named \(p_1\). No such identification is made here.

### 3. Fixed-domain regular-action Hessian and the conditional running-domain extension

#### Theorem 3.1 (P in the fixed CMP109/CMP116 analytic domain)

Let \(W^{E,\mathrm{bulk}}_k\) be the inherited renormalized regular-action fluctuation sum with the fixed analytic domain and the fixed activity bound (2.7), in the source's actual free coordinates. Suppose its real product cutoff obeys \(P_k\le\epsilon_1/(2g_k)\), with \(\epsilon_1>0\) fixed. For sufficiently small admitted shell coupling and sufficiently small fixed \(\gamma>0\),

\[
 \eta_\gamma(W^{E,\mathrm{bulk}}_k)
 \le C_{\mathrm{constr},\gamma} E_0\frac{g_k^2}{\epsilon_1}.
 \tag{3.1}
\]

The bound is uniform in volume and shell index within that fixed-domain construction. It does not assert the same constants for arbitrary CMP119 histories or their changed analytic domains, and it does not estimate the complete \(R_k+B_k\) residual action.

**Counterterm bookkeeping.** The exact source identity being estimated is CMP116 (1.33),
\[
 E_k(U_k(e^{iB'}V^{(k)}))-E_k(U_k(V^{(k)}))
       =\sum_YV'_{k,Y}(B),\qquad B'=g_kCB-hD(g_kCB).
\]
The \(E_k\) here is the already-renormalized inherited regular action. CMP119 (2.25), p. 259, defines it as
\[
 E_k(U)=\sum_{j=1}^k\bigl[
 E^{(j)}(\Lambda_j,g_{j-1},U)
 -E^{(j)}(\Lambda_j,g_{j-1},1)
 -\beta_j(g_{j-1})A(\phi_j,U)\bigr].
\]
Thus its past coupling subtractions, their nonzero fluctuation derivatives, and old Jacobian contributions are included in the identity. The subtracted base value \(E_k(U_k(V^{(k)}))\) is constant in the current free coordinates. The classical Hessian \(H_0\) uses the already-flowed coupling \(g_k\); no extra marginal quadratic is removed from \(V'_Y\), and no past \(\beta_j\)-term is added to \(H_0\) a second time. The new \(\beta_{k+1}\) is generated only after integrating the current shell.

**Proof.** By (2.4), reduce the coupling ceiling so that \(P_k\le\epsilon_1/(2g_k)\). Every real point \(z\) of the actual cutoff has a complex coordinate polydisc of radius \(c_G\epsilon_1/g_k\) contained in the activity domain, where \(c_G>0\) is a fixed norm-equivalence factor for the finite-dimensional Lie algebra. Mixed and diagonal coordinate Cauchy estimates applied to (2.7) give

\[
 |\partial_i\partial_j V'_{k,Y}(z)|
 \le C_{\mathrm{constr}} E_0\frac{g_k^2}{\epsilon_1}
           e^{-\kappa_*d_k(Y)},
 \qquad \kappa_*=(1-2\vartheta)\kappa.
 \tag{3.2}
\]

The derivative is zero unless both free coordinates belong to the carrier's permitted bond set, enlarged by the fixed localization convention if required.

**Coordinate identification.** In this theorem \(z\) means the actual surviving free-bond coordinates after eliminating the exceptional bonds in the linearized constraint. These are named \(B\) in CMP109/CMP116 and \(A\) in CMP119 (3.15)/(3.25). They are not the ambient physical link field \(B'\), nor its division by \(g\). The source already evaluates its analytic activity at the complete argument \(gCB-hD(gCB)\), or its multiscale counterpart. Thus the nonlinear chart and minimizing-field pullback are **inside** the holomorphic activity to which (3.2) is applied. They are not a further unestimated substitution after Cauchy. Conditioning exterior coordinates gives the affine embedding \(B=\zeta^0+\iota_I z\); on a carrier contained in the interior only the indicated current free coordinates occur. This embedding has weighted row/column norm one and zero second derivative. Fixed finite-range reindexings or retained gauge-frame changes cost only a fixed weighted norm constant.

For any genuinely different nonlinear free coordinates \(B=\Psi(\widetilde z)\), the Hessian would instead be
\[
 D^2_{\widetilde z}(V'\circ\Psi)
 =(D\Psi)^T(D^2_BV')(D\Psi)
       +\sum_a(\partial_{B_a}V')D^2\Psi_a.
\]
That extra first-derivative term must be estimated. The theorem does not make that substitution; it uses the source's actual free coordinates.

The carrier sum must be performed on the **current \(M\)-cube graph**, not on unit-lattice animals. A carrier with \(n\) current \(M\)-cubes contains at most \(c_GM^4n\) free coordinates. Its current-lattice diameter is at most \(cM n\), and the geometric estimates used in CMP116 (1.26)–(1.28) bound \(n\) by a fixed multiple of \(1+d_k(Y)\). The number of connected carriers of size \(n\) containing a fixed cube is at most \(c_d^n\). Choosing \(\gamma>0\) sufficiently small compared with the source's remaining decay exponent, and using the same entropy reserve as the source localization sum, yields

\[
 \sup_i\sum_{Y\ni i} \sum_{j\in Y}
 e^{\gamma d(i,j)}e^{-\kappa_*d_k(Y)}
 \le C_{M,\kappa_*,\gamma,G}<\infty.
 \tag{3.3}
\]

Singleton carriers and any fixed enlargement only change this constant. Summing (3.2) and applying (3.3) proves (3.1). No additional sum over old shell indices is introduced: (2.7) applies to the regular effective-action fluctuation expansion **after its old-scale renormalization and resummation**. In particular the proof does not replace this by the generally divergent surrogate \(\sum_{j\le k}g_j^2\). \(\square\)

**What this removes.** The inherited regular action is controlled in the specified fixed-domain homogeneous model. This is the estimate used in Section 9.4 and in Annex F. It is not an established complete-history E-bulk estimate.

#### Conditional running-domain version — C

If the actual fully pulled-back carrier has the bound

\[
|V^E_{k,Y}(B)|\le E_0\epsilon_{1,k}K_k^E e^{-\kappa_*d_k(Y)}
\quad\text{for }\|B\|_\infty<\epsilon_{1,k}/g_k,
\]

and \(\delta_k=g_kP_k<\epsilon_{1,k}\), the same Cauchy/carrier proof gives

\[
\boxed{\eta_\gamma(W_k^{E,\mathrm{bulk}})
\le CE_0K_k^E
\frac{\epsilon_{1,k}g_k^2}{(\epsilon_{1,k}-\delta_k)^2}.}
\tag{3.4}
\]

Here a uniform carrier entropy reserve is also required. The hypothesis concerns the complete localized activity after the nonlinear constrained substitution, including its currents. Neither a native chart tube nor a real activity bound establishes it. Uniform admission, the size of \(K_k^E\), and a small right side in (3.4) remain open for complete histories. Annex G checks the source inequalities that force this qualification.

### 4. Sharper estimate for the explicitly cubic classical carriers

CMP116 (1.38) localizes the classical remainder \(V(H_1B')\), where

\[
 B'=g_kCB-hD(g_kCB).
\]

Its bound (1.39) is explicitly cubic in \(B'\). With the fixed construction parameters and the source's carrier resummation absorbed into constants, this gives, for each such localized classical summand,

\[
 |P^{\mathrm{cub}}_{k,Y}(B)|
 \le Cg_k^{-2}\rho^3 e^{-\kappa_*d_k(Y)}
 \quad\text{if }\|g_kB\|_\infty\le\rho\le\rho_0,
 \tag{4.1}
\]

where \(\rho_0>0\) is a fixed admitted physical radius in this fixed-domain construction. The small correction \(hD\) and the relevant localization operators have been retained. For these particular summands, vanishing through degree two follows already from the cubic bound; it is not inferred from cancellation in the total action. Its extension to running complete-history domains requires the corresponding radius and constant analysis.

#### Proposition 4.1 (P for these explicitly cubic localized summands)

On the actual regular cutoff (2.3), for \(2\delta_k\le\rho_0\),

\[
 \eta_\gamma\left(\sum_{Y\subset\Lambda_{k+1}}
 P^{\mathrm{cub}}_{k,Y}\right)
 \le C_{\mathrm{constr},\gamma}\delta_k
 =C_{\mathrm{constr},\gamma}
 g_kA_1(\log g_k^{-2})^{p_0}.
 \tag{4.2}
\]

**Proof.** Apply (4.1) at physical radius \(2\delta_k\). Every real point of the cutoff then has complex coordinate margin \(c_G\delta_k/g_k\). Cauchy gives

\[
 |\partial_i\partial_jP^{\mathrm{cub}}_{k,Y}(z)|
 \le C\frac{g_k^{-2}\delta_k^3}{(\delta_k/g_k)^2}
 e^{-\kappa_*d_k(Y)}
 =C\delta_ke^{-\kappa_*d_k(Y)}.
\]

Use (3.3). \(\square\)

The same radius scaling can be proved from a bound at one fixed radius by applying the one-variable Schwarz lemma to \(t\mapsto P^{\mathrm{cub}}_{k,Y}(tB)\), since the first three Taylor coefficients vanish. This requires vanishing for that carrier.

#### Why this is not yet the complete classical bulk theorem

The total exact constrained classical remainder is cubic after the true constrained quadratic form is extracted. This alone does not imply that every term in an arbitrary localized decomposition is cubic. For example,

\[
 z^3=(z^2+z^3)+(-z^2)
\]

is a decomposition into analytic localized functions with nonzero individual quadratic jets. Even though the cancellation is exact, a bound obtained by absolute summation of individual Hessians must account for those jets.

CMP116 states after (1.40) that the other terms of \(P^{(k)}\) are treated by the same decompositions and bounds, but the exact complete regrouped quadratic coefficient is precisely (1.43), which is not used below. Therefore (4.2) is claimed only for the explicitly cubic construction (1.38)–(1.39), and for any further summand whose cubic integral-remainder representation is separately verified. For a general localized complete-action extension, one can inspect and use the actual (1.43), or verify carrier by carrier that localization preserves the required jets. That carrier verification remains open. Section 9 below gives a separate direct proof for the complete homogeneous classical action, without making this carrier-level assertion.

### 5. Recentered complex tubes for the exact native chart

#### Proposition 5.1 (P, exact regular chart)

Use the chart on a regular current core,

\[
 a(\zeta)=gC\zeta-hD(gC\zeta),\qquad
 D(q)=N(q-hD(q)),\quad N(0)=DN(0)=0.
 \tag{5.1}
\]

Suppose \(\zeta_0\) is any real point of the cutoff, including its fixed exterior coordinates, such that the same frozen chart has a fixed physical margin. There are fixed \(c,C,\gamma>0\), independent of the number of copies of its regular local template, such that for

\[
 \|\eta\|_\infty<r,\qquad gr<c,
\]

the exact relative link field

\[
 B_{\zeta_0,e}(\eta)
 =\frac1i\log\left(
 e^{ia_e(\zeta_0+\iota_I\eta)}
 e^{-ia_e(\zeta_0)}\right)
 \tag{5.2}
\]

is holomorphic on that tube and satisfies

\[
 |B_{\zeta_0,e}(\eta)|
 \le Cg\sum_{i\in I}e^{-\gamma d(e,i)}|\eta_i|,
 \tag{5.3}
\]

with weighted first- and second-derivative bounds of order \(g\) and \(g^2\), respectively. The background, integration map, coordinate inverse \(h\), and retained variables have not been changed.

**Proof.** In a fixed physical complex neighborhood, the Neumann inverse \((I+DN(a)h)^{-1}\) appearing in the exact implicit derivatives is uniformly bounded in the weighted matrix algebra. The derivative formulas consequently hold at every \(\zeta_0+\iota_I\eta\) in the tube, not just at \(0\). Integrating the first derivative along \(s\mapsto\zeta_0+s\iota_I\eta\) gives the exponentially localized difference bound for \(a(\zeta_0+\iota_I\eta)-a(\zeta_0)\). Matrix multiplication, exponentiation and the local logarithm are holomorphic on a common fixed neighborhood of the identity. Their first two derivatives are uniformly bounded there. Applying their chain rule to (5.2) yields (5.3) and its weighted derivative versions. In particular, no false identity \(e^{ia}e^{-ia_0}=e^{i(a-a_0)}\) is used. \(\square\)

**Consequence for the radius issue.** If a local activity has a uniformly bounded holomorphic extension on relative complex gauge fields of physical size \(\alpha_1\) about every admitted real base, (5.3) allows a coordinate tube of width \(c\alpha_1/g\) about every admitted real cutoff point. One need not have \(\alpha_1/g>P_k\), because both radii are not measured from zero. This corrects an unnecessarily restrictive origin-ball criterion.

#### What the primary multiscale minimizer theorem already gives

CMP102, Proposition 9, printed pp.305–309, proves precisely a recentered analytic extension

\[
 V=V'V_0\longmapsto U_k(V'V_0)
\]

about each admitted real regular base \(V_0\). Its (182)–(190) derive the first-derivative kernel and give the same regularity/decay pattern as the minimizing propagator \(H\). This source result is real support for the moving-tube strategy. It prevents dismissing recentering merely because the original activity proof was written around one fixed source-free background.

#### What is not yet proved for the actual complete pullback

The original analytic activity spaces in CMP119 (2.34)–(2.39) require more than a small link variation. They simultaneously constrain multiscale real regularity, reconstructed fields, currents \(J,J_{p,X}\), local gauges, and relative complex fields with their spatial derivatives. In particular:

1. The actual real point must have a quantitative margin in the current constraints, not merely belong to an open regularity set. CMP119 gives \(\alpha_{0,j}=g_jC_0(\log g_j^{-2})^{q_0}\), \(\alpha_{1,j}=g_jC_1(\log g_j^{-2})^{q_1}\), with \(q_0,q_1>1\) and construction restrictions. No checked explicit relation \(q_0\ge p_0\) is available, and the constants cannot be enlarged freely after the source construction is fixed.
2. The pullback \(U_k\), its currents and their required spatial derivatives must map the complete coordinate tube into every old activity domain. Proposition 9 supplies the first derivative pattern; translating it into these simultaneous quantitative domain margins, including second spatial derivatives of currents and all old layers, has not been performed here.
3. The bound must hold for the **localized complex activities** with decoupled propagators, not just for the unlocalized action evaluated at a real field. Recentered analyticity of the unlocalized minimizing configuration alone does not prove a bound for every localized carrier.
4. The carrier estimates must be summable uniformly over old scales and histories. The source's E renormalization and the R small powers are essential. Replacing them by an accumulation of \(O(g_j^2)\) bounds would not be valid.

Consequently Proposition 5.1 is a genuine exact-chart improvement but not a proof of the complete boundary Hessian budget.

### 6. Why the current origin-ball information is insufficient by itself

This elementary counterexample pinpoints the missing inference; it is not a Yang–Mills counterexample.

Let \(P\ge6R>0\), \(b=P/2\), \(\varepsilon>0\), and

\[
 W_s(z)=-\varepsilon\exp[-(z-b)^2/s^2].
 \tag{6.1}
\]

It is entire, real on the real axis, and \(|W_s(x)|\le\varepsilon\) for every real \(x\). If \(|z|\le R\), then

\[
 \operatorname{Re}(z-b)^2\ge(b-R)^2-R^2=b^2-2bR>0,
\]

so \(|W_s(z)|\le\varepsilon\) on the entire origin-centered complex disc. Nevertheless,

\[
 W_s''(b)=2\varepsilon/s^2.
 \tag{6.2}
\]

Thus the strictly positive cutoff density

\[
 \mathbf1_{[-P,P]}(x)e^{-x^2/2+W_s(x)}dx
\]

has potential Hessian \(1-2\varepsilon/s^2\) at the interior point \(b\), which becomes arbitrarily negative as \(s\downarrow0\). An arbitrarily good real action bound, plus a uniformly bounded holomorphic extension on a smaller ball about zero, does not control the Hessian on a larger real cutoff.

The moving-tube premise would exclude this example. It is therefore an appropriate strengthening to investigate; the unavailable comparison of \(p_1\) and \(p_0\) is not by itself a mathematical impossibility result.

### 9. Direct complete classical estimate in the homogeneous regular geometry

This argument avoids both (1.43) and the per-carrier cancellation issue for the **complete classical action in the homogeneous geometry**. It does not discard R terms from the complete RG/R measure; Section 9.5 explains why those terms remain a separate issue even when the current large-field set is empty.

#### 9.1 The normalized source input is visible in the published text of

CMP102 printed pp.308–309 were given there. In (190), for input on layer \(j'\) and output on layer \(j\), the first two entries have the scale factors

\[
 \left|\frac{\delta\mathcal H_\mu(B,x)}{\delta B_\nu(y')}\right|
 \le C(L^j\eta)^{-1}(L^{j'}\eta)^{-4}e^{-c d(y,y')},
\]

\[
 \left|\nabla_x\frac{\delta\mathcal H_\mu(B,x)}{\delta B_\nu(y')}\right|
 \le C(L^j\eta)^{-2}(L^{j'}\eta)^{-4}e^{-c d(y,y')}.
 \tag{9.1}
\]

The functional-derivative convention includes the corresponding input lattice measure. Proposition 9 explicitly states complex analyticity and that the derivative satisfies (190). In the homogeneous case, rescale the current lattice to unit spacing: \(j=j'=k\), \(L^k\eta=1\). Both factors in (9.1) are then one. The resulting first derivative and its spatial gradient are bounded uniformly, with exponential decay in current-scale distance, throughout an admitted fixed physical complex neighborhood. This is a curvature/scaled-difference statement, not merely a bound on a fine link.

CMP109 pp.265–267 identifies the map used in its exact conditional integral with this minimizing map in Landau gauge; its (2.6)–(2.8) includes the full nonlinear minimizing correction. Its (2.11) identifies the true constrained quadratic form, including the contribution from the quadratic constraint correction.

#### 9.2 Curvature scaling and higher derivatives

Let \(q\) be the physical free coordinates in the exact current constraint chart, and let \(U(q)\) be the complete fine minimizing configuration. The current map from \(q\) into the coarse input of Proposition 9 is the exact analytic map, including \(hD\). Its uniformly localized derivatives were proved. Composition with (9.1) therefore gives, in the homogeneous geometry, the same first-derivative and gradient estimate with input \(q_i\).

Work in the Landau representative supplied by Proposition 9. This is permitted because each Wilson plaquette trace is gauge invariant; no large derivative from a subsequent change to axial gauge need be estimated in the action. Define the physical curvature coordinate

\[
 \mathfrak F_p(q)=\frac1{i\eta^2}\log U(q)_p.
 \tag{9.2}
\]

The principal local logarithm is admissible throughout a smaller fixed complex neighborhood by the regularity estimates. Exact variation of a plaquette is a covariant curl of its tangent link field. In fine lattice notation the link tangent has a factor \(\eta\), and its covariant difference supplies the second factor \(\eta\). Equation (9.1) consequently gives

\[
 |\partial_{q_i}U(q)_p|\le C\eta^2e^{-c d(x_p,i)},
 \qquad
 |\partial_{q_i}\mathfrak F_p(q)|\le Ce^{-c d(x_p,i)}.
 \tag{9.3}
\]

The uniform physical curvature bound used here follows from explicit source regularity, not from fine-link boundedness. The real base has \(|U_{0,p}-1|\le C\epsilon_0\eta^2\) in this homogeneous specialization. Proposition 9 supplies (19)–(21) on the same complex neighborhood, with \(\epsilon_2=B_5\epsilon_1\), hence \(\|\mathcal H\|+\|\nabla_{U_0}\mathcal H\|\le C\epsilon_1\) in current physical units. Expanding the four relative link factors, the plaquette change is bounded by
\[
 |U_p(q)-U_{0,p}|
 \le C\eta^2\left(
 \|\nabla_{U_0}\mathcal H\|+\|\mathcal H\|^2
          +\epsilon_0\|\mathcal H\|\right).
\]
Thus \(\|\mathfrak F_p(q)\|\le C(\epsilon_0+\epsilon_1+\epsilon_1^2)\) uniformly on that complex neighborhood, after choosing the fixed source parameters small enough for the logarithm. This justifies the common bounded set used for the derivatives of \(\mathcal L_\eta\) in (9.5).

For clarity, if \(U_e(q)=e^{i\eta\mathcal H_e(q)}U_{0,e}\), its tangent is

\[
 T_{i,e}(q)=\int_0^1
 \operatorname{Ad}(e^{it\eta\mathcal H_e(q)})
 \partial_{q_i}\mathcal H_e(q)\,dt.
\]

The covariant derivative of \(T_i\) is controlled by \(\nabla\partial_{q_i}\mathcal H\), together with bounded products of \(\mathcal H,\nabla\mathcal H,\partial_{q_i}\mathcal H\). Exact telescoping around the four plaquette edges gives (9.3); it does not estimate four link variations separately and thereby lose one power of \(\eta\).

Uniform complex Cauchy estimates now supply the higher derivatives. On a fixed smaller complex neighborhood,

\[
 |\partial_{q_{i_1}}\cdots\partial_{q_{i_r}}
       \mathfrak F_p(q)|
 \le C_r e^{-c_r\tau(x_p,i_1,\ldots,i_r)},
 \qquad r=1,2,3.
 \tag{9.4}
\]

Here \(\tau\) is a shortest connecting-tree length in current-scale distance. Indeed, apply coordinate Cauchy to (9.3) \(r-1\) times. This first yields the exponential involving any chosen one of the input indices. Symmetry of the mixed derivatives permits the minimum over all such indices. Since

\[
 \tau(x_p,i_1,\ldots,i_r)
 \le\sum_{a=1}^r d(x_p,i_a)
 \le r\max_a d(x_p,i_a),
\]

the minimum of the exponentials is bounded by the right side of (9.4), with a fixed loss in decay. The constants depend on the fixed physical analytic radius, not on \(g,\eta,k\), or the torus volume.

#### 9.3 The complete classical third derivative, with its volume factor retained

In four dimensions write the Wilson action exactly as

\[
 A^\eta(U(q))=\sum_p\eta^4\mathcal L_\eta(\mathfrak F_p(q)),
\]

\[
 \mathcal L_\eta(F)=\eta^{-4}
 \left[1-\frac1{2N}\operatorname{Tr}
       (e^{i\eta^2F}+e^{-i\eta^2F})\right].
 \tag{9.5}
\]

All normalizing group constants can be replaced by the fixed convention used here. The derivatives of \(\mathcal L_\eta\) through order three are uniformly bounded on the admitted physical curvature neighborhood, for \(0<\eta\le1\). This follows directly from the even power series in \(\eta^2F\); its lowest nonconstant term has degree two and carries \(\eta^4\).

The third chain rule has the three kinds of terms

\[
 D^3\mathcal L_\eta(\mathfrak F)[D\mathfrak F]^3,
 \qquad
 D^2\mathcal L_\eta(\mathfrak F)
       [D^2\mathfrak F,D\mathfrak F],
 \qquad
 D\mathcal L_\eta(\mathfrak F)[D^3\mathfrak F].
 \tag{9.6}
\]

The last term is essential: this calculation does not discard the analogue of \(A' a'''\), or the other derivatives of the nonlinear chart/minimizer. By (9.4), every term has tree decay joining the plaquette position and the three input coordinates. The factor \(\eta^4\) in (9.5) survives in every term. Therefore

\[
 \sup_i\sum_{j,l}e^{\gamma d(i,j)}
 \left|\partial_{q_i}\partial_{q_j}\partial_{q_l}
       A^\eta(U(q))\right|
 \le C.
 \tag{9.7}
\]

To see the ultraviolet cancellation explicitly, the plaquette sum is bounded by
\(\sum_p\eta^4e^{-c' d(x_p,i)}\le C\), uniformly in \(\eta\). The two remaining current-coordinate sums are exponentially summable. Estimating the fine link derivatives without the curvature curl in (9.3) would instead produce an inadmissible \(\eta^{-4}\) factor.

For the hard axial gauge used in the exact constrained-coordinate formulation one sets \(G=0\). The optional soft-gauge version has a concrete source check as well: CMP109 (2.5), printed p.266, writes its actual \(G(B')\) as a quadratic block term plus an analytic \(G_3(B')\) beginning at degree three and localized in current blocks. Its uniformly bounded physical third derivatives follow from finite block size and that fixed physical analytic neighborhood. This particular source term therefore also satisfies (9.7), without a fine-lattice volume sum. No assertion about a generic unspecified gauge-fixing functional is needed.

#### Proposition 9.1 (P using the homogeneous specialization of Proposition 9)

In the homogeneous regular geometry, let

\[
 S_g(z)=g^{-2}\bigl[A^\eta(U(gz))+G(gz)\bigr]
\]

be the complete exact classical/gauge-fixing action in the physical chart, and let
\(H_0=D_z^2S_g(0)\) be its true constrained Hessian. If \(\|z\|_\infty\le P(g)\), \(gP(g)\) is sufficiently small, and the line segment to zero lies in the same regular chart, then

\[
 \sup_z\sup_i\sum_j e^{\gamma d(i,j)}
 \left|\partial_i\partial_j
 \left(S_g(z)-S_g(0)-DS_g(0)z
                  -\tfrac12\langle z,H_0z\rangle\right)\right|
 \le CgP(g).
 \tag{9.8}
\]

At the source-free constrained minimum \(DS_g(0)=0\).

**Proof.** Scaling (9.7) by \(q=gz\) and the action prefactor \(g^{-2}\) gives a weighted third-derivative row sum bounded by \(Cg\). Integrate the third derivative along \(tz\), \(0\le t\le1\), and use \(\sup_l|z_l|\le P(g)\). \(\square\)

This is a complete classical-action statement in this homogeneous scope. It uses no artificial assignment of cubicity to each carrier and no numerical coefficient from (1.43).

#### 9.4 A rigorously specified no-R conditional density

Consider the explicitly specified homogeneous density

\[
 d\nu_{\rm sf}(z)\propto
 \mathbf1_{\{\|z\|_\infty\le P(g)\}}
 J(gz)\exp\{-S_g(z)+E_k(U(gz))\}\,dz,
 \tag{9.9}
\]

where \(E_k\) is the complete inherited regular action in the source's small-field construction. Older Jacobian terms are included in this inherited action; \(J\) is the new exact coarea factor only. This is the actual form of the CMP109 small-field conditional integrand, with the indicated further regular-window restriction if its chosen cutoff is larger. It is **not** asserted to equal the homogeneous sector of the complete RG/R construction.

Combining (9.8), the corrected fixed-domain Theorem 3.1 on the whole torus in this geometry, and the \(O(g^2)\) coarea Hessian gives

\[
 \eta_\gamma\bigl(S_g-\tfrac12zH_0z-\log J-E_k\bigr)
 \le C\left[gP(g)+g^2+E_0g^2/\epsilon_1\right].
 \tag{9.10}
\]

Choose the fixed physical window \(gP(g)\) sufficiently small and then \(g\) sufficiently small. The right side is below \(\lambda_0/2\), and the complete real potential in (9.9) has Hessian at least \(\lambda_0I/2\). The cutoff is a product of convex finite-dimensional norm balls. Consequently the weighted Brascamp–Lieb/covariance argument applies to this density, and its native marked cut estimates become unconditional **for this specified density and regular window**.

In particular, for the actual finite native curvature-bilinear family let \(B_*\) bound the reference physical curvature divided by \(g\), and put \(Q=P+B_*\). The exact-chart value/gradient estimates and this coercivity give, for \(m\ge2\),
\[
 |\kappa_{\nu_{\rm sf}}(O_{\alpha_1,x_1},\ldots,O_{\alpha_m,x_m})|
 \le C^{m+1}m!Q^{2m-2}
       e^{-\gamma\tau(x_1,\ldots,x_m)/(m-1)}.
 \tag{9.12}
\]
The one-point and full-moment bounds are respectively \(CQ^2\) and \(C^mQ^{2m}\). If the retained reference has the actual logarithmic-window bound \(B_*\le CP\), replace \(Q\) by a constant times \(P\). That background condition is stated explicitly; a fixed \(\epsilon_0\) curvature bound alone does not imply it. Restoring physical dimension-four normalizations multiplies the order-\(m\) bound by the appropriate physical factor \(h^{-4m}\); nothing here bounds that ultraviolet factor or identifies transported microscopic insertions with these native fields.

There is no source-dependent change of variables in this conclusion. The normalized conditional measure, all regular old effective terms, and the exact new coarea are retained. This result establishes neither comparison with the full Wilson measure nor a negligible-error removal of the further cutoff if one was imposed.

#### 9.5 Why R cannot be silently removed from the complete homogeneous sector

In CMP119, the support property of a B activity requires \(X\cap Z_j\ne\varnothing\); hence B activities vanish if **every relevant old** \(Z_j\) is empty. The exterior/nonconstant-coupling/coordinate-localization corrections also vanish in the genuinely homogeneous geometry. In contrast, R activities in (2.30)–(2.31) are supported inside \(\Lambda_j\) and can remain after earlier R replacements. An empty current large-field set is not an assertion that \(R_k=0\).

CMP119 p.263 also fixes the old-scale loss in its available real value estimate:
\[
 |R_k(U_k)|\le R_1\sum_{n=1}^k|\Gamma_n|
     \sum_{j=1}^ng_j^{\kappa_0}
 <R_1\sum_{n=1}^k|\Gamma_n|g_n^{\kappa_0-6},
 \qquad\kappa_0\ge7.
 \tag{9.11}
\]
This is (2.46); the source prints the exponent \(\kappa_0-6\). It must not be replaced by a guessed \(\kappa_0-2\) in an attribution to this theorem. Formula (2.44) has the unsummed \(g_j^{\kappa_0}\). Those real marginal statements alone do not give the current-coordinate R-bulk Cauchy estimate. The source additionally gives the genuinely complex activity bound in (2.31); the localized current-coordinate pullback and its simultaneous \(U,J\) domain margins are still required.

To identify (9.9) with a positive no-large-field-history branch of the full construction, one would have to show in the original positive history representation that every R map on that branch is the identity, and verify the identification before signed activity regrouping/exponentiation. This has not been done here. Alternatively one can estimate the complete inherited R bulk Hessian. Annex G records the original complex activity norm and a conditional old-scale derivative sum. Its current-coordinate domain admission and complex marginal-transfer premise remain open; the text saying that R parallels E does not discharge them.

In heterogeneous histories, (9.1) retains its factors \((L^j\eta)^{-1}\) and \((L^j\eta)^{-2}\). The simple homogeneous \(\eta^4\)-sum argument does not remove them. The collar, layer, and activity sums must be performed with those factors. Thus Proposition 9.1 and (9.10) do not close the complete-history conditional theorem.

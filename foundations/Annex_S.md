# Numerical AF stability and logarithmic factorial budgets

**Annex S.** All estimates have the hypotheses stated below. Applications to the complete Wilson conditional law use the common assumptions in [the theorem catalogue](../THEOREMS_AND_OPEN_PROBLEMS.md). Identifying the calibrated Gaussian marginal with the Wilson trajectory also requires GC, including the bounded error uniform in depth.


### Mathematical consequences

This note proves statements **B-04** and **F-02**. Both are numerical bridge
theorems with complete elementary proofs. Neither identifies an actual
Yang--Mills coupling recursion, mixing coefficient, or contact coefficient.
Those applications remain separate in B-03/B-05 and F-03--F-07.

The first theorem supplies the precise asymptotically free numerical
asymptotic required by the commuting-product theorem in Annex P, once its
actual recurrence has been established. The second theorem shows that a
power of a running logarithm per insertion can be absorbed into a
**fixed factorial exponent**, while retaining a positive cutoff rate.
It applies in particular to the new mesoscopic Wilson contact error.

### 1. A perturbed asymptotically free recurrence

Fix \(b>0\) and \(A\geq0\). Choose \(u_*>0\) such that
\[
u_*\leq1,\qquad A u_*\leq b/2,\qquad
\frac{3b}{2}u_*\leq\frac12.
\tag{B.1}
\]
For \(A=0\), the middle restriction is empty. Let \(0<u_0\leq u_*\)
and suppose a real sequence is generated by
\[
u_{j+1}=u_j-bu_j^2+r_j,\qquad |r_j|\leq A u_j^3.
\tag{B.2}
\]
The remainder may depend on the complete earlier sequence. No
autonomous beta function, differentiability, or fixed sign of \(r_j\)
is assumed. The bound must hold at each step that is generated.

Write
\[
v_j=u_j^{-1},\qquad \kappa=(bu_0)^{-1}\geq3.
\tag{B.3}
\]

#### Theorem 1.1 — stability, asymptotics and summable tails

Every generated \(u_j\) is positive and at most \(u_0\). Moreover,
\[
\boxed{
v_0+\frac b2 j\leq v_j\leq v_0+3bj.
}
\tag{B.4}
\]
There is a constant \(C\), depending only on \(b,A,u_*\), such that
\[
\boxed{
\left|u_j-\frac1{b(j+\kappa)}\right|
\leq
C\,\frac{\log(1+j/\kappa)}{(j+\kappa)^2}.
}
\tag{B.5}
\]
The right side is zero at \(j=0\), as it should be. In addition,
\[
\sum_{j=n}^{\infty}u_j^2
\leq \frac{8}{b^2(n+\kappa)}.
\tag{B.6}
\]
Finally the sequence
\[
\sum_{j=0}^{n-1}u_j-\frac1b\log\frac{n+\kappa}{\kappa}
\tag{B.7}
\]
has a finite limit. Its tail is at most
\[
C'\frac{1+\log(1+n/\kappa)}{n+\kappa},
\tag{B.8}
\]
with a constant that can be chosen uniformly for \(u_0\leq u_*\).

All conclusions are uniform over the admissible remainders in (B.2).

**Proof.**
Suppose inductively that \(0<u_j\leq u_*\), and set
\[
d_j=bu_j-r_j/u_j.
\]
The assumptions imply
\[
\frac b2u_j\leq d_j\leq\frac{3b}{2}u_j\leq\frac12.
\]
Consequently \(u_{j+1}=u_j(1-d_j)\) lies in
\([u_j/2,u_j)\), proving positivity and invariance of the small
interval by induction.

Taking reciprocals gives the exact formula
\[
v_{j+1}-v_j
=\frac{b-r_j/u_j^2}{1-bu_j+r_j/u_j}.
\tag{B.9}
\]
The numerator lies between \(b/2\) and \(3b/2\), and the denominator
between \(1/2\) and \(1\). This proves (B.4).

Subtract \(b\) in (B.9). Directly,
\[
\begin{aligned}
v_{j+1}-v_j-b
&=
\frac{b^2u_j-br_j/u_j-r_j/u_j^2}
     {1-bu_j+r_j/u_j},\\
|v_{j+1}-v_j-b|
&\leq C_0u_j,\qquad
C_0=2(b^2+A+bAu_*).
\end{aligned}
\tag{B.10}
\]
From (B.4),
\[
u_j\leq\frac1{b(\kappa+j/2)}
\leq\frac2{b(\kappa+j)}.
\tag{B.11}
\]
A comparison with the integral of
\((\kappa+x/2)^{-1}\) gives, for \(n\geq1\),
\[
\sum_{j<n}u_j\leq \frac3b\log(1+n/\kappa).
\tag{B.12}
\]
For an explicit justification, on each interval \([j,j+1]\) the
ratio of the value at \(j\) to the minimum value is at most
\(1+(2\kappa)^{-1}\leq7/6\). Thus the sum is bounded by
\((7/3b)\log(1+n/(2\kappa))\), which is no larger than
the right side of (B.12).

Summing (B.10) now gives
\[
|v_n-b(n+\kappa)|
\leq\frac{3C_0}{b}\log(1+n/\kappa).
\tag{B.13}
\]
Also (B.4) implies
\(v_n\geq \frac b2(n+\kappa)\). Divide (B.13) by
\(v_n\,b(n+\kappa)\) to obtain (B.5), for example with
\(C=6C_0/b^3\). At \(n=0\) the equality is exact.

Squaring (B.11) and comparing the decreasing reciprocal-square sum
with its integral proves
\[
\sum_{j=n}^{\infty}u_j^2
\leq\frac4{b^2}
\left[\frac1{(n+\kappa)^2}+\frac1{n+\kappa}\right]
\leq\frac8{b^2(n+\kappa)},
\]
which is (B.6).

For the last assertion, split (B.7) as
\[
\sum_{j<n}\left(u_j-\frac1{b(j+\kappa)}\right)
+\frac1b\left[
\sum_{j<n}\frac1{j+\kappa}
-\log\frac{n+\kappa}{\kappa}
\right].
\tag{B.14}
\]
The first series converges absolutely by (B.5). For clarity, if
\(g(x)=\log(1+x/\kappa)/(x+\kappa)^2\), then for
\(x\in[j,j+1]\),
\[
g(j)\leq(1+1/\kappa)^2g(x)\leq(16/9)g(x).
\]
Its discrete tail is therefore at most \(16/9\) times the exact
integral
\[
\int_n^\infty g(x)\,dx
=\frac{1+\log(1+n/\kappa)}{n+\kappa}.
\]
This comparison does not incorrectly assume that \(g\) is decreasing
near zero.
For the second bracket, each difference between
\((j+\kappa)^{-1}\) and its integral over \([j,j+1]\) is
nonnegative and at most \((j+\kappa)^{-2}\). That series also
converges, with tail at most \(C/(n+\kappa)\). This proves
(B.7)--(B.8). \(\square\)

#### 1.1 What this closes, and what it does not

The theorem verifies the numerical hypothesis used in Annex P from
the one-step recurrence (B.2). Its \(O(\log j/j^2)\) error and
square-summable tail are sufficient for the logarithmic product
asymptotics stated there.

It does not prove (B.2) for the Wilson RG map. In particular, it does
not infer \(b\) from a convention, identify \(u\) with a specific
multiple of the inverse Wilson coefficient, solve a physical
normalization/shooting problem, or establish the admissibility of all
RG histories. Those are B-03 and B-05. A coherent family of finite
trajectories must be checked there; the present theorem cannot replace
that check by unrelated numerical runs.

### 2. Logarithmic running and factorial growth

Use a dimensionless cutoff \(0<\rho=a/\ell_0\leq1\), and set
\[
\ell(\rho)=1+\log(1/\rho).
\tag{F.1}
\]
Let \(m\geq1\). A norm below may be any nonnegative quantity, including
the dual of a joint Schwartz seminorm or a local response norm.
Let \(\delta>0\), \(p,\gamma,\eta\geq0\), and \(A\geq1\) be fixed.

#### Theorem 2.1 — logarithmic loss absorption with a vanishing rate

Suppose
\[
\|E_{m,\rho}\|_{qm,*}
\leq
A^{m+1}(m!)^p
\rho^\delta\ell(\rho)^{\gamma m+\eta}.
\tag{F.2}
\]
Then for every fixed \(0<\theta<\delta\), a constant \(A_\theta\)
independent of \(m,\rho\) satisfies
\[
\boxed{
\|E_{m,\rho}\|_{qm,*}
\leq
A_\theta^{m+1}(m!)^{p+\gamma}\rho^\theta.
}
\tag{F.3}
\]
There is no increase in the derivative-order parameter \(q\).
The factorial exponent \(p+\gamma\) is fixed; if an integer exponent
is desired, it may be enlarged to its ceiling.

**Proof.**
Put \(t=\log(1/\rho)\geq0\) and
\(\epsilon=(\delta-\theta)/2>0\). The leftover logarithmic factor is
\[
e^{-(\delta-\theta)t}(1+t)^{\gamma m+\eta}
=
\big[e^{-\epsilon t}(1+t)^\eta\big]\,
\big[e^{-\epsilon t}(1+t)^{\gamma m}\big].
\tag{F.4}
\]
The first bracket has a finite supremum \(K_{\epsilon,\eta}\)
independent of \(m\). If \(\gamma=0\), the second bracket is at most
one. If \(\gamma>0\), maximizing its logarithm on \(t\geq0\) gives
\[
e^{-\epsilon t}(1+t)^{\gamma m}
\leq e^\epsilon
\max\left\{1,\left(\frac{\gamma m}{e\epsilon}\right)^{\gamma m}\right\}.
\tag{F.5}
\]
The elementary integral estimate
\[
\log(m!)=\sum_{j=1}^{m}\log j
\geq\int_1^m\log x\,dx
\geq m\log m-m
\]
implies \((m/e)^m\leq m!\). Hence (F.5) is at most
\[
e^\epsilon
\max\{1,(\gamma/\epsilon)^\gamma\}^{m}(m!)^\gamma.
\tag{F.6}
\]
Multiply (F.6) by \(K_{\epsilon,\eta}\), insert into (F.2), and
absorb fixed constants into \(A_\theta^{m+1}\). This proves (F.3).
\(\square\)

The loss in the factorial exponent is governed by the logarithm
power **per insertion**, \(\gamma\). A fixed extra logarithm power
\(\eta\) changes only the exponential constant. Replacing \(\gamma\)
by \(\gamma/(\delta-\theta)\) in the factorial exponent would be an
unnecessary loss; the parameter \(\delta-\theta\) belongs in the
constant in (F.6).

#### Corollary 2.2 — logarithmically many controlled errors

Use a norm or seminorm satisfying the triangle inequality. Suppose
\(E_{m,\rho}=\sum_{\ell=1}^{N(\rho)}E^{(\ell)}_{m,\rho}\),
each term has (F.2) with the **same** constants, and
\[
N(\rho)\leq B\,\ell(\rho)^\sigma
\tag{F.7}
\]
for fixed \(B\geq1,\sigma\geq0\). Then (F.3) still holds with exponent
\(p+\gamma\).

**Proof.**
Sum (F.2), replace \(\eta\) by \(\eta+\sigma\), and apply
Theorem 2.1. \(\square\)

The common-constant premise is essential. A factor of the form
\(C^{mN(\rho)}\) accumulated by an uncontrolled iteration is not
bounded by a fixed power of \(\ell(\rho)\) per insertion, and this
corollary does not remove it.

#### Corollary 2.3 — geometric ultraviolet tails

Let \(\rho_j=L^{-j}\), \(L>1\), and let \(E_{m,j}\) satisfy
(F.2) at \(\rho_j\), uniformly in \(j\). For \(J\geq0\),
\[
\boxed{
\left\|\sum_{j\geq J}E_{m,j}\right\|_{qm,*}
\leq
C_\theta^{m+1}(m!)^{p+\gamma}L^{-\theta J}.
}
\tag{F.8}
\]
The series converges in the stated norm.

**Proof.**
Apply (F.3) term by term and use
\(\sum_{j\geq J}L^{-\theta j}
=L^{-\theta J}/(1-L^{-\theta})\). \(\square\)

This is an abstract sufficient bound. A marginal remainder with no
positive scale gain \(\delta\) does not satisfy its hypotheses.
In particular it does not repair the inverse-running-product
counterexample in Annex P.

### 3. Exact relation to mesoscopic Wilson matching

The mesoscopic matching theorem in the proof of Annex T
starts with the original cutoff-field moment estimate
\[
\|O_{\alpha,a}(x)\|_{L^r}
\leq C a^{-4}r,\qquad r\geq1,
\tag{F.9}
\]
on the actual Wilson law. It projects onto retained variables by
an actual conditional expectation \(Q\), not a formal coarse action.
For mesoscopic blocks of physical size at most \(C\sqrt a\), its
full-product matching error on flat joint tests obeys
\[
|D_{m,a}(F)|
\leq a^{1/2} C^{m+1}m!\,\nu_{10m}(F)
\tag{F.10}
\]
in units \(\ell_0=1\). The proof retains the original \(a^{-4}\)
budget; it does not claim a new current-scale moment estimate.

Suppose, as an additional **application premise**, that actual
one-field normalization coefficients satisfy
\[
\|Z_a\|\leq C\ell(a)^\gamma,\qquad
|c_{\alpha,a}|\leq C a^{-4}\ell(a)^\gamma.
\tag{F.11}
\]
For the finite family
\[
O^R_{\alpha,a}
=\sum_\beta (Z_a)_{\alpha\beta}O_{\beta,a}
-c_{\alpha,a},
\]
finite dimensionality and (F.9) give
\[
\|O^R_{\alpha,a}\|_{L^r}
\leq C' a^{-4}\ell(a)^\gamma r.
\tag{F.12}
\]
Conditional expectation commutes with the deterministic linear
combination and preserves constants. The same mesoscopic proof
therefore changes (F.10) only by \(\ell(a)^{\gamma m}\) and a new
exponential constant. Theorem 2.1 yields, for instance,
\[
\boxed{
|D^R_{m,a}(F)|
\leq
a^{1/4} C_\gamma^{m+1}(m!)^{1+\gamma}\nu_{10m}(F).
}
\tag{F.13}
\]
Thus logarithmic running does not itself destroy the all-order
shrinking-contact theorem. The estimate is valid uniformly over all
source orders with one fixed factorial power.

The proof of (F.11) for the actual desired normalization remains
F-05. Contact coefficients of products are not all individual field
shifts and remain F-06/F-07. Most importantly, neither (F.10) nor
(F.13) bounds separated correlations: away from the shrinking
contact set the exact projected fields have the same unknown
correlations as the original fields.

### 4. Application conditions

**B-04:** complete. The theorem has a specified invariant interval,
explicit reciprocal bounds, a uniform logarithmic asymptotic and
square-summable tails. Its input is a numerical recurrence; B-03
and B-05 remain open.

**F-02:** complete. The theorem has fixed all-order constants and
exponent, gives a positive cutoff rate, and handles a fixed
logarithm power, a power per insertion, finite tensor mixing and
logarithmically many common-budget errors. Its actual-field
application is conditional on (F.11).

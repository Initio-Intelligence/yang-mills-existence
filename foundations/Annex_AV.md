### 3. The Jacobian is analytic, small, and must be retained

Write \(g=g_t\), \(\delta'=\delta'_t\), and \(a\) for one fixed real rescaled old variable. The actual change (CMP119 (1.22), invoked by LFR I p.188) is

\[
 \mathcal R_H(a)
   =\frac1{ig}\log\!\left(e^{iga}e^{-i\chi(a)H}\right),
 \tag{PF.10}
\]

with the actual smooth real bump \(\chi\), or with \(\chi=1\) on the stated remaining bonds. Its derivative scale is

\[
 \|D\chi\|\le Cg/\delta'.
 \tag{PF.11}
\]

For fixed real \(a\), \(\chi(a)\) and its derivatives are fixed coefficients. Thus \(H\mapsto D_a\mathcal R_H(a)\) is holomorphic on the relevant small complex \(H\)-ball even though \(\chi\) need not be holomorphic as a function of \(a\).

The convergent BCH series gives, uniformly on the actual old support,

\[
 \|D_a\mathcal R_H-I\|
       \le C|H|/\delta'.
 \tag{PF.12}
\]

The term differentiating the bump gives precisely (PF.11) times \(H/g\); the remaining BCH terms are bounded by \(C|H|\), hence by the right side of (PF.12). There is no derivative of \(H\) with respect to this old variable: \(H\) is determined by the retained next-level background in (1.56), before the variable in (1.53) is integrated or retained.

The AF comparison and (PF.7) imply

\[
 \epsilon_{b,t}:=
   C\frac{\alpha_b}{\delta'_t}\mathcal P_{b,t}
      e^{-\delta D_{b,t}}\longrightarrow0
 \tag{PF.13}
\]

uniformly in the admitted prefix histories as the common ceiling is lowered. The ratio \(\alpha_b/\delta'_t\) has only a fixed polynomial logarithmic/age cost. Take the ceiling so that \(\epsilon_{b,t}<1/4\).

The logarithm

\[
 j_{b,t}(a;H)=\log\det D_a\mathcal R_H(a)
  =\operatorname{Tr}\sum_{m\ge1}
          \frac{(-1)^{m+1}}m(D_a\mathcal R_H-I)^m
 \tag{PF.14}
\]

is then defined by its branch at \(H=0\), is holomorphic in the native pair, and obeys

\[
 |j_{b,t}|\le C\epsilon_{b,t},\qquad
 |D_Hj_{b,t}[v]|\le C\delta_t'^{-1}|v|.
 \tag{PF.15}
\]

These are local single-bond formulas. The determinant dimension is the fixed Lie algebra dimension, not the total lattice dimension.

### 4. Selectors, old variables, and the action difference

The selector ledger is exact. LFR I p.183 states that all characteristic functions introduced in (1.22)–(1.28) depend on variables localized in the corresponding old component. The source does not say that this final failed-shift contribution is one of the successfully integrated \(C_b^{(n+1)}\). Immediately after (1.59) it restricts to the small \(\chi'^{(t)}\) components before defining (1.60)–(1.63). The failed action difference and (PF.14) must therefore be kept as separate scalar boundary-induced terms.

For the former, take the difference of the complete analytic exponential action before and after (PF.10), on the actual linearized \(t\)-current chart. The \(t\)-variable is the newly introduced coarse variable of (1.53); the older fluctuation variables remain fixed. The analyticity used here is the original new-current analyticity of that one-step expression, explicitly identified with CMP119 (3.15) in LFR I p.188. \[
 g|\mathcal R_H(a)-a|\le C|H|\ll\delta'_t.

Moreover \(6\delta'_t/g_t=O(\ell_t^{p_1})\ll P_t\), since \(p_1<p_0\). Thus the actual failed real support and this complex perturbation have a fixed margin in the already admitted full \(t\)-current contour.

The ordinary action sectors can consequently be estimated before taking the difference:

- The Wilson sector uses the local four-dimensional difference estimate BC15.1, with its fine-cell cancellation and the actual coupling coefficients.
- The \(E,R\), previously generated \(C_b^{(s)}\), and inherited scalar \(B\) sectors use their source local analytic norms and the fixed-old-field chart. For \(C_b^{(s)}\), use its actual modified domain and (1.70), with Section 1's prefix chart inclusion.

Here is the intermediate force row, including its coupling normalization. Use the **angular** new current \(\beta=g_t a\). For a scalar action primitive \(\mathscr A_{s,Y}\) with native value majorant \(M_{s,Y}\), the already proved common-disc and endpoint estimates give
\[
 |\partial_{\beta_u}\mathscr A_{s,Y}|
 \le C\frac{M_{s,Y}}{\alpha_s}
          \mathsf K_{s,t}(Y,u).
 \tag{PF.19a}
\]
The kernel \(\mathsf K\) is the original normalized mixed-history endpoint row, with the two old nodal arguments added for an ordinary quadratic primitive. It includes the physical layer measures, the endpoint decay, and the layer-crossing reserve. For a completed-R scalar primitive its majorant is the anchored absolute majorant, not merely a final pointwise value. For a Wilson term one can use BC15.1 directly; the weaker right side of (PF.19a) also suffices after including its local coupling coefficient in \(M\).

The source value norms in these sectors have at most a fixed inverse-coupling factor \(g_s^{-2}\) and fixed logarithmic factors. Under the endpoint-localized assumptions above, the derivative estimate applies to the corresponding stopped prefix. The complete-law application requires these bounds uniformly on the same positive histories.

For relative carriers, use CR.1–CR.3 with the actual active tags and retain the current guard to pay the one uncharged physical tag diameter. ### 5. Domain of application

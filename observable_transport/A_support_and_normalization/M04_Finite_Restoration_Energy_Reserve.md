# The first-canceled reserve survives a finite exact restoration

This note extends the proved LE upper/lower argument to a declared
finite restoration of ordinary variables. It does not require a
common Gaussian density for two histories. It keeps the exact first
all-P factor, the terminal constraints, the old completed factors,
and the source normalization. An alternative mask still needs a
separate failure-to-energy estimate; the theorem does not define
that implication into existence.

## 1. Scope and exact restored operation

Fix the first-birth branch h=1 with Ω₁ full and the original globally
P/Q-good first selector, a complete first R pattern s of bounded
native support, a complete final fresh class r, and actual retained
output v. The waiting time is the admitted N=R. The first R pattern
has the Gaussian weight w_s^G from RB. No statement here covers an
arbitrary inherited T_h with h>1.

If a retained-only positive factor 𝒞_{r,v} survives the displayed
classical cancellation, keep it in the original operation and set
\(\overline M_{s,*}=M_{s,*}/\mathcal C_{r,v}\),
\(\overline M_s=M_s/\mathcal C_{r,v}\). All unnormalized
upper and lower estimates below are for these barred operations.
Equivalently multiply both sides of ER.6, ER.9 and ER.10 by the
same 𝒞_{r,v}. This factor is fixed once by the source normalization;
it is not chosen separately for the upper estimate or for a history.

Choose a finite, source-admissible enlargement of the ordinary and
preparatory raw/free lists. For example take the two-list master of
PM, and a collar of width T_j at each changed native layer. Keep all
newly exposed ordinary cutoffs as their original small cutoffs.
Retain any additional normalized gauge samplers, with their source
small-w law. Let D_* be the total native scalar rank, including all
newly explicit first coordinates; the global first all-P complement
remains integrated with its exact completion.

Undo each selected integration by its *complete* positive source
identity. If its actual conditional density is f(a,z), the operation
is

\[
 I(z)=\int f(a,z)\,da
 \quad\longmapsto\quad f(a,z)\,da,
                                                               \tag{ER.1}
\]

with I removed from the completed action at the same time. One
must not restore a normalized Gaussian while leaving I, or discard
the cutoff part of I. Further integrations outside the exposed set
are left in their actual completed action. The nonlinear coarea and
all averaging deltas are unchanged. Denote the resulting positive
operation by \(M_{s,*}\). Then

\[
 M_{s,*}(1)=M_s(1).                                           \tag{ER.2}
\]

The exact equality is true whether or not the exposed list is a
literal selected history. An auxiliary retained hole with all its
free entries still small is merely a partial evaluation of the same
positive integral, not a new large-field event.

### Explicit coordinate and coarea condition

The restored list must have an admitted source coordinate cover,
not merely the rank bound D_*. Its promoted raw fields and each
mixed minimum used in the partial integrations must satisfy the
literal native regularity margins. At fixed retained v, the
terminal deltas must have a valid sequence of the original
exceptional-bond solves, or an independently bounded equivalent
section. The total remaining coarea/normalization cost must be
bounded by ER.5 relative to the actual fixed-class reference
measure. This condition is retained explicitly until the short-depth
terminal construction is checked. PM.2 locates these solves in
O(log R) levels; that fact alone is not a determinant estimate.

All conclusions below concerning a finite restoration are stated
with this coordinate/coarea condition. The first-factor algebra does
not require further hypotheses about a common density or cutoff
probability.

## 2. The positive energy that is actually available

Apply the source ordinary expansion on the unintegrated regular
pieces and the forest version of the prepared classical
cancellation. Define E_* to be the sum of the positive later forms
which actually remain in this representation:

\[
 E_* = Q_*+S_*+
        \frac12\sum_{j\ge1} a_j^{\mathsf T}K_{j,*}a_j
        +\sum_{\text{padded gauge sites}} G_j(w_j).             \tag{ER.3}
\]

A retained Schur form in this formula is its complete form, with
its complement completion kept in the interaction. Q_* is the
nonnegative outer covariant-gradient form; S_* is the nonnegative
inner Wilson action after the actual removal of the reference A₀
in LFR II (1.46). Each G_j is the original positive soft gauge
energy. Hard gauge directions remain fixed by their deltas and are
not called positive Gaussian directions.

There is no term for an unexposed mode. In particular ER.3 does
not claim control of all erased ordinary fluctuations.

The same U₀ and ζ₀ are used as in the original fresh class. The
leading retained action A(ζ₀,U₀)/g_k² cancels against precisely the
original fresh reference factor. Variations which preserve the
fixed terminal averages outside Λ obey LFR II (1.4); this is why
restoring a fine mode in Y\Λ does not create a new uncanceled
classical force. The remaining cutoff boundary terms are retained
in W_* and have the source decay estimates summarized in FD.

For a finite enlarged list in the source regular chart, the full
nonenergy and measure ledger has the bound

\[
 |W_*-c_{r,v}|+|\log J_*|_{\rm nonconstant}
       \le C\mathfrak D_* ,                                  \tag{ER.4}
\]

where a conservative admissible choice is

\[
 \mathfrak D_*=
 C\sum_j |Z_{j,*}|\log g_j^{-2}
       +C D_*\log(P_*+R+2)+C N D_*+1.                         \tag{ER.5}
\]

All equalities use one retained-only normalization c_{r,v}; it is
not chosen anew to improve a bound. Any exact remaining exterior
factor is retained in the common first/later measure, as in LE.

### Source justification of ER.4

The ordinary positive integration in CMP119 (1.26) and (3.25) can
be performed on a smaller regular domain while leaving the
additional free entries unintegrated. The analysis of the resulting
logarithm uses the same bounded regular characteristic functions
and the same localized interactions. A large-field *lower* selector
on those newly retained entries is not used in that analysis.
All changed constraints or selectors inside the new collar are
kept explicitly. Thus the source unmarked bounds (2.26)–(2.42)
and their summed estimates (2.43)–(2.49) charge the actual native
volumes of the enlarged list. The auxiliary geometry must satisfy
the same fixed separation; this is why PM retains interface collars.

LFR I (1.60)–(1.70) treats the analogous partial annular integration:
its new terms are boundary terms with the same analytic/localized
bounds. Their full values, including cutoff completion, remain in
W_*. LFR II (1.69) and the normalization discussion after (1.74)
charge the self action and the displayed powers of g by the native
volumes. Nonself terms are retained through the full source action;
for the fixed isolated component their summed variation has the
HS20 type bound. With the logarithmically enlarged restored region,
the distance to the unchanged Y boundary remains cR−C logR. The
same localized sum therefore costs at most a polynomial in the
enlarged native volume times exp(−cR), absorbed in λ_R. No source large-field rarity estimate is substituted for
this absolute ledger.

The finite raw/free coarea is the literal source coarea, as in PM.11.
Its logarithmic powers of g are included once in ER.5. Boundary
terminal constraints remain present. Their direct current-scale
coarea, or a valid sequence of the original local constraint solves,
must be used; a delta is not bounded by one.

This source-fit statement concerns the specified admitted restoration.
It is not permission to expose an extensive set and then charge only
D_* modes. It also does not declare two histories' remaining
completion factors equal.

## 3. First-energy cancellation in the restored operation

Let D₀,* be the finite first-coordinate set needed after the
restoration, and let E₀,* be its actual joint untruncated first
Schur form. The exterior first all-P factor remains exactly as in
EC0. For separated original first components, their full cross
quadratic is kept, or compared by its proved exponentially small
source error. Use the same E₀,* in the canceled upper and in the
weighted marginal lower.

For a fixed 0<θ<1, the direct finite integral estimate is

\[
 \overline M_{s,*}^{\rm self}
       \bigl(e^{E_{0,*}+\theta E_*}\bigr)
       \le \exp(C\mathfrak D_*).                            \tag{ER.6}
\]

**Proof.** Cancel only the literal negative first Schur form by
E₀,*. Its remaining independent entries satisfy |a|≤CP on the
actual globally Q-good first branch, including the original bad R
entries; every additional first entry has its all-P cutoff. Their
finite Lebesgue volume costs at most exp(C|D₀,*|log(P+1)). No
first seed lower bound is used.

All later terms in ER.3 retain the nonnegative fraction 1−θ of
their exponent. One may integrate ordinary Gaussian forms at this
reduced strength, or use the actual finite source cutoff volume
while retaining the displayed normalization. Both cost at most the
ledger ER.5. Group-valued integrations have normalized Haar volume
at most one. The soft gauge normalizations and B-coordinate powers
of g are included once. The complete interaction and its restored
Jacobian are bounded by ER.4 under the explicit coordinate/coarea
condition in Section 1. The terminal deltas are evaluated by that
condition, and are never bounded by one. The common reference constant has
already canceled as in Section 2. These statements prove ER.6. ∎

The proof does not use a common centered Gaussian and does not need
a lower bound on the probability of any global cutoff event.

## 4. Put the first rare weight back before the later integration

Let y_* contain all later variables, including the finite restored
ones. FR.2 still applies: no later source map or positive form reads
A₀ once the full V₁ and the geometric pattern are fixed. Any newly
explicit first A₀ entries remain inside b_s and E₀,*; they do not
enter E_* or the later measure as independent later coordinates. The exact complete
operation therefore uses the same first factor

\[
 Z_P(V_1)\,\Pi_*(dy_*),                                    \tag{ER.7}
\]

where Π_* includes all source completions, coarea and remaining
normalizations. For every admitted V₁, RB and the enlarged EC0
weighted-box proof give

\[
 b_s(V_1;1)\le w_s^G e^{o(1)},\qquad
 b_s(V_1;e^{E_{0,*}})\ge e^{-C|D_{0,*}|-o(1)}.              \tag{ER.8}
\]

The EC0 proof uses a witness in the original bad test cube, with all
added first entries in fixed O(1) balls and a retained first collar.
Enlarging D₀ changes its dimension cost, not its P² cost; the
conditional completion remains present. Combining ER.8 pointwise,
multiplying by the same later integrand, and then integrating ER.7
and using ER.6 yields

\[
 \overline M_{s,*}^{\rm full}(e^{\theta E_*})
       \le w_s^G\exp(C\mathfrak D_*+C\lambda_R).             \tag{ER.9}
\]

This is the order of operations needed to avoid a rare-event inverse.
The first factor is not estimated after an unrelated later integral.
The full/self conversion is the same complete cross-action estimate
used in LE, with the original retained-only normalization.

The original LE lower is unchanged by ER.2:

\[
 \overline M_{s,*}^{\rm full}(1)=\overline M_s^{\rm full}(1)
      \ge w_s^G\exp(-C D_{\rm late}-C\lambda_R).             \tag{ER.10}
\]

Consequently the exact restored conditional law satisfies

\[
 \boxed{\quad
 \nu_{s,*}(e^{\theta E_*})
       \le \exp(C\mathfrak D_*+C D_{\rm late}
                         +C\lambda_R).
 \quad}                                                     \tag{ER.11}
\]

The canceled first rare weight w_s^G has disappeared. There is no
P₀² term on the right.

## 5. Logarithmic collars and the remaining energy implication

Same final Λ determines the quiet domains from n=k−N₀+1 onward:
CG.6a–CG.8 give Ω_n^c=Λ+B_{4M}, Z_n=Λ+B_{6M}, and all subsequent
quiet iterates. Changed ordinary domains therefore lie at levels
j≤n−1. Their source radius M R_j is O(M) in current units; an
auxiliary radius

\[
 T_j=A R_j\log(1+R_j)                                      \tag{ER.12}
\]

has current size only O(MlogR). For weak coupling it stays within
the original Y buffer. It does not change the actual waiting rule
or the fresh kernel.

Such a list has

\[
 D_*\le C R^5\log^4(1+R),\qquad
 \mathfrak D_*
 \le C R^6\log^5(1+R)
      +C R^5\log(g^{-1})\log^4(1+R)+C.                     \tag{ER.13}
\]

Under the stated strict source hierarchy, or explicitly the
operational inequality \(H_R\gg\mathfrak D_*\), ER.11 controls
any actual event with

\[
 E_*\ge cH_R,\qquad H_R=p_1(g)^2/R^9:
 \quad
 \nu_{s,*}(E_*\ge cH_R)
 \le e^{C\mathfrak D_*-c\theta H_R+o(1)}=o(1).             \tag{ER.14}
\]

ER.14 is not yet a proof that every alternative t-mask failure has
this energy. That requires evaluating t's actual relative and gauge
variables on the restored state, including its padded sampler, and
proving the appropriate version of the source Wilson/gradient
inequalities (1.77)–(1.78). An arbitrary polynomial loss in this
comparison is not sufficient: its exponent must fit the strict
source reserve. Far completed cutoffs additionally require the
relative moving-cutoff argument. ER.11 supplies the probability
reserve for a correctly matched finite list without requiring a
common Gaussian density on that list.

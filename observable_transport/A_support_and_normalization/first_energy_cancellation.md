# Cancel the first retained Gaussian energy before bounding the later mass

This note develops the first-reset exchange route. It
uses the normalization of the first-step R-birth comparison;
it does not substitute a globally normalized all-Q-good law for that
normalization. The main new result is a weighted first-birth lower bound
whose exponent has no P^2 birth cost.

## 1. Which quadratic form is canceled

In CMP119 (1.29), printed p.254, the first retained quadratic exponent is

\[
 -\tfrac12\langle A,KA\rangle
 +\tfrac12\langle A,K C^{(0)}(\Lambda_1)K A\rangle
 =-E_0(A_D).                                        \tag{EC0.1}
\]

It is the untruncated Schur quadratic after Gaussian integration on the
regular complement. The completion factor generated by the complement's
actual cutoff remains in the source E/B terms from (1.26). It is not
discarded in the calculation below. At the component factorization in
(2.21), intercomponent Schur entries remain in B; the first retained
component has its own diagonal Schur block.

For the comparison first work with the flat positive precision K on all
free coordinates and let D be the actual first retained birth domain,
including the source boundary-bond convention. In the globally P/Q-good,
R-only case it has `|D|=O(R^4)` and a full MR collar beyond the bad-test
cover. Write O for its regular complement. The exact Gaussian marginal
precision and conditional mean are

\[
 S_D=K_{DD}-K_{DO}K_{OO}^{-1}K_{OD},\qquad
 E_0^G(u)=\tfrac12u^{\mathsf T}S_Du,
 \qquad m(u)=-K_{OO}^{-1}K_{OD}u.                  \tag{EC0.2}
\]

The gap and upper bound of K give uniform positive bounds on S_D.
The flat K has finite range in the actual free coordinates, not merely
in an abstract diagonalization of the Gaussian law.

Let

\[
 p_O(u)=\mathbb P\{G_O+m(u)\in\mathcal K_{P,O}\},
 \qquad G_O\sim N(0,K_{OO}^{-1}),                   \tag{EC0.3}
\]

where `K_{P,O}` is the actual symmetric convex product cutoff of radius P
on the complement. Gaussian disintegration is exact, including the
entire completion factor p_O. If `phi_D` is the normalized untruncated
marginal density, the all-small baseline is

\[
 Z_P^G=\int_{\mathcal K_{P,D}}\phi_D(u)p_O(u)du.     \tag{EC0.4}
\]

## 2. A symmetric-cutoff comparison with no global-volume error

For any symmetric convex domain C and centered Gaussian with positive
precision H,

\[
 e^{-\frac12 m^{\mathsf T}Hm}\,
       \mathbb P(G\in C)
 \le \mathbb P(G+m\in C)
 \le \mathbb P(G\in C).                            \tag{EC0.5}
\]

The lower inequality is the Cameron--Martin change of density followed
by Jensen: the conditional mean of G on the symmetric event C is zero.
For a direct proof of the upper inequality, put
`p(m)=int_C exp[-(x-m)^T H(x-m)/2] dx` up to its constant normalization.
Differentiation gives

\[
 \operatorname{Hess}_m\log p
       =-H+H\operatorname{Cov}_{m,C}(x)H\le0.
\]

The covariance inequality follows by changing variables with `H^{1/2}`
and applying the convex-domain Gaussian Poincare inequality; smooth
convex boundary barriers give the same hard-domain limit. Thus p is
log-concave. It is even, so its maximum is at zero. This proves (EC0.5)
without requiring a product structure after the linear change of
variables or a sign condition on the correlations.

Applied to (EC0.3), it gives

\[
 e^{-u^{\mathsf T}K_{DO}K_{OO}^{-1}K_{OD}u/2}p_O(0)
       \le p_O(u)\le p_O(0).                       \tag{EC0.6}
\]

There is no estimate of the probability that an extensive outside region
is all good. That probability is the common factor p_O(0), which will
cancel exactly.

## 3. A trial box after the marginal quadratic has been canceled

For each bad R-test cube choose one witness coordinate outside every
good-test footprint, as in RB.3. There are only a fixed source number of
such coordinates. Put them in fixed-width balls about vectors of norm
`5P/4`, and put all other D coordinates in fixed-width balls about zero.
Call the product box B. For sufficiently large P it has the required
exact R pattern and satisfies every Q test with a strict margin.

The witness coordinates lie deep inside D. Indeed a bad test footprint
extends by two test layers beyond its test cube, while D extends by an
MR layer beyond the region cover. The fixed hierarchy `M>>L M2` leaves
a positive MR collar. Its width exceeds the fixed range of K. Hence
`K_OD u_witness=0` for the flat finite-range precision. Every coordinate
that contributes to `K_OD u` is O(1) on B. Consequently

\[
 u^{\mathsf T}K_{DO}K_{OO}^{-1}K_{OD}u
      \le C|D|,\qquad u\in B.                     \tag{EC0.7}
\]

An exponentially localized precision would give the same bound with an
additional `CP^2 exp(-cR)` term. The actual flat first-step precision is
finite range, so that weakening is unnecessary here.

Define the Gaussian weighted defect relative to the same all-P baseline
as in RB.10--RB.14:

\[
 b_R^G(e^{E_0^G})
  =\frac{\int {\bf1}_{E_R}{\bf1}_{Q_{2P}}
                e^{E_0^G(u)}\phi_D(u)p_O(u)du}
         {Z_P^G}.                                  \tag{EC0.8}
\]

Only the lower-bound box B is needed, so the upper 2P product and every
R alternative can remain literal. Since
`exp(E_0^G(u))phi_D(u)=(2pi)^{-d_D/2}det(S_D)^{1/2}`
and this constant is bounded below by `c^{d_D}`, (EC0.6)--(EC0.7) give

\[
 \text{numerator in (EC0.8)}\ge
       c^{d_D}\operatorname{Vol}(B)e^{-C|D|}p_O(0)
       \ge e^{-C|D|}p_O(0).
\]

On the other hand, (EC0.4) and the upper bound in (EC0.6) imply
`Z_P^G<=p_O(0)`. Therefore

\[
 \boxed{\qquad b_R^G(e^{E_0^G})\ge e^{-C|D|}.
        \qquad}                                    \tag{EC0.9}
\]

This does not say that the unweighted R birth is inexpensive. Its usual
P^2 cost has been canceled by the explicitly inserted first marginal
energy. The calculation is invalid if one changes the denominator to an
unrelated global all-Q-good normalizer.

## 4. Match the actual retained quadratic and preserve completion factors

The flat comparison RMg.32 applies to every nonnegative local function
on D, including a bounded-on-the-cutoff function of the form
`exp(E_0(V,u)) 1_B(u)`. It remains to match its quadratic with EC0.2.
That match follows from the same local precision estimates, at the
untruncated Gaussian level.

In the local coarse gauge, K(V) differs from Kflat by
`O(R^2 epsilon)` on an R halo of D and has bounded rows globally. The
resolvent identity and the common exponential inverse bounds imply

\[
 \|P_D[K(V)^{-1}-K_{\rm flat}^{-1}]P_D\|
       \le C\{R^2\epsilon+e^{-cR}\}.               \tag{EC0.10}
\]

To verify this estimate, split the precision difference into its
R-halo part, whose operator norm is `O(R^2 epsilon)`, and its remaining
entries. A factor connecting D to those remaining entries has norm
`O(exp(-cR))`, by absolute exponential row/column sums. The inverse
covariances of the D marginals have uniform bounded spectra. Inverting
the compressed covariances therefore gives the same bound for their
Schur precisions. On the 3P product domain,

\[
 |E_0(V,u)-E_0^G(u)|
   \le C|D|P^2\{R^2\epsilon+e^{-cR}\}=o(1).       \tag{EC0.11}
\]

If other retained birth components are present, their Schur cross
entries are kept in B by the source. A source MR collar separates them.
The same inverse estimate makes their effect on this diagonal Schur
comparison exponentially small, rather than treating them as part of
the canceled quadratic. This extension requires that actual separated
first-step geometry; an arbitrary previous-history precision has not
been substituted.

The source re-localization (1.22) preserves the R indicators exactly.
Its displacement is at most `CP exp(-cMR)` in each free coordinate,
and all its Jacobian/completion terms remain in the density. On the
specific lower-bound box, the witness norm `5P/4` is below the printed
`4P/3` threshold where its smooth switch is zero, so the free-coordinate
map is in fact identity there. Either this direct observation or the
uniform displacement bound gives an `o(1)` quadratic correction in
(EC0.11). It does not justify dropping the source boundary terms.

Apply RMg.32--RMg.35 to the weighted local function and to the local
all-P event, with the exact Jacobians retained. The Q sandwich has a
strict margin on B. Equations (EC0.9)--(EC0.11) give the actual lower bound

\[
 \boxed{\qquad
 b_R(V;e^{E_0})\ge e^{-C|D|-o(1)} .
 \qquad}                                           \tag{EC0.12}
\]

Here `b_R(V;A)` means the actual first-step R-sector integral with the
insertion A, divided by the same original ZP(V). The bound is uniform
in admitted coarse V and total volume, on the first-step globally
P/Q-regular, contractible-buffer scope of RB.2. It includes the full
complement cutoff through (EC0.3); no complement factor has been set to
one. The local gauge rotates the trial box but leaves its R/Q admission
and the actual quadratic invariant in the transformed coordinates.

## 5. Consequence for a genuinely factored later law

This explains exactly how the estimate would remove the P^2 cost from
the later-only reserve. Suppose the h=1 annular Fubini identity has
identified the complete actual R-only sector as

\[
 M_R(F)=C_r(v)\int {\bf1}_{G_R}(y)\,
        \Pi(dy)\,Z_P(V_1(y))\,b_R(V_1(y);F),       \tag{EC0.13}
\]

with a common positive later measure Pi and the actual retained readout.
All cross interactions must either be included in Pi or retained in the
integrand with the already proved uniform oscillation comparison; the
formula must not omit them. For an energy E_late depending only on those
later variables, (EC0.12) gives

\[
 \int {\bf1}_{G_R}e^{\theta E_{\rm late}}
                         Z_P(V_1)\,\Pi(dy)
 \le e^{C|D|+o(1)} C_r(v)^{-1}
                         M_R(e^{E_0+\theta E_{\rm late}}).
                                                               \tag{EC0.14}
\]

The right side has the first retained Gaussian energy canceled exactly
in the literal source integrand (EC0.1). On its remaining first retained
coordinates the Q selector gives radius at most `(2+o(1))P`; their volume
cost is `exp(O(|D|log P))`. The complete first-step W is small on that
fixed enlarged chart. The remaining source energies can keep a fixed
positive fraction of their reserve.

LFR II p.383 explicitly estimates the remaining compact integrations
by one, the B integral by its domain volume after keeping the stated
normalization terms, and the ordinary Gaussian integrations by
`exp(C dimension)`. Its finite-history sums are charged to the actual
region counts. On the bounded young h=1 later geometry those counts are
`O(R^5)`, and the same later trial as RT with its birth part removed has
logarithmic cost

\[
 D_{\rm late}=C\{R^6\log(1+R)+R^5\log g^{-1}\}.
                                                               \tag{EC0.15}
\]

Thus, **once the common reassembly and its positive-energy ledger in
(EC0.13) have been checked**, the needed upper is a later-only
`exp(C D_late)` bound; it does not need to be compared with `exp(CP^2)`.
The old total-energy moment RT.18 alone would not prove this refinement.

There is a second required check before comparing two quiet masks: a
common refinement retaining the union of their annular coordinate
lists must carry both tests with the same normalization and positive
reserve. It is not enough that each separate coordinate list has small
rank. The source failed-preparation estimate must be applied to that
actual common integral, not to two unrelated normalized measures.

The operational source penalty, LFR II (1.77)--(1.78), p.383, is

\[
 H_R=c R^{-9}p_1(g)^2
 \quad(d=4),                                       \tag{EC0.16}
\]

and its stated exponent hierarchy bounds the failure factor by
`exp[-p_0(g)]`. A positive deletion-ratio proof would require
`D_late=o(H_R)`. The printed sufficient hierarchy is
`2p_1-9r_0>p_0`, with `p_1<p_0`, where r0 is the radius majorant exponent
used on pp.363 and 383. It forces `p_0>9r_0`. Recording a radius majorant
`r_0>=r` makes (EC0.15) negligible compared with the failure penalty.
This strict exponent comparison should be kept explicit; the mere fact
that the first birth cutoff has the larger exponent p0 is not useful
for dividing an absolute preparation error by a birth probability.

## 6. Proof boundary

EC0.9 and EC0.12 are weighted first-step lower bounds at the same scope
as RB.2. Their completion-factor cancellation and local quadratic
matching are explicit. They do not prove the common later reassembly
(EC0.13), its two-mask refinement, or a depth-uniform microscopic mark
budget. Those are the remaining tasks before the suggested later-only
reserve becomes a complete residual exchange theorem.

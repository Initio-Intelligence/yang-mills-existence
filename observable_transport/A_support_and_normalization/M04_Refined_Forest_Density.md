# The positive density and classical cancellation on a finite refined forest

This note supplies the full-path density algebra and the individual finite-density ledger behind the two-list refinement. A finite common density with deterministic masks additionally needs the completion-factor identification stated below; it is not inferred from the existence of the common path space.
The auxiliary refinement may have several early holes; it is not
declared to be another literal residual history. All actual s and t
selectors are pulled back unchanged. The large retained classical
action is canceled by the same fresh reference factor, and the old
normalizations are kept. The final Gaussian comparison still requires
the quantitative common logarithm-chart interface described in
Section 6. It is not obtained merely by naming a quadratic form.

## 1. Extend the common baseline without changing either history mass

Use LE.4–LE.5, with the fixed class r and its common retained-only
normalization. The common later measure contains Z_P(V₁). Extend it by
the normalized original all-P first conditional q_P(dA₀|V₁):

\[
 Z_P(V_1)\,\Pi(dy)\,q_P(dA_0\mid V_1)
  =\Pi(dy)\,1_{\{|A_0|<P\}}
             \mathcal D_{V_1}(A_0)\,dA_0.
 \tag{FD.1}
\]

Here D is the complete first Wilson/coarea/gauge density. Formula
(FD.1) is initially an identity on the full first field, whose rank may
be extensive. In the finite refinement retain explicitly only the
first coordinates in the declared union D_s∪D_t and its fixed native
collar, of rank O(R⁴). Integrate the remaining first coordinates
exactly, retaining their actual conditional completion factor and
all E/B contributions. Equivalently leave Z_P in its complete
all-small effective action until one of these local modes is needed.
The global first augmentation is never charged to D_ref=O(R⁵). The old first variables are auxiliary in
(FD.1): both later masks depend only on y, and integrating them
returns precisely Z_P. No first R seed or its P² cost is introduced.
The actual first R event is still represented by b_s(V₁) or b_t(V₁)
outside this auxiliary conditional, exactly as in RB and LE.

Take the Ω/Λ refinement and native lists of
[M04 Two List Common Refinement](M04_Two_List_Common_Refinement.md). Reinstate each needed regular
integration with its complete action identity. On each layer this is
the positive identity underlying CMP119 (1.26) or (3.25)–(3.29), and
LFR I (1.25)–(1.29). In particular, the integrated determinant and
cutoff-completion factor are removed from the completed action at
the same time that the corresponding raw integral is restored.

Undoing all required complete identities gives an exact common
positive *full path* measure, with both padded normalized gauge lists.
Let P denote its finite promoted raw readout and let ρ_ref be the
pushforward by P. For the complete history mask H_i define

\[
 h_i(z)=\mathbb E[H_i\mid P=z],\qquad
 \int H_i\,d\rho_{\rm path}
       =\int h_i(z)\,d\rho_{\rm ref}(z),
 \quad 0\le h_i\le1,\quad i=s,t.
 \tag{FD.2}
\]

The first equality is understood for a common finite normalization;
its unnormalized version follows by disintegration. Both projections
have the same exterior current readout. The full cross action remains.

One may replace h_i by a deterministic indicator H_i(z) only after
proving that every noncommon integrated selector has been exposed,
or that its entire conditional completion factor is literally common.
Otherwise the h_i retain genuine fractional completion factors. A
finite raw/free rank bound alone does not prove their measurability.
In particular original quiet R cutoffs outside a restored annulus
can use different global variational backgrounds in the two histories.
Their integrated effects must not silently be absorbed into a common W.

The source does not make Ω_j identical for translated first R births:
although Ω₁ is full in both, (3.5) at the next step uses the preceding
Z_j=Λ_j^c. With no new P/Q fields, Ω_{j+1}^c is the next-grid cover of
Z_j plus eight layers. Thus Ω₂ and its minimizing background already
depend on the first R location. An exact common finite density needs a
further completion-cancellation or relative-completion theorem. The
ordinary source locality estimates are relevant to such a theorem,
but its conclusion is not contained in Fubini alone.

## 2. The source gauge variables can be padded in their original small law

CMP119 (1.5), p.247, is a normalized identity. The variable U(y,x)
appearing there is the source Euclidean average of contour transports,
not a chosen single tree path. Its exact endpoint covariance is
sufficient. At a fixed block center y, put

\[
 w_x=U(y,x)\,u(x)^{-1},\qquad u(y)=1.
 \tag{FD.3}
\]

Then Haar du(x)=dw_x, and the inserted factor has the original law

\[
 \prod_{x\ne y}
 \frac{1}{Z_j}
  1_{\{\|w_x-I\|<\varepsilon_j\}}
  \exp[-g_j^{-2}(1-\operatorname{Re}\operatorname{tr}w_x)]\,dw_x.
 \tag{FD.4}
\]

It is independent of the physical field. Reconstructing the actual
u from (FD.3) retains every subsequent Q/R and preparation test.
LFR I p.182 inserts exactly these source factors on its new annular
gauge sites. A hard axial gauge instead fixes the corresponding
variable to I, with its normalized delta.

Thus an unused gauge sampler belonging only to t can be padded into
the s description with integral one while retaining a small source
support. One need not impose an artificial smallness restriction on
an otherwise arbitrary Haar gauge u. The original soft gauge
exponent and its Z_j are still present in the density. Their
logarithmic normalizations are included in the native ledger below.

## 3. The common reference and the forest force identity

The fresh class fixes the same Λ, Y, ζ₀ and the same reference orbit
and representative. Its field is the source

\[
 U_0=U_{k,Y}(V_\Lambda).
 \tag{FD.5}
\]

LFR II (1.3) states its variational property with the exterior
constraints on Y\Λ. Therefore its current J₀ satisfies

\[
 \langle\delta A,J_0\rangle=0
 \quad\text{whenever }Q_{\mathbb B_k(Y)}\delta A=0
                          \text{ on }Y\setminus\Lambda.
 \tag{FD.6}
\]

In particular J₀=0 on Λ. This statement does not refer to a chain of
inner rectangles. It applies to every mixed infinitesimal variation
H_ref B preserving the same exterior constraints. Gauge fixing must
respect those exterior anchors; it cannot change the constraints.

Let I be the union of the first-scale inner holes of the refined
forest, including their source buffers. There are at most a fixed
number of components, and each has O(R₁⁴) native volume. Merge
touching buffered components if necessary. Choose disjoint smooth
inner cutoff collars and put ζ₁ equal to their sum. Then
0≤ζ₁≤1. On the outer support put ζ=ζ₀(1−ζ₁), exactly as in
LFR II (1.18). The cutoff derivative bounds are the original local
scale bounds, now summed over finitely many components.

The linear term in the Wilson expansion is treated just as follows,
rather than assumed absent. Localize H_ref into its outer-reference
piece and the terms meeting ∂I. The latter have the source
exponential separation from supp ζ. In the former, summation by
parts leaves a term satisfying (FD.6), which is exactly zero, and
terms containing derivatives of ζ₀ or ζ₁. At ∂Y, the fluctuation
propagator gives the original exp(−cMR_k) reserve. At each component
of ∂I, the reference field in the h=1 scale has size L^{−N} times
its current-scale bound. This is the term calculated on p.363,
immediately before (1.30). Adding the component contributions
changes only a fixed multiplicative constant.

This is the actual force cancellation. A general off-shell minimum
would have a nonzero force term and would not give the same bound.

## 4. Forest extension of the classical cancellation

Use the exact average constraints and the actual relative
variables V″=exp(i g_kB) M_{B_ref}U₀ outside I, keeping the inner
V₁ variables. The ordinary variational expansions used in
(1.19)–(1.24) are available for arbitrary admissible mixed
determining domains (CMP102 and CMP119 (2.14)–(2.15)); they are
not a theorem restricted to one rectangular annulus. The fixed
forest gauge is only a way to remove the allowed gauge freedom.

The resulting positive forms are

\[
 Q_{\rm ref}
   =\tfrac12\sum_p\eta^4\zeta(x(p))
                     |D H_{\rm ref}B(p)|^2\ge0,
 \tag{FD.7}
\]

and

\[
 S_{\rm ref}
   =A\!\left(g_k(\cdot)^{-2}\zeta_1,
              U''_{\rm ref}(e^{ig_kB},V_1)\right)\ge0.
 \tag{FD.8}
\]

The reference A₀ has been removed from the arguments of (FD.8),
using the exact representation (1.46), rather than replaced by
zero in the original old field. The positivity in (FD.8) is
literal on the real slice, because its field is G-valued and its
coupling weights are positive. At B=0,V₁=I it is exactly zero.

The large constant A(ζ₀,U₀)/g_k² is canceled by the identical
constant in the fixed fresh denominator, through (1.30)–(1.37).
Since r fixes U₀ and ζ₀, this is the same constant for s, t and
the auxiliary refinement. It is not charged to a bound O(P²).

Here is the remainder ledger. Let P_* bound the dimensionless
relative coordinates on a declared common regular chart, and
assume P_* is a fixed polynomial in R and the source logarithmic
cutoffs. The count D_ref is O(R⁵). The terms left by the source
identities have the following bounds, with fixed finite powers:

| Term | Actual mechanism and bound |
| --- | --- |
| Outer localization | The Taylor terms in (1.17) have an exp(−cMR_k) factor from the gap between supp ζ₀ and ∂Y; the remaining factors are polynomial in R and the cutoffs. |
| Inner-reference comparison | The H-function in (1.22) is sourced at ∂I. The same local decay estimate (1.23) applies separately at each buffered component. |
| Mixed cubic and nonleading Hessian terms | The expansion (1.20)–(1.21) has size g_k times a fixed polynomial in D_ref,R,P_*, plus a term ε_k times such a polynomial. Its original estimate already counts N native scales and uses N≤R. |
| Linear term | The main piece is zero by (FD.6). The two cutoff boundaries give the outer exponential reserve and L^{−N} times a polynomial, as on pp.363–364. |

Every row tends to zero under the original N=R rule and fixed
source hierarchy, on such a regular chart. None uses the first

does not introduce a term proportional to the uncanceled full

The condition on the chart is substantive: a bound for the
native coordinates and the actual mixed variational maps must
justify the displayed polynomial factors. The common logarithm
chart under construction supplies the coordinates; its derivative
and domain bounds are addressed in Section 6.

## 5. The complete finite density ledger

After these exact operations, keep the first all-small Gaussian
forms, all remaining ordinary Schur forms, (FD.7), (FD.8), and
the soft gauge factors in (FD.4) as positive energies. Denote their
sum by E_ref. For each finite auxiliary restoration, the positive measure has the form

\[
 d\widehat\rho
   =C_{r,v}\,1_{\mathcal A}(z)\,
      e^{-E_{\rm ref}(z)+W_{\rm ref}(z)}\,d\lambda_{\rm ref}(z),
 \tag{FD.9}
\]

where λ_ref is the literal remaining Lebesgue/Haar measure with
its graph constraints. Every normalization factor has either
remained in λ_ref/W_ref or belongs to the single retained-only
C_r,v. The old first Z_P is retained through (FD.1) and its exact local
complement completion. When comparing two restorations, (FD.9)
describes each one; their W_ref are not declared equal without the
additional completion identification following (FD.2).
The symbol A denotes the actual common regular support; no claim
that it is convex has yet been made.

The complete nonenergy action, rather than one selected self
piece, is retained in W_ref. Its absolute source estimate is

\[
 \operatorname{osc}W_{\rm ref}
   \le C\left[
       \sum_{j=1}^k |Z_j^{\rm ref}|\log g_j^{-2}
       +D_{\rm ref}\log(1+R)+N D_{\rm ref}\right]+o(1)
   \le C D_{\rm late},
 \tag{FD.10}
\]

on a common source-admitted regular chart.

The justification is the actual source ledger. CMP119's
completed ordinary integration is valid on each admissible
nested block domain; (2.43)–(2.49) bound its E/R/B terms before
the later resummation. LFR I states after (1.71) that the annular
integrations add boundary terms to that same effective action.
Restoring one of those integrations and then treating its finite
refinement retains the full identity, so the number of charged
native domains is the finite union of the two lists, not an
extensive finest-lattice region. Localization/summation assigns
the terms wholly inside the completed current component to its
self action. Their bound is (1.69), counted by the actual old
native determining volumes. Nonself terms retain (1.68), and
their complete state-dependent sum is bounded by HS20.7.

Vacuum and ordinary normalization terms are counted with
log g_j^{-2}, exactly as stated on LFR II p.380. The first
constraint determinant, the relocalization determinant, every
restored Gaussian determinant and the displayed B Haar-coordinate
factor are assigned once. On any fixed-order polynomially
conditioned change of native coordinates, its remaining finite
Jacobian costs at most C D_ref log(1+R); it must be verified for
the chosen chart, not presumed for an arbitrary map. Finite
geometric/orientation choices and an extra conservative N factor
are covered by the last two terms of (FD.10).

No chronological large-field lower factor is used for (FD.10).
Such a factor would be unavailable on the artificial first-all-P
forest. The estimate uses only the absolute unmarked action and
normalization bounds on the admitted chart.

## 6. Precise remaining Gaussian interface

To turn (FD.9)–(FD.10) into CE.1, the following common-chart
statements must be checked for the same finite measure:

1. Choose the boundary-preserving mixed forest gauge and native
   logarithm/free coordinates. All original gauge variables remain
   in the padded form (FD.4). The chart and its inverse must have
   fixed-order derivative and Jacobian bounds polynomial in the
   native count, rather than in the finest-lattice diameter.
2. Impose the common linear constraints and linear gauges on one
   quotient, and let K_ref be the Hessian of the positive energy
   E_ref at its specified reference center. Its physical null
   gauge directions must be removed; a normalized Haar gauge
   direction is not a positive Gaussian direction.
3. Show on a declared common encompassing domain that
   E_ref(z)=c+ell(z)+z^T K_ref z/2+R(z), with
   osc R=o(H_R), and keep ell as an actual force/mean shift.
   The exact force cancellation in Section 3 removes the large
   U₀ term; it does not by itself eliminate every finite cutoff
   or coordinate force.
4. Move the exact nonlinear common support tests into both
   history masks, or prove a common symmetric convex support
   enclosure. A nonlinear domain is not treated as convex just
   because its linearization is convex. Every moved test must
   satisfy the same row/shift/error bound used in CE.3–CE.6.

The GR common weak-link construction and the source's uniform
mixed variational bounds are the relevant tools for item 1 and
for the fixed-order Taylor estimates in item 3. For example,
with D_ref=O(R⁵), a bound |z|∞≤P poly(D_ref) and uniform native
third-derivative rows gives a remainder g_k poly(R,P), which
tends to zero. The four-dimensional source action scaling is
essential: a native cell contributes its scale-normalized
energy; one must not replace that count by all microscopic
plaquettes in Λ.

The content proved here is the exact common full-path disintegration,
the forest force/classical cancellation, and the individual finite
density ledger. A common finite density with deterministic masks
also requires the completion identification stated after (FD.2).
The chart, positive quotient and nonlinear selector interface
above must be supplied before claiming the nonlinear two-mask
comparison. The literal-chain LE theorem is already independent
of this further interface.

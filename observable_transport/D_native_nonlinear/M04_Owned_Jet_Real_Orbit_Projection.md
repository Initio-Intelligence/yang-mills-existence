# Real gauge-orbit projection preserves the owned dilation rows

This note supplies the representation premise needed to combine SD's small owned linear jets with GC's native weak-curvature estimating gauges. The projection is performed on the complete four-copy scalar family over compact real gauge orbits. It preserves the actual physical current origins because it preserves the entire independent-copy amplitude, before differentiating any copy. Its quantitative input is the full orbit-covariant dilation family, not a gauge projection of a flat numerical Hessian after that family has been forgotten.

The statement concerns the fixed three smooth P integrations. No moving hard-Q domain or arbitrary independent complex gauge orbit is used.

## 1. The actual joint gauge action

Let `A(b_M,b_0,b_1,b_2)` be SD's four-copy amplitude at the physical endpoint, expressed in final normalized charts. A single real retained site gauge u acts on all four physical arguments simultaneously:

\[
 U_{r,e}\longmapsto U^u_{r,e}
        =u_{e_-}U_{r,e}u_{e_+}^{-1},
       \qquad r=M,0,1,2.
 \tag{JG.1}
\]

This is not four independent gauge symmetries. Every finer field, reference, target, force and independent innovation is transported by the prescribed block-constant lift of that same u. The lifted free-coordinate action is a real orthogonal adjoint map independent of every field copy and independent of the dilation parameter.

For the literal independent-copy formula TR.27, stopped or completed at these three levels, this simultaneous action is a symmetry of the complete amplitude. Indeed the microscopic Wilson mark is invariant. The source reconstruction maps are equivariant under the fixed lift. A normalized kernel receives the corresponding transformed full target and reference, and the change of its free variables is the fixed real adjoint action. Its P domain, quadratic form, coarea/Haar factor and exact denominator transform together. The same change is made in every occurrence of that free variable in the independent-copy formula. Repeating this argument through the three nested integrations proves

\[
 A(\mathbf U^u)=A(\mathbf U).
 \tag{JG.2}
\]

This is the same full-input covariance used in SW and EC, now applied before the four copies are identified. A new identity reference must not be chosen after the transport. Any auxiliary cap of a real integration coordinate can and must be chosen odd and adjoint equivariant, with its identity plateau containing the original P domain; an odd radial cap in an adjoint-invariant norm has these properties. This is only a coefficient extension and does not alter any physical integral.

## 3. The endpoint must be kept coefficientwise

There is a stronger registry fact than the statement that the total Gaussian endpoint has zero kernel currents.

### Lemma JG.1 — only the microscopic copy enters each endpoint source coefficient

Use the complete local registry of SD before summing its scalar tags. It can be chosen so that at t=0 every complete scalar coefficient is independent of the terminal copies `b_0,b_1,b_2`. This holds uniformly on the real orbit family (JG.3).

**Proof.** Each current-level classical action is subtracted at its exact fiber-critical zero-innovation background. This cancellation is performed in the actual complete physical normal form before the component restrictions are taken; TC's native coefficient continuation then restricts the already canceled coefficients and retains their displayed powers of t. No independently decoupled minimum is declared critical for the original undeformed action. At t=0 the surviving quadratic coefficient is the flat transported precision, independent of all terminal b's and all ancestral normalized innovations. The same is true of each component-restricted precision: its entries and local inverse branches are formed from the limiting transported flat data, with the same complete footprint. The local determinant, its marginal inverse, the fixed P domains and all failed-P factors are therefore independent of every terminal b at that endpoint. The inherited analytic differences and nonlinear fluctuation vertices either vanish there or leave only those b-independent flat Gaussian data. No outside-decoupled reference is reset.

The microscopic source has an actual second-order zero at the flat pure-gauge field before any integration. Dividing by `t^2` leaves its quadratic polynomial evaluated at the linearized microscopic reconstruction. That reconstruction contains the terminal field only through `b_M`, with the three centered innovation terms of SD.20–SD.21. Every local component of that microscopic reconstruction has the same copy ownership; the old kernel copies do not enter it.

Every endpoint primitive used in the complete normalized component expansion is thus independent of `b_0,b_1,b_2`, and the sole marked primitive can depend on `b_M`. Products, component integrations, denominator cancellations and convergent connected sums preserve that absence of variables. This proves the statement tag by tag. The fixed real gauge transport only rotates the data and hence preserves it. \(\square\)

This lemma is the relevant reason a small row estimate can be retained in the same amplitude representation. If one knew only that a sum of nonzero endpoint current tags canceled, it would not justify assigning the small row bound to every coefficient of that unchanged amplitude.

## 4. Joint Haar projection and the owned rows

\[
 (\mathcal P_{\rm joint}A_X)(\mathbf U)

Apply this operation to the complete orbit-covariant t family, after its common scalar parity projection; the two averages commute because one is a fixed real gauge action and the other preserves that gauge while reversing t and the normalized fields.

 r_X^M=j_X^M-d(\mathcal C_Xf_X),\qquad

 \tag{JG.7}

\[
 \sum_{r=M,0,1,2}r_X^r

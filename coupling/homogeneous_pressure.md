# Homogeneous Wilson pressure and coupling feedback

Applying Gaussian drift calibration requires GC. These estimates retain their window and chart restrictions.

## 1. Which coupling is being measured

Write \(g=X_B^{-1/2}\) for the source variable and \(x=X_B/(2N^2)\) for the program variable. Then \(\beta_W=X_B/N=2Nx\). Under coarsening the program decrement is the new bulk projection divided by \(2N^2\). It is the curvature Hessian extracted from the vacuum-subtracted regular birth by the spatial second moment, after the explicit Gaussian and coarea terms are included. The projector is evaluated in infinite volume. A projector on a single torus cannot be exchanged for it without the localization or limit estimate.

For the fixed physical cutoff in CMP109 (2.9), the analysis of the native readout gives
\[
  D_k^G=d k+O_R(1),\qquad d=11\log L/(24\pi^2),
\]
where \(D_k^G\) is the cumulative uncut stationary Gaussian decrement in program units and \(R\) is a fixed compatible quantized-flux test period. the homogeneous comparison 2 §§1–3.1 works with Haar coarea, the stabilizer correction and signed vector/ghost determinant. Its Gaussian sum is independent of the finite-coupling cutoff once the same readout and stationary action are used. O0 (12.11)–(12.13) controls the finite-coupling deviation by \(C x_j^{-1/8}(1+\log x_j)^6\) at each admitted step **of the fixed CMP109 prescription**. the homogeneous comparison 1 §13.1 supplies finite-depth continuity along that prescription. Together these results give weak fixed-terminal shooting in that prescription. The prefix error need only be bounded and the cumulative interacting error is sublinear.

In the complete construction, CMP119 p.253 (1.27) and p.259 (2.24) explicitly identify the coupling with the small-field coefficient. Pages 278–279 identify interior coefficients with the corresponding whole-lattice regular calculation and subtract the Wilson projection from the regular birth. These statements allow use of the actual corresponding whole-lattice construction. Three features of that construction matter.

1. On p.250, (1.18) gives the canonical first-step test \(\|A(b)\|<\delta_0/g_0\) on independent bonds. Here \(\delta_0/g_0\) is a fixed multiple of a power of \(\log g_0^{-2}\). In contrast, the fixed physical cutoff in CMP109 (2.9) gives the radius \(\epsilon_1/g_0\). The O0 estimate explicitly excludes alternative cutoffs. Each step has a different integration domain under the two prescriptions.
2. Before CMP119 (3.26), pp.270–271, the fluctuation differences of sufficiently old, grid-covariant \(R^{(j)}\) are absorbed into the term denoted again by \(E_k\). Recent \(R\) and boundary terms are treated in their separate interpolations. Equation (3.28) consequently retains the absorbed old contribution. There is no separate beta for each random history, but the fact that the new counterterm is subtracted from a term named \(E\) does not remove the absorbed terms. The regular action depends on earlier coupling and action data.
3. The source prints \(A_k'=g_k A_k\) before (3.15) but uses \(\|A_k\|<\delta_k\) at (3.16). The first-step formula prints the inverse coupling explicitly. Annex W §6 already flags the discrepancy. There is stronger evidence than analogy for the ordinary operator. On p.269, immediately after (3.20), the same (3.16) characteristic function is apportioned between the integrated regular domain and its surviving collar in (3.21). The subsequent localization changes the background-dependent factor on the exceptional set and explicitly preserves the other characteristic functions. LFR I p.178 transcribes, before modification, the last ordinary factors of the retained (2.21) operations: (1.8) contains \(\exp(ig_j A_j)\) and (1.9) explicitly uses the cutoff \(\delta_j/g_j\) on the inherited collar. Thus the *matched completed* ordinary prescription uses \(\delta_j/g_j\) for this uniform indicator, including its integrated copy. The different literal reading of CMP119 (3.16) is recorded. It cannot support the Gaussian defect estimate in §2, since its canonical radius would tend to zero. No claim that the author published an erratum is needed.

An extended identification that retains the actual counterterms and old reset contributions requires a strictly positive lower bound on *each* decrement. That condition cannot be deduced from a bounded cumulative transient. Restrictions on the selectable source domain powers also remain. The aggregate arithmetic below makes explicit the weaker numerical inputs one can use if the analytic comparison is developed further.

## 2. First interacting Wilson layer with its actual cutoff

**Proposition.** Fix the block readout and source constants. Let \(\beta_1^{119}(g)\) be the whole-lattice regular first birth's curvature projection, and \(\beta_1^G\) the same projection of the uncut Gaussian determinant including its original Haar/gauge coarea. Then, for sufficiently small \(g>0\),
\[
 |\beta_1^{119}(g)-\beta_1^G|
 \le C g(1+\log g^{-2})^{p_0}.                                      \tag{1}
\]
The constant is independent of the size of the compatible torus used to form the thermodynamic projection. No sign is inferred for the first Gaussian increment. Neither a reset nor an older regular activity is present in this particular coefficient.

**Proof.** Write \(r=\delta_0/g\) and \(\theta=gr\). The source chooses \(r\) to be a fixed positive constant times \((\log g^{-2})^{p_0}\), \(p_0\ge1\). At the bare step on the whole-lattice regular branch, solve the averaged link constraints for the distinguished bonds as in CMP119 p.249. The first test in (1.19) applies to the independent fluctuation coordinates. The second test is one, as proved immediately following (1.19). On a whole lattice the cube products in (1.18) therefore combine into one independent-bond product of indicators of the Lie-algebra balls of radius \(r\). Repeated indicators on a bond give the same indicator. The terminal external regular indicator is outside the integral. No derivative is taken of that indicator. The localized bulk coefficient is defined on its interior at the flat field.

The unnormalized hard Gaussian determinant and coarea from (1.15)–(1.16) can now be divided out, *retaining its background dependence* in \(\beta_1^G\). The residual is the log of the normalized Gaussian expectation of the first-step Wilson and measure remainders times this product cutoff. The bare Hessian is the constrained stationary Hessian including the gauge-fixing action. The first term in the physical-action remainder after division by \(g^2\) has degree three in physical displacement, and the regular measure terms are evaluated with their constant at zero removed. This is the bare case of CMP116 (1.41)–(1.43) and §2, with no incoming activities to continue. A possible field-independent vacuum factor drops out of the projector.

Here is the parameter check needed to apply the local majorant to this cutoff. Use the fixed complex *external* field charts of CMP109 for the bare germ, extending it by the same bare formula. The fluctuation cutoff after the change of variables is independent of the external field. At fixed \(g\), use \(\epsilon_1=\theta\) in the *current physical integration radius* of the one-step CMP116 proof. Its complex background radii, the absolute auxiliary fluctuation chart for analytic Taylor expansion, and its spatial-decay parameters are fixed beforehand. In (1.19)–(1.24) the appearances of that integration radius are upper bounds on \(g\|B\|\), including \(4B_0 C_1 e^{16\kappa_1}\theta\le\alpha_2/2\). These hold when \(g\) is small. After localization, (1.36) and (1.42)–(1.43) bound the coefficients of the bounded remainder and of the quadratic forms on this cutoff by a constant times \(\theta\) with the printed support weights. At the bare step the constants involve the fixed group, block, gauge and analytic chart data and no inherited radius. The absolute sign of individual bounded Taylor remainders plays no role in these majorants.

In the cutoff expansion (2.3) a bad bond has \(\|B\|\ge r\). Source estimates (2.22), (2.26)–(2.35) pay an exponential in \(-c r^2\) per such bond, after a fixed Gaussian reserve. Specifically, (2.31) needs \(c r^2\) larger than a fixed constant, and the empty-interaction component in (2.33) needs \(e^{-c r^2}\le C_0\theta\), where \(C_0>0\) is fixed. This last inequality follows from \(r^2\asymp (\log g^{-2})^{2p_0}\). In this *one* use of (2.31)–(2.34), the upper bound denoted there by \(\gamma\) can be set to the present \(g\). Equivalently the displayed inequalities use the actual ratio \(\epsilon_1/g=r\) at this step. This sets no scale-dependent background chart. The subsequent connected summations (2.35)–(2.41) then give the residual an analytic local representation with coefficients bounded by
\[
  |F_X(U,J)|\le C\theta e^{-\kappa d_1(X)} .
\]
One can reserve a smaller positive decay constant for rooted summation. The separate Gaussian term added on CMP116 p.21 after (2.41) is precisely the term kept outside this bound. This proof uses the actual cutoff in the residual and requires no distributional coupling derivative of its characteristic functions.

Finally the fixed first-step minimizing-field tangent decays exponentially from its varied coarse bond on the native chart (CMP102, Proposition 9, (190)). The first derivative of each invariant scalar coefficient at the identity vanishes by constant color invariance. Cauchy on a smaller fixed chart bounds its second field derivative by the displayed local majorant times a fixed inverse squared radius. The chain rule with the two tangent kernels and the rooted support sum therefore gives \(|P^{119}_1(g;z)-P^G_1(z)|\le C'\theta e^{-c'|z|}\). Indeed, if the two tangent distances from \(X\) are both small relative to \(|z|\), the diameter of \(X\) pays the separation, and otherwise a tangent tail pays it. The argument works on finite periodic boxes with periodic distances and summable images. Thus the spatial second moment costs only a fixed constant, and the source's infinite-volume projection exists with the same estimate. This proves (1).

This is a first-step estimate for the actual interacting projected coefficient and its Gaussian benchmark. The bound is in source inverse-coupling decrement units; divide by \(2N^2\) for program units. It does not prove that the single-step benchmark equals \(d\), or that (1) repeats with a depth-independent constant.

## 3. A growing prefix before the first eligible old reset contribution

For the completed ordinary cutoff just identified, the first-layer proof extends on a controlled prefix. Use \(Y_i=g_i^{-2}=2N^2 x_i\), choose a bare \(Y=Y_0\) sufficiently large, and set
\[
 J(Y)=\max\left(0,\left\lfloor\log_L\left[\frac{M}{L^2}
                         \bigl(\log(Y/2)\bigr)^r\right]\right\rfloor\right).
                                                                    \tag{3}
\]
The source's \(M\) here is its fixed localization-block side in current lattice units. All assertions concern the volume-independent coefficient prescription, evaluated on tori admitting the indicated finite prefix. For these \(J(Y)=r\log_L\log Y+O(1)\) steps the unsubtracted regular births satisfy
\[
 \left|\frac{\beta^{\rm full}_{i+1}-\beta^G_{i+1}}{2N^2}\right|
 \le C Y^{-1/2}(1+\log Y)^{p_0},
 \quad 0\le i<J(Y).                                                \tag{4}
\]
Here \(\beta^{\rm full}_{i+1}\) denotes the bulk Wilson projection *used in the complete source construction*, not the curvature Hessian of the full physical log density on an individual mixed component.

**Construction and induction.** First form the homogeneous regular scalar recursion with independent-bond radii \(\delta_i/g_i\), its actual extracted Wilson counterterms, and all previously generated regular activities. Work with unrestricted whole-lattice activity sums or their source thermodynamic limit. The old action includes all prior vacuum and Wilson subtractions, as in CMP109 (1.7). In CMP116 §1, its complete difference under the fluctuation is localized and summed over *all* old generations at (1.23)–(1.36), before performing the current cluster expansion. The inductive input is a family of old births satisfying the common external analytic bound CMP109 (1.18), with the common constant \(E_0\), regular Ward identities and their prescribed extracted coefficients. The proof at a fixed positive sequence uses those field bounds; the earlier coupling parameters are not varied. Any auxiliary coupling regularity in the formulation of the full CMP109 theorem is not used to obtain the present field bound. In particular we do not differentiate cutoff indicators.

Here are additional details on constants for repeated use. CMP116 pp.5–7, (1.15)–(1.24), constructs the external \((U,J)\) and the solution for auxiliary fluctuation \(B'\) on fixed radii \(\alpha'_0,\alpha'_1,\epsilon_2,\epsilon_3\). On p.15, immediately before (2.14), these external charts are restricted to the smaller absolute \(\alpha_0,\alpha_1\) required for the output. The source's smallness inequality \(4B_0 C_1e^{16\kappa_1}\theta_i\le\alpha_2/2\) is satisfied by *decreasing* the raw cutoff \(\theta_i=\delta_i\), after fixing those chart constants. Equation (2.38), p.20, explicitly gives
\[
 C_3=2(L+2)^4 O(1)\,2 E_0 C_1\alpha_4^{-1}\alpha_6^{-1}M^q e^{C_2\kappa_1}.
\]
The constants in (1.36) are absolute after fixing the static scheme. On p.21 the Gaussian normalizer, independent of the integration cutoff, is allocated \(E_0/2\) in the same norm. The normalized residual is bounded by \(O(1)C_3\theta_i\); choose the uniform weak threshold so it is at most the other \(E_0/2\). The only lower bound on the current cutoff needed in the defect estimates (2.31)–(2.35) is met by \(\delta_i/g_i\) as in §2. Thus the estimate *constructs* an allowed fixed-chart representation of the new regular birth and its small residual. It does not start with an extension of a narrow-chart complete-history activity. The inductive smallness budget is per birth, with the old-age sum already performed in CMP116 §1. There is no factor \(i\) multiplying \(E_0\).

Projection of each new birth is on the physical homogeneous germ. The uniform minimizing tangent estimate used by O0 (12.8) on the all-terminal determining layer gives \(C e^{-c\,\mathrm{dist}(X,y)}\) in native current units uniformly in \(i\). This is CMP102 (190) on that layer, where both scale factors \(L^j\eta\) and \(L^{j'}\eta\) equal one. Apply the two field derivatives and the spatial moment as in §2. This gives \(|\beta_{i+1}^{\rm reg}-\beta_{i+1}^G|\le C\theta_i\) and \(|\beta_{i+1}^G|\le D_0\) on the pure prefix by the fixed Gaussian chart bound. Increasing a constant to \(D\), the inverse-coupling step \(|Y_{i+1}-Y_i|\le D\) as long as \(Y_i\ge Y/2\). The first possible exit from \([Y/2,2Y]\) cannot occur by step \(J(Y)\) once \(DJ(Y)<Y/2\). This constructs the prefix without assuming its drift sign or any desired endpoint.

To identify its coefficients inside the full construction, apply CMP119 (2.5) and pp.270–271. At step \(i<J(Y)\), for every birth index \(1\le j\le i\),
\[
  L^{-(i-j)} M R_j\ge L^{-J(Y)}M(\log(Y/2))^r\ge L^2>L.
\]
Thus **no** birth grid fits in a current \(L\)-block, and the sum \(R'_i\) included in the regular fluctuation term before (3.26) is empty. The sum \(R''_i\), the boundary terms, and the actual reset contributions can all exist in the complete density. The source assigns them separately in (3.26) and pp.278–279. At the first step §2 identifies the physical bulk birth. Inductively, in (3.28) on the whole lattice there is the same Wilson weight, constrained Gaussian, coarea, normalized residual and canonical cutoff as in the pure recursion just constructed. The old \(E\)-difference is the same physical difference by the preceding steps, with the same older subtractions, and no mature \(R'\)-difference is added. The source's interior localization after (3.47) agrees with the whole-lattice regular calculation, while excluded carriers return to the boundary ledger. In taking the bulk projection, sum the unrestricted physical activity first: equality of the physical sums is sufficient, and no equality of two individual off-section analytic localizations is asserted. Absolute local convergence and the available external extension of the pure sum identify its physical derivative at the origin. This proves the induction and (4).

For completeness all coupling comparisons (2.6)–(2.9) on this prefix can be checked without a sign. We have \(|Y_m-Y_n|\le D(n-m)\) and \(DJ(Y)/Y=o(1)\), so the backward ratio \(g_m/g_n\) is at most \(1+\beta_0\) for sufficiently large \(Y\). The other ratio obeys \(g_n/g_m\le (1+D(n-m)/Y_n)^{1/2}\). The same elementary estimates on the fixed powers of \(\log Y_n\) as O0 (10.6)–(10.9) apply, using \(Y_n/Y_m=1+O(DJ(Y)/Y)\) when \(Y_n\ge Y_m\). For the opposite direction use \(Y_m\le Y_n+D(n-m)\). Rounding to powers of \(L\) costs the single explicit factor \(L\) in (2.9). Every given finite prefix can be placed on sufficiently large compatible cubes. This does not demand that an unspecified extension beyond the prefix satisfy those comparisons.

**Cumulative consequence.** For any \(0\le h\le k\le J(Y)\) the difference between the actual program decrement and the Gaussian-window decrement is at most \(CJ(Y)Y^{-1/2}(1+\log Y)^{p_0}=o(1)\), uniformly in \(h,k\). Combined with the earlier signed Gaussian window, this gives \(x_0-x_{J(Y)}=dJ(Y)+O(1)\) and in particular fixes the sign and coefficient of the drift averaged over this increasing number of actual UV steps. At its end \(Y_{J(Y)}=Y+O(\log\log Y)\) still diverges. This cannot reach a prescribed finite physical normalization. Nor can one restart the no-mature-birth argument at this endpoint: early reset grids continue to age when the cutoff depth increases.

**Using the age needed to create a reset birth.** The grid condition alone gives (3). The literal Wilson-start LFR procedure adds an earlier age restriction. Let \(q=\lfloor r\log_L\log(Y/2)\rfloor\) and \(J_2(Y)=\max\{0,2q-3\}\). On the same ordinary prescription (4) holds for all \(0\le i<J_2(Y)\) as well. Indeed if \(Y_h\ge Y/2\) so far, then every earlier \(R_h\ge L^q\). LFR I, pp.177–179, writes the interval of a processing operation as \(h=j-N\ge0\) and imposes \(N>N_0\), with \(L^{N_0-1}=R_{j-N_0+1}\). Thus no processing operation exists by age \(q\). There is no independent initial regular \(R^{(1)}\) in the Wilson-start ordinary transformation, as CMP119 (1.28)–(1.30) shows. The \(R''\) contribution from (3.26) is zero when its earlier \(R\) input is zero; new reset contributions enter in the subsequent R-operation described on p.279. Hence any possibly nonzero \(R^{(j)}\) has \(j>q\). To enter the regular bulk at step \(i\) this very birth must also satisfy \(i-j\ge\log_L(MR_j/L)\ge q-1\), since \(M\ge1\). Therefore no nonzero birth can enter at \(i<2q-3\), leaving slack for endpoint conventions. Before this bound the actual regular beta computation reduces to the same complete old-\(E\) induction. Use its bounded \(|Y_{i+1}-Y_i|\) and \(DJ_2(Y)<Y/2\) to close the weak-prefix argument as in §3. In particular the first merely grid-eligible ages can have an identically zero correction. This doubles the leading \(\log\log Y\) length; it neither establishes that a nonzero projected reset correction ever occurs nor supplies an upper bound on its first occurrence.

## 4. Real pressure and the first eligible feedback comparisons

Let \(k_*\) be the first step at which the source can absorb an old grid into its regular fluctuation expression. It is sufficient here that a grid is eligible: its associated activity can vanish. Before that step the proof in §3 applies at every index, whether or not the conservative bound (3) has been reached. Moreover
\[
 J(Y)\le k_*\le 1+\lceil\log_L(MR_1)\rceil=O(1+\log\log Y)
\]
for trajectories continued that far, since the first grid alone fits by the upper endpoint. One may run the preceding no-absorption induction up to this first eligibility test. Its threshold uses only already determined values of \(g_j\). Taking \(Y\) large, the stepwise drift bound used in §3 gives \(Y/2\le Y_j\le 2Y\) on the generated prehistory, including step \(k_*\). No actual \(R\)-birth is added to the comparison if the selector produces none.

**Real pressure comparison.** At an arbitrary admitted step \(k\), compare two *new* homogeneous regular log integrals on the same real physical output \(W\). Both keep the complete incoming renormalized \(E_k\), including earlier generated effects, all its actual Wilson and vacuum subtractions, and identical Wilson, gauge, Haar/coarea and cutoff factors. The second omits only the current absorbed \(R'_k\)-fluctuation difference. Let \(I\) be the set of birth indices in this difference. Then on the source's admitted real output chart, after subtracting each value at identity,
\[
   V_{k+1}^{-1}\big| F^{\rm full}_{k+1}(W)-F^{E\text{-only}}_{k+1}(W)\big|
    \le 4 L^4 R_1 \sum_{j\in I}g_j^{\kappa_0}.                       \tag{5}
\]
Here \(V_t\) counts sites in current units and \(R_1\) on the right denotes the constant in CMP119 (2.44), not the first grid radius.

Indeed for each \(j\in I\), apply CMP119 Theorem 2, (2.44), p.263, to the full homogeneous domain for the old \(R^{(j)}\). Only the terminal determining layer is present. Its right side is thus \(R_1 g_j^{\kappa_0} V_k\) at an admitted real input. Apply it once to the original constrained minimizing field at the current real fluctuation, and once to the zero-fluctuation minimizing field, using the latter's representation at scale \(k\). Both configurations belong to the incoming regular domain on the matched ordinary cutoff over admitted \(W\): CMP119 (3.3)–(3.5) and (3.17)–(3.20) remove the redundant older indicators on precisely that family. One can use a strict physical neighborhood of flat for \(W\). Thus the absolute change \(D(A,W)\) in the exponent is at most \(2R_1V_k\sum_{j\in I}g_j^{\kappa_0}\). This is a bound on each vacuum-subtracted *sum* in (2.44), sufficient for this estimate on the actual sum in the exponent. Positivity gives \(e^{-\|D\|_\infty}\le \int e^D d\mu_{E,W}\le e^{\|D\|_\infty}\) for the normalized real homogeneous law with incoming \(E_k\). Taking the log, applying the same estimate at identity, and using \(V_k/V_{k+1}=L^4\) proves (5). No inverse probability or volume-dependent lower bound occurs. The endpoint field dependence of the Gaussian determinant cancels between the two integrals. We have not replaced the probability law of actual mixed histories by this homogeneous integral: the latter defines precisely their bulk coefficient.

At the first grid-eligible cut, even if all its indicated births vanish, (5) is \(O((1+\log\log Y)Y^{-\kappa_0/2})\). Crucially, at this particular first step the \(E\)-only comparator has exactly the incoming physical action constructed with a fixed analytic output chart in §3. At a later step the right side of (5) still controls the real pressure change on its admitted domain, but a fixed-chart estimate for the \(E\)-only comparator then requires proof. Neither a curvature estimate nor the O0 Gaussian-modulus bound follows from the pressure inequality by direct differentiation.

**Physical projection at an eligible cut.** The comparison below covers the first grid-eligible cut. More substantively let \(k\le C_*\log\log Y\) be any cut where a nonconstant absorbed old-\(R\) fluctuation first appears in the homogeneous regular bulk, if such a cut occurs in that interval. Every preceding homogeneous bulk birth then agrees with the no-absorption recursion. For (6) and its proof write \(k_*\) for the chosen cut. The proof gives no upper bound on the age of the first nonconstant insertion. Suppose this particular outgoing step is admitted with the source's current and outgoing radii and interscale inequalities. In particular \(Y_{k_*+1}\asymp Y\), by (2.6) and the preceding estimates. No admission of any later step is required. For a fixed source choice with \(\kappa_0\ge7\), the additional program decrement from the entire first eligible old-\(R\) sum is bounded by
\[
 \frac{|\mathcal P_\infty(F^{\rm full}_{k_*+1}-F^{E\text{-only}}_{k_*+1})|}{2N^2}
 \le C Y^{2-\kappa_0/2}(1+\log Y)^C .                                \tag{6}
\]
The projector in (6) acts on the extensive physical functional by its translation-invariant per-site polarization kernel. This estimate compares the two actual real integrals just defined. It remains valid if several old birth indices first become eligible together.

**Flat output neighborhood.** Put \(n=k_*+1\), \(\xi=L^{-n}\), and \(a_* =\min(1,\alpha_{0,n},\alpha_{1,n})\) with the source's actual parameters. The complete outgoing *regular* birth has the analytic carrier bounds (2.26)–(2.28) on the one-scale domains CMP109 (1.11)–(1.16). The multiscale boundary domains (2.34)–(2.39) are not needed for this physical bulk projection. On an identity-centered carrier, a component sup ball
\[
  \|\log U\|_\infty,\ \|J\|_\infty\le c a_*\xi^2
\]
is contained in the strict primitive chart. In the identity gauge the link potential and first difference cost at most \(1/\xi\) and \(2/\xi^2\), respectively, and the plaquette deviation costs a fixed multiple of the link deviation. The stricter (i)–(iii) criterion on CMP109 p.263, immediately after (1.16), bounds the reconstructed normalized plaquettes and currents by a fixed multiple of the smaller input constant. Taking \(c\) small leaves the required margins in condition (iv). This check takes place about the flat pair and is independent of the carrier. For the physical output \(W=\exp(i b)\), the homogeneous global relative-minimizer estimate of CMP102 Proposition 9 gives \(\|\log U_n(W)\|_\infty\le C\|b\|_\infty\) at a fixed smaller flat chart. Its physical current costs at worst \(C\xi^{-3}\) times the link deviation by the finite-difference current formula CMP109 (1.8). Thus \(c'a_*\xi^5\) suffices as an external complex link-amplitude radius. Because \(Y_n\asymp Y\), \(a_*\ge cY^{-1/2}\) by (2.28), and \(L^n\le(1+\log Y)^C\), an external radius \(r=c_1Y^{-1}\) fits for large \(Y\). We reserve a fixed fraction for each of the finitely many Fourier test profiles used below. Its real segment also lies inside the homogeneous physical output cutoff: the flat-centered regularity conditions for each tested reconstruction are satisfied with strict margin by shrinking \(c_1\) and taking \(Y\) large, using the same bounds and \(\epsilon_n\asymp Y^{-1/2}(\log Y)^{p_0}\). The \(E\)-only comparator has its own already constructed fixed chart. Only the two scalar **outputs** need these complex domains. The added old \(R\) term is never integrated on a deformed fluctuation contour.

**From real values to Fourier Hessians.** Use any fixed finite sum of real unit sine/cosine waves on the terminal links, at any compatible lattice momentum and in the fixed color/direction components. Write \(b=t v\) and divide the vacuum-subtracted log difference by \(V_n\). The external coefficient bound and its rooted sum bound the absolute holomorphic pressure on \(|t|<2r\) by \(M\le C Y^b\) for a fixed \(b\); one can absorb polylogarithmic factors into this bound. By (5), on \([-r,r]\) its absolute value is at most \(m=C(1+\log\log Y)Y^{-\kappa_0/2}\). The second derivative at zero is bounded by
\[
 C r^{-2}m\{1+\log^+(M/m)\}^{5}.
\]
To see this, rescale the interval to \([-1,1]\). The Chebyshev coefficients are bounded by \(2m\) using their real integral, and by \(C M \rho^{-j}\) for a fixed \(\rho>1\) using an ellipse in the disk of radius two. Markov's inequality gives second derivative at most \(j^4\) for the degree-\(j\) Chebyshev polynomial. Split the series at \(\lceil\log^+(M/m)/\log\rho\rceil\) and sum the geometric tail. The case \(m=0\) follows by uniqueness of analytic continuation. Polarization with pairs of sine and cosine waves controls every entry of the Fourier Hessian of the difference by \(C Y^2 m(1+\log Y)^5\). Translation covariance of the summed whole-lattice regular birth, restored for the absorbed grid by its very eligibility, is used here. Applying the estimate at all compatible momenta and Fourier inversion bounds every kernel entry by the same supremum. The pressure comparison thus does not require spatial decay of the difference of two real measures.

**Spatial second moment.** For each of the two outputs separately, the carrier decay of the undifferentiated birth, Cauchy in the primitive chart and the two differentiated minimizing tangents from CMP102 (190) give a kernel bound
\[
 |P(0,z)|\le C Y^b\exp[-c |z|/(1+\log Y)^b]                          \tag{7}
\]
for some fixed \(b\). In detail, the local first derivative vanishes at flat by invariance under a constant color rotation. The two first tangents on \(X\) have exponential distance factors in current units, as in O0 (12.8). Converting a link tangent into the unnormalized current costs at most \(C\xi^{-3}\) and a finite stencil enlargement. Two Cauchy factors cost at most \(C(a_*\xi^2)^{-2}\). Summing the rooted native carrier majorant on a smaller decay rate pays any support polynomial. In native output units the cubes entering the regular birth have fixed \(M\) or at worst \(O(MR_n)\) size; uniformly on this first shell the latter is bounded by \(C(1+\log Y)^r\). A long separation is paid either by a long carrier or by one of the two tangent distances. Volume factors in converting between fine and current units are powers of \(\xi^{-1}\), also polylogarithmic here. This proves (7) with periodic distances on compatible boxes and with the same constants in the thermodynamic limit. The argument uses the analytic representation of each new birth; no uniform complex bound on their difference is presumed from (5).

Split the spatial moment at \(C'(1+\log Y)^{b+1}\). The inside four-dimensional weighted site count is polynomial in \(1+\log Y\). Choose \(C'\) large enough that the tail from (7), including the spatial second moment, is \(O(Y^{-\kappa_0})\). The uniform Fourier estimate then proves (6) after the thermodynamic limit. ## 5. Numerical bootstrap from signed windows

A transient may be removed from the marginal coordinate explicitly. Let \(d_j=D_{j+1}^G-D_j^G\), \(a_j=D_j^G-dj\), \(|a_j|\le B\), and write \(e_j=x_j-x_{j+1}-d_j\) along the **same admitted data**. Then \(y_j=x_j+a_j\) satisfies
\[
  y_j-y_{j+1}=d+e_j.
\]
Consequently, on a weak prefix where \(|e_j|\le d/2\), if \(x_j\ge A\ge4B+2\), then for \(0\le h\le k\)
\[
 x_h\ge x_k-2B+(d/2)(k-h),\qquad
 x_h\le x_k+2B+(3d/2)(k-h).

 \sum_{h<k}x_h^{-s}\le C_{s,d}x_k^{1-s},\quad

### A sharper missing inequality for the actual reset memory

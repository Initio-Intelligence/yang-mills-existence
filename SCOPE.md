# Scope of the main estimates

Let $a=L^{-K}\ell_0$ be the lattice spacing and $\ell_0$ the fixed physical scale. A continuum assertion must hold as $K$ and the physical torus size increase, for the same bare parameters and the same renormalized field family. The following distinctions determine which parts of the construction can presently be used together.

**The coupling coordinate.** Write $Y_i=g_i^{-2}$ in the specified Wilson convention and $D_i^G$ for the accumulated projected Gaussian decrement. If $D_i^G=d_B i+O(1)$ uniformly, then $Y_i+D_i^G-d_B i$ differs from $Y_i$ by a bounded amount. Together with the stated comparison and admission estimates, this yields the asymptotically free recurrence in the ultraviolet direction. Computing $d_B$ fixes the slope; it does not show that the accumulated error is bounded. The [transverse Gaussian calculation](native_estimates/gaussian_calibration.md) isolates this last estimate. Even with calibration, [stopping at a positive weak endpoint](physical_scale/positive_stopped_endpoint.md) and [matching an independently prescribed value](coupling/prescribed_endpoint.md) are different assertions. Seven strictly positive reflected measurements at each finite cutoff define finite normalization factors; they provide no cutoff-independent bound on those factors without [quantitative susceptibility estimates](physical_scale/physical_normalization.md).

**The conditional boundary law (C-05, C-06 and C-08).** For birth, evaluation, and forcing levels $b\le n\le j$, the two different ages are $n-b$ and $j-n$. Interpolating two estimates of the *same* Wilson-subtracted scalar gives a summable bound under the stated strict exponent inequalities. The remaining question concerns the scalar to which that bound is applied. Differentiating one minimizing field with respect to its boundary data is different from differentiating a composition of reconstructions with earlier innovations fixed. After integrating interior variables, an exterior derivative remains; only a scalar common to the integration tuple cancels in the conditional ratio, and it must be retained in the mass. The [partial gauge identity and anchored-score criterion, PG.18–PG.20](continuum/partial_gauge_transport.md) specify the missing estimate. Thus the [regular conditional moment and decay theorem](boundary/conditional_law_theorem.md) is available **if** that estimate and the other chart, trajectory, support and coercivity hypotheses hold. The all-history C-05 application remains open. Even if it holds, C-08 alone is not a theorem about the outer law. The [Schur comparison](continuum/outer_conditional_laws.md) explains which term it bounds. Changing the R test or the variables held fixed also requires [compatibility of the conditioning law](native_estimates/local_test.md).

**Transport of physical fields (M-04 through M-07).** If an integration support changes discontinuously with retained data, differentiability of the integrand does not bound the derivative of the integral. The [local-mask construction](observable_transport/README.md) accounts for the support and the normalizer. It gives estimates through three regular integrations, preserving the microscopic insertion and the separate origins of its current. It also gives improvements for specific quadratic channels. The estimates do not yet transfer the whole quadratic function and each of its currents with constants uniform in the number of integrations. Nor does fixed depth control all large-field and moving-core sectors. A conditional order-one contraction for an analytic family is distinct from a proof for every order and history.

**From conditional estimates to a field theory.** The cumulant of the full measure includes cumulants of retained conditional means. Exact decompositions and bounds on coincidence-flat native tests do not themselves imply the required estimate on all physically normalized transported fields. [The hierarchy bound ER.7](continuum/continuum_bounds.md) specifies what would suffice. The rotation theorem likewise needs its actual coefficient and contact estimates. Fixed-torus comparison of transfer operators does not establish the rate on an expanding family of cubic tori; [the energy and periodic-state conditions](THEOREMS_AND_OPEN_PROBLEMS.md#energy-bounds-and-periodic-states) make the distinction precise. Reconstruction, ultraviolet identification, and phase selection all apply to one limiting field family. None supplies these missing estimates by itself.
